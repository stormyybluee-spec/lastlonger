## What changed for the Desk's view

The energy shock eased. Brent fell from about $110 on 2 November to about $92 and the IEA now sees a fourth quarter surplus, so the "Brent near $110, Hormuz mostly closed" line must go, though Hormuz is still contested and talks are unresolved. The watt-dollar thesis is intact and sharper: the market punished AI spending when cash flow turned negative (Alphabet free cash flow minus $9.1B) and when an Oracle financing worry met a political shift, yet NVIDIA guided to $130B. The AI boom is now clearly financed by debt and private credit, and its first strain shows at the weaker borrowers (Oracle, neoclouds). Stablecoin supply shrank to about $289B, so the "captive demand" story is not growing for now, though banks keep building rails (tokenized deposits, a bank dollar stablecoin for 2027). France: no budget passed, Senate and 17 December ordinance dates ahead, spread near 148bp, S&P on 27 November. Malaysia held at 2.75% with growth above 5%.
