# Energy: gold's old job

Landscape chapter 07. Stack: Intelligence (graded, dated findings). Id: ls-07. Facts as of 2026-10-23.

Web page: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/#ls-07 . Text built 2026-11-12. Guide for AI readers: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/llms.txt

*Energy will not replace the dollar you spend. It is replacing the job gold used to do: the scarce physical thing that limits what any money can buy.*

This was the project's founding question: will energy (watts, tonnage, compute) replace the value role that gold, then bonds, then credit played? The answer, Plausible at 65, has three parts. Energy fails as money you spend. It succeeds as the real ceiling on output. And it is entering the dollar system as collateral, a "watt-dollar".

### Part one: why a kilowatt hour is bad money

*[Picture on the web page: The three jobs of money: medium of exchange, store of value and unit of account. A delivered kilowatt hour scored 1 of 5 on both spending and saving, because it is perishable, tied to a place and getting cheaper over time.]*

- No kWh-denominated stablecoin or token of material size exists. Every energy-linked instrument found in 2025 and 2026 settles in dollars (Da-01, energy as spendable money, Refuted through 2050, 5).
- A kWh has no single price. In 2026 PJM wholesale power rose 41% while California hit historic lows.
- A fixed-kWh unit is unstable in both directions (Da-06, Plausible, 80). Solar modules fell from $132 a watt in 1975 to $0.265 in 2024, so over decades a kWh coin would lose value. In 2026 delivered power got dearer, so right now it would gain. Any fix (cap the units, peg to a share of total energy) turns it back into gold, equity or Bitcoin.
- History: every energy currency proposal since Ford's 1921 energy dollar failed.

### Part two: energy as the binding constraint

Gold limited money by law until 1971. After that the limit was only political: how much debt markets would absorb. In an AI and robot economy, output scales with delivered power, and power cannot be printed. The evidence is money committed: PJM's capacity auction cleared at its cap twice with a 6,623 MW shortfall ($16.4 billion), and data centers explain about 5,100 MW of the peak rise. Ruling Da-03: Confirmed for H0, 80. The refinement matters: the binding input is deliverable firm power plus a grid connection in specific load regions, not energy in general.

> The AI debt dividend is an energy-supply bet (Dc-10, Plausible, 55). Musk himself said electricity additions grow about 4% a year while chip output grows far faster. If power lags, the AI boom shows up as power-price inflation and higher real interest rates, not as tax revenue that shrinks the debt. That is the link between this chapter and the treadmill in Chapter 2.

### Part three: the watt-dollar

*[Picture on the web page: Energy as collateral and ceiling under the dollar. Power plants, data centers, power contracts and GPUs back new dollar loans, while delivered power caps how much real output those dollars can claim.]*

Energy is also taking a growing share of the job Treasuries do as prime collateral, but inside the dollar. The Hyperion data center financing ($27.3 billion), 55.9 GW of power purchase agreements, and GPU-secured debt of $48.4 billion (by a T4 tracker) all back dollar loans, and the first compute futures settle in dollars. This is the heir of the 1974 petrodollar, when a physical input became a dollar commodity. Ruling Da-02: Plausible, 75. Two corrections keep it from Confirmed: the collateral is mostly the credit of the big tech firms with energy assets as backup, and the recycling half of the petrodollar (foreign surpluses into Treasuries) is not shown. The scale is still small, about a third of the stablecoin float.

### Musk's physics currency and the allocation problem

Musk's "currency measured in wattage and tonnage" is substantively the 1930s Technocracy plan for energy certificates, a movement his grandfather Joshua Haldeman belonged to from 1936 to 1941. He names no issuer, redemption rule or supply rule. Ruling Da-05: Speculative, 15, for the far future. The robust part of the analysis is the governance problem: a physics unit is either a market price (just a commodity index priced in some money) or an allocation, in which case someone sets the allotments. In Musk's version the natural allocators would be whoever owns generation, launch and compute. That is a concentration of power far larger than central bank discretion.

Compute as a unit of account between machines is Speculative (Da-04, 25). GPU-hours exist as a contract quantity, but nothing is priced in them, and the machine rail actually scaling is the dollar stablecoin.

### Who wins, who loses

The winners are whoever owns firm power and grid connections. People on fixed dollar incomes lose whenever AI demand outruns grid supply (Da-10, Plausible, 60). In the year to September 2026 US energy prices rose 20.4% and real hourly earnings fell 0.7%. No bill anywhere indexes a public transfer to energy or compute. And a tax on watts would not save the budget: 1 cent per kWh on all US electricity would raise about $40 billion, 0.74% of federal receipts.

- **Myth:** Energy will become the new money.
- **Reality:** Energy becomes the new limit and the new collateral, priced in dollars. The sequence the court found: energy as collateral now, energy as an index for bonds or transfers possibly after 2035, and energy as an accounting unit only in a post-scarcity world, where the real question is who controls the allocation.

**Check yourself.** Q: Why would a currency pegged to one kilowatt hour be a poor savings vehicle over 30 years?
A: Because solar keeps pushing the cost of energy down. If a kWh gets cheaper faster than other goods, prices in kWh rise, so each coin buys less. A 4% yearly fall in real kWh cost gives about 4.2% yearly inflation in kWh terms.
