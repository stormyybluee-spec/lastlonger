# The response: how Washington is managing it

Landscape chapter 03. Stack: Intelligence (graded, dated findings). Id: ls-03. Facts as of 2026-10-23.

Web page: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/#ls-03 . Text built 2026-11-12. Guide for AI readers: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/llms.txt

*They are not shrinking the debt. They are managing who must buy it.*

Faced with the treadmill, a government has a short menu: tax more, spend less, grow faster, inflate, default, or make sure there are always buyers for the debt. Washington's revealed choice, read from where money actually moved, is the last one. The project calls it captive demand: write rules and build plumbing so that banks, money funds, stablecoin issuers and foreign governments end up holding Treasury bills. Ruling Db-01: Confirmed, 75.

### The captive demand toolkit

1. Fund with bills. Coupon auction sizes are frozen with guidance to stay that way for several quarters, and the bill share rose to 22.36% ($7.118 trillion), from 21.53% a year earlier. The refunding statement itself talks about sizing bills to private and Fed demand.
2. Free up bank balance sheets. The enhanced supplementary leverage ratio (eSLR) relief lets banks hold more Treasuries.
3. Write bills into the law of digital money. From 2027-01-18 every compliant US stablecoin must hold cash, deposits or Treasury bills of 93 days or less (the GENIUS Act).
4. Buy back long bonds. Treasury bought back $230 billion of older debt in 2026, funded with bills.
5. Keep foreign holders from dumping. Press Japan to borrow against its Treasuries through the Fed's FIMA repo line instead of selling, and support the yen with euros rather than with dollars.

> "Just as President Abraham Lincoln's National Currency Act of 1863 established a national framework for bank-issued currency, the GENIUS Act establishes a federal framework for payment stablecoins in the digital age." (Scott Bessent, OCC release, 2026-10-21)

> The analogy is the tell. Under the 1863 law, national banks issued notes that had to be backed by federal bonds, which created a permanent buyer for Civil War debt. Bessent names the framework, not the bond backing, so this is intent evidence of moderate weight. But the decoding panel graded "stablecoins as a deliberate captive demand tool for Treasury bills" D1 (80), partly because the Treasury Secretary chose that comparison himself.

### What the 2026 stress test showed

Round 3 checked the toolkit against a year of hard data, and the results corrected some of the project's own earlier lines. Three surprises stand out.

- Stablecoins did not carry the load. Total supply went from $304.9 billion (2025-10-21) to $303.8 billion (2026-10-22), flat for a year after a May peak of $315.5 billion. Over the same year bank deposits rose $1,225 billion, money funds about $505 billion to $7.92 trillion, and the Fed's own bill holdings $370 billion to $565.5 billion. In 2026 the real captive buyers were the Fed and money funds, not tokens.
- The crisis line went unused. The Fed's FIMA repo facility was at zero every week from 18 February to 14 October 2026, apart from a $3.0 billion blip on 4 February. Japan said on 3 August it "plans to utilize" FIMA, then did not. Swap lines peaked at $481 million. Round 5 update: FIMA was still zero on 21 October, 35 straight weeks. Japan's reserve data show both 2026 interventions were paid for by selling dollar securities.
- Foreign governments sold instead. Foreign official Treasuries in Fed custody fell $254 billion (9.1%) from the February peak to $2,549.1 billion, while those holders kept $331.9 billion of their own cash at the Fed. Japan spent ¥27.1 trillion on interventions in 2026.

*[Picture on the web page: The dollar recycling loop. Dollars sent abroad come back as purchases of Treasuries. In 2026 part of that loop ran in reverse: official holders sold $254 billion to raise crisis cash.]*

So the project reworded its own summary. FIMA moved from "chosen tool" to "announced but unused" (R3X-03, Confirmed). The holder base has shifted from steady foreign governments to leveraged, price-sensitive private buyers such as basis traders, money funds and stablecoins (Db-12, Confirmed, 80). That keeps the system working in good weather and makes it more fragile in a funding shock.

### The Gulf asked, then borrowed

The UAE asked for a dollar swap line in April, and Bessent said "numerous" Gulf and Asian allies had asked. No Gulf line had been announced by 22 October. Gulf states instead issued a record $102.7 billion of bonds and sukuk in the first half of 2026. The court's reading: the backstop for non-core states is moving from the Fed's technical lines to Treasury's political ones, which buys loyalty but makes access conditional and nudges states toward gold and self-insurance.

### The Fed under Warsh: hiking, not helping

The Fed raised its rate 12-0 on 2026-09-16 to 3.75% to 4.00%, its first hike since 2023. The President wanted rates "near 1% or lower." Markets on 22 October gave an October hike only 11% to 12% and a December 9 hike 75% to 79%, with year-end rates most likely at 4.25%. Warsh being out as chair by mid-2027 was priced at 9.5%. The Supreme Court kept Governor Cook in June. In plain terms, the one institution standing between the White House's cheap-money wish and the outcome is still standing.

The court's ruling on the overall path (Db-08, Plausible, 60) is a three-part muddle: captive demand at the short end, passive inflation in the background, and hope for AI growth. None is enough alone. The likeliest exit in 2028 to 2035 is managed financial repression: inflation modestly above coupons, regulatory demand for bills, and a Fed whose leadership becomes more cooperative after 2028. The decoders agree that repression through plumbing has already started (DCC-L1, D1, 70).

- **Myth:** The Fed will simply print money to bail out the Treasury.
- **Reality:** In 2026 the Fed did the opposite and hiked. The quiet help comes from rules that make others buy bills, and from inflation running above the average cost of the debt. That is repression through plumbing, not printing.

**Check yourself.** Q: Why does a rising share of short-term bills make the system more fragile?
A: Because bills reprice within months, so any rate rise hits the budget fast, and because the new buyers of bills are leveraged and price-sensitive. When stress comes, they sell or demand higher yields at the worst moment.
