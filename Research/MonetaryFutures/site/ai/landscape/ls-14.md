# Behind the curtain: oil dollars, Treasury buyers and the alignment story, graded

Landscape chapter 14. Stack: Intelligence (graded, dated findings). Id: ls-14. Facts as of 2026-10-23.

Web page: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/#ls-14 . Text built 2026-11-12. Guide for AI readers: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/llms.txt

*A three-hour interview claimed that oil dollars, Treasury buyers and the great powers move by hidden agreement. Much of that is not in any public document the desk could find. This chapter is the desk's first try at reasoning past where the documents stop, with every guess graded and dated.*

**In short**
- Gulf states hold more US Treasuries than in January 2023. Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates (UAE) and Kuwait held $330 billion in August 2026, up from $219 billion. The UAE's long-term US bonds fell while its US shares rose, and Saudi holdings fell $19 billion in the 2026 crisis over the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow sea gate that most Gulf oil ships through. Saudi Arabia's main fund also cut its own filed US share portfolio, from $57 billion in 2021 to about $12 billion. A straight swap of bonds for shares is not shown for the three states as a whole.
- China is the largest single buyer of Gulf crude, but it takes about a third, not most of it. No source shows 30% of Gulf oil paid in yuan. The number may come from China's own trade, 28% of which was settled in yuan in early 2025 (structural, about even at 45%). Much of the oil China buys from Russia and Iran is also paid in yuan.
- A 1974 arrangement did exist: a Treasury buying window offered to Saudi Arabia, with Saudi holdings reported only inside a group total. A 1979 US government audit documents the window. It also reports officials saying Treasury pledged secrecy, which Treasury denied. The secrecy ended in 2016. What is left is a public trade of US security for Saudi investment pledges.
- Americans hold about two thirds of US government debt. Since 2019 its growth has come from private buyers and the US central bank, not foreign governments (structural, about 47%). The private buyers are money market funds, hedge funds (funds that often borrow to trade) and owners whose bonds sit with banks in London, Belgium and the Caymans.
- The claim that the US, the Gulf and China are secretly aligned found no channel and no enforcement, and real costs fell on every side. The record fits shared interests and one public cue, safe passage through Hormuz (levels 0 and 1 on a scale from 0 to 3, where 3 means one command). The desk puts about 42% on shared interests and that public cue being the whole story. A quiet deal that can be denied (level 2) is not shown, but public data could miss a small one, so the desk cannot call it ruled out. A secret plan between all three, with the conflict staged, is remote, about 1%, so it stays an open question, not a finding.
- Every inferred point below carries a grade, a likelihood and a date when the record will confirm or kill it. Nothing here is advice.

> **How to read the grades.** This chapter is in the Intelligence part of the site: findings that age, not rules. Each guess is graded. Documented means a source says it. Footprint means its side effects are measured in public records. Structural means it follows from incentives and accounting. Pattern means it rests on a similar case later proven. Conjecture is a coherent story not yet checked. A chain of guesses is only as likely as its links multiplied together, so five links at 70% each make a 17% story. Anything under 10% is shown as an open question, not a finding. A percent beside a grade is the desk's chance that the point is true. Likelihood words: remote is under 15%, unlikely 15 to 40%, about even 40 to 65%, likely 65 to 90%. A very low chance is called open, not closed, because missing marks in public records cannot rule out a small quiet deal. Coordination levels: 0 means the same interests, 1 means following the same public cue, 2 means a quiet deal that can be denied, 3 means one command.

### 1. Where do Gulf oil dollars go now?

The claim, in short: the Gulf agreed in 1974 to sell oil in dollars and buy US government debt. Now the oil goes mostly to China, about 30% is paid in yuan, and Gulf funds buy US shares instead of Treasuries. Each part leaves marks in public records, so each part can be checked.

| The claim | What the records show | Evidence grade | Chance the claim is true |
|---|---|---|---|
| China takes most Gulf oil | China is the largest buyer, near 5.4 million barrels a day from the Middle East in 2025, Iran included. That is 30% to 40% of what passed Hormuz in 2024. Asia as a whole takes 84% of the crude shipped through Hormuz (2024) | Footprint (customs and tanker data via press) | Unlikely as stated |
| About 30% of Gulf oil is paid in yuan | No source found. The yuan is about 3% of payments on SWIFT, a bank messaging network, and that is a floor. China settles 28% of its own goods trade in yuan, a possible origin of the number | Structural, origin about 45% | Remote for all Gulf oil, unlikely for Gulf sales to China |
| Gulf funds buy US shares instead of Treasuries | Gulf Treasury holdings rose 51%, from $219 billion to $330 billion (January 2023 to August 2026). The UAE's US share holdings grew too, as at Norway's oil fund, while Saudi Arabia's main fund cut its filed US shares | Holdings documented. That shares came on top is structural, about 55% | About even for single funds, unlikely for the states as a whole |
| A 1974 deal tied Gulf oil to Treasuries | Narrower: a buying window, opened to all central banks in 1975, and holdings kept inside a group total. Not a treaty on oil prices. A popular story says the deal ran out in June 2024. No such end date is documented, so nothing expired then | Documented (US audit, 1979, and the 2016 release) | True in its narrow form |

> **A hidden deal that was real.** The 1974 arrangement is the desk's best proof that hidden deals do happen. The US Treasury let Saudi Arabia buy its bonds outside the public auctions through an add-on window offered in 1974. Saudi holdings were reported only inside one combined total for several oil exporting countries, so no one could see Saudi Arabia's own number. A 1979 US audit described the window and reported officials saying it came with a secrecy pledge, which Treasury denied. The window was described in public in 1979, but Saudi holdings stayed hidden for over 40 years. Treasury began listing them in May 2016, after a news agency's records request. By the desk's reading, it took a handful of officials and one channel, and the exchange was money for security. That is the shape a real hidden deal has, and the shape to look for next time.

What is left today is public. In November 2025 the US and Saudi Arabia signed a defence agreement. Alongside it came Saudi pledges of close to $1 trillion of investment in the US, aimed at US infrastructure, technology and industry. No Treasury purchases were named. A public exchange of security for money is coordination in the plain sense. But the desk found no published text of the defence agreement, so a Treasury clause is not found, not ruled out. The war with Iran and the closure of Hormuz from March 2026 also cut Gulf flows to China this year. So 2026 numbers carry a shock on top of any trend. One hidden flow sits near Malaysia: Iranian oil reaches China labelled as Malaysian, and China's customs show none from Iran.

### 2. Who really funds the US Treasury market?

The claim, in short: the biggest foreign holders are not countries but fund managers using offshore centres. They are funded by cheap borrowing in Japan. And there are $15 trillion of offshore dollars, more than double the money supply. The records partly support the first part. Custody centres, where banks hold bonds for clients, hold more than Japan and China, but the owners are not shown. The records correct the second part. The third mixes up debt and money.

| What was checked | Finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Who holds the most | Accounts booked in four custody centres (the UK, Belgium, the Caymans and Luxembourg) held $2.41 trillion in August 2026, more than Japan and China together ($1.66 trillion). In January 2023 it was the other way round | Documented (US Treasury) |
| Who is growing | Governments held 66.5% of foreign-owned Treasuries in January 2015 and 40.2% in August 2026. Money funds, hedge funds, the Fed (the US central bank) and private foreigners grew | Shares documented. That they replaced states: structural, about 55% |
| Hidden hedge fund holdings | The Cayman line misses about $1.4 trillion of hedge fund bonds (Fed staff, end of 2024). US money funds are the key cash lenders | Undercount documented. That these funds borrow from US money funds, inside the US: structural, about 55% |
| Japan's fall in 2026 | About $171 billion from February to August, close to what Japan spent to hold up the yen, which means selling dollar assets to buy yen. It was not investors repaying cheap yen loans they had used to buy higher paying assets | Footprint, about 70% |
| $15 trillion of offshore dollars | $14.7 trillion of dollar loans and bonds owed outside the US (BIS). Debt, not money, and about 0.63 times the US money supply | Figure documented. Reading: structural, about 60% |

> **What it means.** No state was found agreeing to fund the US deficit. Each holder follows its own return: money funds lend for interest, hedge funds trade the gap between bond prices and futures, private foreigners buy for yield. That is level 0 on the coordination scale: shared incentives, no agreement found (structural, about 47%). It also means the market leans more on private money that is partly borrowed than it did in 2019, which matters on days when that money needs cash at once.

### 3. Does the three kinds of country lens predict anything?

The interview sorts countries into three kinds. In the first, capital sits above the state (the US, the UK, Europe). In the second, the state kept its resources and bargains through a national fund (the Gulf, Norway, Singapore). In the third, the state sits above capital (China). The desk wrote down what the lens predicts before pulling any data. Then it scored the lens against a plain one. Does the country issue a reserve currency, one other central banks hold as savings? And is it a net lender or borrower to the world?

| Prediction | Three kinds lens | Plain lens |
|---|---|---|
| Whether foreigners hold a large or small share of the government's debt, checked in 5 countries | 4 of 5 countries right (missed Japan) | 4 of 5 countries right |
| Central bank buys bonds in a crisis | Right for the US, UK and Japan | Same three |
| Capital controls | Not a fair test, because the three kinds are defined by who controls capital, so the answer was built in | Right |
| Crisis behaviour in 2020 and 2022 | Matched | Matched |

In this small test it did no better than the plain lens. The desk puts about 46% on this result, that the lens does no better than the plain one, still holding when more countries are added (structural). The chance that the lens adds real predictive power is about 1% on this evidence. The two numbers do not conflict. The first says the tie will probably hold, the second says a win for the lens is very unlikely. It stays an open question, not a finding, because so few countries had data. Debt holder data covered only 5 of the 19 countries (too few to separate the lenses) and the 2008 crisis was not coded. The test continues on the other 14 next round, due early 2027.

### 4. Are the great powers secretly aligned?

The interview's headline is that the US, the Gulf states and China are aligned behind the scenes, and that the talk of a coming world war is only for show. A second claim says finance pushed trade toward China on purpose with the 2025 tariffs. The desk split each into parts with named actors and checked what each part would have to leave behind. The strongest form of the claim is a few quiet understandings on single items, such as shipping, while the conflict is real.

| Part | What the records show | Level shown |
|---|---|---|
| China brokered a Saudi and Iran agreement | True, 10 March 2023 | Documented |
| The Gulf made investment pledges to the US | True as pledges, May and November 2025 | Documented |
| The three share an interest in Gulf oil flowing | Yes, with splits on Iran and payment systems. US and Chinese leaders discussed Iran and Hormuz on 17 May 2026. What they said was not published | Level 0, with Hormuz passage as a public cue (level 1). Structural, about 42% |
| They agreed in secret and the conflict is staged | No channel found. The US and Israel struck Iran from 28 February 2026 and Hormuz shut in early March. On 14 April China called the US blockade of Iranian ports dangerous and irresponsible. A staged war would need many people to stay silent for months | Not shown. Remote, about 1%. An open question |
| Finance pushed trade toward China on purpose | China's exports did move to third countries. But bond investors sold before the tariff pause, a bank chief warned of recession and business groups sued. No mark of finance pushing the tariffs | Not shown. Remote, about 1%. An open question |

> **A new lead, graded.** One new lead came out of the digging. Saudi Arabia's central bank quietly left mBridge, a cross-border payment system run by several central banks, China's among them. It left on 13 May 2025, the same day as the US and Saudi investment package. The exit was reported only in September 2026, 16 months later. The Saudi central bank told the Financial Times the exit was planned, after its trial ended. People close to it said reading it as US pressure would be wrong. The UAE's central bank stayed in. Whether the exit was also a term of the US package is a guess (conjecture, about 14%).

- **Myth:** Whatever the official story says, the opposite is what is really happening.
- **Reality:** No event could prove that rule false, so no test can check it. The desk logs rules like this and does not use them. That does not make secrets rare. Standing arrangements run by small groups, like the 1974 Treasury window, have stayed hidden for decades, and we only know the ones that came out. So the desk treats a small, quiet deal as possible and looks for its marks.

### What this means for the big question: will energy replace money?

> **A reading for the Intelligence stack.** The evidence so far does not show energy trade pulling Gulf money off the dollar. Gulf Treasury holdings are above 2023, though Saudi holdings fell in the 2026 crisis. The biggest oil trade outside the dollar is Russian and Iranian oil sold to China, much of it in yuan, where sanctions block the dollar. The chapter did not test whether energy itself could act as money, or gold. So it gives only weak support (low confidence) to the desk's leading scenario for the big question, which is that the dollar stays central and spreads onto new payment systems. The dated tests below would change it.

**Check yourself.** Q: Gulf Treasury holdings rose by $111 billion while Gulf US share holdings also rose. Does that prove the Gulf is not shifting away from the dollar?
A: No. It shows the three states as a whole are not swapping Treasuries for shares, though the UAE moved that way. Saudi Arabia also borrows abroad (38% of its government debt at the end of 2025), so not every dollar in Treasuries is oil money. China's own fall since 2023 may likewise be partly a move to custody in Belgium. The September data and the annual survey due in early 2027 are the next checks.

### Go deeper: How the desk dug: the method, the exam and the dead ends

This was the first run of the desk's Unseen mode. Each question became a chain of links that starts from a documented fact. Before searching, the desk wrote down which public records each guessed link would have to leave marks in. Each link got a grade and a probability under a cap for that grade, and no chain could hold more than two guesses in a row or four in all. Fourteen chains were dug, and every one passed the desk's rule checker. Four rival readings were scored for each question: the official story, a deliberate deal, shared incentives, and accident or measurement. Detail for section 2. Funding here means choosing to hold US debt: the US pays in dollars it issues, so the question is who holds its debt and at what price. In January 2023 Japan and China together held more than the four custody centres, and custody is not ownership. The US central bank went back to buying short-term bills, about $40 billion a month, from December 2025. Japan spent 11.7 trillion yen on the yen in April and May 2026 and 15.4 trillion yen in late July and early August, about $170 billion by the desk's own conversion. The carry trade means borrowing cheaply in yen to buy assets that pay more. Treasury tables use market value, and the 10-year yield was near 5% in October 2026, so part of any fall in holdings is price, not selling. When the US Treasury sells more short-term bills, money funds buy more of its debt. The $14.7 trillion is from end March 2026 and the money supply figure ($23.4 trillion) from September 2026. A second, larger BIS measure, dollar debt inside currency swaps, was not checked.

Before the digging, a separate agent sat a blind exam on the 1974 question with no access to the desk's files. It found the 1979 audit and the 2016 release on its own and scored 8 of 9 items on a hidden answer key. The miss was a number for the share of Gulf oil sold to Asia.

Dead ends, kept so the next agent does not dig them again: a staged three-way plan between the US, the Gulf and China (no channel found, kept as a remote open question rather than closed), finance pushing the tariffs on purpose (the evidence points the other way), full 2025 customs tables for the smaller Gulf states (not reached), and a Fed update of the Cayman estimate for 2026 (none found). Sources not used by rule: the US filing database that requires an email address in each request. Detail for sections 1 and 3. A Gulf state has several balance sheets, a central bank and its funds, and a claim can hold for one but not the other. Saudi Arabia's central bank has a 50 billion yuan (about $7 billion) swap line with China's central bank, signed in November 2023, and the UAE's has one of 35 billion yuan. Russia was about 18% of China's oil imports in the first half of 2025 and Iran about 13% of its seaborne imports, by press estimates. The US also holds an open lever over one Gulf producer: Iraq's oil revenue sits in an account at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York, the US Treasury barred some Iraqi banks from dollar dealings in 2023, and it halted banknote shipments from that account in April 2026. In the 2020 crisis Bank Indonesia also bought its government's bonds under a burden sharing deal, so reserve currency status does not explain every central bank that bought. Tanker trackers count about 1.4 million barrels a day of Iranian oil reaching China in 2025, much of it moved between ships off Malaysia, and analysts said the 2016 Saudi figure might leave out bonds held with custodians abroad.
