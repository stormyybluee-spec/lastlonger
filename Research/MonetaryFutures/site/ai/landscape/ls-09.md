*The ladder was not pulled up. One rung broke, one got cheap but lottery-like, and ownership decides the climb.*

The owner's deepest worry was about children: when a child turns 18 in this new world, is the classic route still open? Labor to cash, then credit backed by income, then a home or a business. The project's answer (Round 2 Q6, Plausible, 70): yes, but it has changed shape.

*[Picture on the web page: The ladder from first job to owning assets. The first job rung still holds. The housing rung broke for entrants since 2020. A new side ladder, launching a brand, got cheap but steep.]*

### Rung one: the first job still exists

Unemployment for ages 20 to 24 was 8.0% in September 2026, down from 9.2% a year earlier. The bend is in AI-exposed white-collar entry roles: recent graduates aged 22 to 27 had 5.6% unemployment against 4.2% for all workers, which used to be the other way round, and the entry share of postings in exposed jobs fell from nearly 60% in 2020 to 44.7%. The facts are Confirmed (80). AI as the main cause only Plausible (55), because rates and post-2021 overhiring are rival explanations.

### Rung two: the housing rung broke

The median first-time buyer is now a record 40 years old, and first-time buyers are a record 21% of sales. For a 20 to 24-year-old saving 10% of pay, a 10% down payment on a median existing home took about 6.2 years in 1990 and about 10.0 years now (model, using NAR's 1990 median of $92,000 as reported in a secondary table). The monthly payment on a median home went from about 40 to about 68 hours of work since early 2020. 49% of adults under 30 live with a parent. The gate is the down payment, not the monthly payment.

*[Picture on the web page: Hours of work needed, then versus now. Since 1990 food at home got 24% cheaper in hours and quality-adjusted cars 54% cheaper. A median home, a family health premium and public tuition got dearer.]*

> This is the honest version of the movie "In Time", where the poor run out of time. Goods got cheaper in hours of work, entry assets got dearer. Of the $78.1 trillion rise in US household wealth since late 2019, the top 10% took 67.5% and the bottom half 3.1%. A price index tracks groceries, not a first home, so a CPI-indexed wage or UHI keeps up with food and falls behind on shelter.

### Can robots and zoning fix housing?

Only together. Land is 68.4% of single-family home value in San Jose and 39.9% nationally. So a 20% cut in building cost lowers prices about 6% in San Jose and about 16% in St. Louis (model). Factory building has a hard record: Katerra burned through more than $2 billion before its 2021 bankruptcy, and robots are under 0.03% of global construction spend. Broad zoning works: Auckland's 2016 upzoning doubled construction, and Austin's median rent fell from $1,546 to $1,296. Narrow reforms do little: Minneapolis's triplex rule produced 47 homes in five years. Ruling R3-5: Plausible, 65, sooner in the Sun Belt, later on the coasts.

### Rung three: launching got cheap, staying visible got dear

A digital brand can launch for about $525 to $2,850 and a small physical brand for about $1,900 to $7,400 (model). Solo founders are a record 63% of Stripe Atlas companies. But median solo founder revenue fell 23% in 2025 while the top decile earned 61 times the median. Family money is the real seed round: 47% of 18 to 29-year-olds got outside help.

*[Picture on the web page: Where $10 of a mass cosmetic goes. Product cost is about $1.76, the retailer keeps about $4.00, marketing about $1.47, other costs about $2.30 and profit about $0.27. A strong robot and energy shock saves maybe 36 cents.]*

The tolls are rising. Amazon's advertising went from 23.5% to 41.9% of its seller services revenue (2019 to H1 2026), Etsy's take rate from 16.5% to 24.2%, and Meta's price per ad rose 12% to 14% a year in 2026 (R3-7, Confirmed, 70). A human-made premium is real as a label effect in experiments but not yet measured as a cash price (Plausible, 60 after Round 5). Round 5 update: the first marketplace data show an AI discount instead, AI models were 17% of CGTrader uploads but 2.6% of sales. Buying a founder's community is the modern exit: the brand rhode added $293.5 million of sales in its first year inside e.l.f.

### Child accounts: real, but measured in tenths of a point

| Trump Account at 18, 5% real, today's dollars | Value |
|---|---|
| $1,000 seed only | about $2,366 |
| Seed plus $25 a month | about $10,913 |
| Seed plus $100 a month | about $36,556 |
| Seed plus the $5,000 yearly cap | about $144,825 |

The seed covers births from 2025 to 2028, so the first seeded cohort turns 18 in 2043. At 18 the account becomes IRA-like, with education and a $10,000 first-home exception but no business exception. The bottom half of households hold 0.58% of directly held equities, a floor since 2010 after a 1996 peak of 1.65%. Trump Accounts as designed lift that to about 0.60% to 0.70% by 2045. Only a grant of about $500 billion of AI equity reaches about 1.27% (R3-11, Plausible, 70). The SEED OK trial showed what works: automatic deposits, not sign-ups.

**Check yourself.** Q: Why does the "permanent underclass" meme get the mechanism right but the conclusion wrong?
A: It is right that ownership decides outcomes. It is wrong that the door is shut. The route becomes permanent only if three things persist: asset returns beating wages, blocked housing supply, and ownership grants too small to matter.
