### Act one, 2026 to 2028: the pipes switch on while the oil burns

The next few weeks are crowded with tests: Moody's on France (23 October), the Fed (28 October), Q3 GDP (29 October), the midterms (3 November), the Treasury refunding (4 November), the Cook hearing (5 November), China's legal deadline on rare earths (10 November) and the Fed again (9 December, a hike priced at 75% to 79%). Most likely, China extends (72%), the Fed hikes into an oil shock, and the Treasury keeps leaning on bills. Round 5 update: Moody's held France on 23 October, and the desk has written 72 dated predictions for these events, to be scored later.

On 18 January 2027 the GENIUS rules switch on, and every compliant dollar token becomes a buyer of Treasury bills by law. The rails are built, but they do not carry much yet: supply is flat near $304 billion and markets give $500 billion before 2027 only 3%. The Fed and money funds keep doing the heavy lifting. Oil keeps inflation sticky while Hormuz stays a coin flip (normal before 2028: about 48%). The weak link is France, with Poland as the non-euro fiscal risk: France's spread reached 171.8 basis points on 23 October and is watched against 200 and 250 and a Le Pen presidency priced near 46% for 2027. At the end of this act the likeliest description is still dollar extension (62). The main alternative is an oil-shock inflation and term-premium episode met by a Fed that hikes (22). The chance of a bond-market episode forcing the Fed to act at all in this period: about 30.

### Act two, 2028 to 2035: the quiet squeeze

This is the act where the treadmill speeds up on its own. About 70% of the marketable debt reprices at market rates within five years, and if yields hold, interest heads to about $1.6 trillion within three years and $1.9 trillion within five. The Powell governor seat is likely vacated by early 2028 (priced 64% to 72%), and Trump's term ends on 20 January 2029. A more cooperative Fed is the hinge.

The most likely result is managed financial repression, the 1940s and 1970s hybrid the decoders identified: inflation modestly above the average coupon, rules that make banks, funds and token issuers hold bills, and perhaps Fed support at the long end. Not Weimar, not default (judged below 5%). The judges' odds by 2035: a bond episode forcing Fed balance sheet action 45, an explicit yield cap or Fed-Treasury accord on yields 15 (cut from 25 in Round 5), inflation above 5% for two straight years 25, a gold revaluation 12 to 15, a coercive restructuring 4, capital controls on US residents 7.

Meanwhile the edges move. The US CBDC ban reaches its sunset on 31 December 2030, but a Fed retail CBDC by 2035 is only about 7, because private tokens already do the job. The digital euro probably arrives around 2029 to 2030 (about 50 by end 2030). States keep buying gold at home because dollar access now comes with conditions. China's hardware leverage shapes the price of solar, batteries and EVs more than the currency you are paid in. A cash UHI or AI dividend by 2035 stays unlikely (about 10). AI equity routed into child accounts or a public fund is more likely (about 25). End of act: dollar extension 44, disorderly reset 30, neutral reserve layer 23, energy standard 3.

Round 4 update. Europe slides in this act rather than falls. Its income per head in purchasing power has held near 70% to 75% of the US since 2000, and the desk gives 70% to it still sitting below 72% of the US in 2035. The sharp edge is a France- or Italy-led euro crisis, 32% by 2035, while a member leaving the euro stays near 3%. If Europe declines, about 55% of its outbound money goes to North America, Asia wins factories more than money, and the dollar's reserve share has about a 25% chance of printing below 50% by 2035, with no single heir in sight.

### Act three, 2035 to 2050: who owns the output

By now the question is no longer the rails. It is the tax base. If the labor share keeps falling from 52.8% while wages still carry most federal receipts, the collateral under the world's money weakens slowly, which is why dollar extension and disorderly reset end tied at 31. Gold's stock grows about 1.6% a year and is about 39% larger by 2050, still scarce, still neutral, with a neutral reserve layer at 28. Energy may become an index for some bonds or transfers. An energy or compute standard reaches 10 only because machine economies could be large by then, and any energy unit still needs a scarcity rule that pulls it back toward gold or Bitcoin.

By 2050 the dollar has about an even chance of printing below half of world reserves at some point, but about a 90% chance of still being the largest. The likeliest successor is a crowd rather than a country: gold, the euro and many smaller currencies. That is why the neutral layer is now shown as gold and Bitcoin (18) plus a multipolar mix of currencies (10).

The first child seeded with a Trump Account turns 18 in 2043. On current design the bottom half's share of equities rises from 0.58% to only about 0.60% to 0.70% by 2045. Whether that child inherits the two-class world depends on choices made in act two: by-right housing, ownership grants at a scale relative to asset prices, and taxing capital income rather than watts.

### What would change the story

- Toward disorderly reset: net interest above 25% of receipts (19.46% now), the bill share above 25% (22.36% now), foreign official custody below $2.5 trillion, or US recession odds above 30.
- Toward dollar extension: stablecoins growing outside crypto cycles, an independent Fed, Hormuz reopening, productivity above 3% without rising real rates.
- Toward a neutral layer: central bank gold buying above 200 tonnes a quarter and more conditional crisis lines.
- Toward a multipolar mix: the dollar below 54% of reserves in any 2027 release, permanent EU joint bonds above €1 trillion a year, or a fully opened Chinese capital account.

The dollar most likely survives this period, not because it is strong but because the state has wired the new money to its own debt. The cost of that survival is paid quietly, through inflation above savings rates, rent, and a tax base resting on wages. The one force that changes who pays is ownership. That is the thread that ties the whole landscape together.
