*Intent and outcome are different things. Most players want more than they can get, and the gap shows where the real limits are.*

This chapter separates two questions that news coverage usually blends: what does each player want, and what are they actually getting? Wanting is graded with the decoding scale (D1 strong to D4 debunked). Getting is read from money moved and laws passed.

### Trump: a landlord's monetary system

Trump has run one economic worldview for almost 40 years. His paid newspaper letter of 1987-09-02 already said "It's time for us to end our vast deficits by making Japan, and others who can afford it, pay", complained of "a weak yen against a strong dollar", and ended "Tax these wealthy nations, not America." The decoders found four stable wants. In one line: the dollar is the rent everyone must pay, money is cheap for the landlord, and the tenants cover the costs.

### Bessent: the trader in the Treasurer's chair

Bessent is the architect of captive demand. He chose the 1863 analogy, said access to the dollar is "no longer unconditional", welcomed "money flow out of some countries of concern" into Treasuries, and called the current system fiat in July 2026. His signal-heavy style (the photographed note, "I am the house now") moves markets more than his money does. The literal "he sold France" story is Refuted (85). The narrow "he chose to let the euro area absorb the shock" version is Plausible (50).

### Warsh: the independent hawk, for now

Warsh hiked against the President, citing 65 months of elevated inflation and hyperscaler competition for capital. He has floated the idea of a new Fed-Treasury accord. The decoders give a crisis-era accord and Fed yield support after the 2028 leadership turnover only D2 (30 by 2032 after Round 5, down from 40). Round 5 update: the accord Warsh describes would shrink the Fed's bond footprint, not cap yields. An explicit yield cap is now judged 15 by 2035. Markets price the Powell governor seat vacated by early 2028 at 64% to 72%. That date is a fork point for the whole story.

### Musk: belief yes, project no

Musk has said since 2023 that money is "a database for resource allocation" and on 2026-10-20 that "currency will be measured in ... wattage and tonnage." The decoders rate this a sincere belief (D1, 75) that also works as a valuation story for his energy, AI and launch businesses (D1, 70). Their best reading is both at once (65). But he has built nothing that would make energy money real.

- X Money is a dollar wallet on Cross River Bank paying up to 6% in dollars, with a Visa card. X said it "never will" launch a token.
- Tesla still holds the same 11,509 BTC it has held since 2022. No buying during his energy-money talk.
- His 200 GW a year solar claim compares with about 0.3 GW of Tesla solar output today, and his pay plan is measured in market cap (up to $8.5 trillion), not watts.
- "Musk is building a private central bank" is D3 (8). "A Technocracy-style energy credit plan" is D3 as a plan (7), though the family influence is D2 (55).

> Musk wants federal dollar checks for a Universal High Income now ("MUST issue dollars") and says money will not matter later. Read together, dollars are his transition tool and physics units are his end state, with no bridge between them. Meanwhile he accumulates dollar claims on exactly the assets that would be scarce in his future: power, launch and compute.

**Check yourself.** Q: Trump strongly wants cheap money. Why is he not getting it?
A: Because the Fed under Warsh is independent and hiking, the courts kept Governor Cook, and the bond market sets long rates. His other wants (private dollar rails, an owner society) advance through rules and private firms that do not need those institutions.
