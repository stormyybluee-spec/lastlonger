# Connecting the dots: the interconnection map

Landscape chapter 11. Stack: Intelligence (graded, dated findings). Id: ls-11. Facts as of 2026-10-23.

Web page: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/#ls-11 . Text built 2026-11-12. Guide for AI readers: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/llms.txt

*Information arbitrage: the most useful insights sit between topics, where a cause in one area quietly drives an effect in another.*

Most coverage treats debt, crypto, energy, housing and geopolitics as separate beats. The research kept finding the same few pipes running through all of them. This chapter lists the links that are easiest to miss when each topic is read alone. Each is one cause, one effect and the evidence that ties them. Together they explain why a closure of a strait in the Gulf can raise a young American's mortgage rate, and why AI investment can weaken the collateral under the world's money.

> **Picture this.** Think of a city's water system. Houses, factories and fountains look separate, but they all hang off the same mains. A burst pipe across town drops your shower pressure. In the money system the mains are Treasury bills, delivered power and land. Almost every link below runs through one of them.

### Eighteen links

1. Hormuz oil drives the French bond spread: the France to Germany spread was flat in August and blew out from 87 to 157.5 basis points only after Brent passed $100, alongside the Fed and Bank of Japan hikes, which is why the project now sees the likeliest near-term disorderly path as oil-shock inflation with France as the weak link. By 23 October the spread had reached 171.8 basis points, above Italy's.
2. Japan's yen defense feeds into US mortgage rates: instead of borrowing through the unused FIMA line, foreign official holders sold $254 billion of Treasuries in 2026, and the 10-year yield went from 4.68% on 30 July to 5.13% in October.
3. AI investment weakens the collateral under money: firms expensing AI capex cut corporate tax to 7.1% of receipts while wages carry 86.2%, so Treasuries rest more on a labor share that is already at a record low 52.8%, which moved one point from S1 to S4 for 2035 to 2050.
4. Data centers drive household budgets: PJM capacity cleared at its cap with a 6,623 MW shortfall, wholesale power there rose 41%, and US energy prices rose 20.4% while real hourly earnings fell 0.7%, so the AI boom is already taxing every fixed income, including any future UHI.
5. China's solar know-how ban targets the watt-dollar: if AI output is capped by delivered power, the country that controls 97% of wafer capacity holds a lever over the dollar's new collateral, which is why solar technology controls now top the threat table at 3.00.
6. Stablecoin law ties crypto to the bill market: the 93-day reserve rule means every compliant token is a buyer of Treasury bills, so crypto's boom and bust cycle now feeds straight into the Treasury's funding, as the flat $304 billion year showed.
7. Low interest rates would break private seigniorage: Circle's model stops covering costs at a bill yield of about 1.5%, so a future rate-cutting cycle would push issuers to cut distributor payouts and fold into banks, concentrating the new rails in fewer hands.
8. A bill-heavy debt makes the Fed a budget actor: with 22.36% of marketable debt in bills and 35.7% repricing within a year, each hike reaches the federal interest bill within months, which raises the political pressure on Fed appointments after 2028.
9. Sanctions drive gold: the freezing of about €202 billion of Russian euro assets, while its gold at home stayed untouched, is the clearest reason central banks bought 863 tonnes in 2025 and France brought 129 tonnes home.
10. Conditional crisis access pushes toward neutral reserves: the Gulf asked for swap lines and got none announced, borrowing a record $102.7 billion instead, a small push that moved one point toward S2 for 2028 to 2035.
11. Land prices cap both UHI and the robot dividend: shelter takes 40% to 48% of UHI spending and land is 68.4% of home value in San Jose, so robot-built savings and printed checks both end up as land rent unless zoning lets more homes share the land.
12. Platform tolls absorb automation's savings: a strong robot and energy shock saves about 36 cents on a $10 cosmetic while Amazon's ads rose to 41.9% of seller services revenue, so cheaper production reaches shoppers slowly and founders' margins shrink.
13. Interest on the debt flows upward: net interest of $1.05 trillion goes mostly to the top decile, who hold 79% of household-held government securities, while the bottom half owes 51.6% of consumer credit, so a rising interest bill widens the wealth gap on its own.
14. Fed losses turn printing into borrowing: since the Fed pays 3.90% on reserves and carries a $233.2 billion deferred asset, a money-financed UHI or a gold revaluation gain becomes interest-bearing reserves, so neither escapes the debt arithmetic.
15. Europe's savings deepen America's market: European investors hold $16.2 trillion of US securities, 45.9% of everything foreigners hold, and the European Commission counts about €300 billion of EU savings leaving each year, mostly for the US. The same flow that keeps the dollar's market deep keeps Europe short of risk capital at home, which is why 40 of 147 European unicorns moved their headquarters abroad.
16. French politics can strengthen the dollar: a France-led euro crisis would send money into Bunds, Swiss francs, Treasuries and gold, as in 2011 to 2012. That is why the court's rule after such a crisis raises dollar extension for 2028 to 2035 (to 46/24/3/27) instead of lowering it.
17. German power prices move car market shares: industrial electricity in the EU costs more than twice US and Chinese levels, German chemicals output is 24% below 2017 and cars 28% below, and Chinese brands reached a record 11.7% of EU car registrations in August 2026.
18. Frozen reserves raise gold's share: about €202 billion of Russia's euro assets sit immobilized at Euroclear while gold held at home was never frozen. Gold is now worth about 1.8 times all euro reserves, which is why the neutral layer is now shown as gold plus a multipolar mix.

> The owner's instinct that "Set 1 and Set 2 merge" and his worry about a "permanent underclass" turn out to be the same story seen from two ends. The state keeps money anchored to bills it can sell and taxes it can raise. Those taxes fall on wages. The interest on those bills flows to owners. So the plumbing that keeps the dollar strong is also the plumbing that widens the gap, unless ownership itself is spread.
