# The rails: Set 1 absorbs Set 2

Landscape chapter 05. Stack: Intelligence (graded, dated findings). Id: ls-05. Facts as of 2026-10-23.

Web page: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/#ls-05 . Text built 2026-11-11. Guide for AI readers: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/llms.txt

*New digital money is being built on top of the old system, by law. It is an acquisition, not a merger.*

The owner framed the money world as two sets. Set 1 is gold, bonds and bank credit. Set 2 is crypto, Bitcoin, stablecoins and blockchains, used more and more by AI for commerce. His hypothesis was that both survive and merge into "a new monopoly board". The project's ruling (Db-10, Plausible, 65): right in shape, wrong in one important way. Set 2's rails are being absorbed onto Set 1's balance sheet. Set 1 is buying Set 2.

*[Picture on the web page: Four layers of money. Token rails on top move and settle the money. Treasuries and bank deposits underneath are what it is backed by. Gold sits at the bottom as the neutral asset between nations.]*

### Set 1 is three jobs, not one layer

- Gold is the neutral reserve with no issuer, mostly for states.
- Treasuries are the prime collateral that everything else is priced against.
- Banks create most credit, by making loans that create deposits.

Crypto replaces the rails (how money moves and settles), not the balance sheet (what money is backed by). Payments move to tokens first, some deposit funding second, and the reserve role last and only partly (Round 2 Q1, Plausible, 70).

### A stablecoin is a tokenized T-bill fund with a payment button

From 2027-01-18 every compliant US stablecoin must hold cash, deposits or Treasury bills of 93 days or less. Holders get zero interest by law. So the float belongs to the issuer and its distributors. Circle's Q2 2026 results show the split: $667.7 million of reserve income on $76.5 billion of average USDC, of which $410.4 million (61.5%) went to distribution partners, mainly Coinbase, leaving $48 million of net income. Across the market the float is worth about $11.7 billion a year at a 4.25% bill rate (model). Tether alone made more than $10 billion in 2025.

| Where $50,000 of idle cash sits for a year | Rate | You earn |
|---|---|---|
| Bank savings at the national average | 0.38% | $190 |
| Government money fund or T-bill | about 4.25% | $2,125 |
| GENIUS stablecoin, from the issuer | 0% by law | $0 |
| Exchange reward on a stablecoin | 3.75% | $1,875 (legality case by case from 2027-01-18) |

> This is seigniorage, the profit from issuing money, moved into private hands. The Fed used to send the Treasury large profits ($117 billion in 2015, $107.4 billion in 2021). It stopped in September 2022, because the interest it now pays on bank reserves exceeds what its old bonds earn. Its accumulated shortfall, a "deferred asset", was $233.2 billion on 2026-10-14. Today the cleanest seigniorage in the dollar system goes to stablecoin issuers, whose holders lend to them at zero.

### Who really buys the tokens

When stablecoins grew (end 2023 to October 2025, +$174.9 billion), the best estimate is that about two thirds went to holders outside the US (model range 55% to 81%), and about 41% was genuinely new dollar demand from people converting local currency. Offshore USDT is 61% of supply. Per $1 of new token, a foreign new-to-dollar buyer adds about $0.80 of Treasury demand, a switch from a US bank deposit about $0.72, and a switch from a government money fund about $0.30 (model). But for the last twelve months there was no growth at all. Treasury's advisory committee once projected about $2 trillion by 2028. That now needs about +$1.7 trillion in about 26 months from a flat base, and markets give stablecoins above $500 billion before 2027 only 3%.

### Do deposits leave the banks?

Mostly no. The famous "$6.6 trillion of deposits at risk" was all US transactional deposits at end 2024, a ceiling, not a forecast, and when an issuer buys a bill the seller's bank gets the deposit back. What changes is cost (retail deposits at 0.38% become wholesale money near 4%), and a slice of money creation tilts from bank loans toward Treasury bills, as in the 1863 design.

### Borrowing stays with regulated lenders

DeFi lends only against collateral you already own, never against future income. All crypto-backed lending was $56.2 billion in Q2 2026, about 0.4% of the $14.10 trillion of US bank loans. A mortgage, car loan or first business loan needs someone who underwrites your future income and absorbs default, so it comes from a bank or a bank-funded lender through at least 2035, even if the money arrives as a token. Banks are moving upstream: their loans to nonbank lenders rose 19.9% in a year to $2.06 trillion, and nonbanks make about 64% of home purchase loans.

### Why the state keeps Set 1: seigniorage and the freeze button

Every dollar payment ends as a transfer on a ledger the US controls: Fedwire moved $4.59 trillion a day in 2025, CHIPS $2.0 trillion. Foreign central banks keep $2.55 trillion of Treasuries in custody at the New York Fed. That is why one Treasury directive on 2022-02-28 could immobilize Russia's central bank assets held by US persons, and why about €202 billion stayed frozen at Euroclear in June 2026. The power comes from law over the ledger keepers, not from technology. So the state lets new rails grow only on terms that keep both seigniorage and the freeze button (DCD-D01, D1, 80). Stablecoins as a sanctions tool: D1, 85.

**Check yourself.** Q: If stablecoins grow tenfold, does the US government lose control of money?
A: Not under current law. Compliant stablecoins must hold Treasury bills, pay holders nothing, settle through a Fed-controlled system and freeze on lawful orders. Growth would create more demand for US debt and more reach for US sanctions. The open risk is offshore tokens outside that regime.
