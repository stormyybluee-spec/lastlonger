# Labor, robots and a Universal High Income

Landscape chapter 08. Stack: Intelligence (graded, dated findings). Id: ls-08. Facts as of 2026-10-23.

Web page: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/#ls-08 . Text built 2026-11-11. Guide for AI readers: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/llms.txt

*Three tiers behave like two classes. A UHI is limited by rent and the tax base, not by robots, and ownership is what moves people across the line.*

Musk has called for a "Universal HIGH INCOME via checks issued by the Federal government." Online voices warn of a "permanent underclass". The owner's own model has three tiers: asset owners, UHI recipients and people still working. This chapter tests all three ideas.

### Where the robots really are

Labor's share of nonfarm business income is 52.8%, the lowest since 1947, while unit profits rose 43.0% annualized and unemployment held at 4.2%. But robots are not yet the cause at scale. About 14,500 humanoids shipped worldwide in 2025, about 90% Chinese. 603,307 industrial robots were installed in 2025 with 5 million operating. A fleet of one million humanoids by 2028 is below 10%. Ruling Dc-05: robots are not a macro labor substitute before 2030, Plausible, 85. The pre-2030 effect is software AI on entry-level hiring.

*[Picture on the web page: Three tiers become two classes. Workers and UHI recipients both live on income streams. Owners' income grows with assets. The decisive line is ownership.]*

Ruling Dd-01: the three tiers collapse into two classes, owners and income-stream dependents, Plausible, 65. Workers and UHI recipients sit in the same structural place relative to capital. Round 2 confirmed the squeeze is shared: both face housing, health cover and, in 2026, energy up 20.4%.

### Credit is not the trap. Ownership is.

The owner feared UHI recipients would lose access to credit. Under current law that is Refuted, conditional (Dd-02, 80). Lenders may not discriminate against public-assistance income (ECOA), and Fannie Mae counts income that will continue at least three years. On that basis $1,000 a month supports roughly a $69,000 mortgage (model). The exception is a UHI whose amount politicians reset each year, like Alaska's dividend, which fails the three-year test.

Selling the stream is another matter. Social Security has been non-assignable since 1935 (42 USC 407) for good reason: pension advance firms charged up to 183% APR, and one veteran got $1,500 for $18,000 of pension. A $1,000 monthly stream is worth about $181,000 in present value, but buyers historically paid 30 to 60 cents on the dollar. The real gap is ownership and compounding (Dd-03, Plausible, 70): a borrower capped by debt to income rules on $2,500 a month can buy about $178,000 of housing, while asset prices follow the capital share.

### The UHI arithmetic

A $30,000 UHI for about 262 million adults costs about $7.86 trillion a year: 24% of GDP, 106% of all FY2026 outlays and 145% of receipts. Financed by borrowing, it takes debt from 101% of GDP to about 128% in year one, 238% in year five and 374% in year ten at constant rates (Dc-08, Confirmed as arithmetic, 90). Even $10,000 is 8% of GDP and reaches about 211% by year ten. Printing does not escape this in the US: bank reserves earn 3.90%, so money finance is overnight borrowing.

| Kenya GiveDirectly | about 17% of local GDP, donor dollars | 2.5 local multiplier, 0.1% average inflation |

The lesson: small and funded is safe, large and printed is eroded within two to five years. Real value is nominal divided by one plus inflation.

### Housing is the limit, not robots

Round 3 found the decisive reason. A UHI dollar is spent about 40% to 48% on housing, and housing supply grows about 1% a year. So even unlimited robot goods can meet at most 57% to 76% of the extra demand. Round 5 update: housing supply is shrinking in 2026 (completions down 12.5%), and new homes meet only 15% to 25% of a demand shock within ten years, so the shelter limit is, if anything, tighter. Recalibrated, a printed Musk-scale UHI runs about 5.8% to 9.8% inflation now, 1.4% to 5.8% in 2028 to 2035 and 0.3% to 2.7% after 2035 (model, and a lower bound because it has no interest channel). Round 2's 0.9% case needs housing supply to open first. A UHI of 10% of GDP is much easier: about 1.5% to 3.9% now.

> A printed UHI would feel generous on gadgets and food and thin on rent within two to three years. In nearly every model case, shelter is 50% to 100% of the demand left unmet. Robots make the goods. Landlords capture the checks.

### The tax base is moving the wrong way

Treasuries are claims on future taxes, and those taxes rest on wages. In FY2026 individual income and payroll taxes carried 86.2% of federal receipts, and corporate tax only 7.1%, down from 10.8% in FY2024, as firms expensed AI investment (Microsoft's current tax bill fell from $14.1 billion to $2.5 billion). Each point of labor share lost costs about 0.8% of receipts. Ruling R3-3: the tax base shifting to capital, energy or compute is Refuted for now (70). A 5% excise on hyperscaler capex would raise about $35 billion. The big money is in taxing capital income and equity, not watts or tokens.

### What is actually on the table

No federal UHI or AI dividend bill has moved past introduction, and none found is money financed. Sanders' bill takes a one-time 50% stake in large AI firms and pays about $1,000 per person, which needs a $3.2 to $6.8 trillion stake (model). Inside the administration, Bessent reportedly prefers AI equity seeded into Trump Accounts. Working odds by 2035: a cash UHI or AI dividend about 10, AI equity routed into accounts or a public fund about 25. Five states ban local guaranteed income. The first "AI dividend" you see is more likely a locked account for your child than a monthly check.

**Check yourself.** Q: Why can robots not make a large printed UHI non-inflationary on their own?
A: Because almost half of UHI spending goes to housing, which robots cannot multiply quickly. Housing supply grows about 1% a year, so the unmet demand piles up there and shows up as rent and price inflation.
