# Today's board: old anchors, new rails

Rulebook Level 1 (Foundations), chapter 15. Stack: Fundamentals. Id: rb-12.

Web page: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/#rb-12 . Text built 2026-11-11. Guide for AI readers: https://neetmonetary.netlify.app/llms.txt

*The money in your app may look new, but under it sit the oldest anchors in the system: government bonds, bank credit and gold.*

Every chapter so far has built toward this one. Here is the board as it stands in October 2026. The owner divided it into two sets. Set 1 is gold, Treasury bonds and bank credit. Set 2 is stablecoins, tokens and crypto rails. The big question is whether Set 2 replaces Set 1. This project's answer, after three rounds of research, is no. Set 1 is absorbing Set 2. In the project's phrase, it is an acquisition, not a merger.

### Set 1: three jobs, not one layer

- Gold is the neutral reserve. It has no issuer to default and cannot be frozen if you hold it at home. Central banks bought about 863 tonnes in 2025. That buying rose after Russia's reserves were frozen in 2022.
- Treasuries are the prime collateral. Stablecoins, banks, money funds and foreign governments all hold them. They are the asset that the rest of the system stands on.
- Bank credit decides who can borrow against future income. A mortgage, a car loan or a first business loan still needs someone who studies your income and absorbs the loss if you fail.

### Set 2: new rails on old anchors

Stablecoins are tokens on a blockchain that promise to be worth one dollar each. Under the US GENIUS Act, from January 18, 2027, every compliant stablecoin must be backed by cash, bank deposits or Treasury bills of 93 days or less. That means every new compliant stablecoin dollar is new demand for Set 1 assets. Offshore coins are not bound the same way: Tether's USDT also reported about $8.4 billion of Bitcoin and $19.1 billion of gold in its reserves on 30 September 2026. Stablecoins cannot pay holders interest by law. And they cannot lend. They move money, they do not create credit.

> Stablecoin issuers keep the interest. When you hold a dollar stablecoin, the issuer invests your dollar in Treasury bills and keeps the yield. Circle, the issuer of USDC, earned about $668 million of reserve income in one quarter of 2026 and paid about 61.5% of it to distribution partners such as Coinbase. Across the market, this float is worth roughly $11.7 billion a year at a 4.25% bill rate. Idle stablecoins cost you the interest you could have earned.

- **Myth:** Crypto will replace banks, so people with savings will stop borrowing from banks.
- **Reality:** Crypto is replacing some rails, how money moves and settles, not the balance sheet behind it. All crypto-backed lending was about $56 billion in mid-2026, roughly 0.4% of US bank loans, and it only lends against assets you already own. A loan against your future income still comes from a regulated lender. Banks are adapting too: JPMorgan launched a deposit token on a public blockchain in 2025.

- **Myth:** Bitcoin's 21 million coins, times ten, can support about $300 trillion of credit.
- **Reality:** That figure works out to bitcoin at the same total value as all gold, about $1.43 million per coin, then multiplied by ten. Real crypto lending runs the other way: borrowers pledge about $2 of crypto to borrow $1, and all crypto-backed lending is about 0.05 times bitcoin's value, not 10 times.

### Digital central bank money

- United States: a federal ban on a retail central bank digital currency is now law, carried in the same bill as a 2026 housing act. The Fed cannot issue a retail digital dollar. Digital dollars will come from private stablecoins and bank tokens.
- Europe: a digital euro is under negotiation, with a holding cap of around 3,000 euros discussed and issuance in 2029 at the earliest.
- China: the digital yuan, e-CNY, runs domestic pilots, including programmable payments that expire. The cross-border mBridge platform has settled about $55 billion in total. Saudi Arabia left it in May 2025.

### Energy as collateral and ceiling

This project's central research question was whether energy will become money. The answer: energy will not replace the dollar you spend. It is taking over gold's old job as the scarce physical thing that caps how much real output any money can claim. In an economy of AI and robots, output grows with delivered electric power, and power cannot be printed.

Energy is also entering the system as collateral inside the dollar. Power plants, data centers, long electricity contracts and computer chips back new dollar loans. The project calls this the watt-dollar, a descendant of the 1974 petrodollar. US data centers used about 176 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2023, about 4.4% of US power, and projections for 2028 range from 325 to 580. Consumer energy prices rose about 20.4% in the year to September 2026.

*[Picture on the web page: Energy as collateral and as ceiling: dollar loans are increasingly backed by power plants, data centers and chips, while delivered power caps how much real output money can buy.]*

Energy fails as everyday money because a kilowatt-hour is perishable, tied to a place, and keeps getting cheaper as solar spreads. Elon Musk's suggestion of a currency measured in watts and tonnage is closer to a post-scarcity accounting idea, similar to the 1930s Technocracy movement's energy certificates. Its real problem is the same as every planned money: who decides the allocations.

> The rivals' strongest card is hardware, not currency. China's money moves, like selling some Treasuries or building alternative payment rails, have little bite so far. Its export controls on rare earth magnets, battery materials and, since October 2026, solar manufacturing know-how go straight at the watt-dollar, because the US Treasury can issue bills but not magnets.

### The toll booths

On any board, some players earn by owning the squares others must cross. In 2026 the biggest toll booths are not always banks.

| Toll booth | What it charges | From this project's data |
|---|---|---|
| Banks on savings | The gap between what they pay you and what safe bills pay | Average savings 0.38% against bills about 4.25%. On $50,000 that is about $1,935 a year kept by the bank |
| Stablecoin issuers | The full interest on reserves | Holders get 0% by law. Float worth about $11.7 billion a year |
| Online marketplaces | Fees and ads on each sale | Advertising reached about 41.9% of Amazon's seller services revenue in early 2026, up from 23.5% in 2019. Etsy's take rate rose from 16.5% to 24.2% |
| Dollar plumbing | Access to the payment system itself | Sanctions can cut a country out. Hormuz shipping tolls in 2026 were partly demanded in yuan |
| Land | Rent and price of location | Land is about 68% of single-family home value in San Jose and Los Angeles, about 40% nationally |

**Check yourself.** Q: Under the GENIUS Act, what must back a compliant US stablecoin, and who earns the interest?
A: Cash, bank deposits and Treasury bills of 93 days or less. The issuer and its distribution partners earn the interest. Holders cannot be paid interest by the issuer.

**Check yourself.** Q: Why does this project say energy is replacing gold's old job rather than the dollar?
A: Because delivered energy is the scarce physical limit on how much real output money can buy, as gold once was, and energy assets increasingly back dollar loans. But a kilowatt-hour is perishable, local and getting cheaper, so it fails as spendable money.
