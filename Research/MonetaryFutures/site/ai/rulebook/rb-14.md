*What each generation learned the hard way, written down so the next one does not have to.*

Families pass down recipes, tools and stories. They rarely pass down money lessons, because money talk feels private or shameful, and because each generation's crisis feels unique while it is happening. So each generation relearns the same lessons at full price. This chapter is the remedy: a short rulebook, and a list of the mistakes behind it.

> "Those who cannot remember the past are condemned to repeat it." (George Santayana, The Life of Reason, 1905)

### How to use the rulebook

1. Read the rules once straight through. Most will now feel obvious, because the chapters built them.
2. Pick three rules that you are breaking today. Write them where you will see them.
3. Before any big money decision, such as a loan, a house, a business or an investment, scan the list and ask which rules apply.
4. Read the mistakes list with someone younger than you. Ask which mistake they think their own generation is making now.
5. Add your own rule when life teaches you one. A rulebook that stops growing stops being useful.

> **Picture this.** Think of the rulebook as a ship's logbook kept across generations. Each captain wrote down the storms they met, the rocks they hit and the stars that guided them home. The next captain cannot avoid every storm. But a captain who has read the log will recognize the sky before the storm arrives.

The full rulebook and the generational mistakes follow this chapter. They are written to be copied, shared and argued with. They are general education about how money works, not financial advice for any particular person. Where a rule touches your own situation, test it against your own numbers, and against a second opinion.

**Check yourself.** Q: Which rule from the whole guide would have saved the most people in 2008?
A: Several apply, but the strongest is: borrow only what you can service through a bad season without selling. Many households and banks were forced sellers when house prices fell.

**Check yourself.** Q: Why does each generation repeat money mistakes?
A: Because the last crisis fades from memory, calm years breed confidence, and each new boom tells a story about why this time is different. Writing lessons down and passing them on is the cheapest defense.
