4. A second transcript the owner had not watched, for Level 4: Thomas Cover's lecture on growth-optimal and universal portfolios (video wsd8nsJg6D8). Key points, paraphrased: maximize the growth rate of wealth, which means the expected log of the gain, so the geometric mean, not the average, governs a compounding pot. Example: cash plus a stock that doubles or halves with equal odds. Neither goes anywhere, yet rebalancing half and half each period grows about 6% a period, living off the swings. Two real small stocks over about 20 years: one grew 4 times, the other 8 times, a fixed rebalanced mix grew about 75 times in hindsight. A universal portfolio that knows nothing in advance comes within a factor of about the square root of n of the best fixed mix chosen in hindsight. Side information raises the growth rate by at most its mutual information. Margin amplifies. Over 13 months to October 2008 the best long-only choice was all cash. Kelly betting is the horse-race special case.
