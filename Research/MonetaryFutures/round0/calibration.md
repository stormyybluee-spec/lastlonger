# Calibration: "Bessent sold France to help Japan"

Date of analysis: 2026-10-22. Raw input: tweet x.com/yellownutt/status/2110717464762044735 (summary only) plus the owner's hunch: "Bessent sold France to help Japan (first time in history), to solve the US $40T debt problem. Hedge funds follow the Treasury's lead. Not priced in yet."

Method note: built from primary data (ECB SDW API, US Treasury daily yield curve, TIC Table 5, Senate Banking letter, Yahoo price history) plus reported officials' quotes. The excluded podcast was not consulted. The session web search budget ran out before I could re-verify the text of the Miran (Nov 2024) paper, so claims about pre-office writings are tagged as unverified in this session.

Evidence tiers: T0 money moved, T1 law, T2 officials on record, T3 market prices, T4 specialist interpretation, T5 commentary.

## 1. Verdict

**The literal thesis is Refuted as a plan, and the investable conclusion ("not priced in") is Refuted. A narrower version is Plausible: the US deliberately chose a funding currency and a policy mix that pushed the cost of rescuing the yen (and of Japan's capital repatriation) away from the dollar and Treasuries and toward the euro area, where France is the weakest, most Japan-owned link. Bessent welcomes the resulting safe-haven flows, but there is no evidence he engineered the French crisis, and the dollar devaluation leg has not happened.**

What is true (T0/T2):
- On 31 July 2026 the New York Fed, acting for Treasury through the Exchange Stabilization Fund (ESF), **sold euros (not dollars) to buy yen**, through Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley, alongside a much larger Japanese MoF operation. The ECB was told only after settlement. No precedent for a US third-currency yen operation has been found. "First time in history" is defensible for this narrow fact.
- Bessent confirmed the action, said Treasury exchanged "existing ESF foreign-currency assets for yen", and later claimed "asymmetric information" and "I am the house now".

What fails:
- **Size.** Reserve data analysed by FT Alphaville and Brad Setser (via secondary reports) put the US leg at about $0.5bn (euro holdings down about $495m, yen up about $502m). Japan spent ¥15.4tn (about $97bn) from 30 July to 26 August. A $0.5bn euro sale cannot "sell France" in a market of roughly €3.3tn of French debt.
- **Timing.** The OAT-Bund spread was flat in August (monthly average 82bp vs 78bp in July) and only blew out from mid-September (87bp on 10 Sep to 157.5bp on 21 Oct), coinciding with the Fed hike (16 Sep), BoJ hike (18 Sep), the French 2027 budget fight and an oil shock (Brent above $100, Hormuz/Iran war).
- **US debt "solved".** US 10y rose from 4.68% (30 Jul) to 5.31% (5 Oct) and is 5.13% (21 Oct). Japan's Treasury holdings fell from $1,239bn (Feb) to $1,068bn (Aug), $35.6bn in August alone. The operation did not lower US borrowing costs.
- **Dollar devaluation.** The Fed hiked to 3.75-4.00% and EUR/USD fell from 1.170 (21 Aug) to 1.123 (21 Oct). The dollar strengthened.
- **"Not priced in".** UBS (14 Oct) says EUR/JPY risk reversals show the strongest put skew among euro pairs and calls the trade overdone. Allianz (9 Oct) describes a crowded "short Europe" trade. The idea is consensus and crowded, not hidden.

Confidence: high that the orchestration thesis is wrong as stated, medium on the narrower "cost-shifting externality" reading.

## 2. Anomaly

Exact mechanics of the 2026 US-Japan intervention:

| US leg: who | Federal Reserve Bank of New York as fiscal agent for Treasury, using ESF funds, not Fed funds (FOMC minutes, Oct 2026, via Bloomberg 7 Oct) | T0/T2 |
| US leg: what was sold | **Euros**, proceeds used to buy yen. Counterparties Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley (FT, cited in Senator Warren's 13 Aug letter) | T0 (reported) |
| US leg: size | Undisclosed officially. Bessent notepad "Buy Japanese Yen (JPY) $5-10 bil". FT upper bound $26.3bn. Weekly reserve data: about $0.5bn (FT Alphaville/Setser via secondary sources). | Notification | ECB learned after settlement. Lagarde and Bessent spoke the next day. Treasury position: it does not coordinate reserve allocation decisions with foreign authorities | T2 (reported) |
| Legal basis | 31 USC 5302 (Gold Reserve Act 1934, ESF). Bessent: authorizes dealing in foreign exchange "in support of orderly exchange arrangements" | T1 |
| Japan's legal framing | Action taken under the September 2025 Japan-US finance ministers' joint statement | T2 |

The anomalies, ranked:
1. **Third-currency funding.** Every modern US intervention (1995, 1998, 2011) sold dollars. Selling euros forces the adjustment onto EUR/JPY rather than USD/JPY. Truman: "weird". HSBC: "maybe unprecedented". ECB officials: "unprecedented breach of longstanding convention".
2. **No advance notice to the ECB** on a trade in the ECB's own currency. Breaks G7 practice.
3. **The US joined at all.** First US yen support since 1998, first joint operation since 2011.
4. **Loud signalling, small money.** A photographed notepad suggesting $5-10bn, "I am the house now", "asymmetric information", against a probable $0.5bn actual leg. The signal was the instrument.
5. **Parallel push on the Fed** to upsize the FIMA repo facility (cap $60bn) so Japan could raise dollars against Treasuries instead of selling them.

## 3. Default move

What the US would normally have done:
- **Not intervene.** Treasury doctrine since 2011: exchange rates are market determined, intervention only for disorderly moves, and the US criticizes Japan rather than helping it (Japan has sat on the Treasury FX monitoring list).
- **If it intervened, sell dollars** and do it as part of a G7 statement, with the ECB and others informed, as in 2011.
- **Let Japan fund it alone** from its $1.2tn reserves, accepting Japanese Treasury sales as a side effect.

Deviation in one line: the US intervened, but in a way that (a) did not sell dollars (protecting the "strong dollar" message), (b) did not require selling Treasuries or adding supply, (c) left Japan to pay about 99% of the bill, and (d) disregarded the ECB. The deviation is best explained by the US constraint set, not by a plan against France: euros were the only liquid, non-dollar, non-Treasury asset Treasury could sell quickly without Fed or Congressional help.

## 5. Mechanism chain (if the thesis were true) and what the data says at each link

1. **US funds its yen purchase with euros to push the adjustment onto the euro.**
   Data: EUR/JPY 186.99 (30 Jul) to 180.73 (3 Aug), a 3.3% fall in two sessions, vs implied USD/JPY 162.9 to 156.7 (3.8%). By 21 Oct EUR/JPY is down 5.1%, USD/JPY down 3.1%. EUR/USD rose in August (1.148 to 1.170) and only fell after the Fed hike. **Partly consistent, but scale is trivial ($0.5bn).** Status: Plausible as intent, negligible as force.
2. **The signal ("the US backs the yen") plus BoJ hikes tells Japanese investors to come home or rotate.**
   Data: BoJ +25bp on 18 Sep. Japanese net sales of foreign bonds in four straight weeks to 10 Oct, YTD about ¥5.3tn, largest since 2022. Status: Confirmed for flows, but the driver is BoJ and JGB yields, which Bessent publicly pushed for. That push is the one real US fingerprint.
3. **Japanese selling hits France first because Japan is most overweight OATs.**
   Data: Morgan Stanley (MoF country data) finds Japanese **net purchases** of OATs of €4.6bn in June to August, then renewed selling in September. Sumitomo Mitsui DS AM sold its whole OAT book into Bunds and short JGBs. UBS: proceeds recycled into other foreign assets, not repatriated. Status: Plausible, modest and recent. Not the main driver of the spread.
4. **OAT-Bund spread widens and French banks fall.**
   Data: spread 78bp (July average) to 82bp (August) to 97bp (September) to 157.5bp (21 Oct). BNP -20.1%, Société Générale -23.5%, Crédit Agricole -18.8% since 30 Jul, vs Deutsche Bank -11.3% and DAX -1.2%. BTP-Bund only +8bp July to September, Bonos-Bund flat. Status: Confirmed as outcome. France specific. Causation from the July action is not supported: timing lags 6 weeks and the French budget, the oil shock and global term premium explain it.
5. **French stress sends safe-haven money into Treasuries, easing the US debt burden.**
   Data: Bessent on 20 Oct: "We are indeed seeing money flow out of some countries of concern and into US Treasuries". US 10y fell 18bp from 5.31% (5 Oct) to 5.13% (21 Oct) while the OAT spread widened about 14bp to 157.5bp. But since 30 Jul the US 10y is +45bp and MOVE rose 77 to 96 (peak 110 on 30 Sep). Status: marginal effect Plausible, net effect Refuted.
6. **With Europe weakened and Treasuries supported, the US devalues the dollar to reshore manufacturing.**
   Data: Fed hiked on 16 Sep. EUR/USD 1.123. No US intervention since 31 July despite "won't hesitate". Status: Refuted so far (could still come later, see section 9).
7. **Hedge funds follow Treasury, the trade is not priced in.**
   Data: crowded EUR/JPY puts (UBS, 14 Oct), "short Europe" and asset swap unwinds (Allianz, 9 Oct), Bloomberg headlines on Japanese selling of French bonds since 5 Oct. Status: Refuted. It is priced, and UBS argues over-priced.

Conclusion from the chain: links 4 and (partly) 2 and 5 are observed, but they are explained without links 1 and 6, which are the only links that require Bessent's intent. The thesis attaches an intent story to an outcome that has independent causes.

## 9. What would change my mind

Toward the thesis (orchestration):
- NY Fed Q3 FX report (mid November) or ESF quarterly showing a US euro sale of several billion or more, or **repeated euro sales** after 31 July.
- Japan MoF quarterly detail (2 to 9 Nov) showing Japan also sold **euro** reserves, which would indicate a coordinated US-Japan choice to make the euro the funding currency.
- Documents or on-record officials showing US-Japan agreement on where Japanese repatriation flows should come from (euro area rather than Treasuries).
- TIC data for September and October showing Japan's Treasury holdings stabilizing while Japanese OAT holdings fall sharply, plus euro area investors buying Treasuries. That would make the "stress lands in Europe, money lands in Treasuries" pattern a measured outcome rather than a quote.
- Treasury selling dollars in a second intervention, or a Fed cutting cycle combined with a formal currency accord, which would revive the devaluation leg.

Against even the narrow version:
- Disclosure that the euro choice was operational (euros were simply the ESF's largest liquid holding, as reserve data suggests: $26.0bn of $38.6bn) with documented internal reasoning unrelated to Europe.
- French spread compressing back toward 80 to 100bp after the budget passes with no change in Japanese flows, showing the spread was a domestic political premium.

Data gaps to fill next round: AFT non-resident share of French negotiable debt (latest quarter), MoF monthly country breakdown for September, CFTC yen and euro positioning, NY Fed Q3 FX report, the official text of the Miran paper and of the September 2025 Japan-US joint statement.
