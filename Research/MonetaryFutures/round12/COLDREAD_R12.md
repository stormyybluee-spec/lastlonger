Legal tender, bank licence, creditor, deposit insurance and a rescuing central bank arrive together in one table [31], and the safety-net value in [33] is hard to follow. ## Fairness

Both readers would call the author fair to the reader who made the claim. Block [7] says it is serious and parts are right. Each "What question does" box says which part holds. ## Does the courtroom story hold?

Mostly yes. Dock, jury, cross-examination, finding and verdict are used the same way from [4] to [65]. Two cracks. First, the numbering: the chapter says "seven questions" but they are numbered 4 to 10 [7]. Reader 1 will look for questions 1 to 3. The sentence explaining it is short and easy to miss. Second, "the defence" in [4] means the side defending printing, but in [34] it means the case for the safety net. Reader 1 may think the author changed sides.

## Does anything read like financial advice?

No. Block [56] and the box [70] say it is not advice. The three tests are written as ways to read cases, not as steps to take. The closing questions in [67] are for arguing with other people. No product, lender or amount is recommended.
