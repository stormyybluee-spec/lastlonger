Reading note. BNPL is a new door beside the bank gate. It asks for little up front, and it has grown fast (RM7.1 billion to RM11.9 billion a half year within a year). The official record so far says its overdue rate is low and falling, and its size is small against household debt. That is evidence against a story in which every new open door leads to harm. It is also evidence that the state chose to bring the new door under a regulator, rather than leave it ungoverned.

## 3. H4. What lenders ask for, and why (plain words)

A lender is handing over money now for a promise of money later. It cannot see inside the borrower's life, so it asks for three things that stand in for what it cannot see. A record of how the borrower has paid before (credit records). Something it can take and sell if the promise breaks (collateral). Evidence that money will keep coming in (proof of income). Each one lowers the lender's own risk. Each one is also something a poor or new borrower is less likely to have. That is the gate in one sentence.

Islamic finance note, neutral. AIM's financing is structured under Islamic contracts, and its members pay a charge set by the contract rather than conventional interest. This lane did not open AIM's own scheme terms, so the size and name of that charge are a gap. Ar-Rahnu likewise charges a fee for keeping the pledged gold safe, which is the form required by the contract it uses.

Rival reading on AIM. A very low portfolio at risk can mean careful borrowers, strong group pressure, or loans rescheduled before they count as late. The press figures do not say which. Banerjee, Karlan and Zinman's result warns that high repayment is not the same as large gains in income.
