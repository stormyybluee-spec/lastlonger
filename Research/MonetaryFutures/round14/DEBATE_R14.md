## 1. Summary

Points ruled: 121 (95 Verifier rows, 26 red team points). Accept 103. Accept in part 14. Reject 4.
Edits: 83 (E01 to E83). New blocks: 10 (N1 to N10). One table to delete by hand (block 34).

What changes most:
1. Every mismatch the Verifier found is fixed: the 1971 gold cell now reads 10.5 billion in June so the row divides to 2.9, the crisis table is replaced with a correctly labelled one that adds 1919 to 1939 (13.4) and a rich-country banking column, "faster than it ever had before" and "all grew faster" are corrected, and Nixon's and Bernanke's words are now word for word.
2. The chapter now explains causes, not only scores: why the dollar claims piled up (N1), why falling behind on gold forced a choice, with a cloakroom picture (N2), why a floating currency is a limit (N3), what a ringgit fall did to a dollar debt inside a Penang firm (N4, N5), what the oil shocks did, and how private credit grew offshore as eurodollars.
3. Malaysia is told accurately: it did not take an IMF loan, its short-term foreign debt was below its reserves, and the debate on its 1998 controls is given both ways (N6, N7). Its government's ringgit borrowing is no longer called a "lesson learned".
4. Fairness both ways: the interwar gold years and the 2022 recovery now appear, the closing gold number gets a counterweight (N10) and no longer reads as a pitch for gold, a gold-side voice (Lehrman) is added, and the charitable reading of "unlimited" (no metal cap) is granted in words.
5. Main path grows by about 1,320 words: from 2,879 to about 4,190 by the Court's count, which includes table cells and headers (the draft's "about 2,800" reads as 2,879 on this count, so the Editor's own count should land near 4,110). The go-deeper boxes are not counted.

The four findings now read:
- Finding 1: Holds in part. A real limit on governments, a loose one on banks. Deciding numbers: claims 2.9 times the gold, lending 53% of output in 1970 against 111% in 2007, gold cover lifted in 1968 when it bound.
- Finding 2: Does not hold as stated. No metal cap, but elastic, not unlimited.
- Finding 3: Holds in part. It came late, held for long spells and missed badly at times. Crises per 100 country-years 7.9 then 10.7, mostly currency crises.
- Finding 4: Not shown (was "holds in part"). Late developers grew faster after 1973 and the rich world slower, but the evidence does not tie either to the end of the gold link.
