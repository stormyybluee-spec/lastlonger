## The answer in one paragraph
Energy will not replace the dollar you spend. It is replacing gold's old job: the scarce physical thing that caps how much real output any money can claim. In an AI and robot economy output scales with delivered power, and power cannot be printed. Energy is also entering the system as collateral inside the dollar (power plants, data centers, PPAs and GPUs backing dollar loans, compute futures settling in dollars), a "watt-dollar" descended from the 1974 petrodollar. It fails as spendable money because it is perishable, location-bound and getting cheaper. Musk's "currency measured in wattage and tonnage" is a post-scarcity accounting idea close to the 1930s Technocracy plan, and its real problem is who allocates. Confidence 65 (Plausible).

## How the Fed and Treasury are tackling the debt
They are managing who must buy it, not shrinking it. Captive demand is the chosen strategy: stablecoin reserves forced into short bills from 2027-01-18, bank leverage relief, bills sized to money funds, long-bond buybacks, a Fed repo line Japan promised to use but has not (FIMA unused from 18 February to 14 October 2026). The Fed under Warsh is hiking. Inflation above the 3.5% average debt cost erodes the ratio quietly. No gold revaluation, no Bitcoin buying, no formal accord. Set 1 (gold, bonds, credit) is absorbing Set 2 (stablecoins, crypto rails): an acquisition, not a merger.

## Round 2 in one paragraph
New rails, old anchors. Money changes its plumbing (stablecoins, tokenized funds, AI agents paying in dollar tokens) while the anchors stay: Treasuries back the tokens by law, gold is the neutral asset between states, and borrowing against future income stays with regulated lenders. UHI can help you qualify for credit but should not be pledgeable as cash, and every country can only pay what its taxes, funds and currency carry. For an 18-year-old the old ladder still exists but its housing rung is broken and its launch rung is cheaper but crowded. Ownership, not credit, decides who keeps up. Full answers on the Round 2 page and in round2/DEBATE_R2.md.

## Round 3 in one paragraph
The pipes held and the pressure moved: rent, tax and hardware now decide what a dollar buys. Stablecoins were flat for a year ($304.9B to $303.8B) while the Fed added $370B of bills and money funds about $505B, and through the Hormuz shock the FIMA repo line sat at zero while foreign governments sold $254B of Treasuries instead. A UHI is limited by rent and the tax base, not robots. Housing takes 40% to 48% of each UHI dollar and its supply grows about 1% a year, so a printed Musk-scale UHI runs about 5.8% to 9.8% inflation now, and income and payroll taxes carry 86.2% of federal receipts while a tax on watts or compute would raise under 1%. Cheap production does not reach people on its own: land is 40% of US home value (68% in San Jose), platforms take a rising slice of every sale, and the bottom half own 0.58% of directly held stocks, which Trump Accounts as designed lift only to about 0.60% to 0.70% by 2045. China extended the truce politically to 10 January 2027 but left the legal cliff on 10 November (court odds of a full lapse 12%), and markets price the next Fed hike for 9 December, not 28 October. So the scenario odds barely move. Full rulings in round3/DEBATE_R3.md, earlier-round corrections appended to round2/DEBATE_R2.md and round1/RELOOP.md.

## Round 4 in one paragraph
If a giant falls, nobody inherits it whole and Europe's money keeps going West. The dollar's share of world reserves fell from about 71% to 56.70% since 2000, but the yuan has only 2.11%. The lost share went to gold (now about 26% of all reserves, worth about 1.8 times all euro reserves) and to many smaller currencies. China cannot inherit with capital controls, and if it opened, money would first run into dollars. The premise that everyone prays for a US downfall fails, because the biggest creditors lose first. Europe is sliding slowly, not falling: its income per head in purchasing power has held at about 70% to 75% of the US since 2000, and the sharp edge is France (OAT-Bund 171.8bp, Le Pen 46% in a $155.7M market). Breaking away does not help on the evidence (Brexit cost about 6% to 8% of GDP per head). If Europe declines, about 55% of its outbound money goes to North America and about 6% to Latin America, so the owner is right on "West" and wrong on Latin America. Southeast Asia wins factories, not money. Full rulings in round4/DEBATE_R4.md.
