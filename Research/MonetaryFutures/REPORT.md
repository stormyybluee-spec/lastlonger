## The answer in one paragraph
Energy will not replace the dollar you spend. It is replacing gold's old job: the scarce physical thing that caps how much real output any money can claim. In an AI and robot economy output scales with delivered power, and power cannot be printed. Energy is also entering the system as collateral inside the dollar (power plants, data centers, PPAs and GPUs backing dollar loans, compute futures settling in dollars), a "watt-dollar" descended from the 1974 petrodollar. It fails as spendable money because it is perishable, location-bound and getting cheaper. Musk's "currency measured in wattage and tonnage" is a post-scarcity accounting idea close to the 1930s Technocracy plan, and its real problem is who allocates. Confidence 65 (Plausible).

## How the Fed and Treasury are tackling the debt
They are managing who must buy it, not shrinking it. Captive demand is the chosen strategy: stablecoin reserves forced into short bills from 2027-01-18, bank leverage relief, bills sized to money funds, long-bond buybacks, a Fed repo line Japan promised to use but has not (FIMA unused from 18 February to 14 October 2026). The Fed under Warsh is hiking. Inflation above the 3.5% average debt cost erodes the ratio quietly. No gold revaluation, no Bitcoin buying, no formal accord. Set 1 (gold, bonds, credit) and Set 2 (stablecoins, crypto rails) are joining on Set 1's terms. Round 8 restated the old one-line view ("Set 1 is absorbing Set 2, an acquisition, not a merger") as five readings with dated tests: A absorption (old wrappers swallow Bitcoin, fits), B leakage (dollars drain into Bitcoin, fails the money meter), C two-way valve (toward Bitcoin in booms, back in busts, live), D risk transfer (savers carry Bitcoin's risk under a credit label, fits), E collateral bridge (Bitcoin starts to back new dollars, real but too small to measure). First check: 31 December 2026.

## Round 2 in one paragraph
New rails, old anchors. Money changes its plumbing (stablecoins, tokenized funds, AI agents paying in dollar tokens) while the anchors stay: Treasuries back the tokens by law, gold is the neutral asset between states, and borrowing against future income stays with regulated lenders. UHI can help you qualify for credit but should not be pledgeable as cash, and every country can only pay what its taxes, funds and currency carry. For an 18-year-old the old ladder still exists but its housing rung is broken and its launch rung is cheaper but crowded. Ownership, not credit, decides who keeps up. Full answers on the Round 2 page and in round2/DEBATE_R2.md.

## Round 3 in one paragraph
The pipes held and the pressure moved: rent, tax and hardware now decide what a dollar buys. Stablecoins were flat for a year ($304.9B to $303.8B) while the Fed added $370B of bills and money funds about $505B, and through the Hormuz shock the FIMA repo line sat at zero while foreign governments sold $254B of Treasuries instead. A UHI is limited by rent and the tax base, not robots. Housing takes 40% to 48% of each UHI dollar and its supply grows about 1% a year, so a printed Musk-scale UHI runs about 5.8% to 9.8% inflation now, and income and payroll taxes carry 86.2% of federal receipts while a tax on watts or compute would raise under 1%. Cheap production does not reach people on its own: land is 40% of US home value (68% in San Jose), platforms take a rising slice of every sale, and the bottom half own 0.58% of directly held stocks, which Trump Accounts as designed lift only to about 0.60% to 0.70% by 2045. China extended the truce politically to 10 January 2027 but left the legal cliff on 10 November (court odds of a full lapse 12%), and markets price the next Fed hike for 9 December, not 28 October. So the scenario odds barely move. Full rulings in round3/DEBATE_R3.md, earlier-round corrections appended to round2/DEBATE_R2.md and round1/RELOOP.md.

## Round 4 in one paragraph
If a giant falls, nobody inherits it whole and Europe's money keeps going West. The dollar's share of world reserves fell from about 71% to 56.70% since 2000, but the yuan has only 2.11%. The lost share went to gold (now about 26% of all reserves, worth about 1.8 times all euro reserves) and to many smaller currencies. China cannot inherit with capital controls, and if it opened, money would first run into dollars. The premise that everyone prays for a US downfall fails, because the biggest creditors lose first. Europe is sliding slowly, not falling: its income per head in purchasing power has held at about 70% to 75% of the US since 2000, and the sharp edge is France (OAT-Bund 171.8bp, Le Pen 46% in a $155.7M market). Breaking away does not help on the evidence (Brexit cost about 6% to 8% of GDP per head). If Europe declines, about 55% of its outbound money goes to North America and about 6% to Latin America, so the owner is right on "West" and wrong on Latin America. Southeast Asia wins factories, not money. Full rulings in round4/DEBATE_R4.md.

## Round 10 in one paragraph (2026-11-12, first Unseen mode round)
The desk tested claims that oil dollars, Treasury buyers and the great powers move by hidden agreement, grading every guess and dating every test (Landscape chapter 14). Gulf states still add to US Treasuries (up 51% since January 2023) while also buying US shares, so "shares instead of Treasuries" fails for the states as a whole. China takes about a third of Gulf crude and no source shows 30% of Gulf oil paid in yuan. The 1974 Treasury arrangement was real and narrow, and ended by 2016. Private money, booked through custody centres, now funds the growth of the Treasury market. A secret staged three-way alignment of the US, the Gulf and China is remote (about 1%) and stays open. For the live question, energy trade has not pulled Gulf money off the dollar so far, which fits the dollar-extension scenario. A reading, with dated tests.

## Round 11 in one paragraph (2026-11-15, Rulebook Level 3 opens)
Level 3, "The big arguments", now has its first chapter: "Is printing money a scam? The charge, the defence and the toll". It puts printing on trial, makes the case against it as strongly as it can be made, tests it, then puts hard money on trial too. Three questions drive it: could a world on gold have rebuilt after 1945, which money gives new players a fairer start, and does a sword that loses resale value explain the 2% target. The finding: not a scam in the plain sense and not a Ponzi by its features, but a real toll that falls hardest on cash holders, renters and late savers, with a warning on the strict Ponzi test (the US deficit before interest is too large). Hard money has its own toll, on borrowers and the young. So the real argument is who pays, how much, who decides and whether it can be seen. Both cold readers reached the pass mark of 8.

## Round 12 in one paragraph (2026-11-16, Level 3 chapter 02)
"Money from the future: seven harder questions about credit" continues the trial. It takes the owner's four-part thesis as the defence's bigger claim and cross-examines it with questions 4 to 10: were peasants poor from scarce money or scarce output, was credit born in 1971, can a society borrow from the future, why does the law lean toward money made from nothing, why are the biggest printers poor, who gets through the credit gate, and does credit make a person free or owned. The epilogue grades the thesis: all four parts hold in part. The strongest is the last (credit has a gate that favours those who already have). The weakest is the second (the peasants were poor mainly because output was scarce). The rebuilt thesis: plenty comes from output, credit lets people build before they have saved, the law leans toward it because the state, the banks and voters each gain, and the fight is over what credit is used for and who is let through the gate. Both cold readers reached the pass mark of 8.

## Round 14 in one paragraph (2026-11-16, the first deep Level 3 chapter)
"The weekend that cut the anchor: what 1971 really changed" tests the owner's claim that 1971 is the hinge of modern money. Before 1971 gold was a real limit on what America could promise foreign governments and a loose limit on bank lending at home. After 1971 borrowing became elastic, not unlimited: inflation, lenders and foreign-currency debt took the place of gold, and they bind a small country harder than they bind the issuer of the dollar. The governance that replaced gold, the inflation target, came late, held for long spells and missed badly at times, with more frequent crises than the gold-linked years. Late developers, Malaysia among them, grew faster after 1973 and the rich world slower, but the evidence does not tie either to the gold link. Six deep chapters remain.

## Round 15 in one paragraph (2026-11-16, the second deep Level 3 chapter)
"Why the peasants stayed poor: money, output and the missing chain" tests the owner's claim that old poverty came from a missing chain of money, credit, a strong state, schools and knowledge, each needing promises about the future to start. Money helped but was not enough alone: Spain, China and Holland had plenty of money or cheap credit and each later stalled. The other links really were missing, and the owner's schooling figure matches the record. Promises about the future built the state and the great networks, and worked when something real stood behind them, while the first factories were built mostly from their owners' funds and ran on short credit. The loop was real, and what broke it is still argued among people, rules, prices, knowledge, the state and, for some scholars, money and finance. The chapter's evidence is mostly European and it says so. Five deep chapters remain.

## Round 16 in one paragraph (2026-11-19, the third deep Level 3 chapter)
"Who wrote the rules of money, and for whom?" opens in Malaya in the 1950s, when the country's money came from a currency board that could not steer credit, and asks what central banks are for. The first ones were built to fund wars and stop bank panics, and growth was written into their laws later, usually second. Malaysia's law of 2009 asks for stability that helps the economy grow. Mild inflation is on purpose and published, and the big gains to the state come when inflation beats what lenders expected or when rates are held down. Central banks are controlled by governments, laws and rules on what they must publish, not directly by voters, and the public chose the rules at one remove, through lawmakers. Places that tied the issuer's hands got steadier prices and no rescue in a crisis. Four deep chapters remain.

## Round 17 in one paragraph (2026-11-25, the fourth deep Level 3 chapter)
"Paying for tomorrow: who funds the things that pay back later?" opens with the Brontë sisters' railway shares, which rose to 120 pounds and fell to 20 while the railway itself was built and served the country. New technology does cost a fortune before it pays back, and most single bets fail. In the cases tested, the promises that paid were largely shares bought by savers and governments promising to buy the product, as with the microchip in 1962 and NASA's fixed-price cargo contracts. Cheap borrowing fed two of three booms but not the internet boom, and government borrowing bought by a central bank was not tested. Korea did aim created money at export loans while its heavy industry grew, at a price in inflation and debt, while Taiwan and Penang built industries by other roads. Three deep chapters remain.

## Round 18 in one paragraph (2026-11-26, the fifth deep Level 3 chapter)
"Why do the biggest printers end up poor? Speed, trust and the price of money" opens with Stefan Zweig paying 50,000 marks for a newspaper in the morning and 100,000 at night. Money growth and price rises go together over long periods and in high inflation, and loosely in calm times: the largest money creations of the 2010s barely moved shop prices for years, because much of the new money sat still. The speed at which money changes hands rises when trust falls. The four great printers paid their governments' bills with new money while another heavy blow struck, and the line between that and central bank bond buying is one of degree and trust. Malaysia catches part of the world's inflation through food and the dollar, kept prices far calmer than its neighbours in 1998 and than the West in 2022, and the state's budget paid much of the cost. Two deep chapters remain.
