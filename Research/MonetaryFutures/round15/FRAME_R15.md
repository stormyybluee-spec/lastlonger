# Round 15 frame (2026-11-16): Level 3 deep chapter on scarce money or scarce output

Chapter: working id rb-peasant, the second of the seven deep chapters (round13/SERIES_L3.md). Working title: "Why the peasants stayed poor: money, output and the missing chain".
It deepens question 4 of rb-credit and tests the owner's point 3 (round13/OWNER_R13.md).

## Hypotheses to test (the owner's point 3, with the question-mark rule)
- H1. Output was scarce in the past because the cycle of money and credit that creates output was scarce. Test: did places with more money, or more credit, make more? Cases for and against: Spain's silver, Song China's paper money, the Dutch Republic, England after 1688, colonial America's paper money.
- H2. The other links of the chain were missing too: a central state that could tax and keep order, schooling, and shared knowledge. Test: how these moved over time (tax revenue as a share of output, literacy and school enrolment, the spread of print) and whether they moved before or after growth took off.
- H3. Each link needs large promises about the future to start: armies, schools, roads and factories are paid for long before they pay back. Test: how the first modern states and the first factories were in fact financed (government debt, retained profits, local credit, banks), and how much of it was borrowing.
- H4. A society that started and was stopped by war, famine or old practice fell back into a loop. Test: the evidence for a trap in which gains were eaten by population growth or destroyed by war (Malthus, Clark), and the cases that broke out early and then stalled (Song China, northern Italy, the Dutch Republic).

## Lanes
- R15A, data: long-run series for H1 to H4.
- R15B, arguments and human detail: the main explanations of why growth took off, each at full strength, and sourced first-hand detail of ordinary life before it did.
