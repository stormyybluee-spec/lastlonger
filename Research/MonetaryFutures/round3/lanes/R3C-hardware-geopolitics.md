## Answers

### R3-4. Does China let the 2026-11-10 export-control truce lapse, finalize the solar know-how ban, and pair it with yuan-financed supply to the Global South?

**Answer.** Most likely no lapse, yes to the solar ban, and only a thin yuan layer. The political truce has already been extended. Bessent announced on 23 Sept 2026 that the "Busan Agreement" runs to 10 Jan 2027, and MOFCOM confirmed it on 28 Sept, together with a "30-for-30" tariff cut on about $30B of goods each way. But no numbered MOFCOM notice has yet moved the legal end date (10 Nov 2026) of Announcement No. 70, which pauses the October 2025 package: five more rare earths, battery, cathode and graphite-anode items, and the 0.1% extraterritorial rule. China issued the original pause only three days before its deadline in 2025, so silence on 22 Oct tells us little. My odds for 10 Nov: about 72% a numbered extension, about 16% a partial replacement (general licences or a narrower rule), and about 12% a full legal lapse. For the separate 27 Nov gallium, germanium and antimony suspension aimed at the US, about 80% it is extended. The solar move is real and well designed: MOFCOM Announcement 2026 No. 48 (16 Oct) would ban or restrict 12 solar technologies and their core equipment. It explicitly excludes products made with them, so China keeps selling panels and cells while withholding the factories. It also adds 23 AI and 5 biomedicine technologies. My odds that it is finalized in some form are about 25% by 31 Dec 2026 and about 70% by 30 Jun 2027. The yuan-finance pairing is the weakest part. Most Chinese energy lending has been in dollars (94% in 2010 to 2021, per ODI). Chinese renewable project finance abroad was small (3 GW in 2022 to 2023, per BU). The Global South solar boom, Pakistan for example, is mostly bought for cash. The yuan is growing through refinancing old loans instead: Kenya's yuan share of external debt rose from 5% to 11.7%.
- Status: **Plausible**. Confidence **70** on "extend, finalize, thin yuan layer". Horizon **H0** (the controls), **H1** (any yuan-watt bloc).
- Three views. **Governance:** the truce is a rolling two-month option that Beijing keeps short on purpose, and the binding legal text, not the press statement, is what to watch. **Business:** plan to two dates (10 Nov legal, 10 Jan political), expect panels and cells to stay cheap and available, and expect equipment, line know-how and thin-wafer tools to stop flowing. **Ordinary person (wage or UHI):** no change to the money you are paid in. If anything moves, it is the price of EVs, home batteries and solar installs made outside China.

### R3-12. Does electric freight just swap oil dependence for battery dependence?

**Answer.** Partly yes, but it swaps one kind of dependence for a different and less acute kind. By share, batteries are far more concentrated than oil. China made over 80% of battery cells in 2025, about 85% of cathode material, more than 90% of anode material, about 94% of refined graphite and 71% of refined lithium. Oil's top producer (the US) has 22%, the top ten have 73%, and Hormuz carries about 20% of world consumption. By the HHI concentration index (a model), battery mid-stream stages score 5,000 to 9,000. Oil scores about 900 by country and about 2,000 even if OPEC+ is counted as one bloc. But oil is a flow and a battery is a stock. Cut off oil and trucks stop within weeks. Cut off batteries and the existing fleet keeps running, and only fleet growth and the 8 to 12% of packs replaced each year are exposed (model). So dependence moves from a running cost exposed to OPEC+ and Hormuz to a capital cost exposed to China, concentrated in anodes, graphite, LFP cathodes and precursors. The irony is that China itself is the main case of electric freight: 38.1% of heavy trucks sold in January to September 2026 were new-energy, 50.3% in September, mostly on CATL batteries. That is Beijing hedging its own Hormuz exposure. The US barely electrifies heavy freight (about 17,000 electric trucks in 2025, over 95% medium-duty). It already exports oil, so for the US the swap would trade a price exposure for a supply exposure.
- Status: **Plausible**. Confidence **70**. Horizon **H0 to H2** (H0 in China, H1 to H2 in the US and EU).
- Three views. **Governance:** the battery chokepoint sits in mid-stream materials (anode, graphite, LFP cathode, precursors), not cells, so policy should fund those, and a stockpile of packs or anode material works like an SPR for a stock-type dependence. **Business:** an electric fleet's exposure is front-loaded into capex and replacement packs. Lock multi-year pack supply and swap contracts, or diversify chemistry (nickel-based cells can already be made outside China, LFP mostly cannot). **Ordinary person (wage or UHI):** an electric freight system makes goods prices less sensitive to oil shocks like 2026's, but a China squeeze would show up as dearer delivery vans, buses and home batteries, slowly, not as an overnight price spike.

## Key findings

1. **The truce is already extended politically, not legally.** Bessent said on Fox News (23 Sept 2026): "We will extend what we call the 'Busan Agreement' ... that was scheduled to end on November 10 ... until January 10." He added that "some deliverables ... have not been perfect on the Chinese side". MOFCOM's Americas and Oceania department said on 28 Sept that the Kuala Lumpur arrangement was extended to 10 Jan 2027, with a reciprocal tariff cut on about $30B of goods each way (about 90% of lines to MFN), a Board of Trade, an agricultural working group and an investment board. Neither text names Announcement No. 70 or rare earths. As of 14 Oct, MOFCOM's list showed no notice amending No. 70 (Carra Globe). The White House fact sheet of 25 Sept says only that the two sides "continue to work on" rare-earth shortages. [T2/T1, V]
2. **The legal stakes on 10 Nov.** No. 70 pauses Nos. 55, 56, 57, 58, 61 and 62 of 9 Oct 2025: five more rare earths (Eu, Ho, Er, Tm, Yb), lithium-battery, cathode and graphite-anode items (No. 58), the 0.1% foreign-direct-product-style rule (No. 61) and rare-earth technology controls (No. 62). The April 2025 licences for seven elements (No. 18) were never suspended. A separate US-only measure (Article 2 of No. 46, gallium, germanium, antimony, superhard materials) is paused by No. 72 until 27 Nov 2026. [T1, V via rareearthdirectory and Carra Globe]
3. **Timing precedent favors a late notice.** In 2025 the pause (No. 70) was issued on 7 Nov for a 10 Nov deadline, and No. 72 on 9 Nov. In April 2026 the MOFCOM spokesperson declined to say whether the pause would be extended and pointed to the consultation mechanism (udn.com). Silence on 22 Oct is the base case, not a signal. [T2, V/S]
4. **Flows are being used as goodwill.** China's rare-earth magnet exports reached 6,165 t in September 2026, a 2026 high (+23% m/m, +6.7% y/y), with exports to the US up 18% m/m to 604 t. SMM attributes this to backlog release after the consultations, with larger producers getting smoother licensing. [T0 via T4, S]
5. **The solar proposal is a know-how ban, not a goods ban.** Announcement 2026 No. 48 (MOFCOM and MOST, 16 Oct) covers 40 technologies: 23 AI, 5 biomedicine, 12 solar. 14 would be banned and 26 restricted. Proposed bans include: cold-hydrogenation fluidized-bed trichlorosilane (single line ≥250,000 t/yr) and silane fluidized-bed polysilicon (≥20,000 t/yr), wafers ≤70 µm, edge-passivation for cut cells on ≤140 µm wafers, and perovskite and tandem modules (tandem ≥30% efficiency on ≥182 mm wafers, single-junction perovskite ≥1 m² at ≥18%). Proposed restrictions include: polysilicon lines ≥30,000 t/yr, ingot pullers (furnace bore ≥1,600 mm, hot zone ≥35 inch, pull speed ≥1.2 mm/min), wafers ≤130 µm with ≤28 µm tungsten wire, and TOPCon, XBC and HJT process tools. The scope covers core equipment such as fluidized beds, reduction furnaces, crystal pullers, wire saws, LPCVD, PECVD, ALD, boron diffusion and copper plating. Exporters must register before any transfer, including when a domestic owner of the technology becomes foreign-controlled. Comments close 15 Nov 2026. The spokesperson says "products made with the relevant technologies are not within the scope" and that line exports, installation, commissioning and upgrades need a technology export licence. [T1 proposal, V primary annex]
6. **The AI part of No. 48 shows how a Global South pairing would work.** For restricted AI technologies, MOFCOM offers automatic licensing for open-source releases, Chinese firms' own overseas compute centers, offshore chip tape-out and joint R&D. API, SaaS and app access are outside scope. It promises "more facilitation" for technology exports among members of the World AI Cooperation Organization, and says China supports Global South AI capacity building. No such Global South facilitation clause appears for solar. [T2, V]
7. **The US side is de-escalating in parallel.** USTR delayed Section 301 tariffs on Chinese legacy chips to 23 June 2027, at an initial 0% (December 2025). The White House published Board of Trade product lists in October 2026: appliances, toys, holiday decorations and car seats from China, and US farm goods, seafood, logs, cosmetics and medical devices. Officials cautioned there would be no immediate cuts, and Section 301 changes need notice and comment (Covington). I found no October 2026 on-record statement by Lutnick or Commerce on the truce (gap). [T1/T2, V]
8. **Yuan finance is growing by refinancing, not by new solar lending.** Kenya converted three China Exim railway loans from dollars to yuan. The yuan's share of Kenya's external debt rose from 5% to 11.7% in the year to June 2026 (about 665B shillings), and the Treasury claims a saving of about $215M a year. AidData reports interest from Ethiopia, Mozambique, Zambia, Pakistan and Indonesia, and says China Exim is encouraging, and in some cases requiring, yuan borrowing (Sri Lanka, Bangladesh). In contrast, 94% of Chinese energy-sector loans in 2010 to 2021 were in dollars (ODI). Chinese-financed renewable capacity abroad was only about 3 GW in 2022 to 2023, and direct energy lending fell to 10.3% of Chinese overseas development finance (BU GDP). [T0/T4, V/S]
9. **The Global South buys Chinese solar for cash and uses it as a Hormuz hedge.** Pakistan avoided over $12B of oil and gas imports since 2020 through a distributed solar boom (Renewables First and CREA), and rooftop solar is now about a quarter of its energy mix. Most panels are Chinese and bought outright. The "yuan watt" in R2E chain A shows up as Chinese hardware, not Chinese money. [T4, V]
10. **Battery concentration by stage (IEA 2026).** In 2025 China made 70% of electric cars, over 80% of battery cells, about 85% of cathode active material and more than 90% of anode active material (GEVO 2026). Refined lithium is 71% Chinese, refined graphite 94%, and the average top refiner's share across strategic minerals rose from 70% (2020) to about 72% (2025). China mines only about a quarter of lithium (GCMO 2026, via search). Projects outside the dominant supplier cost 20% to over 150% more. [T0/T4, V for GEVO, S for GCMO]
11. **The IEA's "N-1" test shows where the chokepoint is.** Without China, in 2025 the rest of the world could not meet its own needs for anode active material, LFP cathode and its precursors, or nickel-based precursors. LFP batteries "almost exclusively" rely on Chinese supply. About 80% of EV batteries deployed outside China use nickel chemistries, where non-China cell supply is adequate. In 2035 (STEPS) China still has nearly 90% of anode material, about three-quarters of cathode material and two-thirds of batteries. [T4, V]
12. **Oil concentration for comparison.** In 2023 the US had 22% of world liquids output, Saudi Arabia 11%, Russia 11%, and the top ten 73% of 101.8 mb/d (EIA). The six OPEC+ members in the top ten held 36.7% (model). Hormuz carried 20 mb/d in 2024, about 20% of world consumption and over a quarter of seaborne oil trade, plus about a fifth of LNG trade (EIA). The model puts oil's country HHI at 830 to 906, and about 2,000 if OPEC+ acts as one supplier, against an estimated 5,250 (lithium refining) to 9,400 (LFP cathode) for battery stages. [T0 + model]
13. **Electric freight is a Chinese phenomenon.** Electric trucks were 9% of world truck sales in 2025, over 400,000 units, with China at over 90% of them. One in four trucks sold in China was electric, and 28% of heavy trucks. CATL supplied 80% of the batteries in Chinese electric trucks. Swap-capable trucks were about 15% of China's 2025 e-truck sales. Chinese e-truck sales outnumbered LNG truck sales for the first time (above 25% vs 15%). By insurance registrations, the new-energy heavy truck share was 38.10% in January to September 2026 (diesel 40.31%) and 50.28% in September, with tractors at 64.48%. The IEA says e-trucks avoid about 1 mb/d of oil demand by 2035 under current policies. [T0/T4, V]
14. **Swapping is growing in absolute terms but shrinking in share.** CATL launched Tianxing II on 20 Oct 2026: a leased swappable main pack (432 or 513 kWh) plus an onboard range-extender pack, 5-minute swaps, and 13,000 initial units ordered by 14 partners including SF, ZTO and YTO. The targets are 900 Qiji stations by end-2026 and 2,000 by 2027. Stations stood at about 305 at end-2025 and "more than 300" in April 2026. One market estimate has the swap share of e-heavy trucks falling from about 32% to 24% in H1 2026 as cable charging grew faster. CATL says 430,000 e-heavy trucks run on its batteries (company claim). [T2/T4, V/S]
15. **US and EU onshoring is real in nameplate terms, thin in output, and partly Chinese.** Global cell nameplate capacity was above 4 TWh at end-2025. The EU and US hold 6 to 7% each, both growing about 50% y/y. North American-headquartered firms own over 35% of US nameplate (mostly Tesla) but made only about 3% of batteries installed in EVs sold in 2025. Over 50 GWh of US capacity (LGES, Ford) was switched to LFP, aimed mainly at storage, and storage was one-third of US battery deployment in 2025. LGES's 2024 EBIT would have been about -$650M without US tax credits (IEA). Ford's Marshall, Michigan LFP plant runs on CATL licensed technology and is under DOT pressure (Duffy letter, September 2026). In the EU, CATL's Debrecen plant (Hungary) began trial production on 22 Sept 2026 and targets about 100 GWh by 2030. Lyten bought Northvolt's Swedish assets, with Heide (Germany) targeting 2028. Over 20 GWh of planned US storage-cell capacity was scrapped in H1 2025 (CEA). [T0/T4, V/S]
16. **The US heavy segment is barely moving.** US electric truck sales rose 25% to 17,000 in 2025, but over 95% were medium trucks (Rivian vans for Amazon). California's ACT rule was revoked and EPA repealed heavy-vehicle GHG standards. US production was about 12,000 e-trucks with exports down 20%. Nikola, Bollinger, Proterra, Arrival and Volta went bankrupt. Tesla Semi deliveries began in September 2026 (R2 R2D). Electric heavy trucks in Europe were about 1.5% of sales at end-2025. [T0/T4, V]

## Anomalies

1. **The battery hegemon is the fastest electrifier of freight.** Default: the supplier exports and the importers adopt. Observed: China, the largest oil importer and the most Hormuz-exposed big economy, electrified its heavy trucks first (38% YTD 2026) while the US (a net oil exporter) stalls. Reading: for China, electric freight removes an import dependence. For the US it would add one. The same technology has opposite security signs.
2. **Extension without legal text.** Default: an extension is published as an amending notice. Observed: a ministry press statement that does not name No. 70. Reading: deliberate ambiguity keeps the 0.1% rule as a loaded option through the Board of Trade's first round.
3. **New controls during a truce.** Default: no new restrictions while extending a truce. Observed: 40 new technology controls three weeks after the extension. Reading: China separates "goods" (traded for tariffs) from "know-how" (kept as structural advantage). The truce covers goods.
4. **The yuan advances through old debt.** Default: a currency bloc grows through new lending. Observed: Kenya's yuan share doubled with no new money, by redenominating railway loans. Reading: the yuan's route into the Global South is liability management while US rates are high, which reverses if Fed rates fall.
5. **A Round 2 tripwire measured the wrong thing.** The R2 ruling C6 and R3-12 test "US cell capacity above 20 GW by 2028" comes from R1 L3, where it means **solar** cells (about 3.2 GW in October 2025). US **battery** cell nameplate is already about 6 to 7% of over 4 TWh, roughly 240 to 280 GWh (derived). The battery test should be about anode, graphite and LFP cathode, not cells.

## Mechanism chains

**Chain R3C-A (truce cliff, H0).** MOFCOM issues a numbered notice around 7 to 10 Nov extending No. 70 to 10 Jan 2027 (base case 72%) → magnet and battery-material licensing continues on the April 2025 basis → NdPr and magnet prices stay range-bound → focus moves to the Board of Trade list and the 10 Jan cliff. Alternative (12%): no notice → Nos. 57, 58, 61 and 62 apply again from 11 Nov → foreign-made magnet assemblies with 0.1% or more Chinese rare earths need MOFCOM licences, and graphite-anode and cathode exports need licences → US and EU auto, robot and BESS lines feel it in December and January → the US answers with Affiliates Rule or chip measures, and the Board of Trade stalls. Tests: MOFCOM announcement list 1 to 10 Nov, December customs magnet data, Chinese graphite-anode exports.

**Chain R3C-B (know-how denial, H0 to H1).** No. 48 is finalized (H1 2027 likely) → Chinese toolmakers cannot export ≤70 µm wafer tech, fluidized-bed polysilicon, or perovskite and tandem lines, and need licences for TOPCon, HJT and XBC tools and services → US, Indian and Gulf factories run older-generation lines or pay non-Chinese toolmakers more → Chinese finished panels keep a cost and efficiency edge → importers keep buying Chinese modules (allowed) → the "deny tools, sell product" model of R2E chain A is confirmed for solar. Tests: final catalogue text, Indian ALMM cell capacity, US solar cells (SEIA) vs 20 GW by 2028.

**Chain R3C-C (oil flow to battery stock, H0 to H2).** Freight electrifies → oil demand falls about 1 mb/d by 2035 from trucks (IEA) → Hormuz and OPEC+ leverage over the running cost of freight shrinks → battery packs become the capital input, 80 to 94% Chinese at mid-stream stages → a China supply cut stops fleet growth and pack replacement (8 to 12% of the fleet's battery stock a year, model), not operations → the dependence is real but slow-acting, and it can be buffered by pack or anode stockpiles and longer pack life → so the swap lowers price volatility and raises the strategic stake on mid-stream materials. Tests: IEA N-1 bars for anode and LFP cathode above 100% outside China, non-China graphite-anode capacity, US LFP cathode plants operating.

**Chain R3C-D (yuan by refinance, H1).** US rates stay high → Global South borrowers redenominate Chinese dollar loans into yuan (Kenya) → yuan debt service creates yuan demand without yuan income → the borrowers seek yuan trade receipts (exports to China) or swap lines → a modest yuan settlement layer forms in Chinese-built infrastructure countries. This reverses if Fed cuts narrow the rate gap. Tests: Kenya, Ethiopia, Zambia, Pakistan debt-currency data, panda bond issuance by sovereigns, PBoC swap lines.
