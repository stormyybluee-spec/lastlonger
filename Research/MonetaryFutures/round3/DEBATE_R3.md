## The short version

### The pipes held, the pressure moved: rent, tax and hardware now decide what a dollar buys

Round 3 tested Round 2's 'new rails, old anchors' picture against a year of hard data, and the anchors did all the work. Stablecoins were flat for a year ($304.9B to $303.8B) while US bank deposits grew $1.23T, money funds about $505B and the Fed's bill holdings $370B. Through the Hormuz shock and Japan's ¥27.1T of interventions, the Fed's FIMA repo line sat at zero from February to October, and foreign governments raised dollars by selling $254B of Treasuries and spending their own cash. The dollar stayed the crisis currency (yuan swap draws were about $16.4B in a quarter, mostly trade top-ups), but the backstop for non-core states is shifting from Fed technical lines to Treasury political ones. Captive demand is real, but in 2026 the captives were the Fed and money funds, not tokens.

The limits on a UHI turn out to be rent and the tax base, not robots. A UHI dollar goes 40% to 48% on housing, whose supply grows about 1% a year, so even unlimited robot goods can meet at most 57% to 76% of the extra demand. Recalibrated, a printed Musk-scale UHI runs about 5.8% to 9.8% inflation now and 1.4% to 5.8% in 2028 to 2035, not Round 2's 0.9%, which needs housing supply to open first. Meanwhile income and payroll taxes carry 86.2% of federal receipts and corporate tax fell to 7.1% as AI capex is expensed. A tax on watts or compute would raise under 1%. The live AI-dividend plans hand out equity into locked accounts, not printed checks.

Cheap production does not reach people on its own. Land is 68% of home value in San Jose and 40% nationally, so a 20% cut in building cost lowers prices about 6% there and 16% in St. Louis unless zoning lets more homes share the land. Platforms take a rising slice of every sale: Amazon ads went from 23.5% to 41.9% of seller-services revenue, and Meta's price per ad is up 12% to 14% a year in 2026. The bottom half own 0.58% of directly held stocks, and Trump Accounts as designed lift that to about 0.60% to 0.70% by 2045. Ownership still decides who keeps up, and the pipes built so far move it by tenths of a point.

Hardware stays the rivals' real card, used with care. China extended the truce politically to 10 January 2027 but left the legal cliff on 10 November (court odds of a full lapse: 12%), and proposed a solar know-how ban that sells panels but keeps the factories. Electric freight swaps an oil flow dependence for a slower, more concentrated battery stock dependence, and China itself is the one electrifying (38% of heavy trucks sold this year). Prediction markets agree with most desk calls and corrected one date: the next Fed hike is priced for 9 December, not 28 October.

So the scenario odds barely move. H0 stays 62/15/1/22. In 2028 to 2035 the dollar extension slips one point to 44 and the neutral reserve layer gains one to 23, because the token bid proved cyclical. In 2035 to 2050 one point moves from dollar extension to disorderly reset (31 and 31), because Treasuries rest on a wage tax base that shrinks as the labor share falls. The dollar's main risks are now slow and domestic (rent, taxes, oil-shock inflation) more than rival currencies.

### Who buys the tokens, and where crisis dollars come from (r3-plumbing)

The 2026 shock was a live test of the dollar's plumbing. The new token rails stood still, the Fed's crisis line went unused, and governments paid their own way by selling Treasuries.

#### R3-1. Round 3 question

> How much stablecoin demand is new foreign demand for dollars, and how much is money moving out of US money funds and bank deposits?

- **Proponent.** Most growth went abroad when there was growth: a model share of 68% to non-US holders, and the IMF finds North America was the net source of $54B of stablecoin outflows in 2024, 73% of that year's supply growth (R3A-05, R3A-06).
- **Skeptic.** Holder geography is not measured anywhere, the split rests on assumed shares by issuer, and for the last twelve months there was no growth to split (R3A-01, R3A-10).
- **Judge.** Both hold. The flat supply is T0 and decides the near-term answer. The foreign split is a speculative model and decides only the growth-phase answer. Lane ruling kept at 55.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 55. Horizon: H0.
- **Lane evidence.** R3A-01 to R3A-10, R3E-08, R3E-20

**Answer.**

For the last twelve months, neither, because the market stopped growing. Total stablecoins were $304.9B on 2025-10-21 and $303.8B on 2026-10-22 (DefiLlama), after a peak of $315.5B in May. Over the same year US bank deposits rose $1,225B (+6.7%) and money funds about $505B to $7.92T. Tokens took nothing net from either. They are about 1.1% of deposits plus money funds, and 4.27% of Treasury bills against a 10% tripwire.

In the growth phase (end 2023 to October 2025, +$174.9B) the best estimate is that about two thirds (model range 55% to 81%) went to holders outside the US, and about 41% (22% to 61%) was genuinely new dollar demand from people converting local currency. The rest was switching, either offshore dollar savers moving into tokens or US money leaving deposits, funds and crypto trading. Over 80% of stablecoin transactions happen outside the US (Brookings), but nobody publishes holdings by country: the Fed's September 2026 note says chain data lack geography, and Tether reports 650M users with no country split.

What it means for Treasuries: per $1 of new token, a foreign new-to-dollar buyer adds about $0.80 of Treasury demand, a switch from a US bank deposit about $0.72, and a switch from a government money fund about $0.30 (model, issuer holding 80% Treasuries). In 2026 the real captive bill buyer was the Fed, whose bill holdings rose $370B to $565.5B, together with money funds. Markets agree that tokens are not about to take over: Polymarket gives stablecoins above $500B before 2027 only 3%.

Three views. Governance: Do not budget on stablecoins as a dependable Treasury buyer. They were flat for a year while the Fed and money funds carried the bills. Business: Token growth is foreign and tied to the crypto cycle, so build for cross-border and emerging-market users, not for US savers. Ordinary person: A zero-yield token is a poor home for a US paycheck. The people who gain from dollar tokens are savers in weak-currency countries.

#### R3-9. Round 3 question

> In a crisis, do Gulf and Asian states get dollars (Fed swap lines, FIMA repo) or turn to the yuan?

- **Proponent.** All 41 weekly H.4.1 releases of 2026 show FIMA at zero from 18 February to 14 October and swap lines never above $481M, while foreign official Treasuries in custody fell $254B (R3A-11, R3A-12, R3A-14).
- **Skeptic.** A quiet Treasury (ESF) swap with a Gulf state could exist, because the ESF monthly statements were not read, and the PBoC swap figure is a single secondary source (R3A-18, R3A-20).
- **Judge.** The core finding rests on T0 Fed data and holds. The ESF gap limits only the claim that no Gulf line exists. Lane ruling kept at 70.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H0.
- **Lane evidence.** R3A-11 to R3A-21

**Answer.**

They get dollars, but mostly from themselves and from markets, not from the Fed. In all 41 weeks of 2026, through the Hormuz shock and Japan's ¥27.1T of interventions, the Fed's FIMA repo line was zero except a pre-war blip of $3.0B on 4 February (5% of its $60B cap). Swap lines peaked at $481M and only the five standing partners drew. Foreign official Treasuries in Fed custody fell $254B (-9.1%) from the February peak to $2,549.1B, and these holders kept $331.9B of their own cash at the Fed. Japan said on 3 August that it 'plans to utilize' FIMA, then did not draw it.

The Gulf asked and then borrowed. The UAE raised a swap line in April, Treasury Secretary Bessent said 'numerous' Gulf and Asian allies had asked and pointed to the Exchange Stabilization Fund (about $219B), and the court found no announced Gulf line by 22 October. GCC states instead issued a record $102.7B of bonds and sukuk in H1 2026. The one precedent for a Treasury line, Argentina's $20B ESF swap, was repaid in January 2026.

The yuan is a top-up for trade, not a crisis dollar substitute. PBoC swap draws hit a two-year high of RMB 111.6B (about $16.4B) in Q1 2026, mostly Thailand and Indonesia, and Qatar renewed its PBoC swap on 14 October. Carnegie finds Gulf energy trade stays dollar-settled because the pegs are to the dollar. The court also records that claims of Japan drawing FIMA in July, or holding about $350B in the Fed's foreign repo pool, are refuted by H.4.1 (the whole pool is $332B).

Three views. Governance: The US backstop is moving from Fed technical lines to Treasury political lines. That buys loyalty but makes access conditional, which pushes states toward gold and self-insurance. Business: In a shock, expect official holders to sell Treasuries, so long yields rise exactly when cheap funding matters most. Ordinary person: The dollar stays the crisis currency, but each shock routed through Treasury sales raises US mortgage and loan rates.

#### R3-10. Round 3 question

> If T-bill yields fall below about 1.5%, does Congress widen stablecoin reserves (longer bills, gold), or do issuers fold into banks?

- **Proponent.** GENIUS already has an administrative valve for government-issued assets, the OCC kept it on 2026-10-21, and zero-rate maturities cannot reach Circle's breakeven anyway (R3A-24 to R3A-26).
- **Skeptic.** The trigger is not priced: the 3-month bill is 4.25%, a December hike is priced at 75 to 79%, and bills under 1.5% get only 2% to 7% on Kalshi through January 2027 (R3A-22, R3A-23, R3E-03).
- **Judge.** The skeptic sets the timing, not the answer. Conditional on low rates, the bank-consolidation path is better supported than a gold door. Lane ruling kept at 65 with horizon H1 to H2.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 65. Horizon: H1 to H2.
- **Lane evidence.** R3A-22 to R3A-28, R3E-03, R3E-05

**Answer.**

The test is not close. The 3-month bill is 4.25% and the 2-year 4.81% (21 October). Kalshi gives only about 2% to 7% (thin) to a fed funds ceiling at or below 1.5% by January 2027, and both venues price another hike in December (75 to 79%). So this is a question for 2028 to 2035 at the earliest.

If rates do fall that far, widening reserves cannot fix the economics. In the zero-rate years (2010 to 2015 and 2021) the 2-year averaged 0.45% and the 5-year 1.34%, both below Circle's breakeven of about 1.5% at today's 61.5% distributor payout (model). Holding all 2-year notes would lose about $0.31B a year, all 5-year notes about $0.05B. The breakeven falls to 0.97% at a 40% payout and 0.58% at zero, so cutting what is paid to distributors is the first shock absorber. Money funds faced the same squeeze and waived about $36B of fees in 2009 to 2015.

The law already has a quiet widening route. GENIUS Sec. 4(a)(1)(A)(vii) lets the regulator approve 'any other similarly liquid Federal Government-issued asset', and the OCC kept that power on 21 October 2026 with conditions, while declining longer-dated Treasuries for the issuer backstop. Gold is not government-issued, so gold would need a new act of Congress, and none exists. The likely order is payout cuts, then fees, then OCC approval of floating-rate or short notes, then consolidation into bank-type charters (Circle's national trust bank opened on 24 July 2026). Congress and gold come last, if at all.

Three views. Governance: The low-cost lever is administrative approval of floating-rate or short notes, not a gold door. Gold re-entry is speculative before H2. Business: Distribution margins absorb a rate fall first, so exchange-style payouts get cut, then issuers merge into banks or payment networks. Ordinary person: In a low-rate world a zero-yield token costs you less to hold, but fewer, bigger and more bank-like firms will issue it.

### Can robots and taxes pay for a UHI? (r3-supply)

Robots can make goods, but most UHI money is spent on housing, and the federal budget still rests on wages. The live AI-dividend plans hand out equity, not printed cash.

#### R3-2. Round 3 question

> How much of extra demand can robot and AI output actually meet? This single number moves a printed Musk-scale UHI from 0.9% to 9.5% inflation (R2 R2B model).

- **Proponent.** Splitting demand by where a UHI is spent shows shelter takes 40% to 48% and grows about 1% a year, which caps the share met by output at 0.57 to 0.76 whatever robots do (R3B-10 to R3B-13).
- **Skeptic.** Sector responses and exposure weights are assumptions, the low-income tilt is assumed, and the 2020 to 2021 calibration uses memory inputs (R3B-14).
- **Judge.** The mechanism is sound and the measured inputs are T0. The model still has no interest channel, so US figures stay a lower bound (Round 2 ruling C4). Lane ruling kept at 60.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 60. Horizon: H0 to H2.
- **Lane evidence.** R3B-01 to R3B-14, R2 R2B model

**Answer.**

Less than Round 2's 'robot abundance' case assumed, and the reason is mostly rent, not robots. Measured supply gains are real but modest: US productivity grew 3.0% in 2024, 2.1% in 2025 and 2.2% year on year in Q2 2026, utilization-adjusted TFP rose only 0.07% in the year to Q1 2026, and mainstream estimates put AI's extra growth at 0.1 to 1.5 points a year. There is goods slack (capacity utilization 76.9%, 2.5 points below average) but little slack in housing, care or power.

A UHI check is spent about 40% (all households) to 48% (low-income tilt, assumption) on housing, so even unlimited robot goods cannot push the share of extra demand met by output (sigma) above about 0.57 to 0.76 before housing supply opens. Rerunning the unchanged R2B model with these inputs, a printed Musk-scale UHI (24% of GDP) gives sigma 0.28 to 0.51 and about 5.8% to 9.8% CPI in H0, 1.4% to 5.8% in H1 and 0.3% to 2.7% in H2. A UHI of 10% of GDP is much easier: about 1.5% to 3.9% in H0.

Round 2's 0.9% is not impossible, but it is conditional: it needs about 4 to 5 extra points of growth a year in H2, or housing supply opened plus IMF-top AI gains (then about 1.0% CPI). The 2020 to 2021 transfers, which may have added about 3 points to inflation, imply a sigma of about 0.57 to 0.68 in deep slack, so R2B's 0.3 is a fair H0 figure at full employment. Sigma is not a constant: the bigger the UHI, the smaller the share robots can cover, and in nearly every case shelter is 50% to 100% of the demand left unmet.

Three views. Governance: Size any printed UHI to the housing you can deliver, not to the robot forecast, and pair it with by-right building. Business: A UHI dollar lands on landlords and care providers first and goods makers second. Ordinary person: A printed UHI would feel generous on gadgets and food and thin on rent within two to three years.

#### R3-3. Round 3 question

> Will the federal tax base shift to capital, energy or compute as labor's share of income falls?

- **Proponent.** Treasury's own receipts show the base moving toward labor: individual plus payroll taxes 86.2% of FY2026 receipts, corporate 7.1% against 10.8% in FY2024 (R3B-15, R3B-16).
- **Skeptic.** The corporate drop is a timing effect of expensing AI capex and reverses when capex growth stops, and a future Congress could legislate a new base quickly (R3B-16, R3B-20, R3B-21).
- **Judge.** For H0 the shift is refuted by T0 data. For H1 to H2 it needs new law and stays speculative. The skeptic's reversal argument is noted but no bill has a CBO score. Lane ruling kept at 70.
- **Ruling.** Status: refuted. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H0 (H1 to H2 speculative).
- **Lane evidence.** R3B-15 to R3B-26

**Answer.**

Not now, and in 2026 it moved the other way. Federal receipts were $5,403.3B in FY2026: individual income tax 52.7%, payroll 33.5% (86.2% together) and corporate tax only 7.1%, down from 10.8% in FY2024. Corporate receipts fell to $381.4B from $452.1B (-15.6%) in a year when unit profits surged, because the 2025 law lets firms deduct investment faster (CBO, via press). Microsoft's current tax bill fell from $14.1B to $2.5B as AI capex was expensed.

Labor carries the load. About 62% to 68% of federal receipts rest on labor income (model on Treasury and IRS data, wages are 66.1% of reported income), and a dollar of labor income pays about 2.5 times the federal tax of a dollar of capital income. At today's rules each point of labor share lost costs about 0.8% of receipts: a labor share of 45% instead of 52.8% would cut receipts about 6%, roughly 1% of GDP.

The candidate new bases are small. A 1 cent per kWh tax on all US electricity would raise about $40B (0.74% of receipts), on data centers alone $1.8B to $5.8B, and a 5% excise on hyperscaler capex about $35B. The live proposals (Sanders' 50% AI equity stake, the Jacobs H.R. 10044 token or revenue excise) are minority bills with no CBO score. Taxing automation also slows supply: Korea's trimmed robot credit cut installations about 28% against Japan. The big money is in taxing capital income and equity, not watts or tokens.

Three views. Governance: A compute or power tax is a rounding error next to a UHI. The real decision is whether to tax capital income and corporate equity at labor-like rates. Business: Expect the expensing break, not a robot tax, to be the first thing revisited when deficits bite. Ordinary person: While wages carry about two thirds of the federal tax load, workers are funding the transition, not the robots.

#### R3-8. Round 3 question

> When a US UHI or AI-dividend bill appears, how is it financed, and can payments be pledged or programmed?

- **Proponent.** Every live design found is equity or tax financed, none prints money, and none makes payments assignable, tokenized or programmable (R3B-20 to R3B-22, R3B-29).
- **Skeptic.** congress.gov was blocked, so bill details rest on press coverage, and an absence finding can be overturned by one unread text (R3B-28, R3B-29).
- **Judge.** The financing finding is well supported across three independent proposals. The pledgeability finding is an absence and is held at the same confidence with a tripwire. Lane ruling kept at 70.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H0 to H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R3B-20 to R3B-29, R3E calibration row C23, R2 C9

**Answer.**

As of 2026-10-22 no federal UHI or AI-dividend bill has moved past introduction, and none found is money-financed. Sanders' American AI Sovereign Wealth Fund Act (text released 2026-06-18) takes a one-time 50% stake, paid in stock, in AI firms with more than $200M of sales, and pays out 5% of the fund a year, about $1,000 per person. The fund cannot sell, so that payout needs a $3.2T stake (per tax return) to $6.8T (per resident), meaning $6.4T to $13.6T of taxable AI equity (model). The Jacobs bill, H.R. 10044, taxes AI tokens at 2% or AI revenue at 3% but funds jobs, not checks.

Inside the administration Bessent reportedly prefers AI equity seeded into Trump Accounts and Lutnick a sovereign fund, with no decision found since June. A thin Polymarket contract gives Anthropic committing shares to Trump Accounts by 30 November 8.5%. On the court's working odds (judgment, from R3B), a cash UHI or AI dividend by 2035 is about 10%, and AI equity routed into accounts or a public fund about 25%.

No bill found makes payments assignable, tokenized or programmable, and states are moving the other way (five ban local guaranteed income). The nearest real models stay the Alaska dividend (cash, 20% exempt from creditors) and Trump Accounts (equity, locked to 18, then IRA-like). A cash UHI written into the Social Security Act would inherit its anti-assignment rule (42 USC 407), a standalone law would need its own clause. Because of the federal CBDC ban, a programmable federal UHI would have to ride private tokens such as Wyoming's state token.

Three views. Governance: Political energy is behind ownership grants (equity into accounts), which sidesteps inflation but delays income. Business: AI firms face a softer 'donate equity to Trump Accounts' ask now and a real equity-levy risk under a future Democratic trifecta. Ordinary person: The first 'AI dividend' you see is more likely a locked account balance for your child than a monthly check.

### China's controls and the battery swap (r3-hardware)

China keeps the legal cliff on rare earths while trading goods for tariffs, and holds back solar factory know-how while selling panels. Electric freight moves the chokepoint from an oil flow to a battery stock.

#### R3-4. Round 3 question

> Does China let the 2026-11-10 export-control truce lapse, finalize the solar know-how ban, and pair it with yuan-financed supply to the Global South?

- **Proponent.** MOFCOM confirmed a political extension to 10 Jan 2027 on 28 Sept, magnet exports hit a 2026 high in September, and the solar proposal has primary text (R3C-01 to R3C-09).
- **Skeptic.** No numbered notice moves Announcement 70's legal date, Bessent says Chinese deliverables 'have not been perfect', and the three-part claim compounds uncertainty (R3C-03, R3C-05).
- **Judge.** Each part is well evidenced but the answer is a conjunction: extension (about 88% for something short of a full lapse), finalization by mid-2027 (about 70%) and a thin yuan layer. Lowered from 70 to 65 for compounding.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 65. Horizon: H0 to H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R3C-01 to R3C-15, R3E calibration row C15

**Answer.**

Most likely no lapse, yes to the solar ban, and only a thin yuan layer. The political truce is already extended: Bessent announced on 23 September that the arrangement runs to 10 January 2027, and MOFCOM confirmed it on 28 September with a reciprocal tariff cut on about $30B of goods each way. But no numbered MOFCOM notice has moved the legal end date (10 November) of Announcement 70, which pauses five more rare earths, battery, cathode and graphite-anode items and the 0.1% extraterritorial rule. In 2025 China issued the pause only three days before the deadline, so silence now says little. Court odds for 10 November: numbered extension 72%, partial replacement 16%, full legal lapse 12%. For the 27 November gallium, germanium and antimony pause: 80% extended.

The solar move is real and precise. MOFCOM Announcement 2026 No. 48 (16 October) proposes controls on 40 technologies (23 AI, 5 biomedicine, 12 solar), 14 banned and 26 restricted, including wafers of 70 µm or less, fluidized-bed polysilicon, and perovskite and tandem modules. Finished products are explicitly excluded, so China keeps selling panels while withholding the factories. Comments close 15 November. Court odds of finalization in some form: about 25% by 31 December 2026 and about 70% by 30 June 2027.

The yuan pairing is the weakest leg. 94% of Chinese energy loans in 2010 to 2021 were in dollars, Chinese-financed renewables abroad were only about 3 GW in 2022 to 2023, and the Global South solar boom (Pakistan avoided over $12B of oil and gas imports) is bought for cash. The yuan grows by refinancing old debt instead: Kenya's yuan share of external debt rose from 5% to 11.7%. The court rescores the lever table: solar technology controls are now the top threat (3.00), rare-earth controls fall to 2.56 and the system play to 2.40.

Three views. Governance: The truce is a rolling two-month option that both sides keep short on purpose. Watch the numbered legal text, not the press statement. Business: Plan to two dates (10 November legal, 10 January political). Expect panels and cells to stay cheap and equipment and line know-how to stop flowing. Ordinary person: No change to the money you are paid in. If anything moves, it is the price of EVs, home batteries and solar installs made outside China.

#### R3-12. Round 3 question

> Does electric freight just swap oil dependence for battery dependence?

- **Proponent.** Battery mid-stream stages are 80% to 94% Chinese, and the IEA's N-1 test shows the rest of the world cannot meet its anode or LFP cathode needs without China (R3C-16 to R3C-18).
- **Skeptic.** A battery is a stock, not a flow: a cutoff stops fleet growth and pack replacement, not trucks already running, so the dependence is slower and can be stockpiled (R3C-29).
- **Judge.** Both are right and together give the answer: a more concentrated but slower-acting dependence. Case C is split by geography: confirmed in China in H0, speculative in the US. Lane ruling kept at 70.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H0 to H2.
- **Lane evidence.** R3C-16 to R3C-29, R2 C6

**Answer.**

Partly yes, but it swaps one kind of dependence for a different and less acute kind. By share, batteries are far more concentrated than oil. In 2025 China made over 80% of battery cells, about 85% of cathode material and more than 90% of anode material, and it refines about 94% of graphite and 71% of lithium. Oil's top producer (the US) has 22% and the top ten 73%, while Hormuz carries about 20% of world consumption. On a concentration index (HHI, model) battery mid-stream stages score about 5,250 to 9,400, oil about 830 to 906 by country, or about 2,000 if OPEC+ acts as one bloc.

But oil is a flow and a battery is a stock. Cut off oil and trucks stop within weeks. Cut off batteries and the existing fleet keeps running, and only fleet growth and the 8% to 12% of packs replaced each year are exposed (model). The real chokepoint is mid-stream: without China the rest of the world cannot meet its own needs for anode material or LFP cathode, while nickel-based cells, about 80% of EV batteries outside China, can be made elsewhere. A stockpile of packs or anode material works like an oil reserve for this kind of dependence.

The country electrifying freight is China itself: new-energy heavy trucks were 38.1% of sales in January to September 2026 and 50.3% in September, mostly on CATL batteries, which hedges Beijing's own Hormuz exposure. The US sold about 17,000 electric trucks in 2025, over 95% medium-duty, after the ACT rule was revoked. For a net oil exporter the swap trades a price exposure for a supply exposure. The court corrects a Round 2 tripwire: 'US cell capacity above 20 GW by 2028' was a solar-cell line. US battery cell nameplate is already about 240 to 280 GWh, so the right tests are anode, graphite and LFP cathode capacity outside China.

Three views. Governance: Fund mid-stream materials (anode, graphite, LFP cathode, precursors), not cells, and treat a pack or anode stockpile as the battery-age oil reserve. Business: An electric fleet's exposure is front-loaded into capex and replacement packs. Lock multi-year pack supply or use nickel chemistries that can be sourced outside China. Ordinary person: Electric freight makes goods prices less sensitive to oil shocks like 2026's, but a China squeeze would show up slowly as dearer vans, buses and home batteries.

### Homes, human labels, ad tolls and child accounts (r3-ownership)

Cheap production does not reach buyers on its own. Land takes the housing savings unless zoning lets more homes share it, platforms take a rising cut of each sale, and child accounts move ownership by tenths of a point.

#### R3-5. Round 3 question

> Can housing reopen through supply (factory and robot-built homes, zoning reform), or do land prices absorb the savings?

- **Proponent.** Broad upzoning has the best evidence: Auckland doubled construction and left three-bedroom rents 22% to 35% below a synthetic control, and Austin's supply wave cut median rent about 16% (R3D-12, R3D-13).
- **Skeptic.** Land is 59% to 68% of value in the costliest metros, factory builders have a long failure record, and cities stall broad reforms (R3D-04, R3D-08, R3D-09, R3D-17).
- **Judge.** Both happen and the metro decides which wins. Robots cut the cost of the box, zoning cuts the land per box, and only both together reopen the coastal rung. Lane ruling kept at 65.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 65. Horizon: H0 (Sun Belt) to H1 (coastal).
- **Lane evidence.** R3D-04 to R3D-18, R3B-13

**Answer.**

Both happen, and the metro decides which wins. Factory and robot building attacks the structure, but in the costliest metros the structure is the smaller part of a home's value. FHFA's appraisal-based estimates put land at 68.4% of single-family value in San Jose, 67.7% in Los Angeles, 59.3% in San Francisco and 39.9% nationally (2022), and the share rose in big metros from 2012 to 2022 (Los Angeles 58.9% to 67.7%). So a 20% cut in structure cost lowers prices about 6% in San Jose but about 14% to 16% in Houston or St. Louis (model), and only about 3% to 5% on the coasts if scarce land captures half the saving.

The factory route has a bad record and a small 2026 cohort. Katerra burned through more than $2B of SoftBank money before its June 2021 bankruptcy, Veev shut in November 2023 after about 170 units, and Reframe Systems has 10 homes delivered and a factory rated at up to 500 units a year. Robots are under 0.03% of global construction spend. The policy channel did widen: the ROAD to Housing Act (law since 2026-07-11) removed the permanent-chassis rule from the federal manufactured-home code, and HUD issued an action plan on 7 October.

Zoning works when it is broad and by-right. Auckland's 2016 upzoning doubled construction, and Austin's median rent fell from $1,546 (December 2021) to $1,296 (January 2026). Narrow reforms do little: Minneapolis's triplex rule produced 47 homes in five years, California's SB 9 was rarely used, and Los Angeles is seeking to delay SB 79 toward 2030. This ties to R3-2: housing supply, not robot output, is the binding limit on what any UHI can buy.

Three views. Governance: By-right density near transit and jobs turns factory savings into lower prices instead of higher land values. The new chassis-free federal code pays off only where local zoning lets those homes land. Business: Factory builders fail on demand volatility and capital cost, not technology. Survivors will tie to steady pipelines such as manufactured-housing incumbents, build-to-rent owners and public programs. Ordinary person: Expect cheaper new homes first where land is cheap. In expensive metros, rezoning votes matter more than robot news.

#### R3-6. Round 3 question

> Is there a measurable cash price premium for human-made goods?

- **Proponent.** Peer-reviewed experiments find human labels raise ratings and purchase intent, and preregistered auctions find bids fall with stated AI involvement (R3D-19 to R3D-21).
- **Skeptic.** No transaction-based, peer-reviewed cash premium exists, an AI label lowered ratings without lowering willingness to pay in one study, and Etsy's sales fell 5.3% in 2025 (R3D-22, R3D-24, R3D-25).
- **Judge.** The premium is real as a label effect and unmeasured as a cash price. Lane ruling kept at 55 and Round 2 gap closed as partly answered.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 55. Horizon: H0 to H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R3D-19 to R3D-25, R2 R2D-29

**Answer.**

It is measurable in experiments and not yet in market prices. Peer-reviewed lab work finds that the same image labeled 'human-created' scores higher on liking, beauty, profundity and worth than when labeled 'AI-created' (Bellaiche et al. 2023, Millet et al. 2023), and that 'handmade' raises purchase intent mainly for gifts to loved ones, because buyers read it as containing love (Fuchs et al. 2015). A preregistered auction study with real money (Mandel and Imas, working paper revised 2026-10-06) finds bids fall as stated AI involvement rises, but its effect size was not seen.

Market signals are mixed. A story labeled AI-written got lower ratings but the same willingness to pay (IZA DP 17646). At CGTrader AI 3D models are about one in six uploads but 2.6% of sales. AI adopters on a large art platform produced 25% more and got about 50% more favorites per view, so human makers face more competition even if a label premium exists.

And the platform that certifies human-made takes a growing cut: Etsy's take rate rose from 16.5% (2019) to 24.2% (2025) while its gross sales fell 5.3% in 2025 to $11.92B. So the premium is real as a label effect, concentrated in gifts, art and identity goods, of unknown size in cash, and partly captured by platforms. Verifiable labels (content credentials, 'made by' disclosures) decide whether it reaches makers or fakers.

Three views. Governance: A premium needs a verifiable label, so provenance rules decide whether it goes to makers or to fakers. Business: Sell the maker, the story and the gift, not the hand labor, and expect the platform to tax the premium. Ordinary person: Human-made work can earn more where buyers care who made it (gifts, art, personal services), not in commodity goods.

#### R3-7. Round 3 question

> Do attention costs (ad prices, platform fees) rise faster than production costs fall?

- **Proponent.** T0 filings: Amazon ads rose from 23.5% to 41.9% of third-party seller services revenue (2019 to H1 2026), Etsy's take rate from 16.5% to 24.2%, and Meta's price per ad +12% to +14% a year in 2026 (R3D-25 to R3D-28).
- **Skeptic.** Over 2019 to 2025 Meta's unit ad price lagged producer prices by 19%, and company-wide averages mix regions and formats (R3D-30).
- **Judge.** Confirmed on the share version for 2019 to 2026 and on the price version since 2024. The skeptic narrows Round 2's ad-price claim (M13) but does not overturn the answer. Lane ruling kept at 70.
- **Ruling.** Status: confirmed. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H0 to H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R3D-25 to R3D-30, R2 C2, R2 M13

**Answer.**

Production costs have not fallen at all in nominal terms: the producer price index for finished consumer goods less food and energy is up 31.1% from 2019 to 2026 (January to September). Unit ad prices mostly tracked or lagged that until 2024. Meta's average price per ad was only about 2% higher in 2025 than in 2019, 19% lower after producer prices, and Google's search cost per click was about 24% higher, roughly flat after producer prices.

The clear rise is in the toll as a share of each sale. Amazon's advertising revenue grew 5.4 times from 2019 to 2025 while its third-party seller services grew 3.2 times, so ads went from 23.5% to 41.9% (H1 2026) of seller-services revenue. Etsy's take rate rose from 16.5% to 24.2%.

Since 2024 unit prices have turned up too. Meta's price per ad rose 12%, 12% and 14% year on year in the first three quarters of 2026, against about 4% for core consumer-goods producer prices, and Google's cost per click rose 7% in both 2024 and 2025. So yes, the attention toll is outrunning production costs, mainly by taking a bigger slice of each sale and since 2024 also by price. That confirms Round 2's split: launching got cheap, staying visible got dear.

Three views. Governance: Marketplace take rates and self-preferencing rules move consumer prices more than automation policy does, and none of these tolls appears as a CPI line. Business: Model customer acquisition cost rising 5% to 15% a year and own a direct audience, because a cheaper factory does not fix a growing toll. Ordinary person: Cheaply made products still arrive with a growing advertising and platform markup, so store brands and direct sellers show the savings first.

#### R3-11. Round 3 question

> Does the bottom half's share of equities move at all once child accounts are funded?

- **Proponent.** Fed DFA puts the bottom half at 0.58% of directly held equities, a floor since 2010, and the model shows Trump Accounts as designed add only about 0.02 to 0.12 points by 2045 (R3D-01, R3D-02, R3D-37).
- **Skeptic.** The model depends on assumed take-up, the share of children in bottom-half households and how DFA records minors' accounts, and a large AI equity grant would change the result (R3D-37, R3D-38).
- **Judge.** The direction is robust across every sensitivity: tenths of a point, not points, unless public money in the hundreds of billions is aimed at poorer children. Lane ruling kept at 70.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H2.
- **Lane evidence.** R3D-01 to R3D-03, R3D-31 to R3D-38, R2 R2C-18

**Answer.**

It moves, but by tenths of a point, not points. In the Fed's Distributional Financial Accounts the bottom 50% hold 0.58% of directly held corporate equities and mutual fund shares ($374.2B of $64.64T in 2026 Q2), down from a peak of 1.65% in 1996 and stuck between 0.53% and 0.66% since 2010. Including workplace pension holdings their share fell from 3.35% (1994) to 1.46%. Their share of owner-occupied real estate fell from 15.1% (2009) to 9.7%.

Child accounts work for ownership and modestly for families. In the SEED OK randomized trial (2,704 Oklahoma births in 2007), a $1,000 automatic deposit put almost every treatment child into an account (only 2% of control children of color had one), grew to about $2,300 by age 14, and slightly reduced maternal depressive symptoms, but families added little. Connecticut's baby bonds ($3,200 per Medicaid-covered birth since July 2023) pay out from 2041. Trump Account seeds still need a family claim, which is the take-up problem SEED OK solved by auto-depositing.

The court's model of Trump Accounts to 2045 (real 2026 dollars) takes the share from 0.58% to about 0.60% with the seed and Dell gift only, about 0.70% with $10B a year of family money, about 0.82% with a $100B AI equity grant and about 1.27% only with a $500B grant. Counted by family of origin the base case is only 0.62%, because children of richer families appear at 18 as new low-wealth households. A one-off grant aimed only at bottom-half children would need about $266B to lift the family-origin share to 1.00%.

Three views. Governance: Auto-claim the seed, add a progressive top-up (a baby bond layer), and route any AI equity to the bottom half, or the program mainly subsidizes families who already save. Business: Employer contributions of up to $2,500 will flow mostly to children of well-paid staff, a recruiting perk more than a wealth policy. Ordinary person: Claim the account and add even $25 a month. The public seed alone is about $2,400 real at 18, locked until then and IRA-like after.

### Where prediction markets agree and disagree with the desk (r3-markets)

Deep markets agree with most desk calls, corrected one date, and supply three odds the desk lacked. Thin markets are reported but given little weight.

#### R3-13. Round 3 question

> Market-odds calibration: where prediction markets (Kalshi, Polymarket public APIs) price the same events this desk has odds on, how far apart are we, and who is likely right?

- **Proponent.** Two venues agree within 1 point on the deepest contracts (October FOMC, $54.4M on Polymarket and about $11M on Kalshi) and markets confirm three skeptical desk rulings (R3E-01, R3E-08, R3E-11).
- **Skeptic.** Most desk-relevant contracts are thin (stablecoins, Bitcoin purchase, France ratings, Trump Accounts), and the 'who is right' calls are judgment (R3E-09, R3E-12, R3E-13).
- **Judge.** Prices are confirmed as prices, the verdicts are judgment. The calendar correction is already live on the site. The R3E working estimate for a China lapse (25) is replaced by R3C's 12. Lane ruling kept at 75.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 75. Horizon: H0.
- **Lane evidence.** R3E-01 to R3E-26, R3A-23, R3C-05

**Answer.**

Markets price about 25 events that touch the desk's views, and on the big ones they mostly agree in direction. The largest real miss was the desk's own calendar line 'FOMC 10-28, a second hike is priced'. The deepest markets give an October hike only 11% to 12% and put it on 9 December instead (75% to 79%), with year-end fed funds most likely 4.25% (67%). The desk was right about direction and wrong about the date, and the site calendar has been corrected.

Markets back the desk's skeptical rulings: stablecoins above $500B before 2027 at 3%, the CLARITY Act signed in 2026 at 5.25% (on $24.1M), Warsh out by mid-2027 at 9.5% and Cook out by year-end at about 8%. The desk looks more right than a market on a US Treasury Bitcoin purchase by end 2027 (20% in a $4.5k market, against the White House's statement that purchases need Congress, desk under 10). France is the live test: thin markets give a downgrade by 31 October 87.5%, and the court leans to about 70 because Moody's has held through earlier crises. It resolves after the close on 23 October.

The desk should borrow three numbers from deep markets: a US recession starting in 2027 at about 21 (Kalshi, the thin Polymarket contract says 35), Hormuz traffic back to normal before 2028 at about 48, and a Le Pen presidency in 2027 at about 46. They do not move the S1 to S4 odds in H0, because no contract prices the 2028 regime, but they change what S4 rests on: less Fed capture, more oil-shock inflation and euro-periphery stress. The 10-year real yield (2.76%) is already above the 2.5% real-growth line and the site now shows it as breached.

Three views. Governance: Markets see a Fed that stays independent and keeps hiking into an oil shock, so fiscal relief from easy money is not coming before 2027. Business: Plan for policy rates near 4.25% at year-end and bills above 4%, which keeps stablecoin float income high and cheap refinancing unlikely in H0. Ordinary person: Markets expect fuel to stay dear into 2027 and no rate cuts soon, so a fixed income or transfer loses real value and borrowing stays expensive.
