## 1. Answers

### Q3. What does "disorderly reset" actually mean, on a systematic level?

**Answer.** A disorderly reset is what happens when a country's stock of debt and money can no longer be carried at market prices, so the state stops letting markets decide who takes the loss and decides it by decree instead. Every overhang has to be paid by someone: holders of money (inflation), holders of debt (haircuts, caps, below-inflation rates), or taxpayers and benefit recipients (austerity). "Orderly" means the split is legislated in advance and spread over years. "Disorderly" means a market breaks first (an auction, a currency peg, a bank run, a margin call), and then the rules are rewritten fast: the central bank is forced to buy, yields are capped, exits are closed, contracts are changed, and a new framework is announced afterwards. Across ten cases from 1923 to 2022 the sequence is recognisable: trigger, market failure, forced central bank buying, financial repression, capital controls, inflation, restructuring or revaluation, new framework. Not every case runs every stage, and several stopped early (UK 2022 ended at stage 3 because the government reversed course). For the reserve-currency issuer the sequence is different in one decisive way: the US borrows in a currency it prints, so the endpoint is not a payment default but a loss of purchasing power, and part of the loss is exported to foreign holders ("our currency, your problem"). The likeliest US version is therefore a 1940s or 1970s hybrid (yield support plus regulatory captive demand plus years of inflation above coupons), not a Weimar or Argentina story. The desk keeps S4 as the dominant regime at 22% by 2028 and 30% for 2028 to 2035, with the chance that a disorderly episode happens at all at 30% (H0) and 45% (H1). Repression through plumbing has already started (stablecoin bill mandates, bank leverage relief), which is the quiet first half of stage 4.

- **Status:** Anatomy Confirmed (T0 and T1 history). US application Plausible.
- **Confidence:** 70 (anatomy 85, US path 60).
- **Horizon:** H0 for an episode, H1 for an overt repression regime.
- **Three views.**
  - Governance: the state's choice is not whether losses happen but who carries them and how visibly. The 1946 to 1980 US template (caps, captive buyers, inflation) is the politically cheapest and is already partly built.
  - Business: in every case owners of real assets, pricing power and fixed-rate debt won, while long nominal creditors and anyone trapped inside a capital control lost. Short duration, real assets and multi-jurisdiction cash management are the hedges.
  - Ordinary person: the realistic US risk is not a bank holiday or a new currency. It is several years of prices rising faster than your savings rate and your pay, with the government quietly benefiting. Fixed-rate mortgages are protection, long-dated cash savings are exposure.

### Q4. The available CBDC plan options versus what will actually be implemented, in system logic

**Answer.** "CBDC" is one of seven ways to make money digital, and the real design question is whose balance sheet holds the digital money and what assets back it. A retail CBDC is a central bank liability held directly by the public (e-CNY, digital rupee, Sand Dollar, the planned digital euro). A wholesale CBDC is central bank money on a ledger used only between banks (Pontes, live in the euro area since 2026-09-21, and the BIS Project Agorá trials). A stablecoin under GENIUS is a private liability that must hold Treasury bills, a tokenized deposit is a bank liability backed by loans, FedNow is a fast pipe between existing bank accounts, and cash is the only bearer instrument. The US has legally chosen the private versions: a statutory ban on a Fed CBDC through 2030-12-31 (law since 2026-07-11), GENIUS stablecoins from 2027-01-18, tokenized bank deposits, FedNow, and Fed "skinny" payment accounts (proposed May 2026, not final). Europe is building a public retail option with a holding cap and offline privacy (first issuance 2029 at the earliest) plus a live wholesale bridge, while China runs a public retail rail that it converted on 2026-01-01 into an interest-bearing, bank-distributed product and uses for cross-border state payments through mBridge. The fears map onto the designs unevenly: surveillance is real in every digital option including "private" stablecoins, which must be freezable by law, while programmable spending limits and expiring money exist only in targeted subsidy and voucher programmes (China, India), not on general balances anywhere in the G7. The US switch from "no CBDC" to "CBDC" is not where control is decided. Control is decided by who must freeze on request, and under GENIUS that is every licensed issuer.

- **Status:** Options and legal status Confirmed (T1). Implementation forecast Plausible.
- **Confidence:** 75.
- **Horizon:** H0 (GENIUS 2027, Pontes enhancements 2027 to 2028), H1 (digital euro 2029+, US ban sunset 2030-12-31).
- **Three views.**
  - Governance: the US keeps seigniorage and Treasury demand by licensing private issuers that must buy bills, and keeps control by making them freeze on order. Europe buys payment sovereignty with a capped public option. China keeps money and data public.
  - Business: build on licensed dollar rails (stablecoins, deposit tokens, FedNow) and expect sanctions screening and freeze obligations as a cost of access. In Europe plan for digital euro acceptance from about 2029 and Pontes for tokenized securities settlement now.
  - Ordinary person: in the US you will get a "digital dollar" as an app balance from a bank or a licensed firm, not from the Fed. It will be fast, will pay no interest if it is a stablecoin, and will be as visible to the state as your bank account. Keep some cash and some bank deposits, because they carry protections stablecoins do not (deposit insurance, legal tender).

## 3. Anatomy of a disorderly reset (Q3 in depth)

### 3.1 The system definition in one line
A reset reallocates losses on an overhang (debt plus money the economy cannot service at market real rates). It is disorderly when a market breaks first and the reallocation is done by decree, fast, with contracts rewritten after the fact.

The three places a loss can land:
1. Money holders, through inflation or devaluation.
2. Debt holders, through caps, forced holdings, haircuts, or rewritten contracts.
3. Taxpayers and benefit recipients, through austerity.
Orderly paths spread these over years by statute (US 1951 Accord, Japan's 2024 exit). Disorderly paths compress them into weeks and use emergency powers.

### 3.4 What is different for the reserve-currency issuer
1. **No forced default.** The US borrows in dollars. The binding constraint is the value of the dollar, not the ability to pay. Stage 7 becomes "inflation and repression" rather than "haircut". The desk puts explicit default or restructuring below 5% in H0 and H1 (Db-08).
2. **Part of the loss is exported.** About a third of marketable Treasuries are foreign-held (memory, verify on TIC). Every point of inflation or dollar decline is a transfer from foreign holders. Connally's 1971 line to European finance ministers ("our currency, your problem", memory) is the reserve issuer's privilege.
3. **No "failed auction" in the classic sense.** Primary dealers are expected to bid, so a US auction always clears at some yield. The failure signal is price: tails, low bid-to-cover, a jump in term premium, dealer balance sheets full, swap spreads blowing out. 2026 30-year auctions were mixed (2.39x with a 0.5bp tail on 2026-04-09, above-average 2.44x in July), with the weakest recent print in August 2025 (2.27x).
4. **Unique tools delay the sequence.** Swap lines and FIMA repo (lend dollars instead of letting holders sell), buybacks, standing repo, regulatory captive demand (GENIUS, eSLR), sanctions. Stage 3 can be dressed as "market functioning" (2020) rather than yield control.
5. **Capital controls point outward, not inward.** The US precedent is controls on outflows by US persons (1963 to 1974), but the modern levers are aimed at foreigners and exit routes: sanctions, a proposed fee on foreign holders (Miran, Nov 2024 paper, memory), freeze obligations on stablecoins, and potentially gold or crypto exit frictions. US resident outflow controls by 2035: judgment below 10.
6. **The reset changes the world, not just the country.** 1971 reset everyone's anchor. A US S4 ends in a new global arrangement (S1 private-rail dollar, S2 gold neutral layer, or both), which is why the desk scores H1 and H2 S4 at 30.
7. **Gold revaluation is small arithmetic.** US gold of about 261.5M oz at the statutory $42.2222 is about $11.0B. At $4,098.8 it is about $1.07T, about 2.7% of $40.3T debt and roughly one year of net interest. It is a one-off fiscal cushion, not a reset by itself.

### 3.7 "Great Reset" narratives decoded
There are three different things under one name.

1. **The WEF initiative (fact).** The World Economic Forum launched "The Great Reset" on 2020-06-03 with the then Prince of Wales, as a COVID recovery agenda: stakeholder capitalism, ESG metrics, and "Fourth Industrial Revolution" technology. Klaus Schwab and Thierry Malleret published "COVID-19: The Great Reset" in July 2020 (memory). Intent: to use the crisis to push its agenda. Capability: low. The WEF convenes, it does not legislate. Outcome: ESG has been in retreat since 2023 and Schwab left the WEF in April 2025 under an internal probe into whistleblower allegations he denied. Grade of the decoded intent "Davos used COVID to promote its agenda": D1, 90. Grade of "and it worked": low.
2. **The conspiracy versions.** (a) "You'll own nothing and be happy" as WEF policy: the line comes from Danish MP Ida Auken's 2016 speculative essay and a WEF video summarising it. She said it was a scenario, not her goal. Reuters and Logically fact checks found no WEF goal of that kind. D3. (b) A planned pandemic used to impose the reset: no evidence, contradicted by the record. D4. (c) A single integrated "control grid" of CBDC plus digital ID plus carbon allowances: the parts exist separately in some places (EU digital identity wallet, India Aadhaar plus e-rupee, China e-CNY), but no G7 state has integrated them, and the US banned a Fed CBDC. D3, 5.
3. **The monetary reset (serious analyst version).** A new monetary order after a debt crisis: Dalio, Pozsar, Gromen, Alden, Rickards, and in policy form Miran's 2024 paper. This is the desk's S4 plus S2. Several independent decoders, a coherent mechanism, and partial action fit (central bank gold buying, sanctions-driven reserve diversification, interest above defense). Contradicted on timing by a stronger dollar and the absence of any accord. D2, 30 by 2035. Track records matter: Pozsar's 2022 "commodity-backed" call fits the 2024 to 2026 central bank gold surge. Rickards' repeated dollar-collapse dates have not come true. Dalio's debt-cycle work is the most consistent with what happened.

The system logic in one paragraph: a retail CBDC moves money from bank balance sheets to the central bank's (deposits leave banks, the central bank buys more bonds), so it competes with bank lending and must be capped or unremunerated to protect banks (the digital euro's EUR 3,000 cap, zero interest). A stablecoin moves money from bank balance sheets into Treasury bills (the issuer must buy them), which is why the US Treasury prefers it. A tokenized deposit leaves money on bank balance sheets, so credit creation is unchanged. A wholesale CBDC changes only how banks settle with each other. FedNow changes only speed. Control and data sit with whoever keeps the ledger and whoever can be ordered to freeze it.

### 4.2 What will actually be implemented

**United States (H0 and H1).**
1. No Fed retail CBDC through 2030-12-31: banned by H.R. 6644 (law 2026-07-11), including indirect issuance through intermediaries, plus EO 14178. Probability of a Fed retail CBDC by end 2030: about 1. By 2035: 7.
2. Regulated stablecoins become the retail digital dollar from 2027-01-18, issued by banks' subsidiaries and licensed firms, backed by short bills, freezable by order, no issuer yield. Probability GENIUS regime operating on schedule: 90 (rulebook gaps at other agencies, L2).
3. Tokenized bank deposits for institutions (JPMD live) and later for retail. Probability a top-5 US bank offers a retail deposit token by end 2028: 40.
4. Fed settlement access for non-banks through "skinny" payment accounts (no interest on reserves, no discount window). Final rule by mid-2027: 55. This is the US route to a de facto synthetic CBDC.
5. Wholesale: tokenized securities settle inside DTC and NSCC, the Fed keeps Fedwire. A Fed DLT settlement asset like Pontes by 2030: 25, by 2032: 45 (DCC-L14).
6. FedNow keeps growing as the public instant rail between bank accounts. It is not a CBDC.
Net: the US digital dollar is private in issuance, public in backing (Treasury bills) and public in control (freeze, sanctions, Fed settlement). "Control without liability."

**Euro area.** Two tracks. Wholesale is already live (Pontes 2026-09-21, Appia roadmap). Retail digital euro: political deal targeted by end 2026, pilot from September 2027, first issuance 2029 at the earliest, with a holding cap (about EUR 3,000 discussed), no interest, online and offline modes, offline payments not recorded. Probability issued to the public by end 2030: 50, by end 2032: 65. MiCA stablecoins run alongside, with limits on non-euro tokens used for payments (memory).

**China.** Public retail rail already live and now redesigned to win adoption: since 2026-01-01 e-CNY wallet balances pay interest and sit on commercial bank balance sheets, moving it from M0 toward M1. Private RMB stablecoins blocked (PBoC 2025, single-source CSRC 2026 ban). Cross-border through mBridge for state and commodity flows. Probability e-CNY stays the only legal RMB digital money through 2030: 85.

**United Kingdom.** Decision on a digital pound due 2026 (or later), with officials signalling that regulated sterling stablecoins and tokenized deposits may do the job first (Breeden). BoE draft rules for systemic sterling stablecoins published 2026-06-22. Probability the UK commits to building a retail digital pound by end 2027: 25.

**India.** e-rupee pilot continues, used for programmable targeted transfers. Circulation fell in FY2025-26 while UPI dominates. Probability of a full national retail launch by 2030: 40.

## 5. Key findings
1. A disorderly reset is a change in who carries losses, executed by decree after a market breaks. The ten-case sequence is consistent, but stages are optional and several cases stopped early (UK 2022 at stage 3, Japan never reached stage 2).
2. For the US, the reserve-currency privilege turns default into inflation plus repression and exports part of the loss. The relevant precedents are 1933 to 1934, 1942 to 1951 and 1971, not Weimar or Argentina.
3. The US is in stage 0 with live stage 1 triggers and a "lite" stage 4 already legislated (GENIUS bill mandate). Stages 2 and 3 tripwires (MOVE 120 to 130, Fed long-end buying, 10y 5.5 to 6%) are not hit.
4. Gold revaluation is about $1.07T at market, about 2.7% of the debt. It is a cushion, not a reset.
5. "Great Reset" covers three things: a real WEF branding exercise (D1 as intent, weak in effect), conspiracy versions (D3 to D4), and a serious monetary-reset thesis (D2, 30 by 2035) which is the desk's S4.
6. The CBDC debate is mostly about balance sheets. A retail CBDC drains bank deposits to the central bank, a stablecoin drains them into Treasury bills, a tokenized deposit keeps them in banks.
7. The US chose "control without liability": private issuance, Treasury backing, statutory freeze powers. Privacy in the US digital dollar is set by GENIUS and BSA rules, not by the CBDC ban.
8. China's 2026 redesign (interest-bearing, bank liability) is an admission that a pure CBDC lost to Alipay and WeChat. Low adoption of the Sand Dollar, eNaira and e-rupee points the same way: retail CBDCs struggle where private rails already work.
9. Wholesale tokenized central bank money is where real implementation is happening (Pontes live, Agorá live-value trials), and the US Fed is notably absent from the newest public lists.

## 7. Mechanism chains
**Chain A (likeliest US S4 path in H0, 1970s shape).** Hormuz oil shock → inflation above target → Fed hikes (December priced 75 to 79%) → bill-heavy debt reprices fast → net interest toward 25% of receipts → term premium rises, 30y above 5.5 to 6% → MOVE above 120 to 130, dealer balance sheets full, basis trades unwind → Fed buys for "market functioning" (stage 3 dressed as stability) → if inflation persists, conflict between Fed and Treasury. Test: MOVE, 30y auction tails, Fed operation descriptions.

**Chain B (likeliest US S4 in H1, 1940s shape).** Fed leadership turns over (Powell seat early 2028) → new accord gives Treasury a say on balance sheet size → explicit or implicit cap on part of the curve → regulatory captive demand expands (stablecoins, bank liquidity rules, perhaps pension rules) → inflation runs 1 to 3 points above coupons for years → debt to GDP falls about 2 to 4 points a year → savers and foreign holders pay. Test: accord text, breakevens above 2.75%, bill share above 25%.

**Chain C (digital dollar as repression plumbing).** GENIUS effective 2027-01-18 → compliant stablecoins hold bills of 93 days or less → each dollar of supply is a forced bill bid with no yield to holders → 2028-07-18 DASP ban pushes offshore users toward compliant tokens → Treasury funds more at the short end → in a stress, the freeze and sanctions layer gives the state a stage 5 tool without a CBDC. Test: compliant share of supply, stablecoins / T-bills above 10%.

**Chain D (CBDC adoption logic).** Retail CBDC launched where private rails dominate → low uptake (Sand Dollar, eNaira, e-rupee) → central bank either mandates distribution (Bahamas), pays interest via banks (China 2026), or pivots to wholesale and government payments (Nigeria 2026) → the surviving retail CBDCs look more like bank deposits. Test: e-CNY balances after interest, digital euro holding cap.
