### DQ1. What Trump wants the monetary system to be

**Question.** What does Trump (and any secret powers behind him) actually want the monetary system to be like?

- **Status:** plausible. **Confidence:** 75. **Horizon:** H0.
- **Intent (what they want):** Foreigners pay, a weak but dominant dollar, cheap money from a Fed he controls, an owner society. D1, 75 overall (85 for cheap money, 85 for foreigners pay, 75 for weak but dominant).
- **Outcome (what will happen):** Rails yes (GENIUS, CBDC ban). Cheap money no so far. Tariffs damaged by the courts. 35 that he gets most of the four by 2029-01-20.

Trump has run the same economic worldview for almost 40 years, which makes his intent unusually easy to decode. In a paid newspaper letter of 1987-09-02 he complained that Japan kept "a weak yen against a strong dollar" and ended "Tax these wealthy nations, not America". Four wants follow from that worldview and show up in both terms. First, foreigners pay: tariffs, allies paying for protection, $1M "gold card" residency visas. Second, a dollar that is cheaper against surplus countries but still the world's only toll road: on 2026-01-27 he called a four-year dollar low "doing great", while he banned a US central bank digital currency and backed the GENIUS Act so private dollar tokens built on Treasury bills spread worldwide. Third, very low interest rates from a Fed that answers to him: after the Fed hiked on 2026-09-16 he said rates should be "near 1% or lower". Fourth, an "owner society": Trump Accounts for about 70 million children and state equity stakes such as 9.9% of Intel.

In one line, he wants a landlord's monetary system: the dollar is the rent everyone must pay, money is cheap for the landlord, and the tenants (allies) cover the costs. What he shows no intent for matters as much. There is no sign of a gold standard, a Bitcoin standard, a planned crash or a debt "reset". The federal Bitcoin reserve has made zero purchases, US gold is still booked at $42.2222 an ounce, and every self-inflicted market drop so far was followed by a retreat. The panel grades the four wants D1 (several independent decoders, his own words, matching actions) and puts 75 on this being his real intent.

The "secret powers" are mostly visible, in money, appointments and rule text. Four blocs shape the agenda. The Treasury-market establishment (Treasury Secretary Bessent, the dealer banks on Treasury's borrowing committee, the big banks) has the most real power, and its goal is steady buyers for Treasury debt without accidents (D1, 80). Crypto and venture donors (the Fairshake super PAC held about $193M in cash in January 2026) wanted legal rails and no CBDC, and largely got them, though the CLARITY market-structure bill failed 49-50 on 2026-09-15. The Trump family's crypto ventures made an estimated $2.3B pretax from November 2024 to April 2026 (Reuters), a clear motive for open token rails (D1 as motive, D2 that policy is designed for it, 60). Foreign money also bought access: Abu Dhabi's MGX put $2B of the family's USD1 stablecoin into Binance, and Trump later pardoned Binance's founder. The sequence is documented, causation is not. No document shows a single hidden hand such as Peter Thiel writing monetary policy (D3, 15).

Intent is not outcome. By the end of his term (2029-01-20) Trump gets the rails: GENIUS is law and binds from 2027-01-18, and the CBDC ban runs to 2030-12-31. Cheap money he is losing so far: his own Fed chair Warsh hiked 12-0 to 3.75% to 4.00%, the 10-year yield is 5.13%, and the Supreme Court kept Governor Cook in place on 2026-06-29. "Foreigners pay" took a legal hit: the Supreme Court struck down the emergency tariffs 6-3 on 2026-02-20, the effective tariff rate fell to about 7%, and up to about $175B of refunds are owed. The panel's outcome odds by 2029-01-20: compliant stablecoin supply above $500B 60, a dollar index below today's level 55, a Fed majority that cuts on presidential demand 25, a formal currency accord 10, a federal gold revaluation 10, federal Bitcoin buying 10. The binding limits are the bond market and the courts, not Congress.

One apparent contradiction between lanes dissolves here. The Trump lane grades "Trump wants cheap money on command" D1, while the reset lane grades "the Fed supports yields and signs an accord" only D2. Both are right: the first is a want he states openly, the second is whether institutions deliver it. The panel splits the outcome: a bond-market episode that forces Fed buying is 30 by 2028 and 45 by 2035, while a formal yield cap or Fed-Treasury accord is 25 by 2035. The next tell is the White House hearing on removing Governor Cook on 2026-11-05, with a written response due 2026-11-10.

**Three views.**

### DQ2. Does Musk mean an energy or compute standard?

**Question.** Does Elon Musk truly mean he wants an energy or compute standard?

- **Status:** plausible. **Confidence:** 70. **Horizon:** H0 to H3.
- **Intent (what they want):** A sincere far-future belief in an energy and mass unit of account (D1, 75) that doubles as a valuation story (D1, 70).
- **Outcome (what will happen):** No energy or compute money from Musk by 2035 (under 5). His money products stay dollar products.

Yes as a belief, no as a project. Musk has held one idea since at least 2023: money is "a database for resource allocation", and when robots make labor and most goods abundant, what is left to account for are physical quantities like energy, mass and compute. He has repeated it with rising frequency, from "it is impossible to fake energy" (2025-10-14) to the Walter Isaacson interview of 2026-10-20, where he said currency "will be measured in fundamental physics metrics like power, like wattage and tonnage, mass and energy". It fits his long-held science-fiction worldview and echoes his grandfather Joshua Haldeman, a member of the 1930s Technocracy movement, which wanted energy certificates in place of prices. The panel grades the belief D1 and puts 75 on it being sincere.

But what he describes is not an energy standard. A standard needs four things: an issuer, a fixed conversion rate, reserves and the right to redeem (hand in a note, receive a set amount of energy, the way the gold standard redeemed notes for gold). Musk has never described any of the four. His words fit two softer ideas: an energy unit of account (prices quoted in kilowatt-hours with no promise to redeem) and a post-money world where most goods are near free and only energy, mass to orbit and land are rationed. A "compute standard" is weaker still: compute is sold for dollars and AI chips lose most of their value in three to five years. That compute becomes a widely used collateral or pricing unit by 2035 is graded D2 at 40, and that it becomes money at 15.

What he has built in money is entirely dollar-based. X Money, opened to all US Premium subscribers on 2026-07-27, is a dollar wallet on a partner bank (Cross River) with a Visa card and up to 6% interest. X said in 2023 it "never will" launch a token. Tesla still holds the same 11,509 Bitcoin it has held since 2022 and bought none during the "energy money" talk. His universal high income plan is federal dollar checks (he wrote that the government "MUST issue dollars", April 2026). His 2025 Tesla pay plan is measured in market value up to $8.5T, with no energy milestone. Every dollar of the SpaceX and xAI build runs through the dollar system. The panel puts under 5 on Musk or his companies issuing an energy- or compute-redeemable money used outside their own customers by 2035.

The story also pays him. It frames the things he owns (solar factories, batteries, launch tonnage, AI data centers) as the future mint, which supports valuations. Eight of the nine 2025 to 2026 "energy money" statements the Musk lane logged fall within about a month of a Musk capital event (the pay vote, the SpaceX and xAI merger, the SpaceX listing, the Tesla Q3 call). He has a capital event most months, so this is suggestive, not proof (D2). The gap between words and capacity is large: he spoke of 200 gigawatts a year of US solar production while Tesla's Buffalo line makes about 0.3 gigawatts a year. Verdict: both a sincere long-run belief and a valuation story (65 for both, 15 for belief only, 20 for narrative only).

The one observable that would move this verdict toward intent to build is a transferable "compute credit" or "energy credit" balance inside X Money or the Tesla app. Watch the SpaceX lock-up expiry in December 2026 and Tesla's Q4 letter in late January 2027 for the framing in investor materials. Even then, US law pulls him back in: a kilowatt-hour-backed token cannot qualify as a GENIUS payment stablecoin, which must hold dollars and short Treasuries, and wages must be paid in cash.

**Three views.**

- Governance: Treat Musk's line as a lobbying frame for power, permits, grid access and launch, not as a monetary proposal. Legal tender is Congress's call, and GENIUS forces any X Money stablecoin into dollars and Treasuries.
- Business: The investable part is real: power, storage and compute are scarce and priced in dollars. Value Musk companies on delivered gigawatts and compute sold, not on the currency story.
- Ordinary person: Nobody will pay you in kilowatt-hours. What Musk is building for you is a dollar account with a Visa card. If abundance comes, the scarce things worth a claim on are energy, land and compute, and you get that claim by owning shares, bought in dollars.

### DQ3. What a disorderly reset means

**Question.** What does "disorderly reset" actually mean, on a systemic level?

- **Status:** plausible. **Confidence:** 70. **Horizon:** H0 to H1.
- **Intent (what they want):** No actor is decoded as wanting a disorderly break (engineered crash D3, 5). Officials do intend to use any crisis to change rules (60, history).
- **Outcome (what will happen):** Anatomy confirmed (85). US path plausible (60): an episode forcing Fed action 30 by 2028, 45 by 2035, met by inflation and repression rather than default.

A disorderly reset is what happens when a country's debt and money can no longer be carried at market prices, so the state stops letting markets decide who takes the loss and decides it by decree. Every overhang is paid by someone: holders of money (through inflation), holders of debt (through caps, haircuts or below-inflation rates) or taxpayers and benefit recipients (through austerity). "Orderly" means the split is legislated in advance and spread over years, as with the 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord. "Disorderly" means a market breaks first (an auction, a currency peg, a bank run, a margin call) and the rules are rewritten fast afterwards.

Across ten cases from Weimar in 1923 to the UK in 2022 the sequence is recognizable: overhang, trigger, market failure, forced central bank buying or a yield cap, financial repression (rules that push savers into government debt at below-inflation yields), capital controls, inflation, restructuring or revaluation, and a new framework. Stages are optional. The UK in 2022 stopped at stage 3 when the government reversed its budget, and Japan ran yield control from 2016 to 2024 without a market failure. In 1933 to 1934 the US banned private gold holding, voided gold clauses in contracts and revalued gold from $20.67 to $35. From 1942 to 1951 the Fed pegged long bonds at 2.5%. Greece in 2012 imposed a 53.5% haircut on bondholders because it could not print euros.

For the United States the endpoint differs in one decisive way. It borrows in a currency it prints, so the end is not a payment default but a loss of purchasing power, and part of the loss falls on foreigners, who hold $9.33T of Treasuries. The likely US version is a 1940s or 1970s hybrid: yield support plus captive buyers plus years of inflation above the interest rate on the debt. Today the US sits in stage 0 (net interest is 19.46% of federal receipts against a 25% line, and the 10-year real yield of 2.76% is above real growth of 2.5%), with stage 1 triggers active (the Strait of Hormuz closed in practice, another Fed hike priced at 75 to 79% for December). Stages 2 and 3 are not hit: the bond volatility index MOVE is 95.9 against a stress line of 120, and the Fed is not buying long bonds. A quiet, legal first layer of stage 4 is already built: stablecoins must hold short bills from 2027-01-18.

Intent and outcome must be kept apart here too. The serious analysts' version of a "Great Reset" (a new monetary order after a debt crisis, argued by Dalio, Pozsar, Gromen and Rickards) is graded D2 at 30 by 2035. The idea that someone is deliberately engineering a crash to usher in a new system is D3 at 5: the US banned a Fed CBDC, the Fed is hiking against the President's wishes, and no actor gains from an uncontrolled break. What history does show (60) is that officials use any crisis to change rules, as in 1933 and 1971. The World Economic Forum's 2020 "Great Reset" was real branding with no legislative power, and "you'll own nothing" comes from a 2016 scenario essay by a Danish MP, not from WEF policy (D3).

The desk's odds stand: a disorderly regime is dominant at 22% to 2028 and 30% for 2028 to 2035. A bond-market episode that forces Fed balance-sheet action is 30 by 2028 and 45 by 2035. An explicit Fed yield cap or accord is 25, inflation above 5% for two straight years 25, a new formal international framework 15, capital controls on US residents 7, and an explicit Treasury restructuring 4. The tells: MOVE above 120, the 10-year yield above 5.5% (5.13% now), Fed long-end purchases, and 10-year breakeven inflation above 2.75% (2.37% now).

**Three views.**

- Governance: The choice is not whether losses happen but who carries them and how visibly. The 1946 to 1980 template (caps, captive buyers, inflation) is the politically cheapest and is already partly built.
- Business: In every case owners of real assets, pricing power and fixed-rate debt won, while long-dated nominal creditors and anyone trapped inside capital controls lost. Short duration, real assets and cash held in more than one jurisdiction are the hedges.
- Ordinary person: The realistic US risk is not a bank holiday or a new currency. It is several years of prices rising faster than your savings rate and your pay. A fixed-rate mortgage is protection, long-dated cash savings are exposure.

### DQ4. CBDC options versus what gets built

**Question.** What is the difference between the available plan options and what CBDC will actually be implemented, in system logic?

- **Status:** plausible. **Confidence:** 75. **Horizon:** H0 to H1.
- **Intent (what they want):** The US anti-CBDC stance is decoded as keeping digital dollar demand pointed at Treasuries, not mainly privacy (D1, 75). The digital euro is a sovereignty move (D1, 85).
- **Outcome (what will happen):** US: private, bill-backed, freezable stablecoins and deposit tokens, Fed retail CBDC about 1 by 2030. EU: digital euro 50 by end 2030. China: e-CNY stays the only legal RMB digital money (85).

"CBDC" is one of about seven ways to make money digital, and the real design question is simple: whose balance sheet holds the digital money, and what assets stand behind it. A retail CBDC is a direct claim on the central bank held by the public (China's e-CNY, the planned digital euro). A wholesale CBDC is central bank money on a ledger used only between banks (the euro area's Pontes, live since 2026-09-21). A regulated stablecoin is a private company's liability that must hold Treasury bills. A tokenized deposit is a bank deposit on a blockchain, backed by the bank's loans. FedNow is a fast pipe between existing bank accounts, and cash is the only bearer money with no ledger at all.

In system logic each option moves money onto a different balance sheet. A retail CBDC pulls deposits out of banks and onto the central bank, which competes with bank lending, so it gets capped and pays no interest (the digital euro's holding cap of about EUR 3,000 is under negotiation). A stablecoin pulls money into Treasury bills, because the issuer must buy them, which is why the US Treasury prefers it. A tokenized deposit leaves money in the bank, so credit creation is unchanged. A wholesale CBDC changes only how banks settle with each other, and FedNow changes only speed. Control and data sit with whoever keeps the ledger and whoever can be ordered to freeze it.

What will actually be implemented differs by bloc. The US has chosen the private versions by law: a statutory ban on a Fed CBDC, direct or indirect, through 2030-12-31 (law since 2026-07-11), GENIUS stablecoins from 2027-01-18 (90 that the regime runs on schedule), bank deposit tokens (40 that a top-five bank offers one to retail by end 2028) and Fed "skinny" payment accounts for non-banks (55 for a final rule by mid-2027). The panel puts about 1 on a Fed retail CBDC by 2030 and 7 by 2035. Europe is building a public retail option, pilot from September 2027 and first issuance 2029 at the earliest (50 by end 2030), on top of the live wholesale bridge. China runs a public retail rail, turned it on 2026-01-01 into an interest-bearing balance on bank books, and uses mBridge for cross-border state payments ($55.5B cumulative to November 2025, about 95% in e-CNY).

The fears map onto the real designs unevenly. Surveillance is real for every digital option, including "private" stablecoins, which by law must be able to freeze, seize and block on lawful orders (D1, 80 that state visibility rises). Programmable limits on what you can buy and money that expires exist only in targeted subsidy and voucher programs (China's 2020 Shenzhen lottery, India's food-subsidy pilots), not on general balances anywhere in the G7: 5 by 2035 for G7 general balances, 3 for expiring balances, 85 that targeted programmable transfers spread somewhere major. Legal cash abolition in the G7 by 2035 is 5. A link from e-CNY wallets to a behavior score is 8 in China and 1 in the G7.

So the US switch from "no CBDC" to "CBDC" is not where control is decided. Control is decided by who must freeze on request, and under GENIUS that is every licensed issuer. The US digital dollar is private in issuance, public in backing (Treasury bills) and public in control (freeze orders, sanctions, Fed settlement). The panel calls this "control without liability": the state gets the visibility and the bond buyers without carrying the deposits. "Stablecoins are a backdoor CBDC" is graded D2 at 60 as a description of function, not of label, since there is no central bank liability, no single ledger and no state spending rules. The tell is the FinCEN and OFAC rule for issuers and the size of issuer freezes after 2027-01-18.

**Three views.**

- Governance: The US keeps seigniorage and Treasury demand by licensing private issuers that must buy bills, and keeps control by making them freeze on order. Europe buys payment sovereignty with a capped public option. China keeps money and data public.
- Business: Build on licensed dollar rails (stablecoins, deposit tokens, FedNow) and price in sanctions screening and freeze duties as a cost of access. In Europe, plan for digital euro acceptance from about 2029 and Pontes for tokenized securities now.
- Ordinary person: In the US your "digital dollar" will be an app balance from a bank or a licensed firm, not from the Fed. It will be fast, pay no interest if it is a stablecoin, and be as visible to the state as your bank account. Keep some cash and some insured bank deposits.

### DQ5. Seigniorage, sanctions, and what gold can do

**Question.** Why do Set 1 seigniorage and sanctions power live there, why does it matter only at government level, does gold's capped supply (and asteroids) matter, can gold be revalued to $10k to $20k against the debt, how can gold be insurance when it is small against total debt, and how does "what money is backed by" square with the refuted 10x idea (isn't money bonds)?

- **Status:** confirmed. **Confidence:** 80. **Horizon:** H0 to H2.
- **Intent (what they want):** The US keeps new rails on Set 1 balance sheets on purpose to keep seigniorage and the freeze button (D1, 80). Revaluing gold to pay the debt is not a decoded plan (D3, 10).
- **Outcome (what will happen):** Mechanics confirmed (85). Gold tallying with the debt refuted (D4). Official gold stays an insurance asset for states, not money's backing.

Seigniorage is the profit from issuing money: issue something people hold at zero interest, buy something that pays interest, keep the spread. In the dollar system that spread sits with Set 1 balance sheets. The Fed issues cash and reserves and holds $4.57T of Treasuries, and it sent the Treasury $117B in 2015 and $107.4B in 2021. Those payments stopped in September 2022 because the Fed now pays more interest on bank reserves than its older bonds earn (its accumulated shortfall, the "deferred asset", was $233.2B on 2026-10-14). What remains is mostly cash: $2.49T of notes held at 0% saves the government about $106B a year at a 4.25% bill rate, and foreigners provide roughly $48B to $63B of that. Stablecoins copy the model privately: holders get zero by law, and the float earns issuers about $11.6B a year on about $304B (Tether alone made more than $10B in 2025).

Sanctions power lives in Set 1 for a mechanical reason. Every dollar payment ends as a transfer on a ledger the US controls: Fedwire ($4.59T a day in 2025), CHIPS ($2.01T a day) and US correspondent banks. Foreign central banks keep $2.55T of Treasuries as book entries at the New York Fed. So one Treasury directive on 2022-02-28 "effectively immobilizes" Russia's central bank assets held by US persons anywhere, and Europe still holds about EUR 202B immobilized at Euroclear. That is why it matters "only at the government level": the power comes from law over the ledger keepers, not from technology, and a private issuer can freeze only by borrowing state power. These two powers explain why the state licenses new rails rather than letting them compete. They also reach you, as zero interest on your cash and stablecoins and as balances that a lawful order can freeze.

On supply, "about 20% more to mine" is right as a snapshot and wrong as a ceiling. About 222,600 tonnes of gold are above ground and about 61,000 tonnes are proven reserves, which is 21.5% of all gold ever found economic to mine. But reserves rise when prices rise, another 114,400 tonnes of known resources exist, and record 2025 mine output of 3,672 tonnes grows the stock about 1.6% a year (about 39% larger by 2050). The new rails can expand real wealth faster than that, and gold does not need to keep up: it only has to stay scarce relative to money and output, and it already went from 3.7% to 23% of world GDP between 1971 and 2026, mostly through price. Asteroids change nothing before 2050 (under 2): metal asteroids hold about 1 gram of gold per tonne, the grade of an ordinary Earth mine, so 1,000 tonnes of gold would mean processing about a billion tonnes of asteroid metal, and no company has returned any metal yet.

Gold at $10,000 to $20,000 is possible as a market price but not as a debt formula: gold fell from a $5,586 high to $4,078 in 2026 while the debt rose, and the debt-to-gold ratio has wandered between 12x and 53x. Officially the US carries 261.5 million ounces at $42.2222. Marking them to market books a one-off gain of about $1.06T, about half of one year's deficit and 3.2% of the $32.57T debt held by the public. At $10,000 the gain covers 8.0%, at $20,000 16.0%, and covering all public debt would need about $124,600 an ounce. The gain would also arrive as new base money, which saves almost no interest while reserves pay near the bill rate. Panel odds: formal revaluation 10 by 2029-01-20 and 15 by 2035, an official price of $10,000 or more 3, a market price at or above $10,000 by end 2030 20. Gold still works as insurance although it is small (about $29T against global debt of about $365T, the $500T figure could not be sourced), because insurance pays out in one narrow disaster, frozen reserves or a failed currency. Against that job it is large: official gold is about $5T, 27% of official reserves at end-2025 prices against 22% for Treasuries.

Finally, "what money is backed by". The desk never said gold backs money, and it does not. The Round 2 line named the backing as Treasuries, bank deposits and bank credit, and put gold "underneath for nations", meaning the last layer of trust between states. That word was ambiguous and is corrected here: gold sits beside the dollar system, not under it. Your instinct about bonds is half right. Money is not bonds: a Treasury cannot settle a payment, its price moves, and it is a promise to pay dollars later. But bonds are what most money is backed by. Base money ($5.51T) is a Fed liability whose main asset is $4.57T of Treasuries, banks hold Treasuries against $19.6T of deposits, and stablecoins must hold bills. A primary auction does not create money by itself: buyers pay with existing deposits, which return when the government spends. New money appears when a bank or the Fed buys, or when banks lend. That is also why the 10x idea fails: gold is not the base that credit is stacked on.

**Three views.**
