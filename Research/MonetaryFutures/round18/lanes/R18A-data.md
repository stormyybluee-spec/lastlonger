## Evidence for and against H1, H2 and H3

### H1 (money obeys supply and demand, long run)

For:
- Across 144 countries in 2000 to 2019 the average money growth and inflation correlation is 0.88 (row 52), and McCandless and Weber found a high correlation over 30 years in 110 countries (row 01).
- The 2020 to 2022 US episode followed M2 growth of 26.8% with CPI of 8.98% about 16 months later (row 07), and every printer case in H3 shows large money growth with high inflation.

Against, or limiting:
- Among low-inflation countries the cross-country correlation falls to about 0.5 (row 52), Dwyer and Fisher report 0.47 and De Grauwe and Polan near zero (row 02), and US year-to-year correlation is 0.14 and negative after 1985 (row 53).
- Japan 2013 to 2015 doubled its monetary base with CPI ex tax near 1.25% (rows 42, 43), the Fed balance sheet grew about 5 times with CPI under 3.1% (rows 04, 05), and the ECB's rose 7.4 times by 2022 with inflation under 1.5% most years to 2019 (row 46). Only a weaker form of H1, money relative to what people want to hold, survives these.

### H2 (velocity)

For:
- US M2 velocity moved from 2.192 to 1.126 (down 48.6%) and the 2020 money surge did not bring inflation at once (rows 03, 56). It fell from 1.94 to 1.74 after 2008, which fits the post-2008 puzzle (row 03).
- In hyperinflation, turnover rises: German prices grew 5.4 times faster than notes (row 13), Zimbabwe M3 velocity rose 71% (row 54), and Venezuelan real base fell below one tenth (row 55).

Against, or limiting:
- Velocity is not an independent driver, it is partly an outcome of expected inflation and interest on reserves (rows 51, 17). Cagan's own text was not opened and Benati disputes his form (row 57).
- US velocity has also risen back to 1.419 without a matching new inflation burst in the data here, and the lane has no test that predicts velocity in advance.

### H3 (biggest printers print to pay the state's bills)

For:
- Weimar: about 100% of spending paid by note issue in Oct 1923, and inflation stopped when central bank credit to the state stopped (rows 14, 15). Zimbabwe: quasi-fiscal activities of 36% of GDP and IMF text on deficits financed by the central bank (rows 19, 20). Venezuela: base growth over 73,000% driven by spending needs, deficit about 15% of GDP (row 26). Argentina: central bank transfers 0.9% of GDP in 2023, over 60% of GDP in two decades, and no direct financing in 2024 while inflation fell by 93.6 points (rows 31 to 33).

Against, or limiting:
- Rival causes sit in each case: reparations and the Ruhr (Weimar), war destruction and a halving of national income (Hungary 1946), land reform and a 40% earlier output fall (Zimbabwe), the 71% oil price fall and sanctions (Venezuela). Output fell before or alongside the printing, so the order of cause is not settled (rows 15, 21, 23, 28).
- Turkey 2021 to 2023 is inflation through rate cuts and a falling lira with GDP growth positive, and no money financing of the budget was found, so it is not a clean printer case (rows 35 to 39).
- The phrase 'the biggest printers end up poor' was not tested as a ranking of income. Several cases rebounded after fixing finance (Zimbabwe 2009 growth 13.3%, Germany 1924), which suggests the damage is large but not permanent.
