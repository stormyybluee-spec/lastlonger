# Round 18 frame (2026-11-25): Level 3 deep chapter on the printers

Chapter: working id rb-printers, the fifth of the seven deep chapters (round13/SERIES_L3.md). Working title: "Why do the biggest printers end up poor? Speed, trust and the price of money".
It deepens question 8 of rb-credit and tests the owner's point 5 (round13/OWNER_R13.md) on velocity of money, supply and demand of money, imported inflation and deflation, and why a reserve-holding body issues and inflates money.

## Hypotheses to test (with the question-mark rule)
- H1. Money obeys supply and demand: print more than people want to hold and its value falls. Test: the quantity of money against prices over long periods and across countries (the long-run link, and where it breaks, for example Japan after 1990 and the US after 2008).
- H2. Velocity matters: how fast money changes hands decides how much a given stock of money pushes prices. Test: US velocity of M2 since 1960, the collapse after 2008, and velocity in hyperinflations (people spend money as fast as they get it).
- H3. The biggest printers are poor because they print to pay the state's bills, not to fund output. Test: the cases (Zimbabwe, Venezuela, Argentina, Weimar Germany, Hungary 1946, Turkey) with the money growth, the inflation, and what the new money paid for, and the counter-case of central banks that created huge sums without high inflation (Japan, the US and Europe after 2008).
- H4. Imported inflation and deflation: a small open economy imports price changes through its exchange rate and through what it buys abroad. Test: Malaysia's experience (the ringgit's fall in 1997 to 1998 and prices, food and fuel import prices, the 2022 rise), and how pegs pass on another country's inflation or deflation.
- H5. Why a reserve-holding body inflates on purpose. Test: covered in chapter 05. Here, only what the record of the printers adds: the line between mild inflation by design and inflation that runs away.
