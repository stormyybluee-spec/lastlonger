Main path length: by the Court's full count (kicker, tldr, bridge, headings, tables, callouts, everything outside go-deeper boxes) the draft has 3,036 words and the edited chapter about 4,020, a net of +987 (1,510 removed, 2,285 added in edits, 212 in new paragraphs). The extra room went where the red team's section 4 asked: Test 3 (the 2020 bond buying, the end of the German inflation, Germany's output), Test 4 (the 1998 peg, the chicken from farm to plate) and Test 2 (reserves against spendable money). Detail that would have pushed past the limit went into two new go-deeper boxes, with a short version of every tested fact left on the main path.

What changes most:
- The wall comes down. The draft said bond buying in a working economy is one thing and printing to pay the state's bills another. The Court accepts the red team's main point. In 2020 America's central bank bought government bonds while the government ran a record deficit, and prices rose fast in 2021 and 2022. A new paragraph (N2) says the line is a matter of degree, of trust and of whether output can keep up, and that the reader's worry about modern central banks is not foolish. Block 18 also says economists still argue how much of 2021 to 2022 came from money and how much from shocks. This cuts for the reader on the danger side and against the chapter's tidy answer.
- Velocity is described, not crowned. The chapter now says velocity is a year's spending divided by the money stock, so a fall restates that money grew faster than spending. A new paragraph (N1) separates bank reserves, which people cannot spend, from deposits, which people chose to save. Zimbabwe's velocity "rose about 70%". "Holds, strongly" is gone.
- Test 3 is fairer to history. "In every case the economy was already shrinking" is gone: Germany grew until mid 1922 by one study, and Hungary was rebuilding. Germany's inflation ends in October and November 1923 by Sargent, with a budget change people believed, and the new currency in place by 1924. Venezuela's own price caps join the outside blows. Zimbabwe's 36% becomes 29% to 36% of central bank spending of many kinds, paid for partly by printing. Zimbabwe's 13.3% growth in 2009 shows the damage was deep but not permanent. Turkey's savers' scheme and Japan from 1932 go in a box.
- Test 4 adds what was missing. The 1998 peg at 3.80 and the controls on money leaving are on the main path, with the 11% of state-set prices and the IMF's April 1998 advice to scale them back. In 1998 inflation rose about as much as Bank Negara's own rule predicted, so the "so little gets through" story is limited to 2022 and most years. The comparison with "the West" in 1998 is cut (America 1.5%, Britain 1.6%, Malaysia 5.3%), and the table shows all five. The two governors are separate speakers, with no pronoun across them. The chicken story follows the cost back to imported feed, quotes one poultryman as one person, and names the government's help to farmers.
- Scene facts are fixed and the scenes stay. Zweig is at Westerland in June 1922 and the newspaper passage follows. Rojas sits outside a subway station in early 2018. The 144-country check is called the chapter's own calculation from World Bank data on the main path. The closing no longer sets one shopper's choice against a collapse in a way that dismisses her.

How each finding in the verdict (block 52) now reads:
1. Money obeys supply and demand: holds over long periods and in high inflation. Loose in calm times, by how much is disputed.
2. The speed of money matters: holds as a description. Speed is partly a result of trust.
3. A country can import inflation and deflation: holds in part. In most years less gets through than the fall suggests, and the state decides who pays.
The question: in the four cases here, printing to pay the state's bills deepened another heavy blow. The line with buying bonds is one of degree and trust. Whether the biggest printers end up poorest was not tested (said in block 38).
Rebuilt claim (block 54): the "trust can change overnight" line is cut, the second line now says the two kinds of money creation can blur, and the third adds the farm to who pays.
