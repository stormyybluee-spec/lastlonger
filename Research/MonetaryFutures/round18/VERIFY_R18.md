# VERIFY R18: chapter rb-printers

## 1. Headline result

Checked 104 claims against the two lanes: 59 match, 24 overstated, 11 mismatch, 10 unsupported. Re-fetched 20 sources and re-ran the numbers with python3 -I.
The core numbers (Fed, US CPI, M2, velocity, Hungary, Zimbabwe, Venezuela, Bank Negara, Meena Tharshini quote) match. The 144, 88 and America correlations are the lane's own calculation and I reproduced them closely (0.875, 0.50, 0.136, 0.650). Blocks 13 and 15 call it "one study", which hides that.
Most serious: block 37 dates the end of German inflation to 1924 (Sargent says late 1923), block 17 says euro area prices stayed under 1.5% from 2013 to 2019 (2018 was 1.7%), block 49 treats the 2022 governor as a "he" and as the 2017 governor (she is Nor Shamsiah), block 50 says Malaysia beat "the West" in 1998 (America 1.5%, Britain 1.6%), and block 34 puts Rojas at a "bus stop" (the page says subway station).
Also overstated: Zimbabwe velocity "nearly doubled" (it rose 71%), the 36% of output is central bank quasi-fiscal activity and not all printing, doubling "3.5 days" (source 3.70), and the Hong Kong "passes on the dollar's strength" line against the HKMA's own words.
Causal claims in blocks 18, 24, 32, 38 and 47 go further than the lanes, and the pass-through and Argentina numbers are shown from one side only (section 5).

## 5. One-sided numbers and causal overreach

Causal claims the data cannot carry
- Block 18 "So the money did matter in the end": two series that rose in sequence, with supply shocks as a rival cause (lane A-47 rates the money link plausible only).
- Block 24 and 28 "That is why ... did so little": the velocity story is circular for 2020. Velocity here is nominal GDP divided by M2, so when M2 jumps 26.8% in a year, velocity falls by arithmetic. Saying velocity fell and so prices stayed calm adds little for 2020. For 2008 to 2015 the fall is 1.94 to 1.74, far smaller than the "almost half".
- Blocks 32 and 38 "The biggest printers were poor because they printed": the lane says order of cause is not settled, and "biggest printers end up poor" was not tested as a ranking. The draft says in block 33 that the deficit "was already" there, but for Germany no output fall before the printing is shown (VF-50).
- Block 38 and 55 "The printing turned a disaster into a collapse of trust": trust was not measured in any lane row.
- Block 47 "The cap kept the price down, and the shortage moved the pain": the article names festive demand and the cap, and nothing about shortage cause.
- Block 48 "A tie to the dollar ... passes on the dollar's strength": HKMA's own chief says the peg did not contribute to deflation (VF-87).
- Block 50 "In 2022 the state's budget paid much of the cost": no lane row compares cost with inflation avoided.
- Block 55 closing line pairs a family in Kuala Lumpur with Germany. One is a price cap and imported costs, the other a collapse of the currency. The draft says "Both were feeling the price of money", but the Malay Mail piece does not mention the currency at all.

One side gets numbers and the other does not
- Pass-through (block 41 and 52): the draft gives only the average 0.2 points on core. BNM also says 0.4 in depreciation episodes, "increased over time", and import pass-through closer to 2.0 and 4.0 points for 2015 to 2022. The verdict "less gets through than the currency fall suggests" uses only the lower numbers. The IMF cross-country figures (1 point per 10% dollar rise, 2% for emerging markets in 2025) are in the lane but not in the draft. The lane's own advice is to show a range.
- 1998 (block 43 and 44): BNM's own rule gave 28.3 x 0.176 = about 5.0 points, and actual inflation was 5.3%. The draft's framing, "Why so little in shops?", fits 2022 but 1998 prices rose about as much as BNM's rule predicted. BNM still says firms absorbed part, so the story is mixed.
- Japan (block 17): 2015 (0.8%) is chosen, 2014 (2.8%, with a tax rise) is dropped.
- Argentina (block 57.1): 0.9% in 2023 is shown, the 2.7% indirect monetisation is not.
- Euro area (block 17): the 2018 figure of 1.7% breaks the "under 1.5%" claim.
- Hong Kong (block 48): Yam's statement that the peg did not contribute is left out.
- 2022 (block 43): the draft says the ringgit fell 5.4% but not the 12.2% peak to trough fall BNM reports, and uses a year-end figure against a 1998 yearly average.
- Governor (block 49): the lane saved a balancing quote from the same 2017 speech ("individual countries should not be starved of policy autonomy"). The draft prints only the dilemma.
- Hungary (block 31): printing share not found, yet the row is treated like the other three.
- Cross-country (block 13 to 16): 144 countries is the lane's own computation. The low-inflation group was chosen by its outcome. The US figures omit the negative -0.33 after 1985. Block 58 admits "computed for this chapter" but blocks 13 and 15 say "one study".
