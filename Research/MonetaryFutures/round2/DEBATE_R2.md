## The short version

### New rails, old anchors: money changes its plumbing, ownership decides who keeps up

Round 2 answers the owner's ten questions with one picture. The money you use is getting new plumbing (dollar stablecoins, tokens, bank deposit coins), but the assets under it stay the old ones. Every compliant stablecoin must hold Treasury bills of 93 days or less, gold stays the reserve that states buy when they fear sanctions (863t by central banks in 2025), and loans that depend on your future income still come from regulated balance sheets. Set 1 is not disappearing. It is being wrapped in Set 2. The petrodollar was never the dollar's backing, and its recycling job is quietly passing to stablecoins and to Gulf money buying US data centers.

UHI does not escape arithmetic in any country. A check is worth what it buys: real = nominal / (1 + inflation). In the court's toy model a printed UHI worth 10% of GDP keeps about 96% of its value in a US-like economy, but only about 78% in local goods and 64% in imported goods in an Argentina-like one. Iran's fixed transfer lost about 58% of its real value in five years. The US can stretch further because foreigners hold dollars, but that privilege covers a few hundred billion dollars a year, not a UHI, and bank reserves still pay interest. A digital token changes how the money is delivered, programmed and pledged, not how much it can buy. You can borrow against a UHI as income (about $69k of mortgage per $1,000 a month), not by selling the stream.

The real dividing line is ownership, not income source. Since 2019 Q4 the top 10% took 67.5% of the $78.1T rise in US household wealth, and the bottom half took 3.1%. In hours of work, food got 24% cheaper since 1990 and quality-adjusted cars 54% cheaper, while the monthly payment on a median home went from about 40 to about 68 hours since early 2020. That is the honest version of the 'In Time' fear: goods get cheaper, entry assets get dearer. For an 18-year-old the ladder has lost its middle rung (save wages, buy a house before 35) and grown a cheap, steep side ladder (launch a brand for a few hundred to a few thousand dollars, then compete for attention that platforms sell).

For the owner's three tiers. Asset owners keep compounding, buy founder brands, and are the creditors of the state (they hold 79% of household-held government securities). UHI recipients get a floor that should be indexed and non-pledgeable, and whose real value depends on the robot supply curve, not on the printing press. People still working are squeezed in the same places as UHI recipients (housing, health cover and, in 2026, energy, up 20.4% in a year), which confirms Round 1's finding that the three tiers behave like two classes: owners and income-stream dependents. What moves someone across that line is an asset grant at scale, cheaper housing supply, or a business that turns trust into equity.

The rivals' strongest card is hardware, not currency. China's money moves (Treasury sales, mBridge at about $55B in total, BRICS with no currency) have little bite. Its controls on magnets, battery cells and, since 16 October 2026, solar wafer know-how go straight at the watt-dollar, because Treasury can issue bills but not magnets. The next hard date is 10 November 2026, when China's rare-earth truce lapses or is renewed.

## 1. Contradictions between lanes, and rulings

### C1. Borrowing stays bank-based (R2A Q2) vs DeFi lends against UHI balances (R2B M8)

- **Tension.** R2A says a mortgage, car loan or first business loan stays with banks or bank-funded lenders through H1. R2B says a stablecoin UHI balance can be pledged in DeFi today.
- **Ruling.** No real conflict once 'collateral' is split. Both lanes say DeFi lends only against balances already owned, never against future income. Crypto-collateral lending was $56.2B in Q2 2026 (R1 L7) against $14.10T of bank loans (R2A-06), about 0.4%. A loan against a received UHI balance is a savings-backed loan, not income credit. R2A's reading holds: income underwriting stays on regulated balance sheets through H1.

### C2. Launch-cost optimism (R2C M10) vs attention tolls (R2D M13, M15)

- **Tension.** R2C prices a brand launch at $525 to $7,448 (model) and calls the launch rung much cheaper. R2D shows marketing at about 24% of net sales (e.l.f., Estee Lauder, T0), Amazon taking 50 to 60% of seller revenue with ads (T4) and ad prices rising (T5).
- **Ruling.** Both are right about different costs. Fixed launch costs collapsed, variable customer-acquisition costs did not and may be rising. R2C's own data (median solo revenue -23%, top decile 61x the median, R2C-14) fit R2D's toll-booth reading. R2C's $300 to $1,500 ad line is a test budget, not a growth budget. Ruling for R2D on survival economics, for R2C on entry cost. M10 lowered from 65 to 60.

### C3. 'In Time' housing split (R2E) vs broken housing rung (R2C)

- **Tension.** R2E finds the monthly payment on a median home only +6% in hours since 1990 (64 to 68 hours) but +69% since early 2020. R2C finds the payment share of a young earner's gross pay rose from 59% to 71% and years to a 10% down payment from 6.2 to 10.0.
- **Ruling.** Consistent once measures align. The payment burden is near 1990 because 1990 rates were about 10%. The gates are the down payment and the 2020 to 2026 jump. R2C looks worse because it uses the pay of 20 to 24-year-olds (which fell from 69% to 65% of the all-worker median, R2C-05), a 10% down payment and 6.86%, while R2E uses the production wage, 20% down and 7.0%. Both rest on the same memory inputs ($92,000 NAR median and a 10.13% rate for 1990), a single point of failure flagged for verification. Combined ruling: housing is the 'In Time' zone, specifically for entrants since 2020, where inputs are verified.

### C4. Money-financed UHI looks cheap in the US (R2B M6) vs explosive deficit arithmetic (R1 Dc-08)

- **Tension.** R2B's toy model gives US CPI +3.9% for a printed UHI of 10% of GDP, and +0.9% for a Musk-scale one if robots meet 80% of demand. Round 1 Dc-08 found a deficit-financed $30k UHI takes debt to about 238% of GDP in five years, and that money finance still pays IORB.
- **Ruling.** Two models of the same plan from different sides. R2B models prices with no interest channel. Dc-08 models debt at constant rates. With reserves paying 3.90% (R2B limit 3, R1 Dc-08), printing is overnight borrowing, so Dc-08 stands (confirmed as arithmetic, 90). R2B's US inflation figures are a lower bound that holds only with a strong output response or with financial repression (IORB cut toward zero). At Musk scale the two agree: 9.5% CPI at the baseline output response, and a debt path no growth rate rescues. M6 lowered from 60 to 55.

### C5. Saudi mBridge exit: R2A section 3.4 and R1 L5-23 vs R2E

- **Tension.** R2A repeats Round 1's reading that Saudi Arabia left mBridge under US pressure (unverified). R2E cites SAMA via the FT: exit on 13 May 2025 at the end of a proof of concept.
- **Ruling.** R2E wins on a dated primary statement (R2E-11, Forbes 2026-09-28 reporting the FT). Round 1's 2026 'US pressure' reading is withdrawn. The 'security still binds' conclusion survives on other evidence (Saudi Treasuries steady at $141.2B, the UAE asking for a swap line), but this datum no longer supports it.

### C6. Electrified freight localizes goods shocks (R2D Case C) vs China's battery leverage (R2E)

Case C stays speculative and moves to H1 to H2. Its first tests are EO 14421 rules (24 Dec 2026) and US cell capacity above 20 GW by 2028.

### C7. Exorbitant privilege (R2B Q5, M6) vs 'the US is not insulated' (R2E M18)

It does not protect domestic energy priced off a world oil market, where the US as a net exporter shifts income from consumers to producers. ## 3. Full answers

Each item runs the protocol in compressed form: proponent, skeptic, judge. Then the final ruling and the answer for the owner, with the three views (governance, business, ordinary person on wage or UHI).

### Does Set 1 survive the new rails? (r2-set1)

Gold, bonds and bank credit are three jobs, not one layer. The new token rails sit on top of them, by law, and the credit decision stays with regulated balance sheets.

#### Q1. Owner question

> Will Set 1 (gold, bonds, credit) disappear as an irrelevant layer? And what about gold as a scarce resource asset?

- **Proponent.** Each Set 1 job is absorbed or growing: 863t of central bank gold buying in 2025, the GENIUS 93-day bill rule, bank loans to nonbanks +19.9% (R2A-06, R2A-13, R2A-14).
- **Skeptic.** Bank credit is visibly unbundling (nonbanks 64.2% of purchase mortgages, T4) and Treasuries depend on a wage tax base that robots erode. 'Absorbed' may be a phase, not an end state.
- **Judge.** Set 1 changes owner job by job, it does not vanish. Payments move first, deposit funding second, the reserve role last and only partly. Lane ruling kept.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H0 to H2.
- **Lane evidence.** R2A-01 to R2A-07, R2A-13, R2A-14, R2A-24

**Answer.**

No. Set 1 is three jobs, and none of them is going away soon. Gold is the neutral reserve with no issuer, Treasuries are the prime collateral, and banks create most credit. The new rails are built on top of these jobs, not instead of them. Every compliant US stablecoin must hold cash, deposits or Treasury bills of 93 days or less from 2027-01-18, so each new stablecoin dollar is new demand for Set 1 bonds.

Gold is getting more important as a state asset, not less. Central banks bought 863t in 2025 and 289t in Q2 2026, buying into a price about 27% below its January high (about $4,116 now). US secondary sanctions now reach dollar, digital and gold channels, which pushes states to hold metal at home. Gold loses its scarcity only if asteroid mining becomes real, and there is no evidence for that before H3.

What does change is bank credit. Payments and some deposits move to tokens, and banks move upstream: their loans to nonbank lenders rose 19.9% in a year to $2.06T (Fed H.8, September 2026), and nonbanks already make about 64% of home purchase loans. The long-run risk to Treasuries is fiscal, not technological. They are claims on future taxes, and taxes today rest mostly on wages. If robots shrink the wage base, the state has to learn to tax robot and energy output.

Three views. Governance: The state keeps Set 1 because seigniorage and sanctions power live there. It absorbs the new rails rather than letting them compete. Business: Build on token rails, but expect Treasuries and bank credit behind them. Banks become wholesale funders of nonbank lenders. Ordinary person: Your money may look new (a token in an app) but it sits on old assets. Gold stays a hedge against the system, not a tool inside it.

#### Q2. Owner question

> Since stablecoins are basically backed by T-bills, once the robots and space equation enters, is the old petrodollar backing gone? Put another way: if someone has $50,000 of cash today, will they stop borrowing from a bank on the traditional rails?

- **Proponent.** Saudi Treasuries are 1.5% of foreign holdings, the 2024 'pact expiry' story is false, and stablecoins buy bills by law (R2A-17, R2A-18, R2A-25).
- **Skeptic.** Bank-funded fintechs could issue income-underwritten loans on chain, so 'bank rails' may blur. A wealthy enough holder could borrow against tokenized assets instead of income.
- **Judge.** The rails can change, the credit decision stays with regulated balance sheets through H1 (see contradiction C1). The skeptic's point describes who funds the lender, not a new source of credit.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 65. Horizon: H0 to H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2A-05, R2A-08, R2A-09, R2A-16 to R2A-19, R2A-23, R1 L7

**Answer.**

The petrodollar was never the dollar's backing. It had three parts: oil invoiced in dollars, Gulf surpluses recycled into Treasuries, and the 1974 security deal (a secret Treasury-purchase arrangement revealed by a 2016 FOIA request). The viral claim that a 50-year petrodollar pact expired in June 2024 is false. Invoicing erodes slowly (electric vehicles displaced about 1.7 million barrels a day in 2025, about 5 million by 2030 per the IEA). Recycling is already small: Saudi Arabia holds $141.2B of Treasuries, 1.5% of foreign holdings, and in 2026 Gulf states borrowed rather than recycled. Security still binds. Stablecoins take over the recycling job, because foreign savers who want dollars now buy T-bills through tokens.

The $50,000 holder should move some cash, but should not expect to stop borrowing from banks. For one year (model): bank savings at the 0.38% national average earns $190, a government money fund or T-bill at about 4.25% earns $2,125, a GENIUS stablecoin earns $0 from the issuer by law, and an exchange reward of 3.75% earns $1,875 (legality judged case by case from 2027-01-18). The bank keeps about $1,935 of spread over bills on that balance. That rent is what the new rails attack.

Borrowing is different. DeFi lends only against collateral you already own (bitcoin at up to 73% loan to value on Aave), and all crypto-backed lending was $56.2B in Q2 2026, about 0.4% of US bank loans. Stablecoins cannot lend at all. A mortgage, car loan or first business loan needs someone who underwrites your future income and absorbs default, so it comes from a bank or a bank-funded lender through at least H1, even if the money arrives as a token.

Three views. Governance: The US swaps oil-backed foreign demand for regulation-backed token demand, and keeps the security lever. Business: Deposits get dearer for banks, loan pricing rises a little, and nonbank lenders grow. Ordinary person: Hold idle cash where it earns the bill rate, but expect to borrow from a regulated lender who checks your income.

#### Q3. Owner question

> Or does gold remain an individual store of value, while the crypto set replaces the traditional rails?

- **Proponent.** Tokenized gold is 0.02% of the stock, GENIUS excludes gold, XAUT has 0% LTV on Aave (R2A-11, R2A-13).
- **Skeptic.** Tether already holds 146.2t, Texas opens a bullion payment rail on 2027-05-01, and households in weak-currency countries do use gold as money.
- **Judge.** The skeptic's cases are offshore or state-level niches. The US regime pushes gold out of compliant dollar tokens. Lane ruling kept.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 65. Horizon: H0 to H2.
- **Lane evidence.** R2A-11 to R2A-13, R2A-22

**Answer.**

Mostly yes, with two corrections. Crypto replaces the rails (how money moves and settles), not the balance sheet (what money is backed by). Under GENIUS and the OCC rule effective 2027-01-18 that balance sheet stays Treasuries, bank deposits and bank credit. Banks are issuing their own tokens too: JPMorgan's JPMD deposit token went live on Base in November 2025.

Gold stays a store of value, but its center of gravity is states, not individuals. Tokenized gold (XAUT $3.33B, PAXG $1.82B) is about 0.02% of the $29.5T gold stock. US tax treats every spend of gold as a sale. Compliant stablecoins may not hold gold, and on Aave's main market the largest gold token has a 0% loan to value, so it cannot back a loan.

The likely end state is a three-layer stack: token rails on top, Treasuries in the middle, gold underneath for nations. Individuals will hold gold, and increasingly bitcoin, as insurance against the board rather than as everyday money.

Three views. Governance: States hold gold at home and steer citizens toward dollar tokens. Business: Gold is a reserve and collateral product, not a payment product, until clearing houses accept tokenized gold. Ordinary person: A small gold or bitcoin slice is insurance. Your spending money will be a dollar token.

#### M1. Moderator question

> Who earns the float on T-bill-backed stablecoins, and what happens to stablecoin economics if bill yields fall toward zero?

- **Proponent.** Circle's own T0 filing gives the split (R2A-01).
- **Skeptic.** Circle's 3.5% reserve yield sits below the bill rate, and Tether's economics differ, so one issuer is a thin base for a market model.
- **Judge.** Who earns is confirmed by statute and filings. The zero-rate breakeven is a model and stays plausible. Status follows the core question.
- **Ruling.** Status: confirmed. Confidence: 75. Horizon: H0 to H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2A-01, R2A-02, R2A-12, R1 L2

**Answer.**

The issuer and its distributors earn it. The holder earns nothing by law (GENIUS Sec. 4(a)(11)). Circle's Q2 2026 results show the split: $667.7M of reserve income on $76.5B of average USDC (a 3.5% yield), of which $410.4M (61.5%) went to distribution partners, mainly Coinbase. Net income was $48M. Across the whole $305B market the float is worth about $11.7B a year at a 4.25% bill rate (model).

If bill yields fall toward zero the business breaks. At Circle's size and payout terms, the yield where it stops covering costs is about 1.5% (model). Expect what money funds did under zero rates: fee waivers, consolidation into banks and payment networks, and lobbying to widen eligible reserves. A zero-rate world favors low-cost offshore issuers like Tether (reported $1.5B Q2 profit) over compliant ones. Rates are not heading to zero yet: AI capital demand has the 10-year real yield at 2.76%.

The court adds one practical point. If a future UHI were paid as a stablecoin and left idle, the float on it would go to issuers and distributors, about $42.50 a year for each $1,000 of idle balance at a 4.25% bill rate (model).

Three views. Governance: The float is a quiet tax on holders that funds Treasury demand. Business: Distribution, not issuance, captures most of the margin. Ordinary person: Idle stablecoins cost you the bill rate, about $2,125 a year on $50,000.

#### M2. Moderator question

> If deposits migrate from banks to stablecoins (the Treasury Borrowing Advisory Committee estimated up to $6.6T of deposits at risk in 2025), what replaces bank credit creation? Does money creation shift from bank loans to Treasury bills?

- **Proponent.** TBAC's and CEA's own documents say aggregate deposits barely change (R2A-03, R2A-04).
- **Skeptic.** If issuers park cash in money funds that use the Fed's reverse repo, deposits do leave, and repricing alone can cut lending at the margin.
- **Judge.** Volume effect small, price and composition effects real, money origin tilts to bills. Plausible 60 kept. The brief's '$6.6T at risk' wording is corrected to 'ceiling'.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 60. Horizon: H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2A-03, R2A-04, R2A-06, R2A-23

**Answer.**

Mostly the deposits do not leave the banking system. They change owner and price. TBAC's $6.6T is all US transactional deposits at end 2024 ($5.7T demand plus $0.9T other), and the same slide rates the impact highest only for interest-bearing stablecoins, which GENIUS forbids. It is a ceiling, not a forecast. When an issuer buys a bill, the seller's bank gets the deposit back. The White House CEA (April 2026) finds the yield ban adds only $2.1B of lending at baseline and $531B even under stacked worst cases.

What changes is cost. Cheap retail deposits (0.38% savings) become wholesale money near 4%. If $1T moved, banks would pay about $36B a year more, roughly 0.26 points on the $14.1T loan book (model, upper bound). Banks are already shifting toward funding nonbank lenders.

And yes, part of money creation tilts toward bills. A stablecoin dollar is a narrow-bank dollar backed by Treasury debt, so the deficit, not a private loan, creates that slice of money, as in the 1863 national bank note design. TBAC projected about $2T of stablecoins by 2028. At an 80% bill share that is about 22% of today's $7.12T of bills (model).

Three views. Governance: Money creation tilts from bank loans to government bills, which gives Treasury more of the money stock and more rate sensitivity. Business: Banks become wholesalers of credit to nonbank lenders. Ordinary person: Deposit rates should rise as banks compete, and loans get slightly dearer.

#### M3. Moderator question

> Gold's regulatory and on-chain status: Basel treatment, tokenized gold size (XAUT, PAXG), Tether's gold holdings, gold as collateral in new rails.

- **Proponent.** Basel text via WGC and LBMA, CoinGecko, GENIUS statute (R2A-10 to R2A-13).
- **Skeptic.** Basel's crypto review and any GENIUS amendment could change this quickly.
- **Judge.** Confirmed as of today. The skeptic's point is a tripwire, not a dispute.
- **Ruling.** Status: confirmed. Confidence: 80. Horizon: H0.
- **Lane evidence.** R2A-10 to R2A-13, R1 L2

**Answer.**

Under Basel rules gold bullion has a 0% risk weight like cash, but it is not a high-quality liquid asset for the liquidity ratio and carries an 85% stable-funding charge. Banks find it cheap to own and costly to fund. Tokenized gold is small: XAUT plus PAXG are about $5.15B (2026-10-22).

Tether held 146.2t of gold ($18.8B at end June 2026) in USDT reserves, but GENIUS does not list gold as an eligible reserve, so compliant tokens, including Tether's own USAT, cannot hold it. As collateral, gold works at CME only through approved depositories, the World Gold Council's pooled gold pilot is still being built, and XAUT has a 0% loan to value on Aave's main Ethereum market. Texas made gold legal tender from 2026-09-01 and plans bullion-backed payments from 2027-05-01, the only state-level gold payment rail.

Three views. Governance: Gold is a reserve asset for states, kept outside the new dollar rails. Business: Tokenized gold is a niche until clearing houses take it as margin. Ordinary person: A gold token is a way to own gold, not yet a way to borrow against it.

#### M4. Moderator question

> Does a robot-and-solar economy reduce the need for an oil anchor at all (share of oil in world trade, domestic solar replacing imports), and what replaces petrodollar recycling?

- **Proponent.** Oil is a small share of trade, EV displacement is measured by the IEA, Gulf capital is going to US AI equity (R2A-15, R2A-16, R2A-20).
- **Skeptic.** The oil shares rest on memory denominators, and 2026 Hormuz shows oil still moves the whole price level.
- **Judge.** Direction is clear, magnitudes rough. Plausible 60 kept, with R2D's ocean-fuel evidence and R2E's UAE swap request added.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 60. Horizon: H1 to H2.
- **Lane evidence.** R2A-15, R2A-16, R2A-20, R2A-21, R2D-23, R2E-17

**Answer.**

It shrinks the oil anchor without removing the dollar's other supports. Crude is roughly 5% of world goods trade and all fuels about 12% (2023, rough), the US is a net energy exporter, and electric vehicles plus home solar keep cutting oil imports. Oil invoicing matters less each decade, but the dollar never rested on oil alone.

Recycling is being replaced by three flows: foreign savers buying dollar stablecoins (which by law buy T-bills), Gulf sovereign funds buying US AI infrastructure as equity (the MGX and BlackRock $40B Aligned Data Centers deal, MGX's near $50B raise), and US private borrowing against power plants and data centers (Round 1's watt-dollar). Only the first finances the Treasury directly. Ocean freight stays the stubborn dollar-oil leg longest: LNG took 73 of 137 alternative-fuel ship orders in H1 2026.

Chokepoints still choose currencies. In 2026 Iran sought Hormuz tolls in yuan or stablecoins, and at least two vessels paid in yuan. The UAE warned it could sell oil in yuan if it could not get dollars, while asking for a swap line. The real test of the dollar is how readily it is supplied in a crisis.

Three views. Governance: The US anchor moves from oil to chips, compute, security and crisis liquidity. Business: Freight, energy and compute contracts still price in dollars for now. Ordinary person: Fewer oil shocks over time, but your currency's strength rests on US tech and tax capacity, not on Gulf oil.

### UHI: collateral, countries and the math of real value (r2-uhi)

A UHI check is worth what it buys. The funding source decides the currency, output decides the price level, and the law decides whether you can borrow against it.

#### Q4. Owner question

> If UHI arrives before any energy-watt system, can people use UHI as collateral to get more loans or credit?

- **Proponent.** Statute and Supreme Court text are T1, and the abuse record spans three continents (R2B-01 to R2B-07).
- **Skeptic.** Alaska's PFD is only 20% protected and Brazil shows a big market forms once pledging is allowed, so a US UHI law could go either way. Channel (c) has no vehicle.
- **Judge.** The legal answer is confirmed under current law. Design (c) is a proposal (contradiction C9). Confirmed 80 for the question as asked.
- **Ruling.** Status: confirmed. Confidence: 80. Horizon: H0 to H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2B-01 to R2B-08, R2B-25, R2C-19

**Answer.**

Yes as income, no as a sellable stream, and only by design as ownership. Counting UHI as income is already allowed and protected: lenders may not discriminate against public-assistance income (ECOA), and Fannie Mae counts it if it will continue at least three years. On that basis $1,000 a month supports roughly a $69k mortgage at a 43% debt-to-income limit, 30 years and 6.3% (model). A UHI whose amount politicians reset each year, like Alaska's dividend, is harder to underwrite.

Pledging or selling the stream has been banned for Social Security by 42 USC 407 since 1935, and the Supreme Court calls it a broad bar (Philpott, 1973). The ban exists for a reason. US pension-advance firms charged up to 183% APR (CFPB, 2018), and one veteran got $1,500 for $18,000 of pension (Minnesota). Brazil shows both sides: a legal payroll-deducted pension loan market capped at 1.80% a month, and associations skimming R$6.3 billion from about 6 million retirees through the same deduction pipes. A $1,000 monthly stream has a present value of about $181k, but buyers have historically paid 30 to 60 cents on the dollar ($54k to $108k), and then the floor is gone.

The third path is UHI paid partly in ownership units, which can be borrowed against like a brokerage account and grow over time: $12,000 a year of fund units for 10 years at 5% real is about $151k, supporting a $75k margin loan (model). Nobody offers this at national scale. Trump Accounts, the nearest pipe, are locked until 18 and then treated like an IRA, so they cannot be pledged.

Three views. Governance: Keep the cash floor non-assignable. Allow regulated borrowing only against an ownership slice, with rate and share caps. Business: Payroll-deduction lending is the profitable product and also where fraud happens, so expect licensing. Ordinary person: Your UHI helps you qualify for a loan. Anyone offering to buy it for a lump sum is likely charging 100% or more a year.

#### Q5. Owner question

> UHI mostly applies to the US, right? Do other nations have to compete on their own and give their citizens a different UHI value?

- **Proponent.** Seven precedents with sizes and outcomes (R2B-10 to R2B-17).
- **Skeptic.** 'Compete' also means a US dollar-token UHI could crowd out weak local money, which no precedent covers.
- **Judge.** Plausible 70. The privilege is narrowed for energy after cross-examination with R2E (contradiction C7).
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2B-10 to R2B-19, R2E-23

**Answer.**

The loud UHI debate is American, but cash paid to every citizen is already a global practice, and every country pays it from its own budget, in its own currency, at its own value. Hong Kong paid HK$10,000 to about 7 million adults in 2020 (2.7% of GDP, from reserves). Macau pays MOP 10,000 a year. Alaska paid $1,000 plus $200 in 2026. Mongolia paid 21,000 tugrik a month to everyone in 2010 to 2012, and Iran paid 455,000 rials a month from December 2010.

The value each country can pay is set by tax capacity, fund or resource income, and how much new money its own people and foreigners will hold. The US can stretch further because foreigners hold dollars (56.7% of reserves) and its imports are priced in its own currency. That privilege is real but bounded: it covers the few hundred billion dollars a year of new dollars foreigners absorb, not a UHI worth 10% to 24% of GDP, and in 2026 it did not stop US energy prices rising 20.4%. A country that imports its robots and chips has to earn or own its share of robot income, which points to sovereign funds holding foreign AI and energy equity.

They do compete, mostly by rationing. Macau added a 183-day residency rule for 2025. And if a US UHI is paid in dollar stablecoins, citizens of weak-currency countries will want those tokens. That is dollarization pressure, which emerging-market central banks will answer with capital controls or their own digital currencies.

Three views. Governance: Non-reserve countries should fund transfers from taxes or fund income and own foreign capital, never print. Business: Expect very different UHI levels by market, and stablecoin limits in emerging markets. Ordinary person: A UHI number means nothing until you check what it buys, especially in imported goods.

#### Q7. Owner question

> If each country must subsidize its own citizens' UHI, how will the value be set? Show the math, including nominal vs real. Does a new digital value change this, or do the same rules apply?

- **Proponent.** The formula is arithmetic, and the model's ranges match Mongolia, Iran, Kenya, Hong Kong and Macau (R2B-11, R2B-13, R2B-22, R2B-23).
- **Skeptic.** The model has no interest channel and assumed values for foreign absorption and overshoot, so its US numbers flatter money finance.
- **Judge.** Arithmetic and precedents hold. Model numbers are a ranking, and the US figures are a lower bound (contradiction C4). Plausible 70.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2B-11, R2B-13, R2B-22 to R2B-26, R1 Dc-08

**Answer.**

The same rules apply. Nominal is the number on the check, real is what it buys: real = nominal / (1 + inflation). The sustainable real UHI per person is roughly (extra taxes + fund income + non-inflationary money creation + goods foreigners give for your money) divided by population. Anything paid above that comes back as inflation. The non-inflationary room to print is about (real growth + target inflation) x money stock to GDP: about 4% of GDP a year in the US and 1.1% in an Argentina-like economy, and only if banks are not creating that money themselves (model).

Toy model, a UHI worth 10% of GDP, printed, with 30% of the extra demand met by extra output: after two to three years it keeps about 96% of its value in local goods and 99% in imports in a US-like economy, but about 78% in local goods and 64% in imports in an Argentina-like economy where the currency falls 56%. If robots meet 80% of the extra demand, the US, Japan and the Philippines stay under about 3% CPI, but the Argentina-like case still runs about 28%. Worked example: a 1,000 monthly check against a basket of 700 local and 300 imported goods buys what 967 bought a year earlier in the US case, and what 733 bought in the Argentina case.

History matches. Iran's fixed transfer lost about 58% of its real value in five years (CPI 121 to 285), and Mongolia's lost about 19% in two. Paying from a sovereign fund protects the currency, not the local price level. The court adds one correction: the model ignores interest, and US bank reserves pay 3.90%, so in the US money finance is short-term debt finance and Round 1's debt arithmetic (Dc-08) still applies. A digital token changes distribution, programmability (expiry, merchant lists) and pledgeability, not the budget constraint.

Three views. Governance: Index the UHI to prices and cap money finance at the growth of money demand, or the check becomes Iran's. Business: In money-financed countries expect wage and price indexation and dollar pricing of imports. Ordinary person: Judge any UHI by what it buys after a year, and keep part of your savings outside the local currency.

#### M5. Moderator question

> Precedents for national cash transfers, especially in non-reserve-currency countries: Mongolia Human Development Fund (2010 to 2012), Iran 2010 cash transfers, Macau Wealth Partaking, Hong Kong 2020 HK$10,000, Singapore vouchers, Alaska PFD, and the Kenya GiveDirectly local inflation study.

- **Proponent.** World Bank CPI series, peer-reviewed Kenya study, program documents (R2B-10 to R2B-17).
- **Skeptic.** Mongolia's poverty fall came in a 17.3% growth boom, so attribution to the transfer is loose.
- **Judge.** Confirmed 80 on the pattern by financing. Singapore is a gap.
- **Ruling.** Status: confirmed. Confidence: 80. Horizon: H0.
- **Lane evidence.** R2B-10 to R2B-17

**Answer.**

The precedents sort by financing, not by generosity. Paid from reserves or resource income in a pegged or reserve currency (Hong Kong 2020 at 2.7% of GDP, Macau about 1.7% of GDP per person, Alaska about 1.4%), inflation stayed low: Hong Kong 0.3% in 2020, Macau 0.7% in 2024.

Financed by deficits or money in a floating non-reserve currency (Mongolia and Iran, each about 6% of GDP), poverty fell sharply at first (Mongolia 38.8% to 27.4%, Iran 22.5% to 10.6%), then inflation ate the fixed amount (Mongolia CPI 10.0%, 8.4% and 14.3% in 2010 to 2012, Iran's poverty effect down about 40%). Mongolia's fund spent up to 18% of government revenue against mining income about half that, and payments stopped after the 2012 election. Kenya's GiveDirectly study (donor dollars, about 17% of local GDP at peak) found a 2.5 local multiplier and only 0.1% average inflation, because there was slack and the money came from outside.

Singapore's vouchers were not covered by the lane and stay a gap. The lesson: small and funded is safe, large and printed is eroded within two to five years.

Three views. Governance: Small and funded is safe. Large and printed erodes in two to five years. Business: A funded transfer in a slack economy is a demand boost, a printed one is a currency risk. Ordinary person: The first year of a printed handout feels great. The third year depends on whether it was indexed.

#### M6. Moderator question

> Toy model: three country types (reserve issuer like the US, hard-currency non-issuer like Japan or the euro area, emerging market like the Philippines or Argentina) each pay a money-financed UHI. Nominal vs real value after domestic inflation and exchange-rate pass-through.

- **Proponent.** Inputs from World Bank series and Gopinath pass-through, calibrated against precedents (R2B-18, R2B-22).
- **Skeptic.** Foreign absorption (h) and overshoot (psi) are assumptions, and the quantity-theory core ignores rates and expectations.
- **Judge.** Plausible as a ranking, lowered to 55 (contradiction C4).
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 55. Horizon: H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2B-18, R2B-20, R2B-22, R2B-23, R1 Dc-08

**Answer.**

With a printed UHI of 10% of GDP and 30% of extra demand met by output, the model gives: US CPI +3.9% with the dollar down 2.2% and imported goods only +1.0% (pass-through 0.44). Japan-like +0.7%, because its money stock is 247% of GDP. Philippines-like +7.5% with the peso down 8.3%. Argentina-like +32% with the peso down 56%. A Musk-scale US UHI (about 24% of GDP) printed in the baseline case gives about 9.5% CPI, and about 0.9% only if robots meet 80% of the extra demand.

Over five years an Argentina-like printed UHI indexed to nominal GDP keeps its local-goods value but loses about 74% of its value in imported goods such as energy and chips. A fixed nominal US UHI keeps about 84% of its value in local goods after five years.

The court lowers confidence from the lane's 60 to 55 for two reasons. The model has no interest-rate channel, which is why Japan looks able to print (yet the yen fell from about 110 to about 158 through rate gaps), and it treats new US money as free when reserves pay 3.90%. Read it as a lower bound on US inflation and as a ranking of countries, not a forecast.

Three views. Governance: The exorbitant privilege is worth roughly the foreign demand for new dollars, not the whole UHI. Business: Imported inputs are the first casualty in emerging markets. Ordinary person: In a weak-currency country a printed UHI buys rent and food for longer than phones and fuel.

#### M7. Moderator question

> Can benefit streams be pledged? 42 USC 407 anti-assignment, pension-advance and benefit-backed lending abuses, PFD loans in Alaska, payday lending against benefits.

- **Proponent.** T1 statute and case law plus enforcement actions (R2B-01 to R2B-04).
- **Skeptic.** The Alaska product evidence is a snippet, and Brazil's loan book figure is secondary.
- **Judge.** Confirmed 85 on US law. Alaska loans stay speculative (R2B-09).
- **Ruling.** Status: confirmed. Confidence: 85. Horizon: H0.
- **Lane evidence.** R2B-01 to R2B-09

**Answer.**

Not in the US for Social Security. 42 USC 407 makes future payments non-transferable and immune from execution, levy, attachment or garnishment, with exceptions for child support and alimony (42 USC 659), and the Supreme Court reads it broadly. The abuse history is why: pension advances sold as purchases to dodge lending law, at APRs the CFPB put at up to 183% and Minnesota at over 200%, mostly to veterans.

Alaska's dividend is only partly protected: 20% is exempt from creditors, and child support, restitution, defaulted student loans, fines and state debts can take the rest. Notes secured by future dividends appear in a late-1990s legislative file seen only as a search snippet, so a current PFD loan product is unverified. Abroad, pledging is normal: Brazil lets retirees commit up to 45% of a pension to loans and cards for up to 108 months, the INSS payroll-loan book is over R$268 billion (2025, secondary), and the same pipes enabled the R$6.3 billion deduction fraud.

Three views. Governance: If UHI is ever made pledgeable, copy the guardrails (rate cap, share cap, court approval, consent logs), not the free market. Business: A pledgeable UHI creates a large, cheap, low-loss lending market and a large fraud surface. Ordinary person: A pledge lowers your interest rate but locks part of your floor for years.

#### M8. Moderator question

> If UHI is paid as a stablecoin or programmable token, can it be pledged in DeFi? Programmable restrictions (e-CNY expiring vouchers) as a design choice.

- **Proponent.** Shenzhen pilot data and the US CBDC ban and yield ban (R2B-21, R1 L2).
- **Skeptic.** Transferable streaming-payment tokens exist in DeFi (memory, not re-verified), so a permissive issuer could make the stream pledgeable fast.
- **Judge.** Plausible 65. Agrees with R2A on borrowing (contradiction C1).
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 65. Horizon: H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2B-21, R2B-26, R1 L2, R1 L7

**Answer.**

The balance you have already received can be pledged in DeFi today, exactly like pledging savings. The future stream cannot, unless the payer agrees to redirect payments or makes the stream a transferable token, because a smart contract cannot seize money that has not been sent. So pledgeability is the issuer's design choice.

China has shown the restrictive version: Shenzhen's 2020 e-CNY lottery gave 50,000 people 200 yuan each, spendable only at 3,389 local merchants, not transferable, with unspent money recovered (8.76 million of 10 million yuan used). In the US the Fed cannot issue a retail digital dollar, so a token UHI would ride on private stablecoins that cannot pay holders yield under GENIUS. DeFi is small next to banks: crypto-backed lending was $56.2B in Q2 2026.

Three views. Governance: Programmability lets one wallet hold a non-pledgeable floor and a pledgeable ownership slice. Business: DeFi lenders will take received balances, not unsecured future streams, without an issuer redirect. Ordinary person: A programmable UHI can also be one that expires or only works in approved shops. Read the rules.

### Is the entry route still open at 18? (r2-entry)

The ladder has not been pulled up. One rung broke (housing as first collateral), one got cheap but lottery-like (launching a business), and the deciding variable is ownership.

#### Q6. Owner question

> People get credit through asset ownership. The standard route for a new player has been labor to cash, then collateralized income, group funding, or a seed round. When a child reaches 18 in the new world, is that route still possible? (The owner suspects this is what the 'permanent underclass' meme is really about.)

- **Proponent.** BLS, NY Fed, NAR, Fed SHED, Stripe Atlas and Carta data line up behind 'narrowing and moving, not closing' (R2C-01 to R2C-14).
- **Skeptic.** The 1990 house price and rate are memory figures, Gen Z earns more at 28 than Millennials did, and youth unemployment fell, so the 'broken rung' may be a 2020 to 2026 rate shock that reverses.
- **Judge.** Housing is broken for entrants whatever the 1990 anchor, because the 2020 to 2026 payment jump is measured on verified inputs (contradiction C3). Plausible 70 kept.
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H0 to H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2C-01 to R2C-16, R2C-27, R2E-27, R1 RELOOP R-11, R-13

**Answer.**

Yes, but it has changed shape. The first job still exists: unemployment for ages 20 to 24 was 8.0% in September 2026, down from 9.2% a year earlier. It is bending in AI-exposed white-collar entry roles: recent graduates have 5.6% unemployment against 4.2% for all workers, and the entry share of postings in high-exposure jobs fell from nearly 60% in 2020 to 44.7%. Credit history is a speed bump, not a wall (secured cards, authorized-user status, credit-builder loans).

The housing rung is the one that broke. Median first-time buyer age is a record 40 and their share a record 21%. For a 20 to 24-year-old saving 10% of pay, a 10% down payment on a median existing home took about 6.2 years in 1990 and about 10.0 years now (model, 1990 price from memory). The monthly payment on a median home went from about 40 to about 68 hours of work since early 2020. 49% of adults under 30 live with a parent.

The launch rung got much cheaper and much more skewed. A digital brand can launch for about $525 to $2,850 and a private-label physical brand for about $1,900 to $7,400 (model), and solo founders are a record 63% of Stripe Atlas companies. But median solo revenue fell 23% in 2025 while the top decile rose 19%, and attention and shelf space, sold by platforms, are the costs that do not fall. Family money is the de facto seed round: 47% of 18 to 29-year-olds got outside help.

So the meme is right that ownership decides outcomes (the top 1% hold a record 32.5% of net worth, the bottom half 0.6% of equities) and wrong that the door is shut. It becomes permanent only if three things persist into H1: asset returns beating wages, blocked housing supply, and token-sized ownership grants.

Three views. Governance: The binding fix is supply and ownership, not credit: zoning and land release (the modern Homestead) and child accounts seeded at a scale that matters. Business: Firms buy young people's output as contractors rather than train them, which pushes training costs onto families. Ordinary person: Earn while learning in AI-complemented or physical-trust work, build a credit file early, live cheaply, and own diversified equity from the first paycheck.

> Entry-level labor data 2025 to 2026 for young workers.

- **Proponent.** BLS and NY Fed T0 series (R2C-01, R2C-02, R2C-05).
- **Skeptic.** Causation is unproven and the posting data are T4.
- **Judge.** Facts confirmed 80, cause plausible 55.
- **Ruling.** Status: confirmed. Confidence: 80. Horizon: H0.
- **Lane evidence.** R2C-01 to R2C-05, R2C-12

Soft but not collapsing. Unemployment for ages 20 to 24 was 8.0% in September 2026 (9.2% a year earlier) and 14.5% for ages 16 to 19, against 4.2% overall. #### M10. Moderator question

> Does AI lower the capital needed to start a business (solo-founder share, cost to launch), offsetting the underclass meme?

#### M12. Moderator question

> History: when mobility windows closed (enclosure, Gilded Age, post-1980), what reopened them (Homestead Act, public schooling, GI Bill)?

- **Proponent.** NPS, VA and Chetty data (R2C-23 to R2C-25).
- **Skeptic.** Goldin and Katz figures are memory, and enclosure and the Gilded Age were not covered in data.

### Cheap production, expensive attention (r2-founders)

Robots and cheap energy shrink the part of a product's price that was already small. The toll booths are shelf space, platforms and paid attention, and freight seasonality survives any fuel.

#### Q8. Owner question

> Robots push labor's hourly rate down. What about products? A founder wants to start a brand outside electronics (food, Walmart-shelf products, lifestyle products like makeup). Logistics and transport are seasonal supply-demand systems that move goods prices. What happens to them if the petrodollar is gone or replaced during an energy-watt era?

- **Proponent.** T0 10-K cost stacks and EIA and Drewry prices (R2D-01 to R2D-18).
- **Skeptic.** The 40% retailer margin is an assumption, and Case C depends on Chinese-controlled batteries.
- **Judge.** Cost-stack conclusion robust to the retailer assumption because production is small either way. Case C stays speculative (contradiction C6). Plausible 70.

- **Lane evidence.** R2D-01 to R2D-19, R2D-31, R2D-32, R2E-08

**Answer.**

Products get somewhat cheaper, brands much less. For a $10 mass cosmetic, everything the factory and ship touch (materials, packaging, labor, inbound freight, duty) is about $1.76. The retailer keeps about $4.00, marketing about $1.47, the brand's people, warehousing and distribution about $2.30, and profit about $0.27 (model on e.l.f.'s FY2026 10-K). Seasonality stays, because it comes from demand (Q4, Lunar New Year) and capacity, not fuel. Ocean shipping stays on dollar-priced fossil fuel longest. The court adds a caveat from R2E: electric trucks and storage depend on batteries, and China supplied 50% of US lithium-ion imports by value, so electrification swaps an oil dependence for a hardware dependence and the timeline is H1 to H2, not H0.

Three views. Governance: Platform-fee and antitrust rules matter more for consumer prices than robot policy, and freight inflation becomes a grid problem. Business: Spend on owning the customer, rent production and fulfillment, and pick goods with high value per cubic centimeter. Ordinary person: Store brands and commodity goods get cheaper first, branded goods hold price, and your exposure shifts from pump prices to power bills.

### What rivals can do, and whether time becomes the currency (r2-rivals)

Rivals' strongest lever is energy hardware, not currency. The 'In Time' fear is half right: goods got cheaper in hours, entry assets and some services got dearer.

#### Q9. Owner question

> What could rivals do to jeopardize this US plan and steer a different direction, given that energy is currently priced in USD?

- **Proponent.** Dated T1 proposals and T0 trade data (R2E-01 to R2E-10).
- **Skeptic.** Each use of the lever speeds substitution (Japan after 2010), and the lever is bargained away for tariffs.
- **Judge.** Plausible 65. Hardware beats currency as a lever in H0. The yuan-watt bloc is an H1 hypothesis.

**Answer.**

Their strongest card is the hardware that makes new energy, not the currency oil is priced in. Money moves have little bite in 2026: China still holds $590.7B of Treasuries, mBridge has done about $55B in total, and BRICS agreed on no currency in New Delhi in September 2026. Hardware moves are live and tightening: rare-earth licensing since April 2025, an October 2025 package suspended only until 10 November 2026, and on 16 October 2026 proposed bans on exporting 12 solar technologies, including wafers of 70 µm or less. Ordinary person: Squeezes show up as higher power bills and pricier EVs and home batteries, not as a dollar crash.

#### Q10. Owner question

> Because of the private credit problem and a primary deficit the reserve-currency issuer cannot solve, bonds and wealth can keep expanding at the expense of taxpayers and imported inflation. - **Proponent.** Fed DFA, BLS and FHFA T0 data (R2E-19 to R2E-26).
- **Skeptic.** The bottom half's wealth rose about 75% in real terms, and several 1990 time-price inputs are memory figures.

- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 70. Horizon: H0 to H1.
- **Lane evidence.** R2E-19 to R2E-30, R2E-35, R1 Dc-11

**Answer.**

Literally, no. Figuratively, partly, and the data show where. Since February 2020 average hourly pay rose 32.5% and CPI 30.3%, so real pay is up only 1.7% (4.2% for production and nonsupervisory workers), and it fell 0.7% in the year to September 2026 as energy rose 20.4%. Over a similar window house prices rose 59.3% and the S&P 500 about 140%. Of the $78.1T rise in household wealth since 2019 Q4, the top 10% took 67.5% and the bottom half 3.1%.

Three views. Governance: The fix is ownership and supply (housing, child accounts, cheaper care), not just CPI indexing, because CPI misses the asset gap. Business: Goods keep getting cheaper in hours. Customers are squeezed by rent, health cover and energy. Ordinary person: A CPI-indexed wage or UHI keeps up with groceries, not with a first home.

#### M18. Moderator question

> Can the US export its inflation to other countries (1960s Bretton Woods precedent, the dollar cycle), and who absorbs it in 2026?

- **Proponent.** IMF pass-through and 2026 currency records (R2E-31, R2E-32).
- **Skeptic.** Bretton Woods history is from memory, and the 2026 absorbers are suffering an oil shock as much as a dollar shock.
- **Judge.** Plausible 65. Narrows R2B's privilege (contradiction C7).
- **Ruling.** Status: plausible. Confidence: 65. Horizon: H0.
- **Lane evidence.** R2E-23, R2E-31, R2E-32, R2B-18

**Answer.**

Partly, and only when the dollar is strong. Under Bretton Woods, US deficits forced foreign central banks to buy dollars to hold their pegs, importing US inflation until the system broke in 1971. Three views. Governance: Dollar strength exports inflation, and 2026 allies (Japan, India, the Philippines) are paying for it. Business: A weaker-dollar policy raises US input costs. Ordinary person: US workers are not insulated: energy is up 20% and real pay is slightly down this year.
