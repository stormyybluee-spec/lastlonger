## The owner's questions (their own words, lightly cleaned, keep their intent)

Q1. Will Set 1 (gold, bonds, credit) disappear as an irrelevant layer? In terms of gold as a scarce resource asset?
Q2. Since stablecoins are basically backed by T-bills, after the robots and space equation enters later, is the old petrodollar backing gone? Or put another way: if someone has $50,000 of cash today, will they no longer borrow from a bank in the traditional rails sense?
Q3. Or does gold remain an individual store of value, while the crypto set replaces the traditional rails?
Q4. If UHI arrives before any energy-watt system, can people use UHI as collateral to get more loans or credit?
Q5. UHI mostly applies to the US, right? Do other nations have to compete on their own and give their citizens a different "UHI" value?
Q6. People get credit through asset ownership. But the standard route for a new player entering the board has been labor to cash, then collateralized income, or group funding, or a seed round. When a child reaches 18 in the new world, is that route still possible? (The owner thinks this is what the "permanent underclass" meme is really about.)
Q7. If each country must subsidize its own citizens' UHI, how will the value be set? Show the math, including nominal vs real. Does a new digital value change this or do the same rules apply?
Q8. Robots push labor's hourly rate down. What about products? A founder wants to start a brand outside electronics (food, Walmart-shelf products, lifestyle products like makeup). Also, logistics and transport are seasonal supply-demand systems that move goods prices. What happens to them if the petrodollar is gone or replaced during an energy-watt era?
Q9. What could rivals do to jeopardize this US plan and steer a different direction, given that energy is currently priced in USD?
Q10. Because of the private credit problem and a primary deficit the reserve-currency issuer cannot solve, bonds and wealth can keep expanding, at the expense of taxpayers and imported inflation. Does this end in a scenario like the movie "In Time", where time literally becomes the currency and the poor run out of it?
