## 1. Answers

### R5-13 Will Moody's cut France, and does it matter?

**Answer.** No, not this time. On Friday 2026-10-23, after the US close, Moody's kept France at Aa3 with a negative outlook, against most analysts' expectation of a cut to A1 and a thin Polymarket price of 90.5% for a Moody's or S&P downgrade by 31 October. Moody's said the rating "assumes that a deficit-reducing budget for 2027 will be adopted" and that financing costs and social tensions "abate somewhat in the next few months", so the hold is conditional. France now sits at Aa3 negative (Moody's), A+ stable (S&P, last review 2026-05-29, next 2026-11-27) and A+ stable (Fitch, last review 2026-08-28, no further 2026 date found). Mechanically a one-notch cut would have changed very little: index providers that use the middle of the three ratings already treat France as A+, the ECB uses the best rating among its accepted agencies and groups AAA to A- in one haircut band [memory for the haircut band], EU banks hold euro-area sovereigns in their own currency at a 0% risk weight, and the ECB's TPI criteria do not mention ratings at all. History agrees: across 11 French downgrades since 2012 the OAT-Bund spread moved a median of 4bp tighter from the month before to the month after, and the one big widening (June 2024) came from the snap dissolution nine days after S&P's cut, not from the cut. Ratings follow the spread more than they lead it. What would matter is a slide into the BBB band (two or three more notches), which is where ECB haircuts step up and many investment mandates bind.
- Status: confirmed (decision and mechanics), plausible (that a cut would not have moved spreads much). Confidence 80. Horizon H0.
- Three views. Governance: the agencies are grading the budget process, not the debt stock, so the 2027 finance bill and the 49.3 choice are the real rating events, and S&P on 27 November is the next one. Business: a one-notch move is noise for funding costs, the spread (171.8bp on 10-23) already prices more than an A1 rating, and the cliff to watch is BBB. Ordinary person: a rating cut does not change mortgage rates by itself, the 10-year yield does, and that is driven by the budget fight, the ECB and the 2027 election.

### R5-14 How much do new residents cost or contribute to European states?

**Answer.** It depends mostly on why people come, how old they are when they arrive, and whether they work in well-paid jobs, and the official studies agree on that much. The UK Migration Advisory Committee (December 2025) finds the 2022/23 Skilled Worker cohort worth about +£47bn over its lifetime, with main applicants outside health and care at about +£689,000 each in present value, care workers at about -£36,000 and adult dependants of health and care workers at about -£67,000, against a UK resident average of -£39,000. Denmark's Finance Ministry counted a net public cost of DKK 31bn in 2018 for immigrants and descendants of non-Western origin (DKK 24bn of it for the MENAPT group, where asylum and family routes dominate), while immigrants from Western countries made a positive net contribution. The Dutch "Borderless Welfare State" study (van de Beek and co-authors, 2021 and 2023, CBS microdata, not a government report) finds labour migrants positive on average (+€125,000 per person) and family and asylum migrants negative (-€275,000 and -€475,000, including the second generation, 2016 euros). Germany's ZEW study for the Bertelsmann Foundation (2014) shows how much method matters: foreigners living in Germany in 2012 paid €22,300 more each over their lifetimes than they received in individual transfers, but carry -€79,100 each once general spending such as defence and roads is allocated per head. Results differ for clear reasons: how pure public goods are allocated, the discount rate, whether the country is running a deficit (which makes natives negative too), whether children and the second generation are included, and who stays versus who leaves. Age at arrival in the 20s or around 30 gives the best result in every study that reports it, because the host state did not pay for schooling and gets a full working life of taxes.
- Status: confirmed (the drivers: route, employment and earnings, education, age at arrival). Plausible (the size of any single national total, which moves a lot with method). Confidence 80. Horizon H0 to H2.
- Three views. Governance: the fiscal sign is set mostly by admission route and by labour-market access after arrival, so policy levers are selection on work routes, faster work permission and recognition of qualifications, and these studies should be run regularly by official bodies with published methods. Business: employer-sponsored routes are fiscally positive in every official study found, and the gain is concentrated in higher earners (in the UK, 72% of the Skilled Worker gain comes from the top 30% of earners). Ordinary person: the honest summary is "it depends on the route, age and job", and headline totals in either direction usually hide a method choice, above all how defence and other shared costs are split.

### R5-15 Are Europeans actually moving to Latin America or Southeast Asia in rising numbers?

**Answer.** Slightly more in absolute numbers because total emigration rose, but not as a share, and much of the Latin American flow is return migration or citizens by descent going home. In Eurostat's table of emigration by next country of residence for six EU countries with full detail (Italy, Netherlands, Sweden, Belgium, Austria, Finland), Latin America took 4.5% of emigrants in 2019 and 4.1% in 2024, Southeast Asia 1.6% in both years, while the UAE more than doubled from 0.4% to 0.9% (before the 2026 war) and Switzerland rose from 2.9% to 3.8%. Intra-EU moves are over half of all emigration (53.8% in 2024). For German citizens in 2025, Switzerland took 7.9%, the US 3.1%, Thailand 1.0% (2,894 people), and the four Latin American countries in the top 50 together 1.1%. Italy's own statistics office says Latin America took almost 10% of Italian emigrants in 2023 to 2024, "in part" new citizens returning home after acquiring Italian citizenship, and Brazil was the top destination of Italians born abroad. Spain's 137,719 emigrating nationals in 2024 included 49,476 born abroad. Visa data are thin: Mexico issued about 1,800 temporary residence cards to Spaniards and about 970 to Germans in 2024 (secondary source), Portugal issued about 2,500 digital nomad visas in 2023, and EU citizens do not need such visas inside the EU, so the "remote worker" move shows up as intra-EU flows that statistics barely separate.
- Status: refuted (the claim of a meaningful rising trend toward Latin America or Southeast Asia). Confirmed (small, stable shares, and a faster-rising UAE before the war). Confidence 75. Horizon H0.
- Three views. Governance: Europe's emigration is mostly to its own neighbours and to Switzerland, the US and the Gulf, so talent-retention policy should look at those channels, and statistics should split returns and descent-citizens from native movers. Business: Latin America and Southeast Asia are niche destinations for European people (retirees, remote workers, heritage returns), not a talent pool shift, which matches R4 R4D's capital view (Latin America about 6%). Ordinary person: if you hear "everyone is moving to Mexico or Bali", the official numbers say a few thousand a year per large country, and far more go to Switzerland, Spain or Austria.

### Round 4 open item: did the 2026 Gulf war cause a lasting outflow of European residents from the UAE?

**Answer.** Not in the aggregate data available. Dubai's population fell 1.3% in March 2026 (about 61,000 people, the first full month of Iranian drone attacks), then rose each month from April to June and stood about 20,000 above its pre-war level by mid-year at about 4.73 million (AGBI, citing the Dubai Data and Statistics Establishment, which does not split expatriates by nationality). The Financial Times estimated that about 30,000 Britons, roughly one in eight, left after fighting began on 28 February, a figure the British Embassy and Dubai Media Office could not confirm. Before the war the UAE was the fastest-rising non-European destination for EU emigrants (0.4% to 0.9% of the six-country total from 2019 to 2024, German citizens 3,410 in 2025). So the best reading is a shock and partial rotation (some Europeans out, others in), not a lasting exodus. A nationality split would need UAE residence-visa data, which is not public.
- Status: plausible (no lasting aggregate outflow), speculative (any number for Europeans specifically). Confidence 60. Horizon H0.
- Three views. Governance: the UAE's model survived a direct attack in population terms, which supports R4's "dented, not broken" view of the Gulf. Business: residence demand recovered within a quarter, the risk premium shows in insurance and property prices more than in headcount. Ordinary person: some Europeans left and some came back or replaced them, so "everyone left Dubai" is not what the population count shows.

### Round 4 open item: an OECD or Eurostat table of skilled European emigration by destination for 2024 or 2025

**Answer.** Partly found. Eurostat's migr_emi3nxt gives emigration by country of next usual residence for 2019 to 2024 for most member states (updated 2026-09-22), which answers "by destination" but not "skilled", and it counts all citizenships, so returns of foreign nationals are mixed in. No OECD table of skilled emigration by destination for 2024 or 2025 was found: the OECD's emigrant database (DIOC) is still the 2015/16 round, and the International Migration Outlook 2025 covers inflows to OECD countries (permanent migration down 4% in 2024, down 8% in Europe). The best skill-specific national source found is Istat: in 2019 to 2023 the UK and Germany took about a third (29,000) of young Italian graduates who left, France and Switzerland about 8,000 each, and the US 4,000.
- Status: confirmed (what exists and what does not). Confidence 85. Horizon H0.
- Three views. Governance: Europe cannot see its own brain drain by destination and skill in one official table, which is a statistics gap worth closing. Business: the destination pattern for graduates mirrors the general one (UK, Germany, Switzerland, US). Ordinary person: the young graduates who leave mostly go a short flight away.

## 2. Key findings

1. **Moody's held France at Aa3 negative on 2026-10-23** (CNBC headline, Reuters via Yahoo Finance). Reuters had reported that most analysts expected a cut, and Polymarket priced a Moody's or S&P downgrade by 31 October at 87.5% (R3 R3E-13) to 90.5% (R4 R4E M7) on about $10k. After the decision the October contract traded last at 4.
2. **The hold is conditional.** Moody's says Aa3 "assumes that a deficit-reducing budget for 2027 will be adopted" and that financing costs and social tensions ease "in the next few months". The finance ministry said the decision reflected the "credibility of the budget".
3. **Agency calendar.** S&P reviews France on 2026-11-27 (AFT calendar, via search snippet). Fitch's 2026 reviews were in March and on 2026-08-28, no later 2026 date was found. A Fitch warning reported on 2026-10-13 (a weak source, n9ws) said the A+ could fall after a further substantial rise in debt.
4. **Rating history.** S&P AAA to AA+ (2012-01-13), Moody's Aaa to Aa1 (2012-11-19), Fitch AAA to AA+ (2013-07-12), S&P AA+ to AA (2013-11-08), Fitch AA+ to AA (2014-12-12), Moody's Aa1 to Aa2 (2015-09-18), Fitch AA to AA- (2023-04-28), S&P AA to AA- (2024-05-31), Moody's Aa2 to Aa3 (2024-12-14), Fitch AA- to A+ (2025-09-12 [memory for the exact date]), S&P AA- to A+ (2025-10-17, off-calendar). Moody's moved its outlook to negative on 2025-10-24.
5. **Downgrades did not widen spreads.** On ECB monthly convergence yields, the OAT-Bund spread changed by a median of -4bp from the month before to the month after the 11 downgrades (range -14bp to +15bp). The +15bp case (S&P, May 2024) was the June 2024 dissolution. Table 4.2.
6. **Mechanics of a cut are mild above the BBB band.** TPI's four criteria (EU fiscal framework, no severe imbalances, debt sustainability, sound policies) do not include ratings (ECB, 2022-07-21). France has been under an excessive deficit procedure since 2024 [memory], so the live TPI test is "effective action", not ratings. EU banks apply a 0% risk weight to member-state debt in domestic currency (CRR Article 114(4) [memory]). The ECB uses the best rating among accepted agencies (Moody's, S&P, Fitch, Morningstar DBRS, Scope [memory]), and DBRS (AA (high) negative, March 2025) and Scope (AA- negative, September 2025) sit above the big three, per search results. Index families using the middle rating already see A+ since S&P's October 2025 cut [memory for index rules].
7. **The spread is at a 15-year high and volatile.** OAT 5.05%, Bund 3.33%, spread 171.8bp on 10-23 after 174.6bp on 10-22 (R4 R4B-17). Reuters reports the OAT hit 5.14% on 10-22, its highest since July 2002. October daily changes have a standard deviation of about 7.9bp, three to four times a normal month. The ECB's monthly convergence series showed 97bp for September 2026, so October is a step change, not drift.
8. **Budget calendar.** First-part debate 13 to 19 October, resumes 27 October to 2 November, solemn vote on the first part 3 November (Assemblée nationale). The PS motion to reject was defeated 149 to 236 on 13 October. The finance committee rejected the revenue part 31 to 3. Rapporteur Philippe Juvin urged Lecornu to use 49.3 (21 to 22 October), and Lecornu had earlier pledged neither 49.3 nor ordinances unless there was "obstruction". The target is a deficit of 5.0% of GDP in 2027 against 5.4% expected in 2026 (Reuters), although the 2026 budget itself targeted 5.0% (Al Jazeera), so 2026 slipped by about 0.4 points.
9. **Precedent.** The 2026 budget was adopted on 2 February 2026 by 49.3 after four months of deadlock. Two censure motions failed (260 and 135 votes, 289 needed).
10. **Fiscal studies agree on drivers.** Every official study found (UK MAC 2025, Denmark Finance Ministry, Australian Treasury 2021 via MAC, ZEW 2014) and the Dutch academic study rank routes the same way: work routes positive, family routes lower, humanitarian routes the most negative on fiscal accounts, with age at arrival in the 20s to about 30 and earnings as the main drivers.
11. **Method can flip the sign.** ZEW: +€22,300 per foreign resident without general spending, -€79,100 with it allocated per head. MAC: UK residents average -£39,000 (median -£145,000) over their remaining lives at a 3% discount rate because the base year ran a primary deficit, -£163,000 at 1%, -£7,000 at 5%. The Dutch study allocates public goods equally per head (following CPB's 2014 ageing study), while CPB's 2003 immigration study allocated them in proportion to GDP contribution, which narrows group differences.
12. **Who stays matters.** MAC: Skilled Worker main applicants who stay average +£931,000, those who leave +£174,000. The Dutch study reports the opposite selection for its whole immigrant population (those who integrate well leave more often).
13. **Second generation.** A peer-reviewed Danish study (Jensen and Manning, AEJ: Applied 2025, cited by MAC) finds children of immigrants have lower earnings and higher transfers on average, but the gap vanishes once parental socioeconomic background is accounted for. The Dutch study finds the second generation roughly mirrors the first generation's fiscal position.
14. **European emigration is local.** Six-country Eurostat aggregate 2024: intra-EU 53.8%, UK 5.5% (from 9.8% in 2019), US 3.6%, Switzerland 3.8%, Latin America 4.1%, Southeast Asia 1.6%, UAE 0.9%, Canada 1.0%, Australia and New Zealand 1.5%.
15. **Italian emigration fell in 2025.** 109,000 Italian citizens left in 2025, 22.7% fewer than the revised 141,000 of 2024 (Euronews on Istat, 2026-08-03). Istat had first reported 156,000 for 2024.

## 5. Anomalies

1. **The thin market was 90.5% sure and wrong (pending formal resolution on 10-31).** The desk's 70 (R3E) was closer. Thin rating markets anchor on analyst previews.
2. **Moody's, the most cautious agency, is now the outlier high.** Aa3 equals AA-, one notch above the A+ of S&P and Fitch, and it has carried a negative outlook for a full year without acting.
3. **France pays more than the US.** Reuters' related headline: the first time since 2013 (not verified this round). With the US 10-year at about 4.99% (R4 R4E) and the OAT at 5.05% to 5.14%, the arithmetic fits.
4. **Spreads tightened after most downgrades.** Default expectation is widening. The pattern says agencies confirm what markets already priced.
5. **The UAE, a war zone in March 2026, was the fastest-rising non-European destination for EU emigrants in 2019 to 2024** and its population was back above pre-war levels by mid-2026.
6. **A large share of "Italian emigration to Brazil and Argentina" is Italians born there going home.** Brazil was the first destination of Italians born abroad (15,000 in 2023 to 2024).
7. **UK residents are fiscally negative on average over their remaining lives in the MAC's own baseline** (-£39,000), which shows why absolute signs mislead in a deficit country.

## 6. Mechanism chains

1. **Budget chain (H0).** Committee rejects revenue part → first-part vote 3 November fails or is replaced by 49.3 → censure motion (85) → survives (85) as the PS abstains, as in February 2026 → budget by 49.3 in December, or special law and enactment in January to February (P01 40) → S&P 27 November acts on the trajectory (P09 25) → spread holds 150 to 190bp. Test: P02 to P05, P09, P12.
2. **Rating chain (H0 to H1).** Ratings follow spreads and budget outcomes → a cut at A level changes little mechanically → if two more notches by 2028 (BBB band), ECB haircuts and mandate limits bite → forced selling and TPI debate. The BBB line, not A1, is the mechanical tripwire.
3. **Fiscal migration chain (H1 to H2).** Admission route and age at arrival set employment and earnings → earnings set taxes in progressive systems → work routes positive, humanitarian routes negative on fiscal accounts, with integration speed the main lever → method choices (public goods, discount rate) set the headline sign → political debates use totals that hide these choices. Test: next MAC route estimates (family and humanitarian routes promised), the next Danish Finance Ministry release.
4. **Emigration chain (H0).** EU free movement and language keep most moves within Europe and to Switzerland → the US takes a stable 3% to 4% → Latin America's share is propped up by descent-citizenship round trips → Southeast Asia stays a niche (retirees, remote workers, Thailand DTV with new home-embassy and police-record rules from 31 August 2026) → the Gulf grows fastest until a security shock, then recovers. Test: Eurostat migr_emi3nxt 2025 (autumn 2027), Destatis 2026 (mid 2027).

## 10. Proposed changes

1. **Headline scenario odds: no change** (H0 62/15/1/22, H1 44/23/3/30, H2 31/28/10/31, S2 split 13/17/18 plus 2/6/10). The R4 court said "no cut lowers H0 crisis odds by about 1 point". The desk should not apply that point: the hold is explicitly conditional on a 2027 budget, the spread widened from 135bp to 172bp in eight sessions, and the 2026 deficit slipped from 5.0% to 5.4%. France-led euro crisis by 2028 stays at 20.
2. **Rating events lose scenario weight above the BBB band.** Evidence: TPI criteria ignore ratings (R5C-06), the mechanical effects of A-level cuts are nil (table 3.3), and spreads moved a median -4bp after past cuts (R5C-05). Proposed rule: agency actions at A level are signals only, a fall of any two agencies into the BBB band becomes a tripwire for conditional C1 review.
3. **Correct the R4 tripwire calendar.** Replace "2026-10-27 French National Assembly budget vote" with "debate resumes 10-27, solemn vote on the first part 11-03, Article 47 clock mid December". Add "S&P 2026-11-27" as the next agency call and note "no Fitch date scheduled for the rest of 2026". Close R4 open question 1 (Moody's: affirmed Aa3 negative on 10-23).
4. **Scoring note for R3E C16 and R4E M7.** Market 87.5 to 90.5 against the desk's 70 on "Moody's or S&P downgrades France by 31 October". Outcome near certain No (last trade 4). Score on 10-31: the desk beat the thin market.
5. **Lower R4D's "more European emigrants (excluding heritage registrations) go to Latin America than to Southeast Asia" from 75 to 45.** German citizens in 2025 sent more people to Southeast Asia (at least 5,175) than to Latin America (at most about 5,000 including countries below the top 50), and the larger Latin American totals in Eurostat include returns and descent-citizens (R5C-27, R5C-30, R5C-31).
6. **Add to the R4 destination table:** "UAE: fastest-rising non-European destination for EU emigrants 2019 to 2024 (0.4% to 0.9%), population back above pre-war by mid-2026" (R5C-28, R5C-35). Close R4 open question 8 as "no lasting aggregate outflow found, nationality split unavailable".
7. **Close R4 open question 4 (net fiscal contribution by origin and education) with the neutral summary in section 3.5:** route, age at arrival and earnings drive results, method choices set the sign of totals, official studies agree on the ranking of routes. No scenario effect.
8. **Close R4 open question 5 partly:** Eurostat migr_emi3nxt gives destinations for 2019 to 2024 (not skill), Istat gives graduates by destination for 2019 to 2023, no OECD table exists.
