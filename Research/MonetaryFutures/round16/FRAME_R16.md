# Round 16 frame (2026-11-16): Level 3 deep chapter on the law of money

Chapter: working id rb-law, the third of the seven deep chapters (round13/SERIES_L3.md). Working title: "Who wrote the rules of money, and for whom?"
It deepens question 7 of rb-credit and tests the owner's points 2 and 5 (round13/OWNER_R13.md) where they bear on governance: why a body that holds reserves issues and inflates money, and whether it does so for the sake of progress and productivity.

## Hypotheses to test (with the question-mark rule)
- H1. The law leans toward money made by lending because the body that issues it exists to let output and progress grow. Test: what central banks were actually created to do, in their founding laws and by their own account (the Bank of England 1694, the Federal Reserve 1913, Bank Negara Malaysia 1959 and the Act of 2009, the European Central Bank), and what their stated goals are today.
- H2. The issuer inflates on purpose, mildly, and this serves the public. Test: who gains and who loses from 2% inflation and from the profit on issuing money (seigniorage), with sizes. Where does that profit go?
- H3. The governance is controlled and answerable. Test: who appoints, who can remove, who audits, what must be published, and what happened when a central bank was captured by a government (cases of high inflation) or kept apart from it.
- H4. The rules were chosen by the public. Test: the record of votes and laws, and the places where the public was asked directly (Switzerland 2014 and 2018, euro referendums in Denmark and Sweden).
- H5. Alternatives exist and were tried. Test: currency boards (Hong Kong, Malaysia before 1967), dollarisation (Ecuador, El Salvador), free banking (Scotland, Canada), full-reserve proposals, and legal-tender experiments with Bitcoin (El Salvador 2021 to 2025). What happened to output, prices and crises in each?

## Lanes
- R16A, law and institutions: founding laws, mandates, appointment and removal, audit and disclosure, seigniorage and where it goes, Malaysia in detail.
- R16B, record and alternatives: what happened where the rules were different, both sides' best case, and sourced human detail.
