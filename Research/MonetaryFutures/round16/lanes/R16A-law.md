The ECB names price stability first and growth support only "without prejudice". Founding words do not mainly say "to let output grow". Growth enters as a later or lower ranked aim.

Reading for H4: the Fed Act passed on near party lines with 27 senators absent or paired, and no vote of the public. Where the public was asked directly (Switzerland 2014 and 2018, Denmark 2000, Sweden 2003) it chose to keep the existing arrangement each time, by wide margins on the Swiss questions and narrow ones on the euro. That does not show the public chose the present rules. It shows that when offered an alternative, a majority declined. I found no referendum anywhere on the 1971 end of gold convertibility, on the 2% goal or on central bank independence.

## Evidence for and against H1 to H4

### H1 The law leans toward lending-made money because the issuer exists to let output and progress grow

For:
- Fed Act s.2A (1977) ties money and credit growth to "the economy's long run potential" "to increase production" and names maximum employment.
- RBA 1959 names "full employment" and "economic prosperity and welfare of the people". Bank Negara 2009 states stability is sought as "conducive to the sustainable growth of the Malaysian economy" and adds "inclusive financial system". RBI's 2016 preamble adds "keeping in mind the objective of growth".
- Bank of England 1998 ranks support for "growth and employment" second.

Against:
- The Bank of England was founded in 1694 to raise £1.2 million for a war, lent to the government at 8%. The Fed's 1913 title lists an "elastic currency", rediscounting and supervision, not growth.
- The ECB (Art 127) ranks price stability first and supports other policies only "without prejudice". The Bank of England's 1998 duty and Bank Negara's s.22 also put price stability first. Founding words are about financing, stability and supervision more than output.
- No law I read says the issuer exists to "inflate money so that technology advances". That is the owner's reading, not a text.

### H2 The issuer inflates on purpose, mildly, and this serves the public

For:
- The Fed (2012) gives a stated reason: a clear 2% goal "helps keep longer-term inflation expectations firmly anchored" and is "most consistent" with its mandate. The ECB and Bank of England also state 2% targets. The inflation is deliberate and published.
- The target is mild by design and symmetric (ECB 2021).

Against:
- Gainers include the state as debtor: Reinhart and Sbrancia 3 to 4% of GDP a year in the US and UK, 1945 to 1980, and the St. Louis Fed 3.3% of GDP in 2021. Losers include holders of nominal bonds (a "6.5% tax" in 2021).
- The 2% number came from a New Zealand finance ministry and bank decision in 1989 to 1990 (secondary). I found no source that says it was chosen to raise output.
- Seigniorage is not a steady gain. The Fed remitted 109.0 billion in 2021, then nothing from 2023 to 2025, and carries a 243.5 billion deferred asset. Bank Negara pays out part of a profit that depends on reserve returns (RM5.25 billion of RM13.16 billion for 2024).
- Bank Negara has no numeric inflation target in its Act (inference).

### H3 The governance is controlled and answerable

For:
- Appointment, term and removal rules are written: Fed 14-year terms and "for cause" removal, now tested in Trump v. Cook (June 2026, 5-4, on notice and process, merits open). Bank Negara: Governor appointed by the Agong for five years, removal only on listed grounds (s.17).
- Reporting: Bank of England open letters (12 in 2021 to 2024), Bank Negara accounts audited by the Auditor General and laid before Parliament, Fed minutes in three weeks.

Against:
- The Fed audit law excludes monetary policy deliberations and FOMC transactions, and transcripts come after about five years.
- Bank Negara's Cabinet can set policy over the Board's objection (s.72). Government lending is allowed to 12.5% of estimated federal receipts (s.71).
- Capture cases show large inflation (Turkey 85.5% peak, Zimbabwe, Venezuela, Argentina 211%) but each has rival causes. Independence has also failed the public: the FCIC on the Fed before 2008, the 2011 Trichet-Draghi letter.
- Romelli and Garriga show independence rose mostly after 1990 and followed high-inflation periods, which is a political choice, not a founding feature.

### H4 The rules were chosen by the public

For:
- Where asked, Swiss voters (2014, 2018), Danes (2000) and Swedes (2003) each kept the existing arrangement. Congress voted on the Fed Act in 1913.

Against:
- The 1913 Act passed 43 to 25 in the Senate with 27 absent or paired, and 298 to 60 in the House, on near party lines. The public did not vote.
- Turnout on the Swiss money questions was 33.8% in 2018 (47% in 2014 is unconfirmed). The money-creation design itself (private bank credit) was never put to a vote in the other countries I checked.
- Trust is low and uneven: 39% confidence in the Fed chair in 2024 (Gallup), 43% trust in the ECB in late 2023.
