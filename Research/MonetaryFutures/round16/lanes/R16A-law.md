Reading for H1: only the Fed (1977), the RBA (1959), the Bank of England (second ranked, 1998) and Malaysia (2009, as the reason for stability) write growth, output or employment into the law. The Bank of England founding charter (1694) and the Federal Reserve title (1913) list financing a war, an elastic currency, rediscounting and supervision. The ECB names price stability first and growth support only "without prejudice". Founding words do not mainly say "to let output grow". Growth enters as a later or lower ranked aim.

Reading for H2 (my reasoning, not a source claim): seigniorage on notes is the return on assets held against currency issued. Malaysia's RM170.7 billion of notes costs the Bank nothing in interest, so its profit rises and falls with reserve returns, and the part paid out was RM2.85 billion in the year after 2023 and RM5.25 billion for 2024. The Fed's profit disappeared once it paid interest on reserves. Both show the profit depends on the interest rate, not only on inflation.
