- 104 rows checked: 51 match (some with a flag), 36 overstated, 15 unsupported, 1 mismatch, 1 summary row (VF-98).

## 5. One-sided numbers and causal overreach

Causal claims the data cannot carry:

1. [9] "But look at what it cost": the 17 versus 120 gap is made the price of the board rule. The Bank of England gives low monetisation and undeveloped banking as the cause. Money supply, not only notes, is measured, and 17 pounds was the highest in the colonial range of 3 to 17.
2. [12] "when times were bad, a board made them worse": the source says a passive board could not cushion a slump, and this is about "old-style" boards in general.
3. [13] and [53] Malaya "wrote a law to get" a credit-steering issuer and "untied its own" hands: no lane row on motives.
4. [19] A finding of no growth effect from independence is used as evidence against Part 1 (purpose). That is a different question.
5. [35] Zimbabwe, Venezuela and Turkey are shown as "what can happen when a government takes the printing press". Lane B names rival causes (farm seizures and drought, sanctions and oil prices, energy prices and a currency crisis). The Turkey row does not show the central bank financed the state.
6. [36] "Being free of politicians did not make it right": the inquiry did not test independence as a cause. Four commissioners dissented.
7. [47] "The fixed rule ... now kept the state from helping them": the state froze deposits itself. The IEO ties the adjustment to a fixed rate and dollar debts, and names the Brazilian devaluation and the strong dollar as rival causes.
8. [54] Hong Kong "paid for its fixed rate with a 5% fall": the 1998 fall came with a regional crisis, and the IMF names it.
9. [26] and [28]: the 3% to 4% debt effect comes from negative real interest rates helped by financial repression (captive buyers, rate caps, capital controls), not inflation alone. The chapter reads it as a transfer from savers "to the state", while the study counts a fall in the real value of debt.
10. [26] The 6.5% tax is a one year estimate for 2021 from a blog by Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis staff. It is not repeated yearly.
11. [56] Row 3 uses the Zimbabwe note as the "fact that decides" the governance finding, which it cannot do.
