## 1. Answers

### R4-4. Where would Europe's money, firms and talent go?

**Answer.** Mostly West, but "West" means the United States, Switzerland, the UK and Canada, not Latin America. Europe's money already goes there: Europe holds $16.2T of US securities (46% of all foreign holdings, June 2025, TIC), euro area investors keep about 60% of their foreign equities in the US, and the US is the largest single home of EU direct investment (28.3% of EU outward FDI stock, with the UK 18.3% and Switzerland 9.7%). The owner is right that Asia will not be the main home of European financial capital: all of developing Asia together is a small share of European portfolios, and ASEAN's record FDI ($244B in 2025) comes mostly from Asia itself and lands mostly in Singapore. But the Southeast Asian belief is right about one flow, factories: on cost, energy and supply chains, Vietnam, Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and India score as high as the US for plants, and Malaysia (+51%) and Thailand (+30%) both saw FDI jump in 2025. Latin America gets a real but small slice: Iberian and Italian people and family ties (Spain was Mexico's second largest investor in 2025, Argentina still has the largest number of registered Italians abroad), Mexico as a back door into the US market, and EU-Mercosur trade. Its rule of law (Mexico 0.40, rank 121 of 143 on the WJP index) and violence (Mexico and Brazil in ACLED's ten most violent countries) are worse than the Gulf's or Malaysia's. The owner's verdict on the Middle East is supported by 2026 events (Iran war, Dubai attacked, expats leaving in March), but the Gulf was still the world's top magnet for millionaires in 2025, and the UAE outranks every Latin American country except Uruguay, Costa Rica and Chile on rule of law. Wealthy people, retirees and skilled workers mostly stay close: Switzerland, Italy, Portugal, Greece and the UK take more of them than any other region.
**Status:** Plausible (owner's thesis on North America and against Asia as the home of financial capital). Refuted as stated (Latin America as a main destination). Plausible (SEA belief for factories only).
**Confidence:** 70.
**Horizon:** H1 (2028 to 2035), with the pattern already visible in H0.
**Model estimate of where Europe's net outbound capital goes in H1, if the US holds and the EU declines (all forms, judgment):** North America 55 (range 45 to 65), non-EU Europe (UK, Switzerland, Norway) 12, Asia 15 (Japan about 4, Southeast Asia including Singapore about 5, India about 3, China and others about 3), Latin America 6, Gulf and Middle East 3, Africa 2, Central Asia and Russia under 1, other (Australia, offshore centres) 6.

**Three views.**
- **Governance:** Europe's real competitor for its own savings is the US market, not Asia. The EU's own figure is about EUR 300B a year of savings going to non-EU markets. Slowing that takes a deeper EU capital market (Savings and Investments Union) and cheaper energy, not trade deals with distant regions. For the US, a weakening EU is a source of capital and firms. For Latin America, the opening is narrow: Mexico as a US-access platform, and Mercosur if the 2026 agreement is ratified.
- **Business:** Financial capital goes to the deepest market (New York). Plants go to the customer and to cheap power and labour: the US to get inside its tariff wall, Southeast Asia and India for cost and Asian demand, Mexico only if USMCA holds, Morocco and Eastern Europe to serve Europe. Listings and fast-growing start-ups go to the US (about 130 listings moved in the decade to 2024, and 40 of 147 European unicorns from 2008 to 2021 moved their headquarters abroad, most of them to the US).
- **Ordinary person:** Most Europeans who move go somewhere near, with the same language or legal system: Germans to Switzerland, Austria and Spain, Italians to Germany and Spain. The US takes top talent but is getting harder to enter. Southeast Asia and the Gulf suit a small group of the mobile wealthy and remote workers. Latin America draws mainly people with family ties and Spanish speakers.

## 2. Key findings

1. **The money is already in the West, and has been moving there for 17 years.** Europe's holdings of US securities rose from $4.2T (June 2008) to $16.2T (June 2025), a 3.8-fold rise, faster than Asia's (2.7-fold). Europe holds 45.9% of all foreign-held US securities, against 27.8% for Asia (TIC, T0). Euro area holdings of US equities were about $6.02T in May 2026, up from $5.17T in March 2026. Euro area investors did not sell America after the April 2025 shock (TIC via FRED, T0).
2. **The "EUR 300B a year" figure is the Commission's, not Draghi's report text.** The Draghi report (September 2024) gives EU household savings of EUR 1,390B against EUR 840B in the US (2022) and says close to 30% of European unicorns moved their headquarters abroad, mostly to the US. A search of both parts of the report found no 300B figure. The 300B appears in the Commission's March 2025 Savings and Investments Union launch, and Italian press reported Draghi putting 2024 at about EUR 500B in a March 2025 speech (T2, single source).
3. **Asia's FDI boom is real, but it is mostly Asian money, and it is not the money of a falling Europe.** Developing Asia took $644B of FDI in 2025 (UNCTAD WIR 2026). South-East Asia hit a record $244B (+10%), but more than 60% of ASEAN's 2024 inflows went to Singapore, East Asia supplied about 30%, and intra-ASEAN 14%. The EU was only the third largest outside source. FDI into GVC-intensive manufacturing in South-East Asia more than halved, from $31B to $14B, in 2025 (T0/T4).
4. **Latin America is a real but small destination, and its link to Europe runs through Iberia and Italy.** Latin America took $188B (+14%) in 2025. Brazil took $77B and Mexico $41B. Mexico's 2025 FDI record of $40.9B was 67.7% reinvested earnings and only 18% new money, and its announced greenfield projects fell from $44B to $24B. Spain was its second largest investor. Argentina's FDI fell 56% to about $4B even under reform, and MSCI still classes it as "standalone". The EU is the largest foreign investor in Mercosur (about EUR 390B, 2023). The EU-Mercosur agreement was signed on 17 January 2026.
5. **People move short distances to the same language.** German emigrants in 2025: Switzerland about 22,700, Austria about 13,500, Spain about 9,700, the US just under 8,900 (Destatis via press). Italians registered abroad: Argentina 989,901 (15.4%, mostly citizens by descent) and Germany 849,383 (13.2%) at 1 January 2025. New Italian emigrants go mainly to Spain and Germany.
6. **The wealthy go to tax and safety, and Southern Europe is a main destination.** Henley 2025 net millionaire inflows: UAE +9,800, US +7,500, Italy +3,600, Switzerland +3,000, Saudi Arabia +2,400, Singapore +1,600, Portugal +1,400, Greece +1,200. UK -16,500. Henley's June 2026 report published no new flow forecasts. It scored the UAE 85.3, Singapore 79.5, Uruguay 71.8, Panama 71.5, Costa Rica 70.2 and the US lowest of those listed at 62.3. Applications by US nationals doubled in 2025, and nearly half of them target European programmes (T4, a firm that sells these programmes).
7. **History says money runs to the deepest safe market, people to the same language, and both to whoever lets them in first.** In the 1930s, $9B of gold went to the US from January 1934 to late 1939, $1.6B of it in twelve crisis weeks around Munich and Prague (Fed Bulletin, T0/T2). Between 1882 and 1930, 3.30M Spaniards emigrated, 1.59M of them to Argentina and 1.12M to Cuba. Hong Kong's 1984 to 1997 emigrants went to Canada (335,646), the US, Australia and the UK, all English-speaking common-law countries, and many came back. Russians in 2022 went first to visa-free Russian-speaking neighbours (Armenia about 110,000, Kazakhstan 80,000, Georgia 73,500) and to Israel (80,000), and the rich to Dubai. After Brexit, only about 7,000 finance jobs left London (EY, against 12,500 announced), to Paris, Frankfurt and Dublin in the same time zone.
8. **Factories score differently from money.** On the scorecard (Table 3), Southeast Asia and India tie with the US for new plants (6.2 to 6.5) because of labour cost, energy (Indonesia $17.9 per MMBtu against the US $22.3, Brazil $32.5, UK $42.0) and supply chains. For financial capital, people and the wealthy, Switzerland, the US, the UK, Canada and Singapore lead, and Latin America and SEA-4 trail.
9. **The owner's regional reasons are partly supported and partly too broad.** Supported: Russia's rule of law fell the most of any country in 2025 (-4.9%, 0.41), Central Asia took only $5B of FDI, Nigeria ranks among ACLED's extreme conflicts, and the Gulf was attacked in 2026. Too broad: Morocco is outside ACLED's major conflict zones, Rwanda (0.63) and Botswana (0.60) outrank Brazil (0.50) and Mexico (0.40) on rule of law, Africa's 2025 FDI was its third highest in 25 years, Kazakhstan (0.54) equals Argentina, the UAE (0.64) outranks every Latin American country except Uruguay (0.72), Costa Rica (0.68) and Chile (0.66), and Gulf money is flowing into Europe (ADNOC and Covestro, MGX's $43B AI campus in France).
10. **The US is the biggest magnet but not a frictionless one.** The US is the largest FDI host ($277B in 2025) and announced greenfield value rose 30%. But its WJP score fell 2.8% in 2025 (0.68, rank 27, behind Singapore, the UK and Canada), international student inflows fell 12% (OECD), and Henley gives it the lowest score in its 2026 list. Money flows to the US because of depth, not comfort.

### The pattern
1. **Money goes to the deepest safe market, not the fastest growing one.** In 1914 to 1950 the fastest growing regions were not where Europe's money went. It went to the US and Switzerland. In 2010 to 2012 it went to Bunds, francs and Treasuries. In 2016 to 2025 UK listings went to New York, not Singapore or Hong Kong.
2. **People go where the language, law and family networks already are.** Spaniards to Argentina and Cuba, Italians to Argentina and Brazil, Hong Kongers to Canada and Australia, Russians to Armenia and Kazakhstan.
3. **Access decides the first hop.** When the US closed its door in the 1920s, Europeans went south. Russians went where no visa was needed. The rich went where banks still worked (Dubai).
4. **Distance matters for people and regulated activity, not for portfolio money.** Brexit moves stayed in the same time zone. Portfolio money crossed the Atlantic at no cost.
5. **Many return.** About half a million Hong Kongers went back. Emigration is often a hedge.
6. **Latin America has absorbed Europe's people before, but it did not keep their capital.** Argentina took millions of Europeans when it was rich, then its institutions failed in the 20th century [memory]. The owner's thesis has historical precedent for people, much less for money.

### Verdict with probabilities (judgment, H1, conditional on "US holds, EU declines")
- North America receives the largest share of Europe's net outbound portfolio capital: **90**.
- North America receives 50% or more of all Europe's net outbound capital (portfolio plus FDI): **70**.
- All of Asia (including Japan and China) receives more than North America: **5**.
- Latin America receives more than Southeast Asia (including Singapore) in European FDI: **35**. ## 9. Anomalies

1. **Europe got more FDI, not less, in 2025 (+39% to $285B)** while the EU itself fell 29%. The UK ($75B) and Germany ($74B) rose sharply, and some of it was Gulf money (ADNOC-Covestro, MGX in France). A "falling" Europe is still an investment destination for the Gulf. Default expectation for a declining region would be falling inflows.
2. **Europe's US holdings kept rising through the 2025 "sell America" episode and the 2026 Hormuz shock** (euro area US equities $5.17T in March 2026 to $6.02T in May 2026). The default move in a US policy shock would be repatriation. It did not happen at scale.
3. **Spain is Mexico's second largest investor**, ahead of Canada and Japan, despite the US being next door. The Iberian channel to Latin America is real.
4. **Russians did not go to the best economies in 2022, but to the first open door** (Armenia, Kazakhstan, Georgia, Serbia, Dubai). Access beats quality on the first move.
5. **Henley scores the US lowest of its listed jurisdictions (62.3) while the US is the second largest millionaire magnet (+7,500).** The rich go to the US for markets and children's education, not for the tax or residency regime.
6. **The UK lost few jobs after Brexit (7,000) but many millionaires after the non-dom change (-16,500).** Tax moves the wealthy faster than regulation moves firms.

## 10. Mechanism chains

1. **Capital.** EU growth and returns lag → domestic equities and bonds offer less → pension funds, insurers and households raise foreign allocations → the deepest liquid market is the US (equities above twice GDP, Treasuries the world's collateral) → US assets and the dollar are bid, the euro weakens → S1 dollar extension is reinforced. Test: TIC euro area holdings (monthly), ECB balance of payments portfolio outflows, EUR/USD. Kill: euro area US equity holdings fall below $5T for six months with no price fall to explain it.
2. **Factories.** EU energy at about twice US cost (BNEF: Germany $226 vs US $110 per MWh) plus US tariffs → European producers serving the US build in the US (tariff-jumping), those serving Asia build in Southeast Asia or India, those serving Europe move to its periphery → Mexico benefits only if USMCA survives its review. Test: UNCTAD greenfield announcements by source and destination (WIR 2027), Mexico greenfield, the USMCA review outcome.
3. **People.** EU wages and housing stagnate → skilled people move first within Europe (Switzerland, Austria, Spain, the UK) and to English-speaking countries if visas allow → Latin America attracts mostly heritage and Spanish-speaking movers → Asia takes a niche (Singapore, Dubai). Test: Destatis emigration by destination (annual, mid-year), Istat and AIRE, OECD International Migration Outlook (November).
4. **Wealth.** Tax changes (UK non-dom end, German and French wealth-tax debates) → the wealthy move to flat-tax regimes nearby (Italy, Switzerland, Portugal, Greece, Monaco) or to the Gulf and Singapore → the 2026 war shifts some Gulf-based wealth toward Southern Europe, Switzerland and Singapore. Test: Henley 2027, Dubai Land Department transactions, Swiss and Italian flat-tax registrations.
5. **Latin America's ceiling.** Rule of law and violence cap inflows → only Chile, Uruguay, Costa Rica and Panama (and Mexico's US-facing industrial north) clear the bar → Latin America stays under 10% of European outbound capital unless security and courts improve. Test: WJP 2026 (Mexico, Brazil), ACLED December 2026, Argentina on the MSCI watchlist (June 2027).

## 16. Proposed changes

**Unconditional scenario odds: no change.** This lane's evidence (Europe's capital deepening in US assets, no move toward Asian or Latin American markets) is already in the S1 baseline. H0 62/15/1/22, H1 44/23/3/30, H2 31/28/10/31 stand.

**Conditional rule to add (for the desk to apply if a France-led euro crisis happens by 2028):** a euro crisis removes the only other deep reserve market, so money goes first to Bunds and francs, then to Treasuries and gold. Apply for H1: S1 +3, S2 +2, S4 -5, giving 47/25/3/25. Reason: flight to the dollar and gold, as in 2010 to 2012 and 1938 to 1939, makes a US-led disorderly reset less likely in the following years while a neutral layer gains.

**Other proposals.**
1. Correct the desk's attribution: "about EUR 300B a year" is the Commission's SIU figure, not a number in the Draghi report text.

4. Add tripwires: TIC euro area holdings (monthly), Henley and MSCI (June 2027), UNCTAD WIR 2027 (July 2027), USMCA review, EU-Mercosur ratification.
