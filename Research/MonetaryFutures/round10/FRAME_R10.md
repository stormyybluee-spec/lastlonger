## Hole 1. Where do Gulf oil dollars go now? (CLAIMS_R9 C2 with C1)

### 1.1 Question types
| # | Question | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Q1a | Did Gulf states agree in 1974 to price oil in dollars and buy Treasuries, and on what terms? | Event or intent, with a belief audit |
| Q1b | Where does Gulf crude go now, by buyer? | Mechanism (flows) |
| Q1c | What share of Gulf oil sales is settled in yuan? | Mechanism, belief audit |
| Q1d | Where do the dollar proceeds go: reserves, Treasuries, US shares, other assets? | Mechanism (balance sheets) |
| Q1e | Was a shift from Treasuries to shares agreed with anyone? | Event or intent (coordination) |
| Q1f | What does this mean for the live question: is oil becoming a settlement asset in its own right, or still a dollar earner? | Forecast |

### 1.2 Hidden hypotheses (every assumption in the wording)
- H1.1 There was one agreement in 1974, and it covered all the Gulf states, not Saudi Arabia alone.
- H1.2 The agreement bound the Gulf to price oil in dollars. (Pricing and settlement are different things. Oil benchmarks can be quoted in dollars and paid in another currency.)
- H1.3 The agreement bound the Gulf to buy Treasuries, and in a set amount or share.
- H1.4 The agreement still exists, or has ended, at a date someone could name.
- H1.5 "Gulf oil" is one pool. (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain and Iraq have different buyers, different currencies and different funds.)
- H1.6 "Mostly to China" means more than half of Gulf crude exports by volume. (It could mean the largest single buyer, or Asia as a whole.)
- H1.7 "About 30%" is a share of something measurable: barrels, value, contracts, or all Gulf trade. And it is a Gulf figure, not a global or Chinese one.
- H1.8 Yuan settlement means the Gulf keeps yuan. (It could be converted to dollars at once.)
- H1.9 "Gulf sovereign funds" and the central banks are the same balance sheet. (The central banks hold the peg reserves. The funds hold the long-term assets.)
- H1.10 "Instead of" means a substitution: shares rose because Treasuries fell.
- H1.11 The oil money flows straight into US assets. (It also funds domestic budgets, projects and imports.)
- H1.12 Treasury country tables show Gulf holdings fully. (Custody bias, PLAN 8.4.)

### 1.3 Answer spec
An expert answer must contain:
1. The 1974 to 1975 record: what was agreed, by whom, in writing or not, and what is documented (FRUS, Treasury, the 2016 release of Saudi holdings).
2. Gulf crude and product exports by buyer for the latest year and for one earlier regime (2014, 2019 and the latest year), by volume and by value, per country.
3. Yuan settlement: every documented yuan oil cargo or contract, swap line usage, CIPS and mBridge evidence, and the size bound that accounting allows.
4. Each Gulf state's dollar uses on one page: central bank reserves (peg), sovereign fund assets by type, government spending, and US holdings by type (Treasuries, agencies, equities, corporate debt) from TIC country tables, the TIC annual survey and 13F.
5. A gross vs net split: Gulf dollar inflows from oil vs outflows to imports, budgets and investment abroad.
6. The peg constraint: the dollar pegs need dollar reserves, so any yuan share is bounded by how much the peg tolerates.
7. Rival readings filled in, the coordination level stated, and dated tests.
Blind spots: pricing vs settlement, stocks vs flows, central bank vs fund, Saudi vs UAE vs Qatar vs Kuwait, country of holder vs country of custodian, crude vs refined products, 2014 oil-price regime vs 2022 to 2026, Treasuries vs total US assets.

## Hole 2. Does the "three kinds of country" lens predict anything? (CLAIMS_R9 B3)

### 2.1 Question types
| # | Question | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Q2a | Can countries be sorted into the three kinds by rules written in advance, so two readers put each country in the same box? | Mechanism (classification) |
| Q2b | Does the box predict who holds the government's debt? | Forecast (out of sample) |
| Q2c | Does the box predict behaviour in a crisis? | Forecast, tested on past crises |
| Q2d | Does it predict anything that simpler lenses (net creditor or debtor, capital controls, regime type) do not? | Belief audit |

### 2.2 Hidden hypotheses
- H2.1 Three kinds is enough. Every country fits one box. (Japan, Switzerland, Korea, India, Russia and Brazil may not fit.)
- H2.2 The boxes are set by who the state answers to. That is an intent claim, so it needs measurable proxies.
- H2.3 "Capital above the state" covers the US, UK and Europe alike. (The euro area has no single fiscal state. Norway is in Europe but sits in box two.)
- H2.4 A sovereign fund is the mark of box two. (The US states and many others have funds. China has funds too.)
- H2.5 "The state above capital" in China means state banks hold the debt and the state directs crisis responses.
- H2.6 The lens predicts, rather than only describes. A lens that fits after the fact is not a test.
- H2.7 The box is stable over time. (The UK in 1975, or China before 1978, could sit in other boxes.)

### 2.3 Answer spec
1. Written rules for each box, using three or four public proxies: state ownership share of the largest firms, sovereign fund assets as a share of GDP, a capital-controls index, and net international investment position.
2. A list of 15 to 20 countries sorted by those rules before any debt or crisis data is pulled. Pre-register the sort.
3. Predictions written down per box: debt holder mix (domestic banks, central bank, domestic nonbanks, foreign official, foreign private), and crisis behaviour (bailout of private capital, fund drawdown or domestic market support, state-directed bank lending and capital controls).
4. The test: holder mix from the IMF sovereign investor base data and national sources. Crisis behaviour in 2008, 2015 (China stock crash), 2020 and 2022 (UK gilt episode and oil-price swing).
5. A comparison with a simpler lens: does net creditor or debtor plus capital openness predict as well? If yes, the three kinds add no information.
6. Misfits listed, not hidden.
Blind spots: debt held at home vs abroad, central bank vs commercial bank holders, official vs private foreign holders, democracies vs not, oil exporters vs others, the box on paper vs practice in a crisis.

### 3.2 Hidden hypotheses

- H3.2 "Funds" means holds the stock. (The marginal buyer and the repo lender who finances the holder also fund the market.)
- H3.3 Foreigners are the main funders. (Domestic holders, the Fed, banks, money funds and pensions may be larger.)

- H3.5 The Fed study exists and says what the specimen says. Its date and scope matter.
- H3.6 The carry trade is large in dollars and goes into Treasuries rather than shares or credit.
- H3.7 BoJ hikes end the carry trade. (The yen rate gap and hedging costs, not the BoJ rate alone, set the trade.)
- H3.8 "Offshore dollars" are one number. BIS measures several: dollar credit to nonbanks outside the US, cross-border claims, and off-balance-sheet dollar debt in FX swaps.
- H3.9 "Money supply" means M2. (Against M1 or the monetary base the ratio changes a lot.)

6. Auction data: Treasury investor class allotments for coupons and bills.
Blind spots: stock vs marginal flow, holder vs financier, custody country vs owner, official vs private, bills vs coupons, gross vs net (a hedge fund long cash and short futures), the 2020 dash for cash and the 2022 to 2025 QT regime.

## Hole 4. Test of the method: "aligned behind the scenes" and "pushed trade toward China on purpose" (CLAIMS_R9 C3 and C4)

### 4.1 Question types
| # | Question | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Q4a | Are the US, the Gulf states and China aligned behind the scenes while the public sees a march to war? | Event or intent. Untestable as stated (U). Must be rewritten |
| Q4b | Did trade shift toward China after the 2025 tariffs? | Mechanism (flows) |
| Q4c | Who gained from the shift? | Mechanism |
| Q4d | Was the shift the aim of finance or of any named office? | Event or intent |
| Q4e | What coordination level can the desk show for each? | Belief audit of the method itself |

### 4.2 Hidden hypotheses
- H4.1 "The US", "the Gulf states", "China" and "finance" are actors. Under the actor rules they are not. Each must become a named office or entity with a verb (Treasury, the White House, SAMA, PIF, PBoC, named banks or asset managers).
- H4.2 "Aligned" means agreed (level 2), not merely interested in the same outcome (level 0).
- H4.3 "Behind the scenes" means a secret channel exists.
- H4.4 The public story of rising conflict is false, or staged. This is close to the inversion form. If no observation could contradict it, it is grade IX and logged only.
- H4.5 "Finance wants a multipolar world" treats a bloc as one mind.
- H4.6 The tariffs pushed trade toward China. (The direct effect of tariffs on China pushes trade away from China. The claim must mean third-country trade, or China's exports rerouted.)
- H4.7 "On purpose" means the outcome was the aim, not a side effect.
- H4.8 Benefit shows cause. Guarded in PLAN 2C.4: gain alone is a motive, grade I4 at most.

### 4.3 Answer spec
1. Each claim rewritten into testable parts with named actors and verbs, and each part given a checkability class.
2. For C3: the incentive of each named actor on paper (level 0), any focal cue (level 1), and for level 2 the channel, the enforcement and the secrecy load in numbers (how many people, how many years).
3. For C4: trade data before and after the 2025 tariffs (China's exports by destination, US imports by origin, transshipment marks), who gained (shares of trade, firm results), and the record of intent (statements, lobbying, FARA, minutes).
4. The rival readings table with evidence rows and cells marked.
5. A base rate: how often claims of this class were later documented (engine/BASE_RATES.md, verified before use).
6. What the desk cannot show, stated plainly.
Blind spots: stated policy vs practice, level 0 vs level 2, events before vs after the desk's memory (fresh sources only for C5), trade by value vs volume, direct vs rerouted trade.

### 4.5 Rival readings
| Reading | What it says | What separates it |
|---|---|---|
| Official | Tariffs aimed at reshoring and security, trade diversion was a side effect | Statements and orders before the tariffs say so, the diversion was forecast and opposed |
| Coordination | Named offices or firms agreed the outcome | A documented channel, positions taken just before announcements, matching moves by the Gulf and China timed with the US |
| Shared incentives | Each actor followed its own gain and the pattern looks planned | Gainers lobbied against the tariffs, moves were gradual, no timing edge |
| Accident or measurement | Rerouting through third countries makes China's trade look like it shifted when only labels changed | Mirror gaps widen (third country imports from China rise while their exports to the US rise by the same goods) |
Evidence that fits all four, such as "someone gained", is marked worthless.

### 4.6 Coordination level

To show level 2 it would need a named channel, an enforcement and a secrecy load small enough to be believable. The method's pass mark for this hole is stating the level that can be shown, with the gap to the claimed level written out.
