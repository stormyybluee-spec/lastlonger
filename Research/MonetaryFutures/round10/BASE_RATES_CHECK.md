## Grimes (2016, PLOS ONE): what it modelled and criticisms

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Paper | D. R. Grimes, "On the Viability of Conspiratorial Beliefs", PLOS ONE 11(1): e0147905, published 26 January 2016 (received 23 Sep 2015, accepted 6 Jan 2016). Opened via PMC4728076 |
| Model | Exposure is a Poisson process. The chance of at least one fatal leak by time t is L = 1 minus exp(minus t times phi). The per-person leak chance per year is p. With N people it becomes L = 1 minus exp(minus t(1 minus (1 minus p) to the power N)). Three headcount patterns: constant, single event with Gompertzian decay, and exponential decay |
| How p was set | From three cases that did leak, using best-case choices for the plotters: NSA PRISM (up to 30,000 people, about 6 years, p of 4.09 times 10 to the minus 6), Tuskegee (up to 6,700, about 25 years, p of 4.20 times 10 to the minus 6) and the FBI forensics scandal (up to 500, about 6 years, p of 2.45 times 10 to the minus 4). The paper uses 4.09 times 10 to the minus 6 |
| Key results | Time to a 95 percent chance of exposure: moon landing hoax 3.68 years (about 411,000 people), climate hoax 26.77 years if scientists only or 3.70 years with scientific bodies (about 405,000), vaccine conspiracy 34.78 years if CDC and WHO only or 3.15 years with drug firms (about 22,000), suppressed cancer cure 3.17 years (about 714,000). Largest group that keeps exposure under 5 percent: about 2,500 for 5 years, 1,257 for 10 years, 502 for 25 years, 125 for 100 years. The paper's own text and table differ slightly on the 5 year figure (2,521 versus 2,531) |
| Correction | PLOS ONE correction of 1 March 2016 (11(3): e0151003). The original equation assumed a constant rate. The fix uses a time-varying rate. It changes Figures 1 and 4 and matters when headcount changes. The correction text adds that for a single event with Gompertzian decay, odds of failure exceed 5 percent within 10 years at about 1,328 participants. The author says the conclusions for a constant headcount stand |
| Stated limits | Only leaks from inside are modelled and outside investigation is ignored. Any one leak ends the plot. Leaks are independent. p is constant over time and across plots. Headcounts are rough upper bounds. p rests on three cases. The paper notes that a maths argument will not move committed believers |
| Criticisms | (1) Kurtis Hagen, Episteme (online 17 Feb 2022, vol 20 issue 2, 2023), argues the argument does not show that conspiracies tend to fail and that the claim is poorly supported. (2) A Little Atoms blog post (informal) says the headcounts are guesswork (the NSA figure of 30,000 is its example) and that the equation error got past peer review. (3) My own reading of the method, not a source: p comes only from three plots that were exposed, so it is biased toward leaking and the model cannot count plots that held. This is the same selection issue this file is built to correct |
| Source URLs | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4728076 (2016-01-26). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4773114 (2016-03-01). https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/episteme/article/do-conspiracies-tend-to-fail-philosophical-reflections-on-a-poorly-supported-academic-meme/27CE07DBBE72333A6736E3D2D5E45EEC (2022-02-17). Snippet only: https://littleatoms.com/article/maths-paper-disproving-conspiracy-theories-dont-add |
| Fit with this list | Row 8 (weeks to months, a handful of insiders) and row 9 (about 130,000 workers, 3 years, with Soviet spies inside) are consistent with fast leakage. Rows 1 to 5 (decades) are the cases the model treats as unlikely. Use it as a rough upper curve for leaks from inside, not as a prior for how long a structure stays hidden |

### C. Replacement text for the Grimes bullet in section C

A model worth using with care: Grimes (PLOS ONE, 26 January 2016, corrected 1 March 2016) treats leaks as a random process with a fixed per-person yearly leak chance estimated from three plots that did leak (NSA PRISM, Tuskegee, FBI forensics). It finds, for example, that a plot with about 2,500 people has a 5 percent chance of exposure within 5 years, and that the moon landing, climate and cancer cure "plots" would fail within about 3 to 4 years. Criticisms: Hagen (Episteme, 2022) says it does not show that conspiracies tend to fail, headcounts are rough, only inside leaks are modelled, any one leak is assumed fatal, and p comes only from plots that were exposed. Use it as a rough curve for inside leaks, not as a prior.
