# EXAM T9: The 1974 Saudi Treasury deal. Was it real, and has it ended?

Examinee answer. Written 2026-11-12. Unseen mode on (question about a secret deal). Sources were fetched fresh for this file.

## Headline (Level 1)

Yes, there was a secret 1974 arrangement, but it was narrower than the legend. The US Treasury gave Saudi Arabia a private way to buy Treasuries outside the public auctions (the "add-on" facility) and promised to hide Saudi holdings inside a regional "oil exporters" total. A US government audit (GAO, 1979) documents both. The secrecy part ended in May 2016, when Treasury began publishing Saudi holdings. The special purchase window stopped being special in 1975, when it was opened to all central banks. Nothing in the record shows a written "petrodollar" treaty that expired in June 2024. That viral claim is false. Saudi Arabia still holds about $141 billion of Treasuries (August 2026), which is about 1.5% of all foreign holdings.

## 1. Frame

| Question | Type |
|---|---|
| Was there a secret deal in 1974? | Event or intent |
| What did it oblige Saudi Arabia to do? | Mechanism |
| Has it ended? | Event plus belief audit (the June 2024 "expiry" story) |

Hidden hypotheses to falsify:
- H1. A deal existed. 
- H2. It was a written, binding treaty with an end date.
- H3. It required oil to be priced in dollars.
- H4. It required Saudi Arabia to put its oil money into Treasuries.
- H5. It ended (in 2016, 2024 or some other date).
- H6. Gulf oil money is still recycled into Treasuries on a large scale.

Answer spec: the documented terms, the secret parts, the date each part ended, the size then and now, the rival readings, and dated tests.

Blind spots: law on paper vs practice, stocks vs flows, Saudi central bank (SAMA) vs the sovereign fund (PIF), custody country vs real owner, Saudi Arabia vs the wider Gulf.

Anomaly from the data: the popular story says the Gulf "funds the US deficit". The TIC table says Saudi Arabia holds $141.2 billion out of $9,331.7 billion foreign held (August 2026). That is 1.5%. Even in 1978 the GAO put all of OPEC at 1.6% of US government securities. The deal was big for its time in politics, small in the debt market.

## 2. The default story and who tells it

| Camp | Claim | Scope and blind spot |
|---|---|---|
| Popular (viral posts, June 2024) | A 50 year "petrodollar agreement" signed 8 June 1974 expired on 9 June 2024 and Saudi Arabia did not renew it | No document cited. Mixes up the public 1974 Joint Commission with the secret Treasury arrangement |
| Press history (Bloomberg, Andrea Wong, 30 May 2016) | Simon and Parsky agreed in July 1974 that Saudi Arabia would recycle oil money into Treasuries in exchange for US oil purchases and military aid. King Faisal demanded secrecy | Based on National Archives cables and a FOIA request. Strong on secrecy. Says little about whether it was binding or how it ended |
| Fact checkers and bank economists (Asia Fact Check Lab, July 2024. UBS Paul Donovan, June 2024) | No oil pricing agreement expired. The 1974 commission was about economic cooperation | Good on the 2024 hoax. Not a full account of the add-on facility |
| Official (GAO 1979, Treasury) | GAO: Treasury made "special commitments of financial confidentiality to Saudi Arabia and perhaps other OPEC governments in exchange for their purchases". Treasury at the time denied special treatment | GAO is an audit, not the deal text. Treasury's denial is the interested party |
| Academic (David Spiro, The Hidden Hand of American Hegemony, Cornell 1999) | Secret deals steered Saudi surpluses into Treasuries and helped fund US deficits | A book with an argument. Its strongest claims are about motive |
| Gulf view (Gulf News, J. Kechichian, 2018) | Nothing sinister. Riyadh wanted a safe asset and US security | Friendly to Riyadh |

Default move for a 1974 surplus country: put dollars in US bank deposits and Treasuries anyway, because that was the deepest dollar market. Keep this in mind. It is the main rival to "a deal made them do it".

## 3. Actors

| Actor (named office) | Wanted | Could do | Did (documented) |
|---|---|---|---|
| US Treasury (Secretary William Simon, aide Gerald Parsky) | Recycle oil surpluses into US assets, steady the dollar, fund deficits, keep Saudi money from destabilising markets | Sell debt in any form the 1917 Second Liberty Bond Act allows. Decide how TIC data are published | Created the add-on facility in 1974 as "part of a bilateral financial package to Saudi Arabia" (GAO 1979). Reported Saudi holdings only inside a regional total until 2016 |
| Saudi Arabian Monetary Agency (now Saudi Central Bank, SAMA) | Safe, liquid home for surplus dollars. No public link to funding the US while the US backed Israel | Choose currency, issuer and custodian | Bought through add-ons. Asked for confidentiality |
| Federal Reserve Bank of New York | Run the plumbing | Act as agent for foreign central banks | Acted as intermediary for add-ons (GAO 1979) |
| US State Department (Henry Kissinger) | Pull Riyadh back toward Washington after the 1973 embargo | Sign public agreements | Signed the Joint Commission on Economic Cooperation and a military commission with Prince Fahd, 8 June 1974 (public, in the treaty record) |
| Congress and GAO | Know who owns US debt | Audit, hold hearings | GAO 1979 found "no statistical or legal basis" for hiding OPEC country data |

Ledger walk (one add-on purchase in 1975): SAMA's dollar deposit at the New York Fed falls, its Treasury holding rises. The Treasury General Account rises, then is spent. The public auction is unchanged in size because add-ons were "in excess of the announced amount". Public money downstairs only moves. The new fact is that Saudi demand did not show up in the auction. So the add-on hid the buyer, not the money.

Precedent: the stress test of 2020 is the clearest. When oil money dried up, SAMA sold Treasuries fast. A deal that obliged Saudi Arabia to hold them would have shown resistance there. None is visible.

## 5. Mechanism map

| Channel | Sign for Saudi Treasury holdings | Size, latest | Size, stress | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oil surplus kept as SAMA dollar reserves | + | Reserve assets about $495 billion, June 2026 | Reserves fell sharply in 2015 to 2016 and 2020 | SAMA monthly data via Arab News, Jun 2026 |
| Choice of Treasuries within reserves | + | Treasuries about 28% of reserve assets (141.2 / 494.6) | | TIC Aug 2026, SAMA Jun 2026 |
| Transfers from SAMA to PIF (shares, projects, not Treasuries) | minus | Named by analysts for the 2023 low | | Arab News, Oct 2025 (analyst view, T4) |
| Budget deficits funded by drawing reserves | minus | | About minus $59 billion Feb to Apr 2020 | TIC |
| Custody outside the US (Belgium, Luxembourg, UK, Caymans) | Hides, does not change real holdings | Unknown | | TIC notes say custody data "may not be attributed to the actual owners" |
| Secrecy promise (1974 to 2016) | Hid the number, did not set it | Ended May 2016 | | GAO 1979, TIC 2016 |
| Add-on window | Made buying easy and quiet | Special treatment ended 1975, capped 2001 | Oct 1976 debt limit scare | GAO 1979, TreasuryDirect |

Chain: oil surplus, then SAMA reserves, then a share in Treasuries. The deal touched two links (how SAMA bought and whether the public could see it). It never set the size of the surplus or the share. Those are set by oil prices, the riyal peg to the dollar (which needs dollar reserves), and SAMA's investment choices.

Three frames:
- Official and raw data agree: holdings are modest and move with oil and budget needs.
- Critical school (Spiro, hegemony view): the deal mattered because it locked Saudi money into the dollar system at a fragile moment, 1974 to 1979, and bought political loyalty. The data do not refute this for the 1970s. It is a claim about motive and timing, not size today.
- Hard money view: the "petrodollar" props up dollar demand. Partly right. The peg and dollar oil pricing do create dollar demand. But that rests on market depth and the peg, not on a 1974 contract.

## 7. Attack, grades and rival readings

### Documented and inferred links

| Link | Grade | Basis | Probability |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1. In 1974 Treasury gave Saudi Arabia a special, non-auction way to buy Treasuries | I0 | GAO 1979 states it directly | 97% |
| L2. Treasury promised to hide Saudi holdings in regional data in exchange for purchases | I0 | GAO 1979: "special commitments of financial confidentiality ... in exchange for their purchases", plus TIC grouping 1974 to 2016. Treasury denied it in 1979 | 90% |
| L3. Secrecy was demanded by King Faisal, linked to US support for Israel | I0 (press, T2) | Bloomberg 2016 citing National Archives cables, not read directly | 80% |
| L4. The deal was a written binding treaty with a 50 year term | None. Contradicted | No document. The 8 June 1974 agreement is public and covers economic cooperation | Under 5% |
| L5. The deal required oil to be priced in dollars | I4 | No document found. Dollar pricing is explained by market practice. Saudi accepted sterling after 1974 (UBS) | 15% |
| L6. Saudi Arabia was obliged to keep a set amount in Treasuries | I4 | No document. The 2020 sell-off contradicts a hard floor | 15% |
| L7. The secrecy part ended in May 2016 | I0 | Treasury published Saudi holdings 16 May 2016 | 99% |
| L8. The special purchase part ended in 1975, and add-ons stopped being an open window by 2001 | I0 for the 1975 widening and the 2001 cap. I2 that this ended any Saudi privilege | GAO 1979, TreasuryDirect timeline | 75% |
| L9. Some broader understanding (US security for Saudi dollar loyalty) still operates | I2 | Incentives and continued dollar peg, dollar oil sales, large US arms sales and investment pledges. No document of a deal | 55% |
| L10. Real Saudi state holdings are much higher than TIC shows (via offshore custody or PIF) | I4 | A former official told Bloomberg it may be "double or more". No footprint checked | 30% |

Chain for the strong popular claim "a secret 50 year treaty forced Saudi oil money into Treasuries and it expired in 2024": L4 times L6 times L5 is below 1%. Published as false.

Chain for the documented claim "a secret 1974 Treasury arrangement gave Saudi Arabia a quiet buying window and hidden data, now ended": L1 times L2 times L7 is about 87%. Published as a finding.

### Rival readings for why Saudi money went into Treasuries

| Evidence | Official (diversified, safe asset) | Coordination (a deal made them) | Shared incentives (both wanted it, no deal needed) | Error or measurement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GAO: add-on and secrecy package 1974 | Neutral | Consistent | Consistent | Neutral |
| Add-on opened to all central banks 1975 | Consistent | Inconsistent with a unique deal | Consistent | Neutral |
| OPEC only 1.6% of US securities in 1978 | Consistent | Neutral | Consistent | Consistent |
| Fast sale of about $59 billion in early 2020 | Consistent | Inconsistent with a holding obligation | Consistent | Neutral |
| Holdings only 28% of reserves, below the usual central bank dollar share | Consistent | Inconsistent | Consistent | Consistent (custody abroad) |
| Secrecy kept 41 years | Inconsistent | Consistent | Neutral | Neutral |

Least inconsistent: shared incentives, with a documented coordination layer on the secrecy and the purchase method. That is the answer.

Coordination level: level 2 for 1974 to 2016 on secrecy and purchase plumbing (channel shown: Treasury, New York Fed, SAMA. Enforcement: Saudi money would not come without the pledge. Secrecy load: a handful of Treasury and Fed officials for 41 years, with GAO and Congress partly aware). Level 0 to 1 for the wider "petrodollar" alignment today.

### Red team, short

- Treasury debt manager: add-ons were a plumbing tool later used mainly by Germany and Japan. Fixed: stated in the table.
- Economic historian: the public June 1974 commissions and the secret Treasury package are two different things. Fixed: kept apart.
- Hard money critic: dollar oil pricing still props up the dollar. Answered: true in practice, not because of a 1974 contract (L5).
- MMT critic: the US never needed Saudi money to fund itself. Answered: the scale data agree (1.6% then, 1.5% now). The 1970s motive was more about dollar stability and politics.
- Ordinary reader: "so the deal ended in 2024?" Answered in the headline: no, the hidden data part ended in 2016 and nothing expired in 2024.
- Word audit: "small" is backed by 1.5% and 1.6%. "Fast" is backed by $59 billion in two months.

## 8. What the data shows now

- Saudi Arabia: $141.2 billion of Treasuries, August 2026 (TIC table 5). Range in the last two years: $126.4 billion (Feb 2025) to $160.4 billion (Feb 2026).
- UAE: $116.1 billion, August 2026. Kuwait no longer shows in the major holders table.
- Total foreign holdings: $9,331.7 billion, of which foreign official $3,755.6 billion.
- Saudi share of foreign holdings: 1.5%.
- SAMA reserve assets: about $495 billion, June 2026. Foreign securities were 56.6% of reserve assets in March 2026. Treasuries visible in TIC are about 28% of reserves.
- Saudi holdings peaked near $184 billion in early 2020 and have not regained that level.

## 9. What is unknown

- The full text of the July 1974 Simon package. Bloomberg quoted cables, but this desk did not read them directly. FRUS has the 1973 groundwork, not the add-on terms.
- How much Saudi state money sits in Treasuries through custodians in Belgium, Luxembourg, the UK or the Caymans, or through PIF. TIC cannot tell.
- Whether any unwritten security-for-dollars understanding exists today. No document either way. Graded I2.
- What share of Saudi oil is now settled in yuan. No official figure found.

What would change the answer: release of 1974 cables showing a holding floor or a dollar pricing clause (would raise L5 or L6). A Treasury or SAMA statement of yuan settlement volumes. A sharp fall in Saudi TIC holdings matched by no rise in offshore custody.
