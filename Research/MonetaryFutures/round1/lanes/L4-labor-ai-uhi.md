## 1. Key findings (short version)

1. **Hypothesis partly holds. The real split is not between working and not working. It is between owning and not owning.** Workers and UHI recipients both live on income streams. Asset owners live on the capital share, which is now at a record. The US nonfarm business labor share was 52.8% in Q2 2026, the lowest since the series began in 1947. Unit profits of nonfinancial corporations rose at a 43.0% annualized rate in the same quarter (BLS, 2026-09-03, T0). Unemployment is flat at 4.2% (BLS, 2026-10-02, T0). So capital is taking a bigger share of income without mass layoffs. The three tiers collapse into two classes (owners and non-owners). Most real people will be hybrids: some wage, some transfer, a small equity stake.

2. **A statutory UHI would NOT, under current US law, cut recipients off from credit.** ECOA bans credit discrimination because income comes from public assistance (15 USC 1691(a)(2), T1). Fannie Mae already counts public assistance income for mortgages if it is expected to continue 3 years. It counts own-account Social Security without even checking continuance (Fannie Selling Guide B3-3.4-12 and B3-3.4-15, T1). The "permanent underclass via credit exclusion" claim is **Refuted for a statutory, multi-year UHI**. It is **Plausible for a UHI that is appropriated each year at a political amount**. The Alaska dividend is that kind: the legislature set $1,000 in 2026, while the governor had proposed about $3,600.

3. **The real underclass mechanism is asset-price access, not credit access.** If UHI is paid as non-pledgeable cash (the Social Security model, 42 USC 407, T1), recipients can borrow against it as income. They cannot sell or pledge it as collateral. Their borrowing power is capped by debt-to-income rules. Illustration (inference): $2,500 a month of UHI supports roughly a $178k mortgage at 6.3%. Meanwhile asset prices compound with the capital share. The gap widens even with perfect credit access. The fix that matches the mechanism is to pay part of the transfer in **ownership** (equity, fund units), not only in cash. Trump Accounts (US equity index only, launched 2026-07-04) and the Alaska Permanent Fund are the live precedents.

4. **What a worker gets paid for substitutable tasks will be capped by the robot's hourly cost.** Miso's Flippy rents at about $5,000 a month (Fortune, 2026-02-26). At 16 hours a day that is about $10 per robot-hour, and the company claims about twice a cook's output. Today a humanoid at Musk's $20k to $30k target cost works out to roughly $2 to $5 per hour over its life (my arithmetic, section 4). US law requires minimum wages to be paid "in cash or negotiable instrument payable at par" (29 CFR 531.27, T1). Compute credits or energy allotments can therefore only be extras, not the wage floor. The evidence-backed alternatives to wages are equity compensation (real, concentrated in tech) and a skill premium in AI-complemented roles. In the Stanford ADP data, employment in AI-complemented occupations is flat or rising, while in AI-automated occupations young-worker employment is down.

5. **Entry-level displacement is real but narrow so far.** Workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed occupations are about 19% below their less-exposed peers. This comes mainly from fewer hires, not layoffs (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, August 2026 update, T4 via secondary). Challenger counted 116,175 announced cuts citing AI through August 2026, about 22% of all announced cuts (T2 compiled). But total announced cuts through July were 41% BELOW 2025. AI is the leading stated reason inside a shrinking layoff pool. That points to relabeling and budget reallocation, not to a wave of layoffs.

6. **Robots are far from a labor substitute at macro scale before 2030.** Industrial robots: 603,307 installed in 2025 (+11%), 5 million operating worldwide, China 354,000 (59%), US 38,400 (IFR World Robotics 2026, released 2026-09-24, T0 measured). Humanoids: about 14,500 shipped worldwide in 2025, about 90% Chinese (T4, unaudited). Tesla started its Fremont Optimus line, designed for 20,000 a week. It says reaching that rate takes "at least a year", and external deliveries come "around the end of next year", meaning end-2027 (Q3 call, 2026-10-21, T2). The H1 window (2028 to 2035) is the realistic start of scale.

7. **Robots cannot make food cheap in proportion to the hype.** In the USDA ERS Food Dollar for 2024: farm share 11.8 cents, food services 38.6 cents, salaries and benefits 54.1 cents, property income (rent, profit, interest) 32.7 cents, output taxes 7.2 cents (T0). Energy was 4.3 cents in 2023. Labor is 59.1 cents of a restaurant dollar and 47.1 cents of a grocery dollar. Automation attacks the labor slice only, and it adds capital and energy cost back. Land rent, taxes, farm inputs and energy are untouched or rise. Realistic ceiling (inference): commodity restaurant prices could fall about 10 to 25% in real terms by H2, grocery prices about 5 to 12%. That is a strong squeeze on undifferentiated small operators. It does not make food free.

8. **Small F&B splits in two.** Commodity small operators (takeout, fry-and-serve, franchise-lite) lose to automated chains. Experience and "human-made" operators gain from Baumol dynamics: the relative price of human service rises when everything else gets cheaper. UHI-funded leisure time adds demand. Robot-as-a-service partly closes the capital gap for small operators. Sweetgreen's sale of its in-house robotics unit, Spyce, signals that automation is becoming rentable.

9. **"Permanent underclass" is a meme amplified by people with something to sell, with a real but slow economic core.** It started as a San Francisco tech in-joke on X ("You have [N] months to escape the permanent underclass"). Amodei's January 2026 essay ("unemployed or very-low-wage 'underclass'") and Senator Warren's 2026-06-03 post amplified it. Per Pulsar's 2026-10-12 study, it is now used to sell crypto and AI tools as "escape routes" (T4/T5). The serious version is Trammell and Patel's "Capital in the 22nd Century" (2025-12-29, T4): when capital substitutes for labor, "approximately everything will eventually belong to those who are wealthiest when the transition occurs." The record-low labor share fits that direction. It does not prove it. The labor share has been falling since around 2000, before generative AI.

10. **Musk's UHI is cash from the federal government, financed by the claim that output outruns money.** His 2026-04-17 post: "Universal HIGH INCOME via checks issued by the Federal government is the best way to deal with unemployment caused by AI." His companion claim: "you actually MUST issue dollars to people or there will be massive disinflation" (April 2026, T2 via secondary). This is a monetary claim: money-financed transfers absorb robot-driven deflation. It links directly to the L7 debt math. On 2026-09-29 at the White House he also said AI has not raised unemployment and there is "tremendous demand for people" (T2).

## 2. The tier model (core deliverable)

### 2.1 Reformulated hypothesis
The owner's three tiers are best read as **two axes**:
- Axis A: capital ownership (none, small via accounts or pensions, large)
- Axis B: income source (wage, transfer, capital income)

Tier 2 (UHI) and Tier 3 (workers) are both "income-stream people". The gap between them is smaller than the gap between both of them and Tier 1. The decisive policy variable is **whether the UHI is paid in cash (an income stream) or in ownership (a capital claim)**.

### 2.3 Subjective probabilities (inference, not evidence)
- Federal statutory UHI or a universal AI dividend enacted in the US: H0 below 3%, by 2035 about 15%, by 2050 about 40%.
- Worldwide humanoid fleet above 1 million units: by 2028 below 10%, by 2035 about 65%, by 2050 about 90%.
- US nonfarm labor share below 50%: by 2035 about 35%, by 2050 about 55%.
- UHI designed as non-pledgeable (Social Security model) IF enacted: about 70%. Partly paid in equity or fund units: about 35% (overlapping).

## 4. Robots: deployment and unit economics

**Measured deployment (T0):**
- IFR World Robotics 2026 (2026-09-24): 603,307 industrial robots installed in 2025 (+11%), 5 million operating, China 354,000 (59%, +20%), US 38,400 (+12%), Japan 36,200, Germany 24,842 (-8%). Forecast: 655,000 for 2026, 806,000 for 2029. https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally
- Prior year (WR 2025): 542,000 installed in 2024, stock 4.66M, China 295,000 (54%), Chinese suppliers 57% of their home market.

**Humanoids (T4, unaudited company claims):**
- 2025 shipments about 14,500 worldwide, about 90% China. Unitree claims more than 5,500. AgiBot about 5,100 to 5,168. Tesla, Figure and Agility about 150 each (metavert, Caixin 2026-01-24).
- Prices: Unitree R1 $5,900 (R1 Air $4,900), G1 $16,000, H1 $90,000. AgiBot entry $14,500. Agility Digit about $250,000.
- 2026 targets: Unitree 10,000 to 20,000 units, AgiBot 10,000+ (company goals).

**Tesla Optimus (T2, Q3 2026 call, 2026-10-21):**
- Early production at Fremont, "landed most equipment". Line designed for 20,000 a week (about 1M a year), reaching it "would likely take at least a year".
- External deliveries "around the end of next year" (end-2027). Initial uses: "industrial, dangerous, repetitive or around-the-clock work". Hands are the hardest part. Larger factory being built in Austin.
- No unit cost disclosed. Costs "trending toward" Cybercab cost. Musk's long-run target is $20k to $30k (repeated, T2). Third-party estimate of current cost: $50k to $100k (T5).
- Net income -43% to $787M, with Optimus and AI opex rising.
- Incentive: Musk's 2025 performance award includes a 1 million Optimus delivered milestone (memory, approved by shareholders November 2025, not re-verified this session).

**Unit economics (my arithmetic, inference):**
- Humanoid at $25,000, 5-year life, 10% a year maintenance, 0.5 kW average draw at $0.15/kWh, $200 a month software. That is about $10,200 a year. At 4,000 hours a year: **$2.55 per hour**. At 6,000 hours: **$1.70 per hour**. If it is half as productive as a human: about $3.40 to $5 per human-equivalent hour.
- At today's estimated $50k to $100k cost with lower uptime: about $6 to $15 per hour.
- Live data point: Flippy at about $5,000 a month (Fortune 2026-02-26). At 16 hours a day (about 480 hours a month) that is about $10.40 per hour. Miso claims twice a cook's output, so about $5 per human-equivalent hour. Compare California's fast-food minimum of $20 an hour (AB 1228, since April 2024, memory) and the federal minimum of $7.25.
- Conclusion: for fully substitutable tasks, the robot-hour cost becomes the wage ceiling once reliability is proven. Reliability, not price, is the binding constraint today. Miso had only 14 Flippy units deployed at end-2025 and $385k of revenue in 2024, despite "massive" demand claims (Fortune, T0 filings via secondary).

**Realistic timeline:**
- H0: tens of thousands of humanoids a year, mostly Chinese, mostly demos, research, education and light logistics. Tesla external sales start around end-2027.
- H1: plausible ramp to the millions a year if Tesla's line and Chinese makers hit rates. Even 10M humanoids would equal about 0.3% of the global labor force (about 3.6B, memory).
- H2: robots become a meaningful substitute in logistics, food prep, cleaning and agriculture harvesting.

## 6. Food: what automation can and cannot cut

**USDA ERS Food Dollar, 2024 (T0), https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-dollar/summary-findings**
- Farm share 11.8 cents (food at home 18.5, food away from home 7.1). Marketing 88.2 cents. Total food spending $2.58T.
- Industry group: food services 38.6 cents, farm production 6.7 cents, agribusiness 3.0 cents. Energy 4.3 cents (2023, ERS documentation via search).
- Primary factors: salaries and benefits 54.1 cents, property income 32.7 cents, output taxes 7.2 cents.
- Labor per dollar: food away from home 59.1 cents, food at home 47.1 cents. Foodservice establishments: 44.2 cents of labor out of 65.8 cents of output per restaurant dollar.

**What automation attacks (inference):**
- Only the labor slice, and only partly. Every dollar of labor saved comes back partly as robot capital, maintenance, software and energy. In my section 4 arithmetic, at maturity a robot costs maybe 20 to 50% of the labor it replaces.
- Restaurant (commodity QSR): suppose 50 to 70% of the 44.2 cents of foodservice labor is automated, with 30 to 50% of the savings eaten by capital. Net saving is about 11 to 22 cents, so the real price ceiling falls about 10 to 25%.
- Grocery: processing is already heavily automated. Retail, warehouse and farm-labor automation might save 5 to 12 cents.
- Untouched or rising: land and building rent (inside property income), output taxes (7.2), farm inputs (fertilizer is tied to natural gas), energy (AI data centers compete for power, see L3), water, regulation and food safety compliance.
- Ricardian capture: in prime locations, labor savings tend to be bid into commercial rent. Landlords (Tier 1) capture part of the gain.

**Restaurant automation evidence:**
- Sweetgreen Infinite Kitchen: more than 700 bps labor savings vs classic stores of similar age, 32 IK stores as of Q1 2026, about 500 orders an hour. Sold its Spyce robotics unit (company Q4 call, T2). FY2026 guide: same-store sales -2 to -4%. Automation did not save the brand from a demand slump.
- Miso Flippy: $5k a month, 14 units at end-2025 (T0 via Fortune).
- China: Xiaocaiyuan has cooking robots in about 200 to 660 stores (sources conflict). Home Original Chicken has robot kitchens in about 388 stores. Oak Deer claims 4,000 stores in 300 cities (2025). The driver was consumer backlash against pre-made dishes, and robots are now displayed openly (36Kr, Jiemian, T4).
- Chipotle Autocado and Hyphen makeline: pilot-stage. No 2026 rollout data found (gap).

**Small F&B verdict (A6 in PLAN):**
- **Refuted as stated** ("robots will kill small F&B"). **Plausible in a split form:** undifferentiated small operators competing on price lose share to automated chains in H1 and H2. Differentiated, experience-based operators keep or gain share.
- Baumol mechanism: when robot productivity drives down the cost of commodity meals, the relative price of human-cooked, human-served meals rises. They become a status and experience good. The OpenResearch UBI data show recipients spent about $300 a month more, including on food and leisure, and worked 1.3 to 1.4 fewer hours a week. More leisure time plus transfers means more demand for experiences.
- Capital access: robot-as-a-service ($5k a month) lowers the capex barrier, so small operators can rent automation too. Rent, not robots, stays the small operator's killer.

## 7. Credit access for UHI recipients: the legal plumbing today

| Rule | Text | Tier | Implication for UHI |
|---|---|---|---|
| ECOA 15 USC 1691(a)(2) | No discrimination "because all or part of the applicant's income derives from any public assistance program." (b)(2): a lender may ask only to determine "amount and probable continuance". | T1 | UHI income must be counted on equal terms |
| Fannie Mae B3-3.4-12 Public Assistance | Agency letter on amount, frequency, duration. "No minimum history". Must continue at least 3 years. May be grossed up if nontaxable. | T1 | A statutory UHI passes. A UHI appropriated yearly fails the continuance test. |
| Fannie Mae B3-3.4-15 Social Security | Own-account retirement or disability: "Lenders are not required to verify continuance". 15% gross-up baseline. | T1 | A UHI modeled as an entitlement would get the same treatment |
| Fannie Mae B3-3.4-16 Trust Income | Fixed payment from a depleting asset needs 3-year continuance | T1 | An equity-fund UHI paying fixed distributions would be underwritten like trust income |
| 42 USC 407(a) | Future payments "shall not be transferable or assignable", not subject to "execution, levy, attachment, garnishment". | T1 | Social Security model makes UHI non-pledgeable: no borrowing against the stream as collateral |
| 42 USC 659(a) | "Notwithstanding any other provision of law", federal payments based on employment can be garnished for child support and alimony | T1 | Even a protected UHI would have exceptions |
| 31 CFR 212 | Banks must protect 2 months of directly deposited federal benefits from garnishment | T1 | Consumer floor |
| 26 USC 5891 | 40% excise tax on structured-settlement factoring unless a court approves it as in the payee's "best interest" | T1 | Template for regulated pledgeability of a tokenized UHI |
| AS 43.23.140 (Alaska) | 20% of PFD exempt from creditors, but not for child support, restitution, defaulted student loans, fines | T1 | Partial pledgeability by default |
| 29 CFR 531.27 | Wages "in cash or negotiable instrument payable at par" | T1 | Workers cannot be paid minimum wage in compute or energy |

**Synthesis:** credit exclusion of UHI recipients would require a deliberate design choice: a discretionary amount, a short horizon, or a non-statutory basis. It would not happen by default. The open design question is pledgeability:
- **Non-pledgeable (Social Security model):** protects recipients from predatory factoring. Blocks lump-sum capital formation, unless informal mechanisms do it (Kenya ROSCAs).
- **Pledgeable or tokenized:** lets recipients raise capital (start a business, buy a home). Creates a factoring market that historically pays 30 to 60 cents on the dollar (memory, settlement-factoring industry). That is why 26 USC 5891 exists.
- **Middle path:** non-pledgeable cash floor plus an equity component (Trump Account style) that can be borrowed against only through regulated channels.

I found no live US legislative proposal for a tokenized or pledgeable UHI (gap, search budget exhausted).

## 8. "Permanent underclass" narrative

- **Origin:** a San Francisco tech in-joke on X. Template: "You have [N] months to escape the permanent underclass", where N is your AGI timeline (City Journal 2026-08-05, T4). An early version reads "You have 2 years to create a podcast..." Scott Alexander (2026-01-02) traces the intellectual roots to the New Yorker ("Will AI Trap You in the Permanent Underclass?"), L. Rudolf Laine ("Capital, AGI, and Human Ambition"), Philosophy Bear, and Trammell and Patel. The coinage is contested: one Big Think account dates it to a 2022 pseudonymous Substack (not verified, page blocked).
- **Amplifiers:** Amodei's January 2026 essay. Microsoft's Suleyman (most computer-based professional tasks automated within a year). NYT (Jasmine Sun, "Silicon Valley is Bracing for a Permanent Underclass", around April 2026). Senator Warren's 2026-06-03 post, the most engaged in the doom cluster and boosted by Polymarket. Anthropic is 26% of entity mentions (Pulsar 2026-10-12, T4).
- **What is sold:** crypto as an escape route, AI tools and model subscriptions, "learn AI" expertise (Pulsar). Prediction markets gain engagement. Indirectly, the narrative sells urgency for AI adoption and for capital accumulation now.
- **Economic mechanism (is there one?):** Yes, conditionally.
  1. Capital substitutes for labor, so the labor share falls (T0: record low 52.8%).
  2. Returns are capitalized into asset prices, so the top 1% wealth share hits records (32.5% Q2 2026, secondary).
  3. Wealth concentration exceeds income concentration (wealth Gini about 0.75, stock ownership Gini above 0.9, per Trammell).
  4. Historically, capital deepening raised wages and lowered returns (the stabilizer). Trammell argues the stabilizer breaks under substitution.
  - Weakness: the labor-share decline predates AI. Unemployment is low. Scott Alexander argues relative wealth matters little in either abundance or annihilation scenarios.
- **Status:** narrative = T5 marketing. Mechanism = Plausible direction, Speculative magnitude and timing.

## 11. Anomalies (deviation from default)

1. **Record-low labor share with flat unemployment.** Default: productivity gains are shared through wages in a full-employment market. Observed: capital captures them, unit profits +43% annualized. Signals the capital-biased shift starting before any layoffs.
2. **AI is the top stated layoff reason while total layoffs fall.** Default: a new technology cause raises totals. Observed: relabeling.
3. **Alaska labels part of its dividend an "energy relief payment" triggered by oil prices.** The first explicit energy-indexed citizen transfer in the US, a seed for the "energy allotment" idea.
4. **A Republican trifecta built universal child equity accounts invested only in US stocks.** Default: tax credits or cash. Observed: ownership transfer plus a structural equity bid. That is a Tier 1 instrument handed to Tier 2.
5. **The biggest private UBI funder (Altman) walks away from UBI after his study showed modest work reduction.** Default: double down. Observed: pivot to compute and ownership models, which route through his own rails.
6. **Sweetgreen sold its robotics unit while expanding robot kitchens.** Default: vertical integration of a core technology. Observed: automation as a service. Lowers barriers for small competitors over time.
7. **Musk calls for federal checks one year after leading federal spending cuts.** Explained by horizon: cuts now, transfers after robots arrive. And by his incentives: robot demand.

## 12. Mechanism chains

**Chain 1: robot-hour cost sets the wage ceiling**
Robot reliability crosses a threshold (H1) → RaaS price per task-hour ($2 to $10) undercuts human wages ($15 to $20) → wages for substitutable tasks fall toward the robot cost, or jobs vanish → workers move to complemented roles (Stanford: complemented jobs flat or rising), to human-premium roles, or onto transfers → Tier 3 shrinks and Tier 2 grows.
Test: wages in food prep, warehouse and cleaning vs RaaS list prices. Humanoid unit price below $30k with uptime above 90%.

**Chain 2: capital share to asset prices to the access gap (the real underclass)**
Substitution → labor share falls (52.8%, T0) → profits are capitalized → equity and real estate prices rise faster than incomes → income-based credit (DTI-capped) buys a shrinking share of assets → non-owners fall behind even with full credit access → the political answer must be ownership transfers (equity accounts, fund units), not just cash.
Test: labor share, top 1% wealth share, house-price-to-income, Trump Account balances.

**Chain 3: Musk's monetary UHI**
Robots raise output faster than money → goods deflation ("massive disinflation") → the government issues dollars to people to keep demand → if output really grows, no inflation and the debt-to-GDP ratio falls (links to L7 and Musk's "only way to solve the debt is AI and robots") → if output lags, inflation → Tier 1 real assets hedge, Tier 2 cash erodes → divergence widens.
Test: core goods CPI going negative persistently while services CPI rises (the Baumol split). Any bill for AI-funded transfers.

**Chain 4: food**
Automation cuts foodservice labor (44.2 cents of the restaurant dollar) → automated chains cut prices in the commodity segment → small undifferentiated operators lose → part of the savings goes to landlords (property income 32.7 cents) and energy → grocery prices stay anchored by farm, energy, tax and land costs (about 30 to 35 cents not automatable) → the human-made premium segment grows with leisure and transfers (Baumol).
Test: CPI food away from home vs food at home. Restaurant establishment counts by size (Census CBP, QCEW). Restaurant robot unit counts.

**Chain 5: UHI design decides tier mobility**
UHI as statutory cash → ECOA and Fannie make it underwritable → recipients get consumer credit and modest mortgages → no asset path beyond savings. UHI with an equity component → recipients own a slice of the capital share → can borrow against it in a regulated way → upward path to Tier 1. UHI appropriated each year → fails 3-year continuance → real credit exclusion. That is the only route by which the "underclass" credit claim comes true.
