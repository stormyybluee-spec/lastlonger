## 1. Key findings

1. **The biggest change since these interviews is the Fed hike.** On 2026-09-16 the FOMC under Kevin Warsh raised the target range 25bp to 3.75 to 4.00%, unanimously, the first hike since 2023, and the minutes say most members see another hike by year end ([MUFG recap](https://www.mufgresearch.com/articles/september-2026-fomc-recap)). This **refutes ARK's Staudt** (who forecast 2 cuts, with upside risk to 3 or 4, and said Warsh was likely nominated "with some assurances that rate cuts were in the future"). It **fits Mallers** (fiscal-dominance treadmill: higher rates make the debt spiral worse). It also **contradicts Tait's claim** that interest rates are only a "transitory" driver of gold: gold fell about 16% in Q2 2026 as the Fed repriced hawkish ([Morningstar](https://global.morningstar.com/en-nd/markets/gold-has-retreated-record-highs-whats-next), [Crux](https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/profit-taking-pulls-gold-lower-central-banks-buy-a-record-289-tonnes)).
2. **Gold and Bitcoin moved against Tait's framing after he spoke.** Gold closed $4,643.85 on 2026-05-05 ([pricegold.net](https://pricegold.net/2026/may/5/)) and $4,165.58 on 2026-10-16 ([pricegold.net](https://pricegold.net/2026/october/16/)), about minus 10%. Bitcoin went from roughly $59k to $60k in early May (weak evidence, a CoinDesk page ticker) to about $83k on 2026-09-28 ([Manifold](https://manifold.markets/sun/will-bitcoins-daily-close-on-octobe)). Six months is no test of "Bitcoin goes to zero", but it is the opposite of what his frame implies over the period.
3. **Central banks bought gold into the falling price.** Net 289 t in Q2 2026, a record for a second quarter, while the price fell about 16% and gold ETFs saw 45 t of outflows ([Engineering News, WGC Q2](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/central-banks-bought-far-less-gold-than-thought-at-start-of-year-2026-07-30)). This is T0 support for Tait's core mechanism (official buyers fear sovereign debt and buy regardless of price). It also shows the marginal *private* holder is rate-sensitive. Both things are true at once.
4. **US debt math, now (T0).** Total public debt $40.44T on 2026-10-20 ([Treasury Debt to the Penny](https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v2/accounting/od/debt_to_penny)). Average interest rate on marketable debt 3.518% at 2026-09-30, up from 3.016% at 2023-09-30 ([Treasury Average Interest Rates](https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service/v2/accounting/od/avg_interest_rates)). FY2026 deficit $1.992T, $218B larger than FY2025 ([AAF on final MTS](https://www.americanactionforum.org/press-release/u-s-treasury-fy-2026-deficit-totaled-2-trillion/), [CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/system/files/2026-10/61985-MBR.pdf)). Net interest about $1.05T (secondary source, not verified against the MTS table). This backs Tait's "trillion dollars" interest line and Alden's "nothing stops this train". It also corrects Mallers' framing: the average coupon is already 3.5%, not 2%, so the remaining refinancing gap against a 10y of about 5.1 to 5.3% is about 160 to 180bp, not 300bp.
5. **Mallers' component facts mostly check out.** The ESF did sell euros to buy yen (dated July 28 in a Senate Banking letter, July 31 to Aug 1 in press, size undisclosed, $5B to $10B reported) ([Senate Banking letter](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/letter_to_bessent_on_yen_intervention.pdf), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/us-treasury-euros-yen-intervention/)). Non-residents held 58.4% of French general-government securities at end June 2026, up from 55.9% at end March ([Banque de France, 2026-10-13](https://www.banque-france.fr/en/statistics/loans/securities-issues-french-residents-2026-q2)). The OAT-Bund 10y spread widened to about 147bp by 2026-10-05, the widest since 2011-12 and wider than Italy's ([BabyPips explainer](https://www.babypips.com/news/explainer-why-the-oat-bund-spread-matters-for-the-euro-2026-10-12)). 30y mortgage 7.40% (Freddie Mac, week of 2026-10-15, [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/?g=1XU7Y)). The *intent* layer (the US steering Japan to sell Europe to fracture the euro) remains unproven. Mallers himself calls it unprovable.
6. **Musk's "200 GW/yr US solar" is a target, not capacity.** It is Tesla 100 GW plus SpaceX 100 GW, announced at Davos in January 2026 with a three-year horizon. Actual US module capacity is about 60 GW and cell capacity about 3.2 GW ([pv magazine](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/26/assessing-elon-musks-massive-100-gw-solar-ambitions/), [Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2025/10/the-entire-solar-supply-chain-has-now-been-reshored-in-the-u-s/)). This is the cleanest say/do gap in the set.
7. **Musk's energy-as-currency view is far-horizon and self-consistent across 2025-26.** Nov 2025: "money will stop being relevant at some point". Jan 2026: "the future currency is essentially the watt". Feb 2026 X post: "Just mass and energy". May 2026: Bitcoin as an "unclonable physics-based energy currency" ([AOL/Benzinga](https://www.aol.com/articles/musk-trust-tesla-ceo-bets-134636708.html), [Fanatical Futurist](https://www.fanaticalfuturist.com/2026/05/elon-musk-calls-bitcoin-an-unclonable-physics-based-energy-currency/)). He ties it to an economy many orders of magnitude larger, which is an H3 claim, not H0.
8. **Stablecoins: the experts agree on mechanism and disagree on who captures value.** Collins: "the stablecoin is a dollar, just formatted differently". Alden: stablecoins are "the final evolution of fiat currency", the top layer of a four-layer centralized stack, and holders are diluted because they earn no yield. Tait: useful at institutional level, but gold stablecoins have "been a disaster" ($2B to $4B AUM). Collins also says the debt-rescue story is overstated: stablecoins are "one extra customer buying a couple hundred billion dollars of treasuries". Market size is roughly $300B to $320B (peak about $320B in May 2026, about $310B mid-July), well short of the OCC's $500B year-end projection ([CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/one-year-later-genius-act-just-made-stablecoins-easier-to-sell/), [AMBCrypto](https://ambcrypto.com/one-year-of-the-genius-act-progress-gaps-and-what-comes-next/)). GENIUS Act final rules missed the 2026-07-18 deadline, with a statutory backstop of 2027-01-18 ([Motley Fool research](https://www.fool.com/research/banks-payment-processors-stablecoins/)).
9. **The brief's labels for Staudt need correcting.** In the transcript Staudt did *not* say retirement saving no longer matters. That was Musk's claim, which the host quoted, and Staudt reframed it ("has to be put into proper context", meaning that "retirement" will mean something different). He also declined to give a $100k allocation and said only: know your horizon and risk tolerance, size positions, rebalance. Finally, the "I'm a long-term TSMC holder" line was the host's, not Staudt's.
10. **Tait contradicts himself on Bitcoin.** He says "my personal opinion is that it's going to go to zero", and in the same answer: "if you have gold in your portfolio, you should have Bitcoin and vice versa". Separately, the WGC is building tokenized gold infrastructure ("Gold as a Service", called "the Apple Store for gold") on the same crypto rails, pitched at a crypto conference.

### 2.3 Lyn Alden, Lyn Alden Investment Strategy, author of Broken Money
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SoROeULO3Ck (published 2025-08-13). Tier T4.

**Core mechanism claims**
- **Money is a ledger.** Commodity money uses nature as the ledger. Credit money uses social groups, then institutions and states. Technology changes which ledger works best.
- **Barter was rare.** Early economies ran on informal credit (the Godfather "favor" example), later formalized by third-party arbiters.
- **Telegraph shift.** Before about the 1860s, transactions and settlement moved at the same "speed of matter", so gold and silver won on stock-to-flow. After the telegraph, transactions moved at light speed but settlement stayed physical, which forced reliance on bank, central bank and nation-state ledgers. Bitcoin closes that gap with fast final settlement outside a central ledger. Three eras: commodity, central banking, and now decentralized base layer.
- **Jevons (1875)**: "almost every friction in finance is solved by another layer of centralization". Four layers: bank, central bank, reserve central bank, fintech on top.
- **Stablecoins** = the offshore dollar account, compressed and offered to anyone online. They are "the final evolution of fiat currency", the top of the centralized stack. Bitcoin is a parallel new foundation.
- **Impossible trinity**: a country can pick two of exchange rate, interest rate, open capital account. Weak-currency governments impose capital controls and fake pegs (Nigeria, Egypt: official 30 vs street 50 EGP).
- **Debasement is a hidden tax.** Broad money grows about 6 to 8% a year in developed countries over decades, often above 10% in developing ones. Every wage and contract is silently diluted. That raises friction and lowers productivity.
- **"Nothing stops this train"**: US structural deficits are unfixable in 5 to 10 years, and the US is in fiscal dominance. But the breaking point "is farther out than many bears expect". It is both bearish and bullish.
- **Cantillon / hierarchy of capital access**: those who receive deficits and can borrow cheaply in dollars to buy scarce assets (her example: MicroStrategy) gain. Wage earners, the foreign dollar sector and stablecoin holders (no yield) are diluted.
- **"The rich's biggest asset is the poor"**: partly true. The mechanism is that the rich can short the currency at low rates while the poor hold it or borrow at credit-card rates. Industrial-era schooling trains employees, not owners, mostly by emergent incentive, not design.
- **Fixed-supply money**: investing becomes optional (risk for more) rather than mandatory (to stand still). Echoes Jeff Booth.
- **AI and money**: things will not become "nearly free" because the physical world (energy, copper, transformers, housing, health care) bounds it. A copper mine takes about 20 years. There is a five-plus-year US transformer and electrical-parts backlog. The real risk is about 50% of people unable to do anything a machine cannot. Portable AI is "a thousand times harder" than data-center AI: the brain runs on about 20 W vs about 20 MW for a comparable supercomputer, a million-fold gap. So skilled field blue-collar work is the most AI-resistant.

**Explicit predictions with dates**
- US structurally high deficits for the next 5 to 10 years (from mid 2025, so to 2030-35).
- Over years and decades, the weakest currencies disappear first, consolidating toward larger ones, stablecoins and Bitcoin. Bitcoin grows and coexists with fiat, and eventually some pricing happens in Bitcoin.
- Humans keep an edge in messy physical work "for the next couple decades".

**Key numbers**: 6 to 8% developed money growth, above 10% developing. More than 40 currencies in Africa, more than 30 in Latin America, about 180 worldwide. Egypt: 38% inflation, M2 growth above 20% a year. Brain 20 W vs supercomputer 20 MW. Dilution rule: 7% supply growth minus 4% yield equals 3% net dilution.

**Positions**: Gold: still a gold bull, keeps some. Bitcoin: largest conviction, a new foundation layer. Stablecoins: useful, empowering in weak-currency countries, but fiat's last form and diluting. Dollar: debases steadily but outlasts weaker currencies. AI: big disruption, not post-scarcity, labor-displacement risk. Energy: AI is physically bounded by power and materials, and Bitcoin has a physical energy component. People: "own scarce things, short unscarce things safely", always ask "am I being diluted?", avoid status spending, build physical-world skills.

**Incentives and conflicts**: discloses she is "short the dollar and long other things" and that Bitcoin is a large share of her net worth. She is a general partner at a Bitcoin-focused venture fund (Ego Death Capital, from pre-cutoff knowledge, verify). She sells a newsletter and a book (giveaway in this video). Her public framing is conservative (diversified, keeps gold), which lowers but does not remove the conflict.

**What changed since**: FY2026 deficit $1.99T, up $218B on FY2025 ([AAF](https://www.americanactionforum.org/press-release/u-s-treasury-fy-2026-deficit-totaled-2-trillion/)), which confirms "nothing stops this train" one year in. A 2026-10-14 German report says she repeated the line in October 2026, arguing that neither rate hikes nor AI change the deficit dynamic, and citing a CBO 2026 deficit of 5.8% of GDP with 3.3 points interest ([stock3](https://stock3.com/news/zwei-billionen-us-dollar-defizit-darum-setzt-lyn-alden-auf-bitcoin-und-gold-17333120), secondary, unverified). The Fed hiking into a deficit regime is consistent with her "fiscal dominance makes rate hikes less effective" view: mortgage rates are above 7% while deficits keep growing.

### 2.4 Reeve Collins, co-founder Tether, co-founder and chairman STBL and WeFi
Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BQP28KH4VGw (published 2026-01-30, recording date uncertain, possibly late 2025). Tier T5 (promoter).

**Core mechanism claims**
- Origin: 2013-14, "put the dollar on the blockchain" so dollars move "globally, instantly, for free". Tether went parabolic with the 2017 ICO boom after Bitfinex principals took over.
- First-generation stablecoins have two flaws: (1) opaque backing and (2) issuers capture all the yield ("extremely lucrative"). This is why every institution wants its own stablecoin.
- "Stablecoin 2.0" (STBL): collateral is fully on-chain (tokenized T-bills), and the yield is stripped into a separate token (a "yield NFT", which is the security), while the spendable principal (USST) stays GENIUS-compliant because it pays no yield.
- GENIUS-compliant reserves are T-bills and money market funds at 2 to 4%. Non-US white-label "ecosystem specific stablecoins" can use credit or other RWAs at 5 to 10%, "just as safe and just as liquid" (an unsupported and risky claim).
- RWA-as-stablecoin-collateral is "the killer app" that will pull traditional assets on-chain. In future, baskets with haircuts (e.g. real estate at 20% LTV) could back stablecoins.
- Every large company (his example: Walmart coin) will issue its own stablecoin. Clearing houses make them fungible, and AI agents handle the routing so users never care which chain.
- "The stablecoin is a dollar. It's just formatted differently." Fiat "will always have its place". Later the format reverses: "a dollar backed by a token".
- On the US debt: the government gained "a new customer" (issuers must hold dollars and T-bills). But "I don't think it can solve all its problems just because there's one extra customer buying a couple hundred billion dollars of treasuries."
- Weak currencies: citizens would abandon them if allowed. Governments will "fight to the death" but can only slow it (Uber analogy). CBDCs are good for governments and bad for citizens.

**Explicit predictions with dates**: none with dates. "We don't know" if it is 3 or 10 years. All big institutions move to hybrid on-chain banking. Large corporates issue stablecoins.

**Key numbers**: yields of 2 to 3% (US) vs 5 to 10% (non-US RWA). Real estate at 20% LTV as stablecoin backing. "A couple hundred billion" of Treasuries held by issuers.

**Positions**: Gold: not discussed. Bitcoin: early "foreign currency in code", inspiration for moving value. Stablecoins: the core of the future financial system. Dollar: extended by stablecoins, wins against weak local currencies. AI: will abstract away chains and stablecoin brands. Energy: not discussed. People: use stablecoins and web3 banks that pass rewards back to users.

**Incentives and conflicts**: very high. He sells STBL (USST, YLD) and WeFi (on-chain bank with a token), and admits "me as a founder of STBL, I have tokens". His "Stablecoin 2.0" framing is a pitch, and his critique of issuers capturing yield is a critique of competitors (including Tether, which he no longer owns). The yield-strip design is regulatory arbitrage around GENIUS's ban on paying yield to holders.

**What changed since**: STBL's USST launched (or was scheduled) on Stellar around 2026-07-01 with tokenized Treasury collateral (USDY), and Franklin BENJI integration is undated ([KuCoin flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/stbl-launches-rwa-backed-stablecoin-usst-on-stellar-network), sponsored-heavy coverage). Stablecoin market about $300B to $320B, short of the OCC's $500B projection. GENIUS final rules late (backstop 2027-01-18). His "couple hundred billion" in T-bills fits a market of about $310B, of which USDT is about $184B and USDC about $73B.

### 2.5 Elon Musk, The Builders with Walter Isaacson (2026-10-20)

## 6. Mechanism chains

**M1. Treadmill (Mallers, Alden, Tait)**
Deficit about $2T a year, so more issuance. Average coupon 3.5% is below the marginal 10y at 5.1 to 5.3%, so interest cost rises as debt rolls over. Net interest above $1T leads to a bigger deficit and more issuance. If r stays above g, debt/GDP rises. Then either (a) yields hit a pain point (MOVE above 130, failed auctions), forcing the Fed or Treasury to intervene (buybacks, QE, bill-heavy issuance, SLR relief), leading to debasement, with gold and BTC up (Tait and Mallers phase 2), or (b) AI lifts g above r (Musk) and the treadmill reverses.
Tests: 10y vs average coupon gap, MOVE, auction tails, Treasury refunding mix, Fed balance sheet, productivity.

**M2. Euro fracture (Mallers, Tait partial)**
Japan's yen weakens, so Japanese institutions repatriate. The US does not want Treasury selling, so the ESF sells euros (a signal). Japanese selling tilts to European bonds. France (58% foreign-held, deficit above 5%, election coming) sees its spread widen. The ECB faces a choice: TPI, or let France reprice. Either the euro weakens (Germany loses competitiveness support only if the euro *strengthens*, which needs a breakup or a France exit, a long chain) or the ECB monetizes. The US then pivots to a weaker dollar.
Weak link in the chain: a France crisis more likely weakens the euro than strengthens it, which would hurt rather than help a US weak-dollar reshoring aim. The step "Germany loses its weak currency" needs fragmentation, not just stress. Flag as speculative.
Tests: Japan MoF monthly portfolio flows by region, OAT-Bund above 150bp, ECB TPI activation, EUR/JPY, BNP and SocGen equity.

**M3. Stablecoin dollar extension (Collins, Alden)**
GENIUS forces US-facing issuers into T-bills and MMFs. Global users swap weak local currencies for USD tokens, raising bill demand at the front end. Treasury can tilt issuance to bills at slightly lower rates. The dollar's reach extends (Alden: the "offshore account for everyone"). Local monetary sovereignty erodes (the impossible trinity, enforced from outside). Governments respond with capital controls or CBDCs.
Scale check: about $310B total now. Even $1T would be under 3% of debt. It is a front-end and dollar-reach story, not a solvency story.

**M4. Energy as the unit of account (Musk, Alden partial)**
AI and robots make labor abundant, so the binding inputs become energy, mass and launch capacity. Prices of outputs fall relative to joules and kilograms. Over a long horizon the unit of account tracks physical inputs. Fiat "does not matter". Alden's counter: physical inputs (copper, transformers, power) are slow, so this takes decades, and in the meantime they act as constraints, not money.
Tests: energy share of AI capex, $/kWh for data centers, robot unit costs, whether any contract settles in kWh or compute units.
