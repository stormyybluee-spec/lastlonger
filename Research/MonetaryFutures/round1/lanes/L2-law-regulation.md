## 0. One-paragraph read

The binding US legal stack as of October 2026 points in one direction: dollar money moves onto private, regulated, Treasury-bill-backed rails, while the state-issued digital alternative is banned by statute. GENIUS is law, the first federal rule (OCC) is final, and the whole regime switches on 2027-01-18. A CBDC ban became law on 2026-07-11 (riding on a housing bill, without the president's signature). The Strategic Bitcoin Reserve exists only as a custody pool of forfeited coins with zero purchases, and the bill that would codify it (ARMA, H.R. 8957) explicitly forbids borrowing or new taxes to buy. The broad market-structure bill (CLARITY) failed cloture 49-50 on 2026-09-15, so the SEC is building the tokenization regime by exemption instead. The Supreme Court protected the Fed from at-will removal (Trump v. Cook, 2026-06-29) while removing that protection from almost every other agency (Trump v. Slaughter), and the White House has responded by opening a "for cause" process against Governor Cook (hearing set for 2026-11-05). Trump Accounts are live (about 70 million children auto-enrolled, $4.5B deposited by 2026-10-07), and the Treasury Secretary reportedly wants to seed them with government equity in AI firms. That last item is the closest thing to a legal UHI channel now in motion, and it is an equity (ownership) channel, not an income channel.

## 1. Key findings (by item)

### 1.1 GENIUS Act (Pub. L. 119-27, signed 2025-07-18)

What is binding (statute, T1, read on govinfo):
- Reserves 1:1 in identifiable assets: coins and currency, deposits, Treasury bills, notes or bonds with remaining maturity of 93 days or less, repo backed by T-bills of 93 days or less, and related forms (Sec. 4(a)(1)(A)). The 93-day rule makes stablecoin reserves structurally a front-end T-bill buyer.
- Yield ban (Sec. 4(a)(11)): no permitted or foreign issuer may pay holders interest or yield "solely in connection with the holding, use, or retention" of the stablecoin. The word "solely" is the loophole that exchange reward programs use.
- Effective date (Sec. 20): earlier of 18 months after enactment (2027-01-18) or 120 days after final regulations. In practice: 2027-01-18.
- State option up to $10B outstanding (Sec. 4(c)(1)). Above $10B, transition to federal supervision or stop new issuance, waiver possible (Sec. 4(d)).
- Three years after enactment (2028-07-18), digital asset service providers may not offer or sell to US persons a payment stablecoin that is not from a permitted issuer (Sec. 3(b)(1)), subject to the foreign-issuer path in Sec. 18.
- Foreign issuers (Sec. 18): exempt from Sec. 3 prohibitions only if Treasury finds the home regime comparable, which requires a recommendation from every other member of the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee, plus OCC registration, plus reserves held in a US financial institution unless a reciprocal arrangement says otherwise.
- Payment stablecoins from permitted issuers are not securities and not commodities (Sec. 17). Holders get first priority in issuer bankruptcy (Sec. 11, new 11 U.S.C. 507(e)).

Implementing rules (status 2026-10-22):
- OCC final rule issued 2026-10-21, effective 2027-01-18 (new 12 CFR part 15: reserves, redemption, risk management, custody, capital and operational backstop, foreign issuer exams). BSA/AML/OFAC parts split into a separate rulemaking with Treasury. OCC has not yet opened applications. Changes from the Feb 2026 proposal: dropped "rebuttable presumptions" against third-party rewards, so rewards are judged case by case, no safe harbor for existing programs, reserve diversification made principles-based. Redemption generally within two business days (press reporting). OCC explicitly refused to take the pending CLARITY Act into account: the yield ban is implemented "as enacted". (T1)
- Treasury: ANPRM Sept 2025, NPRM published 2026-08-18 on who may issue, offer or sell (new 12 CFR part 1523), including exchange due diligence on foreign issuers. Comments closed 2026-10-19. No comparability finding yet for any foreign regime. (T1 proposal)
- FinCEN/OFAC joint proposal April 2026: issuers must be able to freeze or stop transactions. (T1 proposal)
- FDIC: application proposal Dec 2025, state-chartered issuer approach April 2026. Fed: proposals Sept 2026 for member-bank stablecoin subsidiaries. NCUA: no update found. All agencies missed the statute's mid-2026 rulemaking deadline. Full rulebook by 2027-01-18 looks unlikely.

Who is issuing (T0 market data, 2026-10-21): total stablecoins about $301B (DefiLlama $301.2B). USDT about $182.8B (about 61 percent share), USDC about $72.2B. Tether's US-compliant token USAT (issued via Anchorage Digital Bank, reserves in cash and short Treasuries at Cantor Fitzgerald) launched 2026-01-27 and is only about $183M. Offshore USDT, not regulated by GENIUS, still dominates. Reward programs paid through exchanges: PayPal 4 percent on PYUSD, Coinbase One 3.75 percent on USDC, Kraken+ up to 4.5 percent on USDG (CoinDesk 2026-10-21).

Plumbing effect: every new dollar of compliant stablecoin is a forced bid for T-bills of 93 days or less, repo, or bank deposits. It shortens the effective maturity of marginal Treasury demand and gives Treasury a price-insensitive front-end buyer. Combined with the 3-year DASP ban, it also pushes the offshore giant (Tether) toward either a comparability finding or a US subsidiary (USAT).

Who benefits: Treasury (bill demand), Circle and bank-affiliated issuers, custodians (BNY, Anchorage, Cantor), exchanges that keep reward programs. Losers: banks' deposit franchise at the margin (they lobbied to close the "solely" loophole via CLARITY and lost when CLARITY stalled).

Intent signal (T2): Bessent, in the OCC release of 2026-10-21, compared GENIUS to Lincoln's 1863 National Currency Act, which "established a national framework for bank-issued currency". The 1863 Act created national bank notes that had to be backed by US government bonds, precisely to build captive demand for Civil War debt. The Treasury Secretary chose that analogy himself. This is the strongest on-record evidence for the "stablecoin as debt-demand extension" reading.

### 1.2 CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633, market structure)

- House passed July 2025. Senate Banking advanced its version May 2026 (all Republicans plus two Democrats). (T1 process)
- Senate cloture on motion to proceed failed 49-50 on 2026-09-15. Republicans Collins, Hawley and Moran voted no. Tillis switched to no to enter a motion to reconsider, so the bill is technically alive. Stated objections: narrow ethics language on officials' (including the president's) crypto income, DOJ-only enforcement, ethics sunset 2029-01-20, consumer protection, illicit finance, state preemption (18 state AGs opposed). Lummis was reported to call it dead. (T1 vote, T2 statements)
- Polymarket H.R. 3633 contract about 7.9 percent in mid-September (T3, stale).
- Outlook: lame duck after 2026-11-03 midterms, otherwise reintroduction required in the 120th Congress (January 2027).
- Plumbing effect of failure: no statutory SEC/CFTC split for crypto. Regulation by exemption and agency rule continues (see 1.5). Banks did not get their fix on stablecoin rewards.
- Intent reveal: the failure point was not technical market structure but the president's personal crypto income. The say/do gap is on the actor, not the policy.

### 1.3 CBDC ban

- EO 14178 (2025-01-23) bars agencies from any action to establish a US CBDC (T1, memory, well-documented pre-cutoff).
- NDAA FY2026 (Dec 2025): CBDC ban stripped from final text (secondary reporting, T4). Anti-CBDC Surveillance State Act (H.R. 1919) passed House 2025-07-17, was folded into a House amendment to S.1318 on 2026-04-29, Senate refused to proceed 47-52 on 2026-06-05.
- Enacted: 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R. 6644). Senate concurred 85-5 on 2026-06-22, House passed 358-32 on 2026-06-23, presented 2026-06-29, became law 2026-07-11 without signature (Trump withheld signature in protest over the Senate not passing the SAVE America Act, but did not veto). (T1)
- Content: the Board or any Reserve Bank "may not issue or create a central bank digital currency", directly or "indirectly through a financial institution or other intermediary", through 2030-12-31. Carve-out for dollar-denominated digital currency that is open, permissionless and private, which protects private stablecoins. (T1)
- Plumbing effect: the retail digital dollar is legally reserved for private issuers for at least four years. The Fed keeps wholesale and payments-account tools (see 1.5).
- Intent reveal: the US chose private rails backed by public debt over a public liability. A CBDC would be a Fed liability that competes with bank deposits and earns nothing for Treasury demand. A GENIUS stablecoin is a private liability that must hold Treasury bills. Same "digital dollar", opposite balance sheet effect for Treasury.
- Anomaly: a crypto-friendly president refused to sign the bill that carries the anti-CBDC promise he campaigned on. His reason was unrelated (SAVE Act leverage). The ban is temporary (sunset 2030-12-31), which is weaker than the House's permanent version.

### 1.4 Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and Digital Asset Stockpile

- EO 14233 (2025-03-06) created the SBR (forfeited BTC, no sales) and the Digital Asset Stockpile (other forfeited tokens, Treasury may sell). Treasury and Commerce were told to develop "budget-neutral" acquisition strategies with no incremental taxpayer cost. (T1)
- Money moved: none. No federal purchase has occurred (T0 absence, confirmed by White House adviser statements). Holdings estimates vary: 198,000 BTC (fully forfeited, on-chain trackers) to 319,000 to 328,000 BTC (including contested or pending forfeitures). The government has not published an official count. The EO's 30-day accounting and 60-day legislative-proposal deadlines passed with no public output.
- Process: Patrick Witt (White House digital asset council) promised a major announcement in April and again in May 2026 ("next few weeks"). It did not arrive. July 2026 reporting (Bloomberg via secondary) says custody is being moved toward Commerce rather than Treasury, with DOJ reviewing legal authority. On 2026-10-14 Witt said the priority is safeguarding and accounting for existing BTC, no purchase strategy is set, and open-market buying would need Congress. (T2)
- Legislation: ARMA (American Reserve Modernization Act of 2026, H.R. 8957, Begich and Golden, introduced 2026-05-21) replaces the BITCOIN Act's 1 million BTC purchase target. House Financial Services reported it 28-21 on 2026-09-16 with a Steil substitute: Treasury must set up the SBR and Stockpile within 180 days, 20-year minimum hold, then sales capped at 10 percent per two years, annual audited proof of reserves, GAO oversight, a voluntary program for states to store BTC in segregated accounts. No acquisition authority. Treasury and Commerce get 180 days to study budget-neutral options. The text explicitly does not authorize borrowing, new taxes, deficit spending, or pledging federal assets as collateral. One report says the committee substitute dropped language to study funding the reserve via revaluation of Treasury gold certificates (needs check against committee text). No House floor vote yet. Senate BITCOIN Act (S.954, Lummis) not moved. (T1 process)
- States (T1, T0): New Hampshire HB 302 (May 2025, first state law, ETF route allowed), Arizona HB 2749 (May 2025, seized and unclaimed crypto only, direct-buy bills vetoed), Texas SB 21 (June 2025, $10M allocation). Texas is the only state that has bought: about $5M of IBIT in late Nov 2025, advisory committee formed May 2026.
- Plumbing effect: today, none. BTC is a confiscated-asset ledger entry, not a reserve asset used for anything. If ARMA passes, it legally freezes BTC in the government's hands for 20 years, which removes supply but does not add demand.
- Intent reveal: the administration's words are maximal ("Fort Knox of digital assets"), its money is zero. The "budget-neutral" condition, written into both the EO and ARMA, is the tell: the only budget-neutral paths are revaluing gold certificates, using tariff or ESF money, or swapping other assets. Each of those needs either Congress or an act the administration has not taken. Bitcoin is being given political status without balance sheet status.

### 1.5 SEC tokenization agenda, bank custody, Basel, Fed and OCC actions

SEC (T1):
- DTC received an SEC no-action letter in Dec 2025 for a three-year post-trade tokenization pilot covering Russell 1000 stocks, US Treasury securities and major index ETFs.
- SEC approved Nasdaq's tokenized-securities rule in March 2026: tokenized and traditional shares on the same book, same CUSIP, settling T+1 via NSCC/DTC.
- SEC "Innovation Exemption" order, 2026-09-17: temporary conditional exemptions through 2031-09-17 letting tokenized NMS stock trade on "Tokenized Securities Venues" without exchange registration, and exempting certain AMM liquidity providers from dealer status. Atkins called it provisional and invited comment.
- "Regulation Crypto Assets" proposal (reported 2026-08-18): offering regime with a small startup exemption (about $5M), a fundraising exemption up to about $75M per 12 months and an investment-contract safe harbor. Figures from a single secondary source, verify on sec.gov.
- Tokenized Treasuries (T0): about $14.7B to $15.4B outstanding (rwa.xyz 2026-10-16, Chainalysis mid-2026), up about 125 percent year on year. About two thirds held outside the US (Chainalysis). BlackRock BUIDL about $2.7B. Lending against tokenized Treasuries fell 23 percent, so collateral use is not yet scaling.

Banking agencies (T1):
- OCC conditional national trust charters to five crypto firms including Paxos, Circle and Ripple (Dec 2025).
- Kraken Financial (Wyoming) got a limited-purpose Fed master account from the Kansas City Fed on 2026-03-04, one-year initial term, no discount window, no interest on reserves. First crypto firm with direct Fedwire access.
- Fed "payment account" (skinny master account): RFI Dec 2025, proposal May 2026, Fed target to have accounts available by Q4 2026. No final rule found.
- OCC and FDIC final rule removing reputation risk from supervision (Federal Register 2026-04-10). On 2026-06-02 the three agencies removed "reputational risk" from interagency documents. No new interagency crypto custody guidance found. OCC confirmed banks may outsource permissible custody and execution to third parties.
- eSLR final rule effective 2026-04-01 (early adoption 2026-01-01): GSIB buffer becomes 50 percent of method 1 surcharge, bank subsidiary standard capped at 3 percent plus up to 1 percent. Fed vote 5-2, Cook dissented. This frees balance sheet for Treasury holdings and repo intermediation.

Basel (T1): SCO60 cryptoasset standard in force from 2026-01-01 in the Basel Framework. US and UK declined to adopt it. At its 2026-02-24/25 meeting the Basel Committee fast-tracked a targeted review, focused on the rule that puts all assets on public permissionless blockchains in punitive Group 2. Update promised later in 2026. No text found yet.

Plumbing effect: securities and Treasuries get a tokenized wrapper that settles inside existing DTC and bank plumbing, not outside it. Crypto firms get direct access to Fed rails, but without the two privileges that make a bank a bank (discount window, interest on reserves). The pattern is "on-chain format, incumbent custody, Fed-controlled settlement".

Intent reveal: when Congress failed (CLARITY), the agencies did not wait. The administration's priority is putting dollars and Treasuries on new rails, not decentralizing them.

### 1.6 Trump Accounts and the sovereign wealth fund

Trump Accounts (T1 statute, T0 deposits):
- Created by the 2025 reconciliation law (OBBBA), Internal Revenue Code section 530A, an IRA-type account. BNY is financial agent, Robinhood builds the app.
- $1,000 Treasury seed for US citizen children born 2025-01-01 through 2028-12-31. Private contributions up to $5,000 per child per year, employers up to $2,500, government and 501(c)(3) contributions outside the cap. Investments limited to low-cost broad US equity index funds (expense ratio up to 0.1 percent). No withdrawals before the year the child turns 18. JCT cost estimate $15B through 2034.
- App launched 2026-05-28. Deposits opened 2026-07-04. On 2026-10-07 the White House said every eligible minor was auto-enrolled (about 70 million), more than 10 million accounts funded, $4.5B deposited since July 4, more than 70 companies committed to contribute. Michael and Susan Dell pledged $6.25B ($250 each for up to 25 million children, ZIP codes with median family income up to $150k). Brad Gerstner said contributions go into the S&P 500 within a day.
- Single-source (Wikipedia, unverified): Gwynne Shotwell pledged one SpaceX share per child for two million children, and Elon Musk announced in October 2026 a donation of one SpaceX share per US child in 2027 (about $10.5B). Needs verification.
- Trump on 2026-10-07: Social Security "should have been invested in the stock market", the program will have a large effect against "socialism and communism". (T2)

Sovereign wealth fund and AI equity (T1 EO, T2 reports):
- EO 14196 (2025-02-03) ordered an SWF plan, "subject to the availability of appropriations". No fund created. Semafor (2026-06-17) reports the broad SWF idea was abandoned in 2025 over national-debt concerns.
- Government equity is instead spread across agencies: Intel about 9.9 percent (converted from CHIPS grants, Aug 2025), plus a count of up to 30 company stakes since June 2025 (Forbes, T4).
- Semafor 2026-06-17: officials weighing equity stakes in major AI companies. Bessent favored putting the equity into Trump Accounts. Lutnick favored an SWF-type vehicle. No decision. FT (July 2026, via secondary): OpenAI considering offering the government a 5 percent stake. Reuters: no stake discussed with Anthropic. Trump to TIME (interview 2026-09-28, published 2026-10-01): no nationalization, but an Intel-style stake "I might. Maybe I could do that."
- Plumbing effect: Trump Accounts create a universal, state-registered, equity-only, locked-until-18 account for every American child. It is a ready-made pipe for distributing capital (not income) at population scale. If AI equity is routed into it, the US gets a "universal basic capital" channel without a new statute for the accounts themselves (the equity acquisition itself would likely need Congress, per reporting).
- Intent reveal: the administration's answer to "who owns the robots" is broad passive index ownership plus possible government-held AI equity, not taxes and transfers. It also builds a large, price-insensitive, perpetual buyer of US large-cap equities.

### 1.7 UBI, UHI, AI displacement, robot or AI taxes

- No enacted federal UBI, UHI or AI dividend (T1 absence as of 2026-10-22).
- Sanders, American AI Sovereign Wealth Fund Act, introduced 2026-06-18: one-time 50 percent tax paid in stock on companies with at least $200M annual AI receipts (covering data centers, compute, AI services, advanced robotics), plus 50 percent of future share issuance, held by a fund with seven commissioners that cannot sell, no bailouts, estimated dividend about $1,000 per person per year, fund size estimated by Sanders at $7T. No markup reported. David Sacks called it a "stupidity tax". (T1 bill text via press, T2)
- H.R. 5830 guaranteed income pilot (introduced 2025-10-24, $495M FY2026 to FY2030, 11 Democratic cosponsors), in Ways and Means, no action. (single source)
- Campaign-level: Alex Bores (NY House race) "AI Dividend" funded by an AI consumption tax and frontier-lab equity. Warren calls for taxing AI companies. (T2/T5)
- No federal or state robot tax bill found. No state UBI law found. Search budget ran out before a full state scan, so this is a gap.
- Intent reveal: both parties are converging on equity (ownership) rather than a payroll-style robot tax. Right: Trump Accounts plus possible AI stakes. Left: Sanders 50 percent stock levy. That convergence matters for the UHI question: the emerging design is "universal capital with dividends", which is pledgeable or not depending on how the accounts are locked. Trump Accounts are locked until 18 and then become IRA-like, which makes them weak collateral by design.

### 1.8 Fed independence and mandate

- Trump v. Slaughter (2026-06-29, 6-3): Humphrey's Executor overruled, FTC commissioners removable at will, most independent agencies now under presidential control. (T1)
- Trump v. Cook (2026-06-29, 5-4, Roberts with Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, Jackson): Governor Cook stays while litigation continues. The "for cause" protection is real, and the governor is entitled to notice and some opportunity to respond before removal, which need not be a full trial. Court did not decide if cause exists. The opinion grounds the Fed carve-out in the tradition of insulating central banking. (T1)
- Response: presidential memorandum 2026-10-07 set up a three-member panel (Kevin Hassett, acting OGE director Keith Sonderling, EEOC chair Andrea Lucas) to examine Cook's mortgage statements. In-person White House hearing 2026-11-05, four hours. Cook's lawyers say she is in Europe on Fed business that day and question the panel's impartiality. Final written response reportedly due 2026-11-10. (T1 memo via press, T2)
- Fed chair: Kevin Warsh sworn in 2026-05-22 (oath by Justice Thomas at the White House). Warsh has floated a new Fed-Treasury accord in which Treasury would have more say over balance sheet size. No accord text exists. (T2)
- No law changing the Fed's mandate found as of 2026-10-22 (search gap noted).
- Plumbing effect: the Court built a narrow legal moat around the Fed (for-cause plus notice), and the executive is now testing how thin "notice and opportunity" can be. If a White House-staffed panel can supply "cause", the moat becomes procedural only.
- Intent reveal: the administration is pursuing influence over the Fed through personnel (Warsh, the Cook process) and through an accord, not through statute. That is the path that does not need 60 Senate votes.

### 1.9 Gold

- Federal: no gold revaluation bill. Statutory price $42.2222 per ounce (31 U.S.C. 5117), Treasury holds about 261.5M oz (about 8,133 t), book value about $11.04B (Treasury Fiscal Data via Kitco, 2026-08-03). At about $4,050 per oz (Kitco quote 2026-08-03) the market value is over $1T. Gold Reserve Transparency Act (S.3218, Lee, audit) introduced 2025-11-19, no action. The original ARMA text listed gold-certificate revaluation as one of five budget-neutral funding mechanisms to study. One report says the committee substitute removed it. Treasury officials have reportedly said they are not pursuing revaluation (secondary). (T1 statute, T3 price)
- Texas HB 1056 (signed June 2025): legal-tender recognition of qualifying privately produced gold and silver took effect 2026-09-01, acceptance voluntary. Electronic payment systems backed by metal in the Texas Bullion Depository take effect 2027-05-01, dependent on the comptroller. Federal tax still treats each spend as a sale (collectibles rate up to 28 percent). (T1)
- Other states: Utah and Wyoming already recognize gold and silver as legal tender (prior law), Missouri, Arkansas, Florida had 2026 bills. Status of each not verified (search budget). (T4)
- Plumbing effect: gold is being re-legitimized at the state level as a payment instrument, but federal capital-gains treatment blocks real circulation. At the federal level, gold is a dormant $1T accounting option, the cleanest "budget-neutral" funding source for anything, used by nobody yet.

## 3. Anomalies (deviation from default)

1. Treasury Secretary picked the 1863 National Currency Act as GENIUS's analogy. Default would be a payments or innovation framing. 1863 is about forcing private money issuers to hold federal debt.
2. GENIUS caps reserve Treasuries at 93 days. Default for a "safe asset" rule would allow any Treasury maturity with haircuts. The cap ties stablecoin growth to bill issuance, exactly where Treasury has been tilting issuance.
3. The CBDC ban passed on a housing bill, with a sunset, without the president's signature. Default: a standalone permanent ban signed with ceremony.
4. The SBR has existed for 19 months with zero purchases and no published holdings count. Default for a "strategic reserve" (SPR analogy) is a stated size target and a fill plan.
5. ARMA's text explicitly bars pledging federal assets as collateral and bars borrowing for BTC. Default for a pro-BTC bill would be to leave funding open. Someone wanted to close the gold-revaluation and ESF doors.
6. Trump v. Cook and Trump v. Slaughter were decided the same day with opposite outcomes for the Fed versus the FTC. Default (from the Court's direction since Seila Law) would have been to fold the Fed into the unitary-executive rule.
7. Treasury Secretary wants AI equity routed into children's index accounts rather than a sovereign fund. Default for state equity is a fund controlled by the executive.
8. The Fed gave Kraken a master account (March 2026) before finalizing its own payment-account framework. Default is framework first.

## 4. Mechanism chains

Chain A: stablecoins as captive bill demand (status: Confirmed for legal mechanism, Plausible for scale)
GENIUS 93-day reserve rule (T1) → each compliant stablecoin dollar must sit in bills, short repo or deposits → as compliant supply grows (and the 2028-07-18 DASP ban pushes offshore users toward compliant tokens), stablecoin issuers become a structural front-end buyer → Treasury can keep shifting issuance to bills at lower cost → terming-out pressure on the long end falls → the dollar's reach extends to offshore users who never open a US bank account. Test: compliant stablecoin supply (USDC, USAT, bank tokens) versus USDT after 2027-01-18, issuer attestation T-bill holdings, Treasury bill share in refunding statements, bill-OIS spreads.

Chain B: private rails, public settlement (Plausible)
CBDC ban (T1) + Fed payment accounts (proposal) + Kraken master account (T1) → private tokens settle in central bank money through Fedwire but without interest on reserves or discount window → the Fed keeps control of the settlement layer while private issuers carry retail distribution risk → "digital dollar" without the Fed owning a retail liability. Test: number of payment accounts granted by end 2027, whether any stablecoin issuer gets interest on balances.

Chain C: Fed capture by procedure (Plausible)
Slaughter makes every other regulator at-will (T1) → Cook keeps a for-cause plus notice moat for the Fed (T1) → White House builds a notice-and-hearing process run by its own officials (T1 memo) → if a removal on that record survives the D.C. Circuit, "for cause" becomes "for a reason the White House documented" → the Board majority becomes appointable within a term → coordination via a Fed-Treasury accord on balance sheet size. Test: outcome of the 2026-11-05 hearing, any removal order, court injunction timing, Board composition in 2027.

Chain D: universal capital instead of universal income (Plausible to Speculative)
