## 0. Context you must hold before reading anything else (state of the world, Oct 2026)

These background facts change how every reserve and FX question reads. Several are newer than my training data. Each one was checked against fresh sources this round.

- **A war is under way and Hormuz is shut.** The 2026 Iran war began on 28 Feb 2026. The Strait of Hormuz has been effectively closed for most of the year. The ceasefire collapsed on 8 Jul. A live tracker counted day 234 of closure on 21 Oct, with only about 5% of normal vessel traffic. Brent is near $110. (Wikipedia 2026 Iran war, straits.live, Trading Economics.)
- **Rate cycle: hikes, not cuts.** The Fed (chair Kevin Warsh) hiked 25bp on 16 Sep 2026, to roughly 3.75 to 4.00%. A December hike is roughly 75% priced. The ECB hiked in June and again on 10 Sep (deposit rate 2.50%). The BoJ hiked in July and again in September (to 1.25%, a 31-year high).
- **Global bond selloff.** US 10y is about 5.11%, French 10y about 4.99%, Bund about 3.42%, JGB 10y about 2.90% (it touched about 3.1% on 7 Oct).
- **Dollar firm, gold well off its peak.** DXY is 101.7, up 2.8% year on year. EUR/USD is 1.123, down 3.3% year on year, after peaking in January 2026. USD/JPY is about 158 (52-week range 151.5 to 164). USD/CNY is 6.67, the strongest yuan since mid-2022. Gold is about $4,112/oz, roughly 27% below its January 2026 all-time high of about $5,608.

So any "dollar devaluation" or "flight from the dollar" thesis has to be tested against a year in which the dollar rose, gold fell, and the US was hiking.

## 4. Anomalies (deviation from default)

1. **The US sold euros, not dollars, for yen, and did not warn the ECB.** Default: the US prints or sells dollars, or does nothing and lets Japan intervene alone. Truman called it "weird". Brooks said it "undercuts the efficacy". Obstfeld reads it as signalling "yen-strengthening rather than dollar-weakening". The size looks small (about $1B by weekly data). The *message* is the payload: the US will spend Europe's currency, not its own, and will not coordinate with Frankfurt.
2. **The US joined a yen defense at all.** Default since 1998 was no US participation except in a G7 disaster. The motive is the Treasury market. Every dollar of yen Japan buys alone is a dollar of Treasuries or deposits sold. The FIMA push is the tell.
3. **Central banks bought the most gold in a quarter (Q2) when the price was falling, and almost none (57t) when it was at records (Q1).** Default story: "central banks drive gold up". Observed: they are price-sensitive value buyers and supply-elastic. The January spike and crash were speculative (margin hikes, Warsh nomination).
4. **Russia is a net gold seller.** Default rhetoric: Russia leads the gold bloc. Reality: war financing forces sales.
5. **The yuan is at a 4-year high during a trade war.** Default: China weakens the yuan to offset tariffs. Observed: steady appreciation via the fixing. This is consistent with an implicit "Mar-a-Lago-lite" bargain, or with Beijing using yuan strength to win Europe and the Global South.
6. **France, not Italy, is the epicentre.** Contagion runs core-to-periphery for the first time. Italy and Spain spreads widened about 20bp *after* France.
7. **The Bund yield is flat while every other G7 yield rises.** Germany is the euro area's safe asset in a France scare, despite its own fiscal expansion.
8. **The UK overtook $1T of Treasuries just as Japan and China fell.** This likely reflects custodial and hedge-fund holdings (basis trades) replacing official buyers.

## 5. Mechanism chains

### 5.1 The "Japan sells Europe, not America" chain (Mallers thesis), tested link by link

| Link | Claim | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | BoJ hikes and JGB yields rise, so Japanese money is pulled home | BoJ to 1.25%, JGB 10y about 2.9 to 3.1%. Japanese net sold ¥5.34T of foreign bonds YTD (Reuters/MOF) | Partly confirmed. Mostly banks. Insurers "bring buyers back to JGBs, not money home" (Deutsche) |
| B | US steers Japan to sell Europe rather than Treasuries | US euro sale (signal). FIMA push. MoF pledges to use FIMA | Plausible as intent. But T0 shows Japan *official* sold Treasuries heavily (-$171B Feb to Aug) |
| C | France is the weakest link (foreign-held, no own central bank) | 36% held by foreign banks and non-banks, Japan about 5%. Allianz: "first victim of the Japanese repatriation trade" | Confirmed as vulnerability. Flow attribution contested (AFT: no Japanese selling seen. UBS: recycled abroad) |
| D | France cracks | Spread 87 to 157.5bp in 6 weeks. 24-year-high OAT yield. BNP -14% in a month. Riots. Moody's 23 Oct | Underway, not complete. TPI line (about 200bp) untested |
| E | The euro strains | EUR/USD 1.20 area (Jan) to 1.12. Record-ish euro shorts | Confirmed (moderate) |
| F | Germany loses its cheap shared currency | Euro *weaker*, German surplus *higher*, Bund a safe haven | Refuted so far. The chain would need a euro break-up scare (redenomination risk) to flip Germany's payoff |
| G | US then weakens the dollar vs a stronger Germany or DM | DXY +2.8% year on year | Not happening in 2026. Dollar weakness is being engineered only vs JPY/CNY |

Net: links A to E have real support. F and G do not. The thesis works better as "Europe is the shock absorber for US-Japan Treasury-market management" than as a deliberate plan to break France. The size of the US euro sale (about $1B against an OAT market of about €3.6T) rules out the sale *itself* as the mechanism. It was a signal at most.

### 5.2 The Treasury-market protection chain (stronger evidence)
War oil shock leads to global inflation and hikes. The yen falls (rate gap). Japan must intervene by selling dollar assets, so Treasury supply rises at a moment of 5%+ yields and a shrinking official holder base (-$254B since Feb). The US response has three parts: (a) a joint intervention so Japan sells less, (b) FIMA repo so Japan borrows dollars against Treasuries instead of selling them, (c) stablecoin T-bill demand as a new captive buyer (see L2). Observable test: FIMA usage on the H.4.1 during the next intervention, and Japan's TIC holdings stabilizing even while it intervenes.

### 5.3 Neutral reserve layer chain
Sanctions escalation (Russia 2022, Iran "Economic Outcast" 2026, which explicitly covers gold and digital assets) raises the confiscation risk of dollar and euro custody. Reserve managers respond in two ways: (a) buy gold, (b) *repatriate custody* of gold (France from NY, Poland's onshore push). Gold's reserve share rises mostly by price. Bitcoin stays marginal for central banks. Russia legalizes crypto for trade and citizens but keeps the CBR out. No G20 central bank is confirmed holding BTC in this lane's evidence. Gold is the neutral layer that is actually being built. Bitcoin is a talking point. Dollar stablecoins are the US counter-layer.

### 5.4 China-Europe market competition chain
US tariffs push Chinese exports elsewhere. With a record $938B surplus YTD, Europe becomes a key outlet. A weak euro (1.12) and a strong yuan (6.67) mean EUR/CNY has fallen sharply, making Chinese goods dearer in Europe. That partly offsets the diversion and reduces EU trade-defense pressure. Observable: EU anti-subsidy actions, China-EU trade data, EUR/CNY. (Evidence thin this round. See gaps.)

## 10. Bottom line for the energy-money question

The reserve system in 2026 is not moving to a new standard. It is splitting into three layers, each tested by a war-driven energy and rate shock:

1. **Dollar extension layer (US-led).** Treasuries plus FIMA plus stablecoin T-bill demand plus secondary sanctions.
2. **Neutral layer.** Physical gold in domestic custody. Bought by price-sensitive reserve managers, sold by those under war or FX stress (Russia, Turkey).
3. **Shock-absorber layer.** The euro and Europe's weakest sovereign, France, which take the adjustment when the US and Japan manage the yen and Treasury market.

Energy enters as the driver of the *rate shock*, not yet as money. Hormuz caused the inflation, the inflation caused the hikes, and the hikes exposed the weakest balance sheets (Japan's FX reserves, France's fiscal position). The strongest observable link to the energy question: whoever controls energy chokepoints can now set the *currency of passage* (the unconfirmed Iranian yuan-for-passage reports). That is the petrodollar mechanism in reverse, and it is the tripwire to watch.
