## 1. Key findings

1. **Kevin Warsh chairs the Fed.** Confirmed by the Senate 54-45 on 2026-05-13 and sworn in 2026-05-22 (T1). The Board now has Warsh, Jefferson, Bowman, Barr, Cook, Powell and Waller. Miran resigned (letter 2026-05-14). Cook is still a governor. Powell stayed on as a governor. His governor term runs to January 2028.
2. **The Fed hiked in 2026, against the President.** Path: cuts in Sept, Oct and Dec 2025 to 3.50-3.75%. Holds from January to July 2026. In July, Hammack, Kashkari and Logan dissented for a hike. Then a unanimous 12-0 hike on 2026-09-16 to 3.75-4.00% (T1). Most participants think another hike "would likely be appropriate by year end" (Sept minutes, T2). The SEP median has the funds rate at 4.1% at end 2026 and end 2027, and puts 2% PCE in 2029. Trump wanted cuts. His own pick raised rates.
3. **Treasury runs debt management short and leans on captive buyers.** Nominal coupon auction sizes have been frozen at the same levels since at least November 2025, with guidance for "at least the next several quarters" (T1). The bill share of marketable debt is 22.36% (Sept 2026, up from 21.53% a year earlier). Bills outstanding are $7.118T (T0). Net bill supply rose by more than $550B in July and August 2026 alone, and money funds absorbed about 85% of it (T2, Deputy Secretary Brooke).
4. **Treasury is doing a mild "twist" itself while the Fed refuses to.** Long-end buybacks went from $2B to "at least $4B" per operation from 2026-09-09. Operations actually ran at $6B in Sept and Oct and were fully filled (T0). Buybacks so far in 2026 total $230B, and about $510B since 2024 (T0). Long bonds are retired with money raised in bills. The Sept FOMC minutes record that uncertainty around "the U.S. Treasury's announcement and implementation of the buyback program" helped push term premiums up (T2).
5. **The July 31 2026 US yen intervention was tiny: $500 million.** The ESF's own August 2026 statement says yen holdings rose by $500M and euro holdings fell by $500M, from operations on 2026-07-31 that settled 2026-08-05 (T0). The Fed's SOMA did not take part. Usually the Fed matches the ESF. The press reported a notepad note saying "$5-10 bil", and Bessent said "whatever it takes". The money actually moved was roughly 1/10 to 1/20 of that. This was a signal, not a balance-sheet operation.
6. **The real purpose of the yen operation was protecting the Treasury market.** Japan's Treasury holdings fell from $1,239B (Feb 2026) to $1,068B (Aug 2026), down $171B or 14% (T0, TIC). Foreign official holdings of bills fell from $453B to $312B over the same months. Foreign official custody at the NY Fed is down $202B year on year (T0, H.4.1). Bessent asked the Fed to upsize the FIMA repo facility so Japan could raise dollars without selling Treasuries (T2, reported). The Sept minutes show no FOMC action on FIMA (T0 absence).
7. **Long yields are breaking higher on real rates, not inflation expectations.** On 2026-10-21 the 10y was at 5.13% and the 30y at 5.42%. Peaks were 5.31% for the 10y (Oct 5) and 5.67% for the 30y (Oct 7). The 10y TIPS real yield went from 1.94% (Jan 2) to 2.76%. The 10y breakeven is only about 2.37% (T3). The market is charging the government a higher real price. It is not pricing an inflation exit.
8. **Interest is above defense, and the gap is widening.** FY2026 net interest was $1,051.8B against $965.2B for national defense. Net interest was roughly equal to Medicare ($1,072.6B) (T0, MTS). Interest took 19.5% of receipts. The FY2026 deficit was $1,992.5B, up 12.2% from FY2025's $1,775.4B. The average rate on marketable debt was 3.518% (Sept 2026) against a 10y at 5.1% and bills at about 4.1-4.3%, so the average must keep rising (T0/T3).
9. **Inflation is quietly doing the stabilizing.** PCE inflation is about 3.6-3.8% and real growth about 2.3%, so nominal GDP growth is about 6%. The average debt cost is 3.5%. With a primary deficit near 2.9% of GDP, debt held by the public to GDP rises only about 0.4pp a year at 6% nominal growth, against about 1.4pp a year at 5% (inference, L1 arithmetic). This is passive repression happening in the data, although the Fed is fighting it.
10. **Captive demand is the most consistent revealed preference.** Evidence: the eSLR final rule (effective 2026-04-01), dealer Treasury inventories peaking above $550B in March 2026, bank Treasury holdings up more than $300B since end 2024, Fed bill purchases of more than $300B in 2026, stablecoins holding "nearly $200 billion" of bills, GENIUS rules being written for a 2027-01-18 effective date, a Treasury plan to invest TGA cash in overnight repo, and the FIMA push (T0/T1/T2).
11. **No asset-revaluation exit is visible.** US gold is still carried at $42.2222/oz: 261.5M oz, book value $11.04B, as of 2026-09-30 (T0). At about $4,100/oz (the TBAC figure for early August) the gold is worth about $1.07T. That is roughly 2.6% of the debt, or about one year of net interest. The Federal Register shows nothing on gold certificates since 2025. It shows no presidential action on the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve after the 2025-03-11 EO. Treasury press releases from Dec 2025 to Oct 2026 never mention gold revaluation or the SBR.
12. **The Mar-a-Lago playbook is only partly live.** Tariffs and the "imbalances" agenda are live: there is a G20 consensus on "excessive and persistent imbalances" (T2), the FX report puts 10 economies on the monitoring list (T1), and there was a joint intervention to strengthen the yen. Century bonds and user fees on foreign official holders do not appear in any refunding statement or Federal Register rule (T0/T1 absence). Miran is gone from both the Fed and the CEA. Phelan chairs the CEA.
13. **Fed independence held under maximum test.** Trump v. Cook: the application to remove Cook was denied on 2026-06-29. Roberts wrote the opinion, joined by Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh and Jackson (T1). The same day, Trump v. Slaughter ended for-cause protection at the FTC but explicitly left open a Federal Reserve exception (T1). Warsh says "independence is a two-way street" (T2). On the evidence, he acts like it.
14. **Warsh's doctrine is anti-QE and anti-forward-guidance, with "money matters".** He says unconventional policy should be used "sparingly, if at all". He wants to limit the Fed's "footprint in the Treasury market" and has set up a balance sheet task force (Dynan, Rajan, Stein). Dealers expect SOMA to drift toward "substantially shorter duration and a higher allocation to T-bills" (T2, TBAC minutes).
15. **Personnel signal.** David Zervos (ex-Jefferies) was appointed Counselor to the Secretary on 2026-09-28 (T1). He is a macro strategist known for market-friendly, dovish views. Read it as Treasury adding market-communication firepower ahead of the Nov 4 refunding, where a coupon-size decision is due (inference).

## 3. The Federal Reserve

### 3.1 Leadership and independence
- Warsh was confirmed 54-45 on 2026-05-13, the slimmest margin ever for a Fed chair per Bloomberg Law (T1/T4). Powell was chair pro tempore from May 15 to May 22. Warsh took the oath on 2026-05-22. The FOMC chose him unanimously as its chair (T1, Fed press release other20260522a).
- Miran sat from 2025-09-16 in the unexpired term ending 2026-01-31. He kept serving past expiry and resigned on 2026-05-14, effective when his successor (Warsh) was sworn in (T1). While on the Board he dissented for cuts in Jan, Mar and Apr 2026 (T1, FOMC statements).
- Nomination politics: Senator Tillis held up the Warsh nomination while the DOJ investigated Powell (T4, Bloomberg Law and banking press). Outcome of that probe: not verified.
- Trump v. Cook (25A312): the application was denied on 2026-06-29. Thomas dissented. Alito dissented, joined by Gorsuch. Barrett dissented (T1, docket). Cook stays.
- Trump v. Slaughter (25-332), also 2026-06-29: the FTC's for-cause protection was struck down. The syllabus says the Court left open that the Federal Reserve may not fall under the Myers rule "to the extent that it follows in the tradition of the First and Second Banks of the United States" (T1).
- Reported pressure: at the Sept press conference a reporter cited a presidential message "threatening to cut off trade to certain countries unless rates were lowered". Warsh declined to discuss talks with the President (T2 question, not independently verified).

- Warsh's statements are now very short and carry no forward guidance. "I'm not in the forward guidance business" (T2, Sept press conference). Guidance has moved into the minutes and the SEP, where "most participants assessed that another increase ... would likely be appropriate by year end" (T2).
- Inflation context: PCE inflation was 3.7% (12m) and 4.1% (6m annualized) per Jackson Hole. Under BEA's new method, staff estimated August at 3.6% headline and 3.2% core (T2). The drivers named are the Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz energy shock (Brent peaked near $126 in spring), tariffs, and AI-buildout goods prices (memory prices up about 300% y/y per TBAC) (T2).
- Warsh on why yields rose: "economic strength", "competition for capital" from hyperscalers, and "geopolitics". Asked "Is debt part of that issue?" he did not mention deficits (T2).

### 3.3 Balance sheet
- QT ended 2025-12-01 (T1). MBS principal is reinvested into bills. Reserve management purchases (RMPs) of bills are authorized "when appropriate", plus "if needed, other Treasury securities with remaining maturities of 3 years or less" (T1, directive in force since at least July 2026).
- Desk paused RMPs in the July to September intermeeting period (T2, Sept minutes). Fed bill holdings are $566B, up $370B y/y (T0). Treasury says the Fed bought more than $300B of bills in 2026 (T2, Brooke).
- Warsh's Balance Sheet Policy task force (Dynan, Rajan, Stein) is to "examine the costs, benefits, and institutional implications of the Federal Reserve's current balance sheet regime" (T1, 2026-07-09). A few participants want to limit "the Federal Reserve's footprint in the Treasury market" (T2, Sept minutes).
- The Fed did not participate in the yen intervention: "the System Open Market Account portfolio was not involved" (T2, Sept minutes). It did not upsize FIMA, at least through the Sept meeting (T0 absence).
- Jackson Hole doctrine (2026-08-28): "Short-term interest rates are the predominant tool ... Unconventional policies ... should otherwise be used sparingly, if at all." "Money matters." "The responsibility for 65 months of sustained, elevated inflation sits squarely with the central bank" (T2).

### 3.4 Miran at the Fed (relevant to the captive-demand thesis)
- "A Global Stablecoin Glut" (2025-11-07): Fed staff's interquartile range of private estimates puts stablecoins at "$1 trillion and $3 trillion by the end of the decade". He argued this pushes r* down and so justifies lower policy rates. "Stablecoins may become a multitrillion dollar elephant in the room" (T2).
- "Regulatory Dominance of the Federal Reserve's Balance Sheet" (2025-11-19): the Fed's large balance sheet is "downstream of the bank regulatory framework". Fix the rules and banks hold Treasuries directly instead of reserves (T2).
- "Prospects for Shrinking the Fed's Balance Sheet" (2026-03-26): a smaller balance sheet keeps "the duration profile of the public debt as a fiscal policy item" (T2). This is the clearest statement of the design: shrink the Fed, push duration management to Treasury, and use rule changes to make private balance sheets the buyers.

## 4. The Treasury

### 4.1 Issuance
- Coupon sizes unchanged quarter after quarter: 2y 69, 3y 58, 5y 70, 7y 44, 10y 42/39, 20y 16/13, 30y 25/22, FRN 28/30 ($B). Identical tables appear in the Feb, May and Aug 2026 refunding statements (T1). Guidance: "Treasury anticipates maintaining nominal coupon and FRN auction sizes for at least the next several quarters" (T1, 2026-08-05).
- New language: "Treasury is monitoring SOMA purchases of Treasury bills and growing demand for Treasury bills from the private sector" (T1). Treasury is explicitly matching supply to captive bill demand.
- Implied bill funding: +$409B in Jul-Sep 2026 and +$317B in Oct-Dec 2026 at constant coupons (T2, Treasury TBAC presentation). Treasury modeled a "Prorated" scenario that holds the bill share at 22.2% (T2).
- TBAC (2026-08-04): the median dealer forecast "implies a $1.45 trillion funding shortfall in FY2027-28 based on current coupon auction sizes and privately-held bill supply". TBAC unanimously recommends no change now. It "continues to believe that increases in coupon issuance could be warranted in FY2027". It asks Treasury to update forward guidance "to preserve flexibility" (T2). Next refunding: 2026-11-04.
- New plumbing ideas: invest TGA cash in overnight repo (dealers were surveyed, "a plurality ... prefers a stable repo investment allocation"). Quarterly new-issue 7-year notes. Intraday repo. Blockchain and tokenization for intraday liquidity (T2, TBAC minutes).

### 4.2 Buybacks
- Aug 2026 quarter plan: up to $38B of liquidity support and up to $25B of cash management (T1). On 2026-08-19 long-end operations (10-20y and 20-30y) were raised from a $2B max to "at least $4B", effective Sept 9 to Nov 4 (T1). Actual max per operation was $6B in late Sept and October, and the 10-20y and 20-30y operations were filled at $6B (T0).
- Long-end buybacks by month in 2026 ($B accepted): Jan 6.9, Feb 6.7, Mar 4.4, Apr 6.0, May 2.8, Jun 7.3, Jul 8.4, Aug 4.0, Sep 12.8, Oct (to 21st) 18.8 (T0). Brooke: "we have seen the greatest sponsorship ... for buybacks of longer maturity bonds, and accordingly we have increased the frequency and sizes" (T2).
- Scale check: $25B of long-end buybacks in Q3 against $375B of net coupon issuance (T0). The "twist" is small in size but clearly growing (inference).

### 4.3 Bank and dealer balance sheets
- eSLR final rule (2025-11-25, effective 2026-04-01, optional from 2026-01-01). The stated aim is to "reduce disincentives ... to engage in lower-risk activities, such as intermediating in U.S. Treasury markets". The depository-level eSLR is capped at 1%, so the total is no more than 4% (T1).
- Results claimed by Treasury: dealer Treasury inventories peaked above $550B in March 2026, and bank Treasury holdings are up more than $300B since end 2024 (T2, Brooke 2026-09-22).
- Capital "modernization" proposals were published 2026-03-27: the standardized approach re-proposal, GSIB surcharge and Category I/II (T1, Federal Register). Stress test transparency rule finalized 2026-09-30 (T1).
- Counter-signal: Bessent's "Reset on Liquidity Regulation" (2026-03-03, delivered by McKernan) complains that "25 percent of large banks' balance sheets are allocated to safe assets" and wants liquidity rules to give "capped recognition" of discount-window capacity. The goal is to free bank balance sheets to lend to "AI hyperscalers" (T2). So the bank-regulation channel is not a plain "force banks to buy Treasuries" story. It does two things. It enlarges dealer intermediation capacity (eSLR). It moves liquidity insurance from banks' Treasury holdings to the Fed's discount window. The second step moves the Fed closer to being the standing backstop of the system.

### 4.4 Dollar and FX
- FX report (2026-07-23): no manipulator. Monitoring list: China, Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Thailand, Singapore, Vietnam, Germany, Ireland, Switzerland. Bessent: "Treasury is committed to aggressively and vigilantly monitoring and combatting unfair currency practices" (T1/T2).
- G20 Asheville (Sept 2026): consensus "to address excessive and persistent imbalances" (T2, Bessent House testimony 2026-09-15). IMFC statement (2026-10-14): the IMF must strengthen analysis of "the role non-market policies and practices and exchange rates play in driving external imbalances" (T2).
- Bessent at the Economic Club of NY (2026-06-23): the dollar's role "bestows enormous advantages, among them lower borrowing costs ... enhanced sanctions capabilities". Its benefits "are no longer unconditional". Countries "cannot participate in the dollar-based financial system while serving as conduits for the evasion of sanctions" (T2). This is the dollar as conditional access, a Miran-paper theme.
- Dollar path: up about 1.5% YTD by early August per TBAC, then a moderate decline into September. The Fed Desk explicitly said the yen intervention "directly contributed to dollar depreciation" (T2/T3).
- "Strong dollar policy" in 2026: no on-record 2026 use found in Treasury releases from Dec 2025 to Oct 2026. Earlier (2025) statements could not be re-verified this session.

### 4.5 The July 31 2026 yen operation (detail for L5 and the A8 audit)
- Sequence. On 2026-07-28 the yen hit a 40-year low. Japan's finance minister said Bessent had agreed "decisive steps" could be taken. Treasury told banks to "stand ready". On July 31 the NY Fed, as Treasury's agent, sold euros from the ESF for yen through Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley (T4, via the Warren letter citing Reuters and FT). Japan said the operation was done under the September 2025 joint statement of finance ministers (T4).
- Size: $500M (T0, ESF August statement, note 2: "Yen FCDAs increased by $500 million, while Euro FCDAs decreased by $500 million, following operations undertaken on July 31, 2026 that settled on August 5, 2026").
- Official justification. "Friday's coordinated foreign exchange actions countered disorderly yen movements" (T2). The US will do "whatever it takes" to support Japan (T2, CNBC 2026-08-04 via Reuters/Kitco). The reply to Warren (Aug 27): disorderly yen moves can destabilize markets and "raise U.S. borrowing costs". "Japan owes Treasury nothing." No new appropriation was used and no credit was extended (T2, reported).
- The ECB was not told in advance (T4, FT via the Warren letter).
- FIMA: Bessent called FIMA "an important backstop" and urged the Fed to upsize it. Japan's finance minister said Tokyo intends to use FIMA for future interventions (T2/T4). No FOMC action as of the Sept minutes (T0 absence).

## 5. Miran's "User's Guide" (Nov 2024) and the Mar-a-Lago Accord: what is implemented

Paper content (T2, pre-office). From prior knowledge: the PDF did not render this session and the URL is listed for L6 to re-pull. Tariffs as leverage. A multilateral or unilateral currency arrangement to weaken the dollar. Pushing reserve holders to term out into very long or century bonds. A "user fee" withheld from interest paid to foreign official holders. Use of IEEPA. Linking security guarantees and Fed swap lines to cooperation.

## 6. Stablecoins as captive T-bill buyers
- GENIUS Act implementation. Treasury NPRMs on state regimes (2026-04-01), illicit finance (2026-04-08) and more (2026-08-17). Fed proposals for Board-supervised issuers (2026-09-24) and CIP (2026-06-18). A Fed "payment account" proposal (2026-05-20). Expected effective date 2027-01-18 (T1).
- Current scale: stablecoin providers "already own nearly $200 billion of Treasury bills and other close-to-maturity Treasury securities. More important ... is the significant growth opportunity" (T2, Brooke 2026-09-22).
- Official projections: Fed staff's range of private estimates is $1-3T by 2030 (T2, Miran). TBAC (Apr 2025) floated about $2T by 2028 (T4 via secondary reporting). Bessent's own 2025 figures ($2T, and the about $3.7T cited in commentary) could not be re-verified this session. Treat them as unverified.
- Mechanism: a $1-3T stablecoin float at a 50-80% bill share means about $0.5-2.4T of new bill demand by 2030. Set against a $1.45T FY27-28 funding gap, stablecoins could cover a material part of it, but only if growth arrives (inference).
- Bessent framing: GENIUS will "cement the role of the U.S. dollar as the world's reserve currency" (T2, 2026-08-17).

## 7. Gold and Bitcoin
- Gold: the statutory book value is still $42.2222/oz on 261,498,926 oz (T0). No rule, EO or Treasury statement on revaluation was found (T1/T2 absence). Market context from TBAC: gold peaked near $5,400 in late January 2026, then fell to about $4,100 in early August. TBAC (Feb 2026) noted gold up 67% y/y. Revaluation at $4,100 would create about $1.06T in Fed gold certificate credit to the TGA. That is a one-time cash windfall of roughly one year of net interest. It does not change the debt stock.
- Bitcoin: EO 14233 (2025-03-11) established the SBR and Digital Asset Stockpile (T1). There have been no presidential documents on it since (T1 absence, Federal Register API). No Treasury press release from Dec 2025 to Oct 2026 discusses SBR holdings or purchases (T2 absence). Treasury's 2026 bitcoin mentions are about sanctions (for example Iran's Hormuz Safe accepting BTC). Status of any budget-neutral acquisition: not verified.

The synthesis is a division of labor:
1. The Fed under Warsh guards price stability and shrinks its Treasury footprint. It refuses quantitative easing, FIMA upsizing and FX participation.
2. Treasury takes over duration management. It freezes coupons, issues more bills, and buys back long bonds.
3. Rules route demand to the short end: money funds, stablecoins, bank portfolios and dealer balance sheets.
4. Foreign official holders are kept from dumping. Partner currencies are supported (Japan) and the Fed is pressed to backstop them (FIMA).
5. Inflation above target quietly helps the ratio.

This is "financial repression lite through the plumbing" plus "growth hope". It is neither a classic central-bank repression nor an accord.

## 10. Anomalies (deviation from the default move)

1. **The US intervened with euros, not dollars, and only $500M.** Default: a joint G7 intervention selling dollars, split between the ESF and SOMA, sized in billions. Deviations: currency (euro), size (tiny), no Fed participation, no ECB heads-up. Reading: the US wanted a signal that it backs Japan's defense, so Japan sells less of its Treasuries. It did this without adding dollars, without the Fed, and without committing real money. Selling euros keeps US reserves neutral in size and avoids an explicit "weak dollar" act (inference). For the A8 audit: a $500M euro sale is too small to "sell France". The Mallers mechanism fails on size even if the signaling logic holds.
2. **The Fed declined the SOMA half of the intervention.** Historically the Fed often matched the ESF. Under Warsh it did not (T2 minutes, T4 precedent). That is an institutional boundary being drawn.
3. **Treasury doubled to tripled long-end buybacks mid-quarter (Aug 19), outside the normal refunding cycle.** The default is to change at refundings. The timing came after the yen episode and during a long-end selloff. Its own minutes say this added term-premium uncertainty.
4. **A Trump-appointed chair hiked rates within four months of taking office.** The default expectation was a dovish loyalist. Revealed preference: Warsh is putting credibility before the President's wishes.
5. **Treasury is planning to lend TGA cash into repo.** Treasury acting as a cash provider in money markets is a quasi-central-bank function, financed by bills.
6. **Japan cut its Treasuries by $171B in six months while the UK, Belgium, Canada and Cayman (hedge fund and basis-trade proxies) added.** Official money is leaving. Leveraged private money is replacing it.
7. **Gold went from about $5,400 to about $4,100 in H1 2026 while yields rose.** That is not a debasement-panic pattern. It fits rising real rates (T3, TBAC).

## 11. Mechanism chains

**Chain 1: the short-end captive loop.**
Deficit about $2T a year and coupons frozen → bills fill the gap (+$409B in Q3) → money funds (~$8T AUM), stablecoins (~$200B, GENIUS from 2027), bank portfolios and Fed RMPs absorb them → bill yields stay near the policy rate → Treasury buys back long bonds → the public holds less duration → the term premium is contained at the margin. Weak point: the government's interest bill now resets with the policy rate. Each Fed hike raises costs within months (bills are 22.4% of the stock and FRNs are another $0.7T). This creates a growing fiscal cost of Fed hawkishness and so a political incentive to lean on the Fed. Test: the bill-share trend and the size of the Nov 4 buybacks.

**Chain 2: keep Japan from selling.**
Yen at a 40-year low → Japan must defend the yen → the default is selling dollars, meaning Treasuries (holdings already down $171B) → US yields rise → the US supports with a signal ($500M euro sale) and presses the Fed to upsize FIMA (dollars against Treasuries as collateral) → Japan can intervene without dumping. If the Fed refuses FIMA, Japan keeps selling and the long end stays heavy. Test: Japan's TIC line, H.4.1 foreign official custody, FIMA repo usage on H.4.1.

**Chain 3: Warsh's discipline versus fiscal arithmetic.**
Warsh hikes → bill and FRN costs rise fast → net interest climbs (already $1.05T) → the deficit widens → more bills → more sensitivity to the funds rate. That loop ends in one of three ways. Inflation falls and Warsh cuts. The Fed accepts a quieter role on the long end while Treasury manages duration. Or political pressure escalates (Powell's seat in 2028, other vacancies). Test: whether the Oct and Dec FOMCs hike again, and the Treasury reaction.

**Chain 4: inflation as silent payer.**
Nominal GDP growth (~6%) exceeds the average debt cost (3.5%) → the debt ratio drifts up only about 0.4pp a year despite a ~2.9% primary deficit → if inflation falls to 2% and real rates stay at 2.7%, average costs converge upward toward about 4.5% → the ratio climbs faster. So disinflation without lower real rates worsens the debt dynamics. That is the trap the captive-demand strategy tries to avoid.
