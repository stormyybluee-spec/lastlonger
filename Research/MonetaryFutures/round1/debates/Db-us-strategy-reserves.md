## 1. Proponent

The owner is right on the big picture: the US is not choosing between Set 1 and Set 2. It is wiring Set 2 into Set 1 so that new digital money becomes new demand for old debt. The strongest evidence:

1. **Treasury is funding the deficit at the short end and engineering buyers for it (T0/T1).** Nominal coupon sizes have been frozen for at least four refundings with guidance "for at least the next several quarters" (L1-11). Bills are 22.36% of marketable debt, up from 21.53% a year earlier, at $7.118T (L1-12). Net bill supply rose by more than $550B in July and August 2026 and money funds took about 85% (L1-16). The August refunding statement says Treasury "is monitoring SOMA purchases of Treasury bills and growing demand for Treasury bills from the private sector". That is supply being sized to captive demand, in Treasury's own binding words.
2. **Every regulatory lever points the same way (T1).** The eSLR final rule (effective 2026-04-01) exists to ease bank Treasury intermediation (L1-18). Bank Treasury holdings are up more than $300B since end 2024 and dealer inventories peaked above $550B (L1-19, T2). The GENIUS Act forces payment stablecoin reserves into cash, deposits, Treasuries of 93 days or less and bill repo (L2-01). The 93-day cap is not a safety necessity (any Treasury with haircuts would be safe). It ties stablecoin growth to exactly the part of the curve where Treasury is issuing.
3. **Treasury is shrinking long-duration supply itself (T0).** Long-end buybacks went from $2B to "at least $4B" per operation and actually ran at $6B in Sept and Oct (L1-13). 2026 buybacks total $230B, about $510B since 2024 (L1-14).
4. **Foreign official holders are being kept in the tent (T0/T2).** Bessent pushed the Fed to upsize the FIMA repo facility so Japan can raise dollars against Treasuries instead of selling them, and Japan's MoF said it "plans to use" FIMA (L5-12). The 31 July yen operation was paid in euros, so the US sold no dollars and no Treasuries (L1-08).
5. **The Secretary chose the 1863 analogy himself (T2, verified this session).** The National Currency Act and National Bank Act of 1863 to 1864 created national bank notes that had to be backed by deposits of US government bonds, a captive buyer for Civil War debt. Bessent compares GENIUS to that act.
6. **The digital dollar is private by statute, which is the Treasury-friendly version (T1).** The CBDC ban (H.R. 6644, law 2026-07-11, to 2030-12-31) forbids a Fed retail digital currency but exempts private dollar tokens (L2-08). A CBDC would be a Fed liability that buys nothing. A GENIUS stablecoin is a private liability that must buy bills.
7. **Set 2 is already settling inside Set 1 plumbing (T1).** DTC's tokenization pilot includes Treasuries, Nasdaq's tokenized shares settle T+1 through NSCC/DTC (L2-14), Kraken got a limited Fed master account with no discount window and no interest on reserves (L2-16), and the Fed targets "payment accounts" for Q4 2026. Even Tether, the largest offshore issuer, backs USDT with $115B of T-bills, 146t of gold and 98,933 BTC (L7 inputs, T0 via T4). One token, all three assets: that is the merged board in miniature.
8. **Gold is being rebuilt as the neutral layer between states (T0).** Central banks bought 289t in Q2 2026 while the price fell (L5-04). At end-2025 prices gold was 27% of official reserves against 22% for Treasuries (ECB, L5-02). France pulled all 129t out of the NY Fed (L5-29). Poland targets 700t.

Proponent's summary: the revealed US strategy is "captive demand through the plumbing", Set 2 is the newest pipe, and gold is the neutral floor under the whole thing.

## 2. Skeptic

**Attack on the strongest point (captive demand as a designed strategy).** Short issuance is the default for a Treasury running a $2T deficit with 10y yields above 5%. It does not need a grand design. Three facts cut against "designed captive demand":
- Bessent's own liability-rule speech (2026-03-03) complains that "25 percent of large banks' balance sheets are allocated to safe assets" and wants to free them to lend to AI hyperscalers (L1-31). That is the opposite of forcing banks to hold Treasuries.
- TBAC expects a $1.45T funding shortfall in FY2027 to 2028 at current sizes and "continues to believe that increases in coupon issuance could be warranted in FY2027" (L1-15). The bill tilt may be a pause, not a doctrine. The 4 Nov 2026 refunding can falsify the strategy within two weeks.
- Bessent's actual 1863 words are about a "national framework for bank-issued currency". He does not mention bonds. The captive-demand reading is the listener's inference.

**Scale.** Stablecoins are $301 to 305B, 4.3% of bills and about 0.6% of debt held by the public (L7-16). Tether's direct bills are 1.6% of bills. Supply peaked near $320B in May 2026 and stalled even after GENIUS passed, well short of the OCC's $500B year-end projection (L6 finding 8). Even Tether's co-founder Collins says stablecoins are "one extra customer buying a couple hundred billion dollars of treasuries" (L6). Calling this a "petrodollar" is a category error at today's size.

**The captive strategy is not working where it matters.** Japan cut Treasury holdings by $171B from Feb to Aug 2026, China by $104B, foreign official holdings fell $254B (L5-07). The 10y rose from 4.68% (30 Jul) to 5.13% (21 Oct), the 30y to 5.42%, the 10y real yield from 1.94% to 2.76% (L1-23). Leveraged and custodial holders (UK above $1T, Belgium, Canada, Cayman) replaced official ones, which is a weaker base, not a stronger one.

**The Fed is not playing.** Warsh hiked 12-0 on 2026-09-16, refused the SOMA half of the yen trade, did not upsize FIMA and calls QE something to use "sparingly, if at all" (L1-03, L1-07, L1-30). Captive demand without the central bank is a partial strategy.

**Gold and Bitcoin.** Gold is 27% below its January high and rose as a reserve share mostly by price (ECB: 16% at end-2023 prices). H1 2026 official buying of 345t was the lowest first half since 2022, and Russia was the largest seller (L5-04). Bitcoin has no G20 central bank holder in the lanes, the US has bought zero, and crypto-collateralised lending is $56B, down 40% from peak (L7-12).

**France.** The literal "sold France" story fails on size ($500M against roughly €3.3T of French debt), timing (spread flat in August, wider only from mid-September) and outcome (dollar up, US yields up). See Db-06.

**Base rate.** Since 1945 every prediction of a near-term replacement of the dollar system has failed. The default prior for H0 is "same system, more plumbing".

## 3. Accountant

Whose asset is whose liability, and where does the money come from?

1. **Stablecoin chain.** Holder's asset (token) = issuer's liability. Issuer's asset (bill or repo) = Treasury's liability (bill) or a dealer's liability (repo, which funds the dealer's own Treasury position). Net new demand for Treasuries arises only when the dollars entering the stablecoin did not already sit in Treasury-holding vehicles. Three cases:
   - A US money fund investor switches to USDC: near zero net new Treasury demand (one bill holder replaces another).
   - A US bank depositor switches: the bank loses deposits and reserves, and may sell securities. Net effect small and it shrinks bank credit capacity.
   - A foreign saver swaps local currency for USDT or USDC: genuinely new dollar and bill demand, funded by a fall in local-currency deposits or local reserves. This is the only "petrodollar-like" leg, and it is a transfer of seigniorage from weak-currency countries to US issuers and the US Treasury.
   So the gross bill holdings of stablecoins (about $200B per Treasury, L1-17) overstate the net addition to Treasury demand. No lane quantifies the split. Gap.
2. **Circle is mostly repo, not bills.** USDC reserves: $49.6B reverse repo, $14.5B T-bills, $10.4B deposits (L7 inputs, T4). Repo is indirect Treasury support (it finances dealers' holdings) but it is not a direct bill bid.
3. **Who keeps the float income.** GENIUS bans issuer-paid yield "solely" for holding (L2). At bill rates near 4.1 to 4.3%, a $300B float earns roughly $12 to 13B a year (arithmetic, judge). Part goes back through exchange "rewards" (PayPal 4%, Coinbase One 3.75%, Kraken+ up to 4.5%, L2). The rest is the toll. Holders who receive nothing are paying an inflation tax equal to the bill rate.
4. **Captive demand does not reduce the debt.** It lowers the rate on the marginal bill relative to where it would be. But bills reprice with the policy rate. With 35.7% of marketable debt repricing within a year (L7-05), each Fed hike now raises federal interest within months. That is the hidden cost of the bill strategy and the political pressure point on the Fed.
5. **Interest escalator (T0 plus model).** Average rate on marketable debt 3.518% against a blended market rate of about 4.88% (L7-04). If yields hold, interest on marketable debt goes from about $1.12T (FY2026 run rate) to about $1.6T in year 3 and $1.9T in year 5 (4.8% of GDP). FY2026 net interest $1,051.8B exceeded defense ($965.2B) and took 19.5% of receipts (L7-03).
6. **Financial repression arithmetic.** Effective nominal rate (net interest over debt held by the public) 3.23% against 10-year nominal GDP growth of 5.70% (L7). With debt held by the public at about 100% of GDP, that gap erodes the ratio by about 2.5pp a year, close to offsetting the 2.92% primary deficit. L1 estimates the ratio rising only about 0.4pp a year at 6% nominal growth. At the margin, though, the blended market rate (4.88%) equals trend nominal growth (4.93%), so the inflation escape valve closes as 70% of the debt reprices within five years (L7-05). Passive repression is real today and fading.
7. **Gold revaluation balances, but only once.** 261.5M oz at $42.2222 = $11.04B book. At about $4,100 the market value is about $1.07T (L1-24, L7-11). Revaluation would let Treasury issue new gold certificates to the Fed and credit the TGA with about $1.06T: a one-off cash windfall equal to about 2.6% of gross debt, or one year of net interest. It does not cut the stock unless used for buybacks, and when spent it adds reserves to the banking system. It does not touch a deficit that recurs every year.
8. **Bitcoin stockpile in debt terms.** 198,000 to 328,000 forfeited BTC (L2) at $84,040 = about $16.6B to $27.6B (arithmetic). That is 0.04% to 0.07% of gross debt. It is not a fiscal tool at any plausible H0 price.
9. **The yen operation balance sheet.** US leg $500M (euro assets down, yen assets up, ESF size unchanged). Japan's leg ¥15.4T, about $97B (round0). The US share is about 0.5%. Japan's reserves fell from $1,369.8B to $1,178.4B (L5-09). The money that moved was Japan's. The US signal was costless to US balance sheets, which is the point.
10. **Claims that do not balance.** "Stablecoins solve the $40T debt": refuted by item 1 and scale (under 1% of debt). "Gold x10 equals world debt" and "BTC x10 collateral": refuted in L7 (ratio wanders 12x to 53x, crypto lending runs at about 0.05x BTC value). "Selling France lowered US yields": US 10y +45bp since 30 Jul.

## 4. Historian

| Precedent | What happened | What it predicts here |
|---|---|---|
| **1863 to 1864 National Currency / National Bank Acts** | National bank notes had to be backed by US bonds deposited with the Comptroller. A private-issue currency was made into a captive buyer of war debt. A 10% tax in 1865 killed state bank notes | GENIUS is the closest structural twin: private issuers, federal charter, reserve rule pointing at federal debt, and the 2028 ban on non-permitted stablecoins plays the role of the 1865 tax. Prediction: compliant tokens crowd out offshore ones by H1, and the issuer set consolidates around banks and a few licensed firms |
| **Free banking era 1837 to 1863** | Many private notes of uneven quality, discounts by distance, failures | Pre-GENIUS stablecoins and offshore USDT. Predicts discounting and runs on weaker issuers if supervision is patchy |
| **1933 to 1934 Gold Reserve Act** | Gold nationalised, revalued from $20.67 to $35, profit used to create the ESF | The ESF used in July 2026 is the child of that act. Revaluation is the one precedent for a gold-based fiscal windfall, and it happened in a crisis with deflation, not with 3.6% inflation. Predicts: no revaluation in H0 unless a crisis (S4) |
| **1942 to 1951 yield cap and the 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord** | Fed pegged yields, inflation eroded war debt, then the Accord restored Fed independence | Warsh has floated a new Fed-Treasury accord giving Treasury more say over balance sheet size (L2). Classic repression needs a cooperative central bank. Today's Fed is hiking. Predicts repression arrives with a personnel change, not before |
| **Eurodollar market 1950s to 1970s** | Private offshore dollar deposits grew outside US rules. Washington tolerated them because they extended dollar demand | The best analog for offshore USDT: a private dollar the US does not issue but benefits from. Predicts the US prefers to license and gate (Sec. 18 comparability) rather than ban |
| **1971 Nixon shock** | Gold window closed, dollar devalued, a decade of inflation | The lesson for S4: when the anchor breaks, the dollar did not lose its role. Its holders lost purchasing power |
| **1974 petrodollar recycling** | Oil surpluses recycled into Treasuries and US banks, backed by security ties | The stablecoin "petrodollar" analogy is about function (foreign dollar demand recycled into Treasuries) not size. The Saudi mBridge exit (reported, unverified) shows security ties still steer reserve choices |
| **1985 Plaza and 1987 Louvre** | Coordinated dollar weakening, mostly against the yen and DM, then a halt | 2026 is a selective mini-Plaza aimed at Asian currencies (yen support, yuan at 6.67), done ad hoc and without a G5 communique. Plaza needed Fed easing. Predicts no broad devaluation while the Fed hikes |
| **1995 reverse Plaza and 1998 joint yen intervention** | US bought yen with Japan in 1998 (selling dollars) | July 2026 is the first US yen support since 1998, but funded in euros. That novelty is the "first time" kernel of the owner's story |
| **2008 and 2020 swap lines, FIMA repo (created 2020)** | Fed became global dollar lender of last resort | FIMA is the tool Bessent wants upsized. Precedent says the Fed expands it in a crisis, not in calm. Predicts FIMA upsizing only if a disorderly episode hits |
| **Technocracy 1930s energy certificates** | Proposal to replace money with energy units. Never adopted | S3 has a failed precedent. L7 shows a fixed-kWh unit would be inflationary as energy gets cheaper |

Historian's bottom line: every reserve transition took decades and happened after a war or a crisis broke the old holder base. The pattern since 1863 is that the US absorbs new forms of money into federal debt demand rather than letting them compete. Precedent favours S1 in H0 and makes S4 the only route to a fast change.

### 6.2 Reasoning per sub-thesis

**Db-01 Captive demand. Confirmed, 75.** Decisive evidence is T0/T1: frozen coupons with binding guidance, bill share rising to 22.36%, the refunding statement's own words about sizing bills to private and SOMA demand, eSLR, GENIUS 93-day reserves, $230B of 2026 buybacks, the FIMA push and the euro-funded yen operation that avoided selling Treasuries. The pattern is Confirmed. Whether it is a "design" or the path of least resistance is Plausible: the Skeptic is right that short issuance is the default with a $2T deficit, and Bessent's liquidity speech shows he also wants banks to hold fewer safe assets. The 1863 quote is verified T2, but it names the framework, not bond-backing, so it is intent evidence of moderate weight (the choice of analogy is still telling). Strongest dissent: TBAC expects coupon increases in FY2027, so the bill tilt may be temporary. Falsifier: the 4 Nov 2026 refunding raises coupon sizes materially and drops the "several quarters" guidance.

**Db-02 Stablecoins as the petrodollar of the 2020s. Plausible in function for H1, Refuted on scale for H0. Confidence 55.** Now: $301 to 305B, 4.3% of bills, about $200B of bill and short Treasury holdings, about 0.6% of debt held by the public. Growth stalled near $320B since May. Offshore USDT is 61% of supply. In H0 the "too small to matter" side wins for the debt and the "petrodollar" side wins only as a direction of travel. H1: Fed staff's range of private estimates is $1 to 3T by 2030 (Miran speech, T2). At a 50 to 80% bill share that is $0.5 to 2.4T of bill demand, against a $1.45T FY2027-28 funding gap (L1). At the top of that range stablecoins become a structural front-end buyer next to money funds. The deeper petrodollar parallel is political: the dollars that matter come from foreign savers leaving weak currencies (RBI and PBoC both warn of this, L5). The Accountant's netting point means gross holdings overstate new demand. Dissent: Collins (an issuer) says they "can't solve" the debt, and he is right. Tripwires: supply above $500B, compliant share after 2027-01-18, stablecoins above 10% of bills.

**Db-03 Private rails vs public CBDC. Confirmed (split), 80. Meaning Plausible.** T1 on both sides of the US: GENIUS plus the H.R. 6644 ban to 2030-12-31 with a carve-out for private dollar tokens. China keeps digital money public (e-CNY framework from 2026-01-01) and blocks private RMB stablecoins (PBoC 2025 crackdown T1/T4, CSRC 2026 ban single-source T4). The EU does both (MiCA plus a digital euro aimed at 2029). What the split means: (a) the US converts digital dollar demand into Treasury demand and keeps banks and licensed firms as the distributors, (b) control is not given up: issuers must be able to freeze (FinCEN/OFAC proposal) and the Fed keeps settlement (payment accounts, Kraken), so "private" means private issuance, not private control, (c) China keeps data and capital-flow control, which limits e-CNY reach outside state-to-state deals, (d) for the rest of the world the contest is US private apps versus state rails, and the US rail can reach individuals without a host government's permission, which is why RBI and PBoC object. Dissent: the US ban sunsets in 2030 and could flip in a crisis.

**Db-04 Gold as neutral reserve layer. Confirmed, 75.** T0: Q2 289t bought into a falling price, Poland and China leading, France repatriating 129t, 89% of central banks expecting official gold to rise. The ECB's 27% share is mostly price: 16% at end-2023 prices. Say/do gaps are real: Russia sold 22t, Turkey sold, H1 345t was the weakest first half since 2022. Reading: gold is the layer states use for settlement-of-last-resort and sanction insurance, accumulated by value-sensitive reserve managers, not a monetary standard. It is not coming back as a peg (gold x10 = debt is a coincidence, L7-09). The US itself keeps gold at $42.22 and does not use it. Dissent: a falling price plus record official buying could equally be a few large buyers (China, Poland) and does not prove a broad move. Gap: Q3 2026 WGC data (due late Oct).

**Db-05 Bitcoin's H0 role. Confirmed, 80.** The US holds 198,000 to 328,000 forfeited BTC, has bought none, missed its own EO deadlines, and the White House (Witt, 2026-10-14) says purchases need Congress. ARMA, as reported, bars borrowing, taxes and pledging federal assets and imposes a 20-year hold. No G20 central bank holding is established. Collateral use is tiny ($56B of all crypto-backed lending). Price behaviour: rose while gold fell May to Sept, fell in hawkish episodes, 52-week range $57.7k to $116k. Ruling: in H0 Bitcoin is a risk asset with political status and a frozen sovereign stockpile. It is "reserve" only in name. Its live monetary niches are sanctions evasion (Iran, Russia) and private savings in weak-currency countries. See Db-11 for the future case.

**Db-06 "Bessent sold France to help Japan". Literal: Refuted, 85. Narrow: Plausible, 50.** Combined evidence: the US leg was $500M (ESF statement, T0), about 0.5% of Japan's ¥15.4T operation and trivial against roughly €3.3T of French debt. The OAT-Bund spread averaged 82bp in August against 78bp in July and blew out only from mid-September (87bp on 10 Sep to 157.5bp on 21 Oct), after the Fed hike (16 Sep), the BoJ hike (18 Sep), the 2027 budget fight and the Hormuz oil shock. Japanese investors were net buyers of OATs June to August (Morgan Stanley via MoF data, T4), non-residents' share of French government securities rose to 58.4% at end-June (Banque de France, L6), and France's debt agency says it has not seen Japanese selling. Germany is winning (surplus €146.0B Jan-Aug vs €140.4B, Bund flat as a haven). The dollar rose (DXY +2.8% y/y, EUR/USD 1.170 to 1.123 since 21 Aug), and US 10y yields rose 45bp, so the US debt was not helped. "Not priced in" is refuted: the short-Europe trade is crowded (UBS, Allianz). What survives is the narrow version (card Db-06N): the US chose a funding currency (euros) and a policy mix (pressing BoJ hikes, FIMA instead of Treasury sales) that pushed the cost of yen defence and Japanese portfolio rotation away from the dollar and Treasuries and toward the euro area, where France is the most exposed and most Japan-held link, and Bessent openly welcomes the resulting flows into Treasuries ("money flow out of some countries of concern", 20 Oct). Intent to harm France is not shown. The likeliest reason for euros is operational: euros were the ESF's largest liquid foreign asset. Decisive pending tests: the NY Fed Q3 FX report (mid-Nov), Japan MoF quarterly detail (2 to 9 Nov) on which currencies Japan sold, AFT holder data by nationality.

**Db-07 Mar-a-Lago in practice. Selective: Plausible, 65 (as a description of 2026). Broad devaluation: Refuted for 2026, 80.** The live parts: tariffs, a G20 "imbalances" consensus, a 10-economy FX monitoring list, the US backing yen strength, the yuan at 6.67 (+6.35% y/y, strongest since 2022), the dollar framed as conditional access ("no longer unconditional"). The dead parts: century bonds and foreign-holder user fees (absent from every refunding and the Federal Register), Miran out of office, the Fed hiking. DXY is up 2.8% y/y. Outlook: selective weakness against Asian surplus currencies continues in H0. A broad devaluation needs the Fed to ease into it, so its trigger is Fed personnel or a crisis (2027 to 2028 at the earliest). Judge's estimate: broad dollar decline of 10% or more on DXY by end 2028 about 30%, mainly through S4 paths. Dissent: Plaza in 1985 also started with a strong dollar and a hawkish-looking Fed, and the yuan rise shows a quiet bargain may already be in place.

**Db-08 Fiscal path. Plausible, 60.** The government's revealed path is a three-part muddle: captive demand at the short end, passive inflation doing the stabilizing work (nominal growth about 5.7 to 6% against a 3.2 to 3.5% average cost), and hope for AI growth. None of the three is enough alone. Passive repression fades as 70% of the debt reprices within five years at about 4.9%, interest heads to $1.6T in three years and $1.9T in five if yields hold (L7). The market is not pricing an inflation exit (10y breakeven 2.37%, the selloff is in real yields). AI fixes the arithmetic only if spending does not rise with it and real rates do not follow growth (L7: with r rising one-for-one, debt reaches about 129% of GDP, the same as no AI). Default or explicit restructuring has no evidence (no user fee, no coercive term-out) and the judge puts it below 5% in H0 and H1. Ruling: in H1 the likeliest exit is **managed financial repression**, meaning inflation modestly above coupons plus regulatory demand for bills plus a Fed whose leadership becomes more cooperative after 2028, rather than either AI salvation or a hard default. A disorderly version (S4) is the main alternative. Dissent: Warsh's Fed is hiking against the President today, and if inflation falls to 2% with real rates at 2.7%, the debt ratio climbs faster (L1 chain 4), which forces fiscal action instead of repression.

**Db-09 Scenarios. Speculative (judgment), 50.** See 6.3.

**Db-10 The owner's merger hypothesis. Plausible, 65.** Partly right, partly wrong. Right: Set 1 and Set 2 are merging and neither is dying. Wrong in one important way: it is not a merger of equals. Set 2's rails are being absorbed onto Set 1's balance sheet. The dollar stablecoin is a tokenized T-bill fund with a payment function. Tokenized Treasuries ($14.7 to 15.4B) settle inside DTC. Crypto firms get Fed access without the bank privileges. Bitcoin is frozen as a stockpile, and gold is tokenized by the WGC. The "new monopoly board" in 6.4 is one board with a dollar bank, a Treasury-bill property set, licensed toll booths and a gold corner for nations. Dissent: offshore USDT (61% of supply, partly backed by gold and BTC) and China's public rails are a second board the US does not control. If GENIUS comparability fails and Tether stays offshore, the merger is only half-done.

**Db-11 Bitcoin as a future neutral reserve. Speculative, 20 (H2).** For: a fixed supply with no counterparty, Putin's "who can prohibit it?", Russia's crypto legalization, sanctions escalation ("Economic Outcast" now covers digital assets and gold), Tether holding 98,933 BTC, US ETFs holding 1.29M BTC. Against: no central bank buying confirmed, the US freezes rather than accumulates, the sanctions net now explicitly targets digital assets, volatility, and gold already fills the neutral role. Needs: at least one G20 central bank disclosing a BTC reserve position. Until then, Speculative.

**Db-12 Holder-base shift. Confirmed, 80.** Foreign official Treasury holdings fell $254B Feb to Aug 2026 (Japan -$171B, China -$104B) while UK, Belgium, Canada and Cayman custody rose (L5 table 2.1), and NY Fed foreign official custody is down $202B y/y (L1). The marginal Treasury buyer is now leveraged and price-sensitive (basis trades, money funds, stablecoins). This is why captive-demand plumbing matters to Treasury and also why the system is more fragile to a funding shock. Dissent: part of the fall is valuation from higher yields, and Japan's sales funded intervention rather than a strategic exit.

### 6.3 Scenario probabilities (Db-09)

Definitions:
- **S1 Dollar extension.** Stablecoins and tokenized Treasuries carry the dollar onto new rails. The US remains the main reserve issuer.
- **S2 Neutral reserve layer.** Gold and/or Bitcoin become the settlement asset between nations, while the dollar stays for daily and private use.
- **S3 Energy/compute standard.** A physics-referenced unit of account becomes dominant for machine commerce.
- **S4 Disorderly reset.** Fiscal dominance shows up as a crisis or overt repression first (forced Fed yield control, inflation well above target for years, a failed auction or reserve-holder run), and a new arrangement follows.

How to read the table. These scenarios can coexist (stablecoins can grow while central banks buy gold, and S1 plumbing can be the tool of S4 repression). So the table answers one question per horizon: **which scenario will best describe the system at the end of that horizon (the dominant regime)?** Each row sums to 100. A second table gives the judge's estimate that each element is materially present at all, which does not sum to 100.

Reasoning:
- **H0, S1 62.** Everything T1 points this way (GENIUS switch-on 2027-01-18, OCC rule final, CBDC ban, SEC tokenization exemptions, DTC pilot). The dollar is up, not down. Stablecoins stalled at about $300B is the main drag, so S1 is not higher.
- **H0, S4 22.** Real risks in the data: a war-driven oil shock with Hormuz closed, 10y at 5.13% and 30y at 5.42%, the official holder base shrinking, France at 157.5bp, interest above defense, and the bill strategy making the budget sensitive to every hike. A bond-market accident that forces the Fed to act is not a tail event. But a reset to a new system inside two years is, which caps S4.
- **H0, S2 15.** Gold's neutral role is already present (65% in the second table) but it will not be the best description of the system by 2028.
- **H1 shifts.** The CBDC ban sunsets 2030-12-31, the 2028 DASP ban pushes offshore tokens into the US regime or out, about 70% of the debt reprices at market rates, interest approaches 5% of GDP, and Fed leadership turns over (Powell's governor term to Jan 2028, the Cook case). That raises S4 to 30. Gold accumulation compounds, which raises S2 to 22. S1 stays the single most likely regime at 45.
- **H2.** Wider uncertainty spreads probability out. S3 rises to 10 only because Musk-style machine economies could be large by then. L7 shows any energy unit needs a scarcity rule, which pulls it back toward a gold or Bitcoin problem (so some S3 worlds are really S2 worlds). Da (energy-money panel) rated a machine compute unit Speculative.
- **Most likely joint path (judge's single narrative):** S1 plumbing grows through H0, a repression episode (S4-lite) in late H0 or H1 is run partly through that plumbing, and gold's neutral role expands quietly underneath. Bitcoin stays a private and sanctions-zone asset unless a G20 central bank breaks ranks.

### 6.5 The judge's answer to the owner's question

How are the Fed and Treasury tackling the debt? They are not tackling the stock. Treasury is managing the flow: it funds the $2T deficit with bills, writes rules that make banks, money funds and stablecoins buy those bills, buys back long bonds, and keeps Japan from dumping Treasuries with signals and FIMA rather than money. The Fed under Warsh refuses to help directly and is hiking, but inflation above the average coupon is doing quiet repression in the background. Nobody in power is using gold revaluation, Bitcoin, default or a formal currency accord. The system that results is one dollar board: new digital rails on top, short Treasuries underneath as the required collateral, licensed issuers and the Fed at the toll booths, and gold in the corner as the neutral asset between nations. Your merger instinct is right in shape, but it is an acquisition, not a merger: Set 1 is buying Set 2.

### 6.6 Strongest overall dissent

The captive-demand strategy may be a fair-weather plan. It shortens the debt, ties federal interest to the policy rate, and leans on leveraged and price-sensitive buyers just as official buyers leave. If the war-driven inflation shock persists and the Fed keeps hiking, the cost of bills rises fast, and the first real stress could force the Fed into yield control, which would make S4 rather than S1 the defining story of H0 and H1.
