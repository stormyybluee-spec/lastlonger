## 1. Proponent

The thesis of this cluster, in its strongest defensible form: energy is taking over the job that gold did in the old system (the scarce physical thing that limits how much real output any money can claim) and part of the job Treasuries do (prime collateral behind new credit). It does this inside the dollar system, not against it.

1. **The constraint is already binding, and money is moving because of it (T0/T3).** PJM's 2027/2028 capacity auction cleared at the FERC-approved cap of $333.44/MW-day for the second year running, about $16.4B, and still came up 6,623 MW short of the reliability requirement. Data centers explain about 5,100 MW of the 5,250 MW rise in forecast peak (L3-20). EIA's 2026-10-06 STEO has US wholesale power up 11% in 2026 and PJM up 41% (L3-21). IEA has data centers at about 415 TWh (2024), about 485 TWh (2025, growth 17 to 20% against about 3% for all power) and about 945 to 950 TWh by 2030 (L3-19). Turbines, transformers and grid connections are rationed. Alden's five-plus-year transformer backlog and her 20 W brain versus 20 MW supercomputer point (L6 2.3) say the same thing from the physical side.

2. **Energy and compute are already the collateral behind a fast-growing slice of dollar credit (T0 via T4).** Meta's Hyperion campus: about $27.3B of senior notes through an SPV 80% owned by Blue Owl (L3-24). GPU-secured debt: $48.4B in 2026 to 2026-10-10 against $6.6B in all of 2025, within $103B of GPU and data center debt (L3-23, tracker). Corporate clean PPAs 55.9 GW in 2025 with the four hyperscalers at 49% (L3-25). Hyperscaler capex guidance about $700 to $725B for 2026, about 77% above 2025 (L3-22). A dollar stablecoin (USD.AI) funds loans secured on B300 and B200 GPUs with a $100M Bullish facility (L3-28).

3. **The units are being standardized, and they settle in dollars (T1).** CME's notice SER-9785 (2026-08-11) set out H100 and B200 rental-index futures of 730 GPU-hours per contract, cash-settled in USD. Kalshi launched GPU compute forward curves on 2026-07-14 (L3-26). A physical quantity unit (GPU-hours, MWh) is becoming a contract standard, priced in dollars. That is exactly how oil became a dollar commodity.

4. **The market itself ranks energy's best monetary use (T0/T3).** Listed Bitcoin miners signed more than $70B of AI/HPC contracts while hashprice hit a five-year low of about $28 to $30 per PH/s per day and cash cost per BTC sat near $80k (L3-14). Bhutan sold about 70% of its hydro-mined BTC (L3-15). Owners of power and interconnects prefer to sell watts as compute for dollars rather than as Bitcoin.

5. **The petrodollar precedent says this strengthens the issuer of the pricing currency.** The only energy-anchored monetary arrangement that ever scaled (1974) did not make energy the money. It made energy demand into dollar demand (L3 1.3). The US is now a net energy exporter (EIA, cited in L3 1.4) and hosts most AI capex. A dollar-priced watt is good for the dollar.

6. **The long-run endpoint has a coherent theory behind it.** Musk's stable view since 2023 is that money is "a database for resource allocation" (L3-04). If robots make labor abundant, energy and mass are what remain scarce, so the accounting unit drifts toward them (L6 M4). Altman says the cost of intelligence "should eventually converge to near the cost of electricity" (L3-17). Sgouridis (2014, peer reviewed) shows a non-redeemable energy-referenced unit is at least designable at national scale (L3-33).

Proponent's bottom line: energy is the new anchor and the new collateral. It will not be the cash in your pocket, but it will increasingly be what the cash is a claim on.

## 2. Skeptic

**Attack on the proponent's strongest point (point 2, energy as collateral).** The collateral behind these deals is mostly not energy. It is corporate credit wearing an energy costume.
- Hyperion's notes are serviced by a Meta lease. Bond buyers are pricing Meta's promise to pay rent, not the megawatts. If Meta walked away, the campus would be worth what another tenant pays, which depends on the GPU and power market at that time.
- GPU collateral is a fast-depreciating, volatile asset. Blackwell rental prices rose 48% in two months (L3-27). That volatility runs both ways, which is a warning, not a feature. The single-source report of a failed $4B CoreWeave site syndication (L3 incentive map) is a hint that the market already discounts this collateral.
- Scale is small. $48.4B of GPU-secured debt and $103B of GPU and data center debt (a T4 tracker, not primary data) sit next to $40.44T of US federal debt alone (L7-01) and about $365T of global debt (L7 inputs, IIF via secondary). Even stablecoins ($304.9B, L7-16) are three times larger than all GPU and data center debt. Calling this the new backing of the dollar overstates it by orders of magnitude for now.
- The petrodollar analogy is loose. The 1974 deal recycled a foreign state's surplus into US government debt in exchange for security. The watt-dollar is mostly US private borrowing against US assets. It creates dollar credit supply, not foreign official demand for Treasuries. Different balance sheet, different politics (see Accountant).

**Point 1 (binding constraint).** The constraint is regional and institutional, not physical scarcity of energy. In the same EIA release that has PJM up 41%, Mid-Columbia fell 23% and CAISO hit historic lows (L3-21). That is a grid, interconnect and permitting bottleneck, which policy can loosen. Staudt (ARK) calls short-term energy fears "overblown" because China alone adds power fast enough (L6 2.2). Efficiency also improves. Chips, high-bandwidth memory and packaging may bind first in some quarters (no lane quantifies this, so it is a gap, not evidence). The 2025 PPA decline (L3-25) has competing explanations (policy uncertainty, negative prices), so it is weak support.

**Point 3 (units).** CME's launch is unconfirmed (two fetch attempts failed today). Even if it lists, a 730 GPU-hour contract cash-settled in USD proves the dollar is the unit and compute is the commodity. It refutes, rather than supports, any energy unit of account.

**Point 4 (miners).** Part of the miner pivot is cyclical (post-halving economics, BTC at $84,040 against an $80k cash cost, L7 inputs and L3-14). It says grid-connected sites near load centers are worth more to hyperscalers. It does not say all power earns more as compute. Stranded, flared and curtailed power still has only one global buyer of last resort, and that is Bitcoin.

**Point 6 (long-run theory).** Base rate: every energy-money proposal since 1921 failed (Ford, Soddy, Technocracy, Fuller, kWh tokens like WePower, L3 1.3 and 1.7). No kWh-denominated stablecoin of material size exists as of 2026-10-22 (L3-29). Musk's 2021 position was the opposite of his 2025 position (L3 1.1), and his "200 GW a year" solar line describes a target against about 3.2 GW of actual US cell capacity (L3-05, L6-17). The theory is coming from the person who would own the mint.

**The functions test kills energy as money outright.** A delivered kWh scores 1 of 5 as medium of exchange and 1 of 5 as store of value (L3 1.9). It is perishable, location-bound and its production cost falls. L7's arithmetic shows a fixed-kWh unit is inflationary whenever energy gets cheaper faster than goods (L7-15). Oil itself went to negative prices in April 2020 because storage ran out. No store of value does that.

Skeptic's bottom line: energy is a constraint and a commodity. Calling it money, anchor or backing is a category error that flatters energy owners.

## 3. Accountant

Rule: every financial asset is someone's liability, except outside money (gold, Bitcoin) and real assets. Check each claim.

| Instrument or claim | Holder's asset | Issuer's liability | What actually backs it | Balances? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dollar (bank deposit, Treasury, stablecoin) | Claim on bank, Treasury, issuer | Bank, US government, stablecoin issuer | Bank loans, future taxes, T-bills | Yes |
| Hyperion SPV notes (about $27.3B) | Claim on SPV | SPV (80% Blue Owl) | Meta lease payments, then campus resale value | Yes, but the energy asset is second-loss collateral behind Meta's credit |
| GPU-secured loans ($48.4B 2026, T4) | Loan receivable | Neocloud or AI lab | Rental cash flows, then GPU resale | Yes, but collateral value is volatile and depreciating |
| USD.AI GPU loans | Stablecoin token | USD.AI | GPU loans, funded partly by a $100M facility | Balances only if loan losses stay inside the equity cushion. Whether this structure fits GENIUS reserve rules is not covered in the assigned lanes (gap for L2) |
| Corporate PPA (55.9 GW, 2025) | Contracted power or price hedge | Generator obligation to deliver, buyer obligation to pay | Plant output plus buyer credit | Yes. A PPA is a forward contract, it adds no net financial wealth |
| CME GPU-hour future (planned) | Long position | Short position | Nothing. Zero-sum, cash-settled in USD | Yes, net zero |
| A kWh token redeemable for power | Claim on delivery | Utility or token issuer | Generation capacity, at one place and time | Only if the issuer can deliver at that node. A Texas kWh is not an Estonia kWh (L3 1.7) |
| A unit pegged to "a share of total energy flow" (L7 scarcity version) | Claim on a share of output | Generators | Generator cash flows | This is equity in power producers, not money. Utility stocks already exist |
| Bitcoin | Bearer asset | Nobody | Nothing redeemable. Miners' energy spend tracks price, not the other way | Outside money. Energy is a cost of issuance, not a claim (L3-13) |
| Technocracy energy certificate | Ration right | The "technate" (central allocator) | Planned national energy output | A liability of the planner. Non-transferable, so it never circulates (L3-07) |
| Musk's "currency in wattage and tonnage" | Unspecified | Unspecified | Unspecified | Does not balance yet. No issuer, no redemption rule, no supply rule is named. Flag |

Findings:
1. **The petrodollar and the watt-dollar have different balance sheets.** Petrodollar (1974): Saudi current account surplus (Saudi asset) became Treasuries (US government liability). Foreign official demand financed US deficits. Watt-dollar: US private borrowers issue new dollar liabilities against US real assets. This raises dollar credit supply and dollar collateral, and it can pull in foreign private and sovereign-fund buyers of the bonds, but it does not, by itself, finance the Treasury. Saudi Treasury holdings are only $141.2B, about 1.5% of $9,331.7B of foreign holdings (L3-10). So "watt-dollar extends petrodollar logic" is true for pricing and invoicing (energy and compute priced in dollars) and false for the recycling channel unless Gulf or Asian surplus money is shown flowing into US data center debt. That flow is not measured in the lanes (gap).
2. **Where does the money come from?** From bank and bond-market credit creation against expected AI revenue. If AI revenue disappoints, the losses land on private credit funds, bondholders and (for stablecoin-funded loans) token holders. This is a 2008-shaped risk (credit against a real asset whose value depends on continued credit), not a monetary-standard change.
3. **An energy unit of account needs a supply rule, and every supply rule turns it into something else.** Fixed kWh: soft money that anyone with panels can mint (L7-15). Share of energy flow: equity. Capped token whose issuance costs energy: Bitcoin. This is the balance-sheet form of Da-06.
4. **Scale check (L7 numbers).** World broad money was $151.5T (2024). World primary energy was 166,754 TWh (2025). That is roughly $0.91 of broad money per kWh of annual primary energy (mixing 2024 money and 2025 energy, so approximate). World GDP is $0.71 per kWh of primary energy and $3.72 per kWh of electricity (L7-14). Any energy unit would have to absorb a money stock bigger than a year of world output, which is why every serious design (Sgouridis) is a reference index, not a redeemable claim.
5. **Musk's debt claim balances only conditionally.** L7 shows a +25% output level over 10 years adds about $8.1T a year to US GDP and about $1.36T of revenue, more than the $0.94T primary deficit, but only if spending does not rise with income and real rates do not rise with growth (L7-08). Chain B in L3 adds a third condition: energy supply must grow with output, otherwise the gain shows up as power-price inflation.

## 4. Historian

| Precedent | What happened | What it predicts for energy money |
|---|---|---|
| 1921 Ford and Edison, Muscle Shoals | Proposed financing a dam with new currency instead of bonds, with energy as the moral backing. Withdrawn 1924, public ownership (TVA, 1933) won (L3-08) | Energy-money ideas are usually debt-financing ideas in disguise. Musk's "solve the debt with AI and robots" has the same shape |
| 1926 to 1933 Soddy | Real wealth decays, debt compounds. Proposed 100% reserves and price-index money, never an energy standard (L3 1.3) | The durable part is the diagnosis (debt can outgrow the physical economy). Expect energy to enter as a constraint on credit, not as the money |
| 1933 Technocracy peak, Gold Reserve Act era | Public interest in energy certificates peaked 1932 to 1933. The state instead took gold, revalued it and kept managed money. Technocracy was banned in Canada in 1940, Musk's grandfather arrested (L3-06, L3-07) | In a monetary crisis states choose more discretion, not a physics rule that removes it. Energy certificates were rationing coupons that needed a central allocator |
| 1944 Bretton Woods | The country holding most of the scarce reserve asset (US gold) set the system | Whoever controls the marginal supply of the scarce input sets the terms. In an energy-referenced world that points to the owners of firm power and solar manufacturing (US for firm power and gas, China for panels, L3 Chain C) |
| 1971 Nixon shock | Gold anchor removed. Credit money backed by Treasuries and taxes replaced it | Anchors get dropped when they bind the state. An energy anchor that binds would be dropped the same way |
| 1974 petrodollar | Oil invoiced in dollars, surpluses recycled into Treasuries, secretly (L3-09) | The only energy-money arrangement that scaled made energy demand into dollar demand. Base case for H0 to H1: a watt-dollar, not a watt standard |
| 1979 to 1980 oil shock | Energy priced in dollars drove inflation, then Volcker's hikes | Energy shocks hurt holders of fixed nominal incomes. Relevant to UHI (Da-10). 2026 echo: WTI $98, Q2 PCE +5.0% SAAR, Fed hiked to 3.75 to 4.00% (L7 inputs, L6-01) |
| 1985 Plaza | Coordinated devaluation by the issuer of the reserve currency | Pricing power over the anchor is political. A dollar-priced watt keeps that power in Washington |
| 1873 and later railroad bonds, 2008 MBS | Each era's hot real asset became the collateral of its credit boom, then the center of its bust | GPU and data center debt is this era's candidate. Growth from $6.6B to $48.4B in one year is the classic early signature |
| 1998 LTCM, 2022 CeFi crypto lending | Collateral chains broke when collateral value fell fast | GPU collateral with 48% two-month price swings carries the same risk. Crypto lending is still 40% below its 2025 peak (L7 anomaly 5) |
| 2017 to 2019 kWh tokens (WePower, Power Ledger) | Tokenized kWh. Estonia pilot concluded full decentralized use not feasible. Tokens detached from their kWh (L3 1.7) | Redeemable energy money fails at scale because the grid, not the token, settles physical delivery |
| April 2020 negative oil | WTI futures below zero when storage ran out | Energy can carry negative value. Disqualifies it as a store of value |
| 1860s telegraph (Alden) | Transactions moved at light speed, settlement stayed physical, so ledgers centralized (L6 2.3) | Machine commerce will favor whatever unit settles fastest and with finality. Today that is dollar stablecoins, not kWh |

Historian's prediction: the base rate for an energy money replacing state money is zero for zero across a century. The base rate for a new real asset becoming the collateral of a dollar credit boom is high, and so is the base rate for that boom overshooting.

### 6.2 Reasoning per sub-thesis

**Da-01 Energy as spendable money. Refuted, 5.** Decisive evidence: no kWh-denominated stablecoin or token of material size exists (L3-29). Every energy-linked instrument found in 2025 to 2026 settles in dollars, including the first compute futures (L3-26). The functions test scores a delivered kWh 1 of 5 for medium of exchange and store of value (L3 1.9). The 2026 regional price split (PJM +41%, CAISO historic lows, L3-21) shows a kWh has no single price. The century base rate is zero. The 5 is left for prepaid compute or energy allotments (Altman's universal basic compute) being used like scrip inside closed systems by H2, which would still be convertible into dollars. Strongest dissent: compute credits could circulate as a de facto money inside AI platforms. Gap: any data on compute credits used as compensation.

**Da-02 Watt-dollar. Plausible, 75.** The collateral layer is real and growing (Hyperion $27.3B, PPAs 55.9 GW, GPU-secured debt $48.4B by the tracker, USD.AI). Pricing and invoicing follow the petrodollar pattern: a physical input becomes a dollar commodity. The skeptic and the accountant win two corrections. (a) The collateral is mostly hyperscaler credit with energy assets as second-loss backing. (b) The recycling half of the petrodollar (foreign surpluses into Treasuries) is not shown. So the card is Plausible, not Confirmed, and the scale is still small (all GPU and data center debt is about a third of the stablecoin float and a rounding error against $40.44T of federal debt). Strongest dissent: this is a private credit cycle (railroads, MBS), not a monetary regime, and it may end in a bust that discredits energy collateral. Gaps: primary data for GPU and data center debt (the tracker is T4), foreign (Gulf, Japan, Singapore) participation in US data center debt, CME futures launch and open interest, default and recovery data on GPU loans.

**Da-03 Binding constraint. Confirmed for H0, 80.** PJM clearing at cap twice with a 6,623 MW shortfall is money committed ($16.4B), so it clears the T0 bar, backed by EIA prices (T3) and IEA projections (T4). The judge refines the statement: the binding input is deliverable firm power plus grid connection in specific load regions, not energy in general (Mid-C and CAISO prices fell). For H1 it stays Plausible because policy (permitting, gas turbines, nuclear restarts, space solar) can loosen it and chips may bind first. Strongest dissent: Staudt (constraint "overblown", China adds power fast enough). Gaps: interconnection queue lengths, transformer and turbine lead times (only qualitative in lanes), share of AI build delays attributed to power versus chips, 2026 IEA update (IEA site returned 403 to L3).

**Da-04 Machine-to-machine energy or compute unit of account. Speculative, 25.** What exists: GPU-hours as a contract quantity (730 GPU-hour CME contract, T1 notice) and indices (Ornn, Silicon Data). What does not exist: any price quoted in GPU-hours or kWh rather than dollars. The machine rail that is actually scaling is dollar stablecoins ($304.9B, L7-16). Compute is too volatile to be a yardstick (Blackwell rental +48% in two months, L3-27). Most likely H1 outcome: compute and kWh become shadow indices inside agent pricing logic (tokens per dollar, dollars per GPU-hour), while settlement and accounting stay in dollar stablecoins. The judge rules the thesis as posed (a compute or energy unit of account) Speculative at 25 and flags the alternative (dollar stablecoin as the machine unit with a compute index) as the stronger hypothesis for the reloop. Strongest dissent: agents optimizing compute budgets may internally price everything in compute, and internal units can become external (airline miles precedent is not in the lanes, so not used as evidence). Gaps: CME launch status, any agent-payment protocol volume, Altman's convergence test ($/token versus $/MWh cointegration, L3 gap 9, L7 not run).

**Da-05 Musk's physics currency in H3, and the governance problem. Speculative, 15.** Musk's view is consistent since 2023 (money as an allocation database) and tied by him to a far-horizon economy "a million times" today's (L6 2.5). The design is substantively Technocracy's energy certificate (L3-36), an idea present in his own family history (L3-06). He names no issuer, redemption rule or supply rule (Accountant). The 15 covers a post-scarcity world in which a joule or kilogram-to-orbit accounting unit rations what stays scarce. The governance problem is the robust part of this card and the judge rules it with high confidence conditional on the scenario: a physics unit is either (a) a market price, in which case it is just a commodity index priced in some money, or (b) an allocation, in which case someone must set the allotments. Technocracy needed a "technate". In Musk's version the natural allocators are whoever owns generation, launch and compute, which today means his companies and a few states. That is a concentration-of-power problem far larger than central bank discretion, and it would need constitutional rules (who sets allotments, how they are audited, how they change). Strongest dissent: in true abundance there is little to allocate, so the governance stakes shrink with the money's role. Gaps: verification of the "wattage and tonnage" wording against the full transcript, whether Musk has ever described a transferable, market-priced unit (which would break the Technocracy parallel).

**Da-06 kWh-pegged unit inflationary while energy cost falls, needs a scarcity rule. Plausible, 80.** The arithmetic is an identity: goods priced in kWh rise when a kWh gets cheaper faster than goods (L7 section 5: a 4% a year real kWh cost fall with flat goods costs gives about +4.2% a year kWh inflation). Solar module cost fell from $132/W (1975) to $0.265/W (2024) (L7-15). The judge sharpens the claim: a fixed-kWh unit is unstable in both directions. In 2026 delivered power is getting dearer (US wholesale +11%, PJM +41%), so right now a kWh unit would be deflationary. Over decades of solar learning it is inflationary. Either way it fails as a yardstick, and any fix (cap the units, peg to a share of total energy flow, make issuance cost energy) turns it back into gold, equity or Bitcoin. It is Plausible rather than Confirmed because the long-run fall in delivered firm kWh cost is not proven: module cost is now a small share of the delivered cost (L7 note). Strongest dissent: Jevons demand growth could keep the real kWh price flat for long periods, making the unit roughly stable by accident. Gaps: a long real retail and wholesale electricity price series (US and world), the delivered firm-power cost curve including storage and transmission.

**Da-07 Bitcoin energy-priced, miner pivot as revealed preference. Confirmed, 85.** Difficulty adjustment makes energy spend follow price (protocol fact, L3-13). BTC is not redeemable for energy. The pivot is T0: more than $70B of AI/HPC contracts by listed miners, hashprice at a five-year low, Bhutan's 70% drawdown of holdings (L3-14, L3-15). Refinement: the revealed preference applies to interconnected firm power near load and fiber. Stranded and curtailed power still earns most as Bitcoin, which remains the only way to turn remote power into a globally transferable bearer asset. Strongest dissent: the pivot is partly cyclical (post-halving, BTC $84,040 against about $80k cash cost) and could reverse if BTC rallies or GPU rents fall. Gaps: share of miner revenue from AI in Q3 2026 filings, hashrate trend since October 2025 peak, Cambridge CBECI live figure.

**Da-08 The owner's question. Plausible, 65.** Card text is the paragraph in 6.4. It is Plausible because its two load-bearing parts (binding constraint and dollar-denominated collateral) have T0 to T3 support, while its forward parts (energy indexing in H2, accounting unit in H3) do not.

**Da-09 China as the energy mint (added by the judge from L3 Chain C). Plausible, 50.** If value is measured in energy, the marginal issuer is whoever makes the marginal energy capacity. China holds about 1,100 GW of module, 1,400 GW of cell and 1,500 GW of wafer capacity (L3-31, CPIA, T4). Yet China cannot price energy in yuan at scale (L3-12), and it cut Treasuries to $590.7B (L3-10). The US is a net energy exporter with most AI capex. This creates a strategic reason for the US to keep energy and compute dollar-priced and to onshore solar. The mechanism is coherent but supported mostly by T4 data. Dissent: panel capacity is not energy, and firm power (gas, nuclear, grid) is where the US is strong. Gaps: yuan share of oil settlement in 2026, US cell capacity growth, LNG export volumes.

Plausible, 60.** Data centers are bidding up capacity (PJM at cap, $16.4B), wholesale PJM +41%, WTI $98, Q2 PCE +5.0% SAAR. ### 6.3 Strongest overall dissent

The skeptic's category-error argument: energy is a constraint and a commodity, and labeling it money or backing flatters energy owners. The judge accepts it for Da-01, Da-04 and Da-05 and partly for Da-02, where the "backing" is mostly corporate credit. It fails against Da-03 and Da-07, where money has already moved.

### 6.4 The judge's single-paragraph answer to the owner's question (card Da-08)

Energy is not going to replace the dollar you spend or the number on the price tag. What it is replacing is the job gold used to do: being the scarce physical thing that limits how much real output any money can buy. Gold played that role by law until 1971. After 1971 the limit was only political (how much debt markets would absorb). In an AI and robot economy, output scales with delivered power, and power cannot be printed, so energy becomes the real ceiling on what every dollar can claim. It is also taking a growing share of the job Treasuries do as prime collateral, but inside the dollar: power plants, data center campuses, power contracts and GPUs are now the assets behind tens of billions of new dollar loans, and the first compute futures settle in dollars. That is a "watt-dollar", the heir of the 1974 petrodollar, and it strengthens whichever currency prices the watts (today the dollar). Energy fails as a spendable money and as savings because it is perishable, tied to a place, and gets cheaper as solar scales (so a kWh coin would lose value), which is why every energy currency since 1921 failed. The sequence is: energy as collateral now (H0 to H1), energy as an index for bonds or income transfers possibly in H2, and energy as an accounting unit only in a post-scarcity H3, where the real question becomes who controls the allocation. The winners are whoever owns firm power and grid connections. Ordinary people on fixed dollar incomes lose unless their income is indexed to energy or compute costs.
