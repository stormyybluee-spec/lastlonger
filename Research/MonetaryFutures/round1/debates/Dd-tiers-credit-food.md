# Debate Dd: The Three Tiers, Credit Access, and Robots in Food

Round 1 debate court. Written 2026-10-22. Cluster id prefix "Dd".
Lanes used: L4 (labor, AI, UHI, primary), L2 (law and regulation), L7 (quant), L3 (energy money).
Web: search budget exhausted. One WebFetch used, on Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-3.4-12, to settle the credit-continuance rule (result below). No numbers were invented. Where a number is missing it is listed as a gap.

Confidence convention: the judge's confidence number is the confidence that the ruling (the status given) is correct, not the probability that the original claim is true. So "Refuted, 85" means 85% confident the claim as stated is wrong.

## The owner's framing under test

Society splits into (1) asset owners, (2) UHI recipients, (3) people still working. Worry: people who live on UHI lose access to credit and assets, so newborns become a "permanent underclass". Questions: what does a worker get paid in, what does a UHI recipient receive (money or watts), and will robots make food cheap and kill small food-and-beverage (F&B) businesses?

## 1. Proponent

The owner's instinct is right about the destination and slightly wrong about the road.

**The split is real, and the data already shows it.** The US nonfarm labor share was 52.8% in Q2 2026, the lowest since the series began in 1947, while unit profits of nonfinancial corporations rose at a 43.0% annualized rate (BLS, 2026-09-03, T0). Unemployment stayed at 4.2% (BLS, 2026-10-02, T0). Capital is taking a bigger slice without mass layoffs. The top 1% held a record 32.5% of US net worth in Q2 2026, and the top 10% hold about 87% of equities (Fed DFA via secondary, T0 via T5). Workers and UHI recipients both live on income streams. Owners live on the capital share. So the three tiers are really two classes: people who own the machines and people who rent their time or receive a check. That is Dd-01.

**Credit is the wrong fear, and the law already answers it.** ECOA bans credit discrimination "because all or part of the applicant's income derives from any public assistance program" (15 USC 1691(a)(2), T1). Fannie Mae counts public assistance income with no minimum history if it will continue three years (B3-3.4-12, verified today, T1), and does not even check continuance for own-account Social Security (B3-3.4-15, T1). A statutory, multi-year UHI is underwritable income. That is Dd-02.

**The real trap is compounding.** Even with perfect credit access, income-based lending is capped by debt-to-income rules. L4's illustration: $2,500 a month of UHI supports roughly a $178k mortgage at 6.3% (my check: the payment is about $1,102 a month, 44% of income, principal and interest only). Asset prices compound with the capital share. An income stream, however protected, does not. That is Dd-03, and it matches Trammell and Patel's serious mechanism: if capital substitutes for labor, "approximately everything will eventually belong to those who are wealthiest when the transition occurs" (2025-12-29, T4).

**The fix is already being built, and both parties are converging on it.** Trump Accounts (IRC 530A, T1) auto-enrolled about 70 million children, with more than 10 million funded and $4.5B deposited by 2026-10-07 (White House remarks, T0/T2). Bessent favored seeding them with government equity in AI firms (Semafor 2026-06-17, T4). Sanders introduced a 50% stock levy on AI firms to fund an AI sovereign wealth fund paying about $1,000 per person per year (2026-06-18, T1 bill, no action). Right and left both now answer "who owns the robots" with ownership, not a payroll robot tax. That is Dd-04.

**Workers get cash, capped by robots where tasks are substitutable.** Minimum wage must be paid "in cash or negotiable instrument payable at par" (29 CFR 531.27, T1). Compute or energy can only be extras. Miso's Flippy rents at about $5,000 a month (Fortune 2026-02-26), about $10.40 per robot-hour at 16 hours a day, with a claimed double output. A humanoid at Musk's $25k target works out to about $2.55 per hour at 4,000 hours a year (L4 arithmetic, inference). Entry-level displacement is visible: workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed jobs are about 19% below peers (Stanford, August 2026, T4). Complemented roles hold up. That is Dd-05.

**A UHI recipient gets dollars first, then possibly energy-indexed claims.** Every live proto-UHI is dollars: Social Security, SNAP, EITC, Alaska PFD ($1,000 plus a $200 "energy relief payment" in 2026, T0). Alaska's energy relief top-up is the first explicit energy-triggered citizen transfer in the US, a seed for H2 indexing. L3 places "UHI indexed to an energy-or-compute basket" as plausible in H2 and physics allocations only in H3. That is Dd-06.

**Food gets cheaper, but not free.** In the 2024 USDA ERS Food Dollar, labor is 54.1 cents, property income 32.7, output taxes 7.2, the farm share 11.8 (T0). Labor is 59.1 cents per restaurant dollar and 47.1 per grocery dollar. Robots attack only the labor slice and add capital and energy back. L4's ceiling: commodity restaurant prices about 10 to 25% lower in real terms by H2, grocery about 5 to 12%. That is Dd-07. Small F&B splits: commodity operators are squeezed, experience operators gain from Baumol dynamics, and robot-as-a-service rentals partly close the capital gap (Dd-08).

**The "permanent underclass" meme is sold, but its core is real.** It began as a San Francisco X joke ("You have [N] months to escape the permanent underclass"), was amplified by Amodei (January 2026), Warren (2026-06-03) and Polymarket, and is now used to sell crypto and AI tools (Pulsar 2026-10-12, T4). The economic core is the labor-share mechanism above (Dd-09).

**For an ordinary person, the answer is ownership, not borrowing power** (Dd-10).

## 2. Skeptic

**First, the proponent's strongest point: the record-low labor share proves the two-class split.** It does not. L4 itself says the labor share has been falling since around 2000, before generative AI. A single quarter at 52.8% with unit profits up 43% annualized is a cyclical profits print as much as a structural break. Unemployment is 4.2%, and total announced layoffs through July were 41% below 2025 (Challenger, T2). AI is cited for about 22% of cuts in a shrinking pool, which L4 reads as relabeling. Anthropic's own head of economics found no material AI effect on US unemployment (Fortune 2026-07-24, T2/T4). So the T0 data supports "capital is gaining share", which was already true. It does not show AI substitution driving a class split yet. The top 1% and top 10% figures are secondary, not checked against the Fed DFA page. Also, most real people are hybrids: a wage, a 401(k), a house, a Trump Account for the kids. Two classes is a clean model, not a census.

**Second, the credit refutation is too tidy.** ECOA protects against discrimination by income source. It does not force a lender to lend. Three real channels remain:
1. Continuance: a UHI appropriated yearly fails Fannie's three-year test. The only live resource dividend, Alaska's PFD, is exactly that type: the legislature set $1,000 in 2026 while the governor proposed about $3,600. Whether Fannie counts the PFD at all was not checked (L4 gap 9).
2. Recovery: Social Security style anti-assignment (42 USC 407) and the two-month garnishment shield (31 CFR 212) lower what an unsecured lender can recover. Lenders may legally price that through limits or rates without "discriminating".
3. Thin files: credit scores are built on repayment history, and a person who never had a W-2 or a loan has a thin file regardless of income source. That is a practical exclusion ECOA does not touch.
So "Refuted" should be conditional, and the conditions describe the most likely political design (yearly appropriation).

**Third, ownership-UHI is small and slow.** $1,000 at 7% for 18 years is about $3,380 (5% real gives about $2,400). The $4.5B deposited across 10 million funded accounts averages about $450 each, and across 70 million enrolled about $64. Private contributions up to $5,000 a year favor families who already have money, which is regressive. The first cohort reaches 18 in 2043. The Bessent AI-equity idea is a press report with no decision, and the AI equity acquisition would likely need Congress (L2). Sanders' bill has no markup. Neither design is near the scale needed to offset the capital share.

**Fourth, the robot wage cap is arithmetic on targets.** Musk's $20k to $30k humanoid price is a target. About 14,500 humanoids shipped worldwide in 2025, about 90% Chinese, and Tesla, Figure and Agility shipped about 150 each (T4, unaudited). Tesla external Optimus deliveries are guided for around end-2027, after earlier dates slipped one to two years. Miso had 14 Flippy units deployed at end-2025 and $385k revenue in 2024. Reliability, not price, binds. The wage cap is a 2030s story at the earliest.

**Fifth, the food numbers are mechanical.** No T0 data shows automation passing through to retail prices yet. Sweetgreen, the leading US robot kitchen operator, guided same-store sales down 2 to 4% for FY2026 and sold its robotics unit. Labor savings went to margins, not prices. Ricardian capture means landlords in prime locations will bid up rent. Energy for automation competes with data centers that already pushed PJM wholesale power up 41% (L3). The 10 to 25% ceiling could be a 0 to 10% reality.

**Sixth, the small F&B split is untested.** L4 did not pull Census County Business Patterns by size class. Baumol is a theory of relative prices. It does not guarantee that the experience segment grows enough to absorb displaced operators.

**Seventh, the "practical answer" risks becoming the very sales pitch Dd-09 criticizes.** "Buy assets now or be left behind" is exactly the urgency the crypto and AI-course sellers use. Any answer must be general, diversified and modest about what small savers can achieve against a 32.5% top 1% wealth share.

## 3. Accountant

Whose asset is whose liability, and where does the money come from?

**A. Cash UHI.** A federal payment is a Treasury outflow (liability side via debt, or reduced cash) and a household deposit (asset). Three funding routes, each with a counterparty:
- Taxes: a transfer from Tier 1 and Tier 3 to Tier 2. Balances.
- Debt: Treasury issues bonds, held mostly by Tier 1 and institutions. Tier 1 becomes the creditor of the state that pays Tier 2, and earns interest on it. FY2026 net interest was already $1,051.8B, above defense at $965.2B (L7, T0). With the real 10-year yield only 0.26 pp above trend real growth (L7), debt-to-GDP rises by roughly the primary deficit each year, so any UHI adds directly to the ratio.
- Money finance: new reserves and deposits. Musk's claim "you actually MUST issue dollars to people or there will be massive disinflation" (T2 via secondary) balances only if real output rises at least as fast. If output lags, the inflation tax falls on holders of cash and nominal claims (Tier 2) while real-asset owners hedge (Tier 1).

**Scale check (flag).** Musk's federal checks at $30k per adult for about 262M adults is about $7.9T a year (L4, adult count from memory). FY2026 federal outlays were $7,395.8B and receipts $5,403.3B (L7, T0). A "high" cash UHI is larger than the whole current federal budget. It does not balance from taxes at today's receipts ratio. L7's AI fiscal model also shows the AI growth dividend only stabilizes debt if spending does not rise with income. A large UHI is exactly that spending. Conclusion: a high cash UHI requires either very large real output growth plus money finance, or an ownership channel that pays from the capital share directly.

**B. Ownership UHI.** The asset must come from somewhere.
- Sanders levy: a 50% stock levy is a direct transfer of claims from current AI shareholders (Tier 1) to the fund. The $7T fund size and the $1,000 per person dividend imply a payout of about 4.9% a year (about $342B on $7T, using L7's assumed 342M population). The fund cannot sell. So the dividend balances only if the underlying firms pay roughly 5% of their value in cash dividends each year. Many AI firms pay little or no dividend (memory, not verified this session). **This claim does not balance as stated** unless payout policy changes or the fund borrows. Gap: Sanders' own scoring method.
- Bessent route: Intel-style conversion turned already-spent CHIPS grants into a 9.9% stake (L2, T1/T4). The asset is new, the liability was already booked. Routing such equity into Trump Accounts moves a government asset onto children's balance sheets.
- OpenAI's reported 5% offer (FT via secondary, T5): a gift from shareholders, which in balance-sheet terms is a payment for regulatory goodwill.
- Trump Accounts seed: a Treasury outlay (JCT $15B through 2034) that becomes an index-fund asset in the child's name. It also creates a price-insensitive, perpetual buyer of US large caps (L2). Money flows into existing shares, so part of the benefit accrues to current holders through prices.

**C. Credit against UHI.** A loan is a lender asset and a borrower liability serviced from UHI. Anti-assignment (42 USC 407) means the lender cannot take the stream as collateral, except child support and alimony (42 USC 659). If UHI is made pledgeable, a factoring market appears. Structured settlement factoring historically paid 30 to 60 cents on the dollar (memory), which is why 26 USC 5891 imposes a 40% excise tax without court approval (T1). A pledgeable UHI is a transfer from Tier 2 to Tier 1 financiers at a discount. Non-pledgeable protects the stream but blocks lump-sum capital formation. Kenya's long-term UBI arm showed people building lump sums informally anyway (ROSCA participation +11.3%, contributions +69.9%, T4).

**D. Worker pay.** Equity compensation is the employer issuing shares (dilution of current owners, asset to the worker). Compute or energy credits are an employer's prepaid service liability, not cash, and cannot count toward the minimum wage (29 CFR 531.27, T1). Robot rental (RaaS) revenue is income to robot owners (Tier 1). When a $5,000 a month Flippy replaces cook hours, the wage bill (Tier 3 income) becomes rent to the robot owner (Tier 1) plus energy cost plus consumer savings.

**E. Food.** Savings from automating the 44.2 cents of foodservice labor per restaurant dollar (ERS, T0) split three ways: robot capital and maintenance (Tier 1), energy suppliers, and consumers. In prime sites, landlords capture part through rent (property income is 32.7 cents of the food dollar). Cheaper food is real consumer surplus for Tier 2, but it is partly funded by a transfer of income from Tier 3 cooks to Tier 1 owners.

**F. Money per person (context only).** US M2 divided by US population is about $68,250 (mean, heavily skewed, L7), world broad money per person about $19,500 (2025 estimate). These are stocks of deposits, not a pool a UHI can be paid from. A UHI is a flow funded by taxes, debt, money creation or capital income. The per-person figure says nothing about UHI capacity.

**G. Energy allotment.** A fixed kWh allotment loses purchasing power over goods whenever energy gets cheaper faster than goods (L7: kWh cost falling 4% a year with flat goods costs means goods priced in kWh rise about 4.2% a year). A dollar UHI loses purchasing power over energy when energy gets dearer (PJM wholesale +41%, L3). The design that holds value in both cases is a claim on a share of total energy output (equity in generation), not a fixed number of kWh. Technocracy's energy certificates were non-transferable and expiring (L3, T4), so they could not be pledged or saved. They were rations, not assets.

Unbalanced claims flagged: (1) Sanders' $1,000 dividend from a fund that cannot sell, (2) a $30k cash UHI against current receipts, (3) "robots make food free" (labor is only about half the food dollar and part of the saving returns as capital and energy cost).

## 4. Historian

**1862 Homestead Act (memory).** The US once distributed productive assets, not income, at population scale (160 acres). It created a broad owner class because the grant was large relative to the economy, and it excluded many groups. Prediction: an ownership UHI prevents an underclass only if the grant is large relative to asset prices. A $1,000 seed is not.

**1930s Technocracy (L3, T4).** Energy certificates equal to a share of national energy output, non-transferable, expiring, tied to ID. Musk's maternal grandfather was a member (1936 to 1941). Prediction for H3: a physics allocation is a ration. There is no credit market on a non-transferable, expiring claim. The power sits with whoever sets the allocation and owns the generation.

**1935 Social Security Act (memory, codified in 42 USC 407, T1).** Benefits made non-assignable from the start. Recipients still get mortgages because lenders underwrite the income (Fannie B3-3.4-15). Prediction: a protected income stream and credit access coexist.

**1944 GI Bill (memory, T4).** VA loan guarantees turned a status (veteran) into credit plus home equity and built the mid-century owner class. Access was administered unevenly by race and locality. Prediction: underclass formation is decided by how asset access is administered, not by whether income comes from work or the state.

**1971 to 1974 (memory, contested).** The end of Bretton Woods and the 1974 oil shock opened an era some analysts link to wages decoupling from productivity. L4 dates the current labor-share fall to about 2000. Prediction: capital-share drift is multi-decade and predates AI, so AI should be judged on acceleration, not direction.

**1974 ECOA and its 1976 amendments (memory on dates, statute text T1).** Congress added "public assistance" as a protected income source precisely because lenders were excluding welfare and alimony income. The owner's worry has already been legislated against once. Prediction: if lenders exclude UHI income, the political response is fast and has a template.

**1976 Alaska Permanent Fund, dividends from 1982 (memory).** A resource dividend whose amount became a legislative fight. In 2026 the legislature set $1,000 against a roughly $3,600 proposal (T0/T1). Prediction: politically set dividends become the weak point for underwriting continuance.

**Food automation precedents (memory).** Piggly Wiggly self-service (1916) moved grocery toward chains over decades, and small grocers shrank while specialty shops survived. Horn and Hardart Automats (1902 to 1991) automated service and still lost to fast food and changing tastes. They did not wipe out small restaurants. Twentieth-century farm mechanization drove the farm share of food spending down, which is why it is only 11.8 cents now (T0) and why further farm automation has little leverage on retail prices. Prediction: robots shift market structure over a decade or more, with the commodity segment consolidating and the specialty segment persisting.

**Baumol (1966, memory).** Performing arts costs rise relative to goods because their productivity cannot rise. Prediction: human-made food and service become relatively more expensive, which supports the experience segment's prices (if not necessarily its volume).

**2008 (memory, T4).** Credit access was abundant (subprime), and households with income but little equity lost homes. Credit without asset quality destroyed wealth. Prediction: credit access is not the protector. Asset ownership with low leverage is.

**2020 (memory, T4).** Pandemic cash checks plus a surge in asset prices produced a K-shaped recovery. Cash transfers did not change wealth ranks. The top 1% share is now a record 32.5% (secondary). Prediction: a cash UHI alongside rising asset prices widens the wealth gap even as it lifts consumption.

**What the precedents jointly predict:** income-source credit discrimination gets legislated away, ownership grants work only at scale, food automation consolidates the commodity segment slowly, and cash transfers raise consumption without moving wealth ranks.

### Rulings with reasons

**Dd-01, Plausible 65.** Decisive: labor share 52.8%, lowest since 1947, with unit profits +43.0% annualized (T0). The two-axis reframe (ownership vs income source) is analytically stronger than the three tiers, because workers and UHI recipients share the same structural position relative to capital. Strongest dissent (Skeptic): the decline predates AI, unemployment is low, and most people are hybrids. Not Confirmed because there is no AI-attributable substitution in T0 data yet. Gap: verify Fed DFA top 1% and top 10% equity shares directly.

**Dd-02, Refuted (conditional) 80.** Decisive: ECOA 15 USC 1691(a)(2) (T1) and Fannie B3-3.4-12, verified today: three-year continuance, no minimum history (T1). Own-account Social Security needs no continuance check (B3-3.4-15). A statutory multi-year UHI is underwritable. The claim is true only for a UHI set each year by politicians (Alaska PFD type), which fails the three-year test. Strongest dissent: protected streams lower lender recovery, thin credit files exclude in practice, and yearly appropriation is the most likely political design. Ruling note: the realistic risk is higher cost and lower limits for unsecured credit, not exclusion. Gaps: does Fannie count the Alaska PFD, how credit bureaus treat transfer-only files, and Social Security recipients' approval rates vs wage earners at equal income.

**Dd-03, Plausible 70.** Decisive: DTI-capped borrowing on a $2,500 monthly income buys about $178k of housing, while asset values follow the capital share (T0 labor share plus inference). The 2008 and 2020 precedents both show credit or cash without ownership failing to move wealth ranks. Dissent: if AI makes goods abundant, relative wealth may matter less (Scott Alexander, via L4). Gap: house-price-to-income series and real returns to broad equity vs wage growth, 2000 to 2026.

**Dd-04, Plausible 60.** The design logic is sound (ownership is the only channel that pays from the capital share). The "who pushes what" map is Confirmed as statements: Bessent (AI equity into Trump Accounts, T4 press), Lutnick (SWF), Sanders (50% levy, T1 bill), Altman (compute, T2, plus 2021 equity fund idea), Musk (federal checks, T2). Downgrade reasons: current scale is trivial ($1,000 seed grows to about $3,380, average deposit about $450 per funded account), private top-ups are regressive, no AI equity has moved, and Sanders' dividend does not balance. Dissent: an equity grant small relative to asset prices is a symbol, not a bridge. Gaps: any statute or deal moving AI equity into 530A accounts, Sanders' scoring, Musk SpaceX share donation verification (single source).

**Dd-05, Plausible 65.** Cash-only minimum wage is Confirmed (29 CFR 531.27, T1). Complemented-role resilience is T4 (Stanford). The robot-hour wage cap is Speculative arithmetic on target prices (Flippy about $10.40 per hour is the only live data point, 14 units deployed). Equity pay is real but concentrated. Dissent: reliability binds, so the cap is a 2030s effect. Gaps: RaaS price lists, wage series in food prep and warehouse vs robot rental prices.

**Dd-06, Plausible 60.** Dollars in H0 to H1 are near certain (every live transfer is dollars, CBDC banned through 2030-12-31 by statute, GENIUS private rails, T1). H2 energy or compute indexing is Speculative: only Alaska's oil-price triggered $200 top-up exists, no bill anywhere indexes a transfer to energy or compute (L3 gap 6). H3 physics allocations are Speculative and historically look like Technocracy rations. Key refinement from the Accountant and L7: a fixed kWh allotment loses value as energy gets cheaper, so if energy enters the design it should be as a share of total output (equity in generation), not a kWh count. Dissent: compute and energy are perishable and location-bound, so they make poor stores of value for recipients. Gap: any proposal to index a benefit to energy or compute.

**Dd-07, Plausible 70.** Decisive: ERS Food Dollar 2024 (T0). Labor is about half, and property income, taxes, farm inputs and energy are not automatable. Ceiling inference: restaurants 10 to 25%, grocery 5 to 12% real by H2. Dissent: no pass-through yet, savings going to margins and rent, and energy competition with data centers. Gap: CPI food away from home vs at home tracking, ERS 2024 energy cents.

**Dd-08, Plausible 60.** Mechanism (Baumol plus RaaS plus chain scale) is coherent and precedents (self-service grocery, automats) fit a slow consolidation. Status "robots kill small F&B" as stated is Refuted by mechanism, not yet by data.
