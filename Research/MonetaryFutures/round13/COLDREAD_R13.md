## Strongest and weakest scene

- Reader 1 strongest: the grandmother's tin and the granddaughter [33, 34]. It is her own country, her own money, and the line "the tin was not foolish" is kind. Runner up is the closing line about the man in the field [54].
- Reader 2 strongest: the two women in the queue [19 to 21]. Two people, one line, one rule, opposite fates. This is the best scene for both readers' sense of fairness.

## Fairness, imagined people, advice

- Fair to both hard money and credit? Mostly yes, for both readers. Hard money gets three people. The farmer and the first woman are hurt by it. The second woman and, in a way, the German mother show its good side and its cost. Credit gets Britain, the first family and the highways on the good side, and Latin America, the second family, the moneylender and the bankrupt on the bad side. Reader 2 leans slightly toward "new money is the bad guy" because the toll lands on named, sympathetic people and "nobody asked her" is strong. Reader 1 feels it as balanced, because the final verdict names the farmer and the first woman against hard money.
- Is it clear which people are imagined? Yes in nearly every scene, thanks to the word "Picture" and the callout at [2]. Two small snags. First, there are two farmers, the 1522 man [4] and the 1931 debtor [20], and [51] says only "the farmer". Second, in [41] "the first is like the typical owner" leaves readers briefly unsure whether the family is real.
- Does anything read like financial advice? No, and the callout [57] says so. But it sits at the very end, after the table, so a reader who stops at [54] never sees it. Two lines come close. [33] "Money in a bank lost less in most of those decades" could be heard as "use a bank". [39] "Hard money would tell her to save first. Money that stretches tells her to start now and owe" is framed as what each money would say, not what to do. Neither is a real problem.

## Ranked fixes (12)

Each fix gives the block, the exact OLD text and the NEW text.

1. Block [0]. Define the toll in plain words the first time it appears.
   - OLD: `but it charges a quiet toll, and its gate opens`
   - NEW: `but it charges a quiet toll, the slow loss of what each coin can buy, and its gate opens`

2. Block [20]. Say what "hard money" means.
   - OLD: `Money is hard in her world.`
   - NEW: `Money is hard in her world, which means it is tied to gold and there is little of it.`

3. Block [20]. Say why the farmer's debt grows.
   - OLD: `That makes debts about a third heavier.`
   - NEW: `That makes debts about a third heavier, because he still owes the same dollars but each dollar is harder to earn.`

4. Block [39]. Drop the reference to a chapter the reader has not seen.
   - OLD: `This is the old Monopoly board from the first chapter. The streets were bought long before she sat down.`
   - NEW: `Think of a Monopoly game that began before she sat down. The streets were bought long before she came.`

5. Block [51]. Set up the "scam" claim so the verdict has something to answer.
   - OLD: `Printing is not a scam in the plain sense.`
   - NEW: `Some people call new money a scam. It is not a scam in the plain sense.`

6. Block [39]. Make the saving line clear.
   - OLD: `While she saves, the coins gain value. Most of them are held by people who came before her.`
   - NEW: `While she saves, money gains value. But most of the money is already held by people who came before her.`

7. Block [30]. Tell the reader that "the reader" is a reader of this site.
   - OLD: `The same reader has a sharper way to say what changed.`
   - NEW: `The same reader of this site has a sharper way to say what changed.`

8. Block [51]. Keep the two farmers apart.
   - OLD: `Ask the farmer whose debt grew heavier every year he worked.`
   - NEW: `Ask the farmer from the 1930s, whose debt grew heavier every year he worked.`

9. Block [36]. Plain words for "median".
   - OLD: `In 2019 the median house in Malaysia cost 4.1 times what the median household earns in a year.`
   - NEW: `In 2019 the middle-priced house in Malaysia cost 4.1 times what the middle household earns in a year.`

10. Block [23]. Say what the Swiss vote was about.
    - OLD: `whether to stop banks creating money`
    - NEW: `whether to stop banks from making new money when they lend`

11. Block [10]. Give the Spanish boy a fate, so the story does not drop him.
    - OLD: `He is wrong, and it will hurt.`
    - NEW: `He is wrong, and it will hurt. He will grow up in a country where everything costs more.`

12. Block [27]. Say which inflation stops.
    - OLD: `And the inflation stops, and trade begins to move again.`
    - NEW: `And the rising prices stop, and trade begins to move again.`
