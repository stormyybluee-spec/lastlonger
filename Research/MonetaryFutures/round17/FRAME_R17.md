# Round 17 frame (2026-11-19): Level 3 deep chapter on borrowing from the future

Chapter: working id rb-future, the fourth of the seven deep chapters (round13/SERIES_L3.md). Working title: "Paying for tomorrow: who funds the things that pay back later?"
It deepens question 6 of rb-credit and tests the owner's point 5 (round13/OWNER_R13.md): new technology and new discovery often cost an enormous price before they give people a better and more stable life, and promises about the future (credit, and money issued by a reserve-holding body) are what pay for them. The owner's example is a rocket company and the growth and new technology that may follow from it.
It also carries forward the hypothesis from Round 15 that the reader's chain of promises fits late developers better than the first industrial country.

## Hypotheses to test (with the question-mark rule)
- H1. Big new technologies cost a great deal before they pay back. Test: the cost and the years to payback for a few landmark cases (railways, electricity grids, the semiconductor, the internet, space launch), and the share that failed.
- H2. They were paid for with promises about the future. Test: who actually paid, in what form: government budgets from taxes, government borrowing, bank loans, bonds, shares sold to investors, venture capital, retained profits, and military or space contracts. How much of each.
- H3. Money created by a central bank or by bank lending was what made this possible. Test: whether the big technology waves lined up with easy money, with tight money, or with neither. Evidence on bubbles that funded technology and then burst (railway mania 1840s, the dot-com boom 1995 to 2000), and what was left behind.
- H4. The reader's rocket example. Test: how the reusable rocket industry has been paid for to date: private investors, government contracts (NASA commercial cargo and crew), and revenue. What the public sector's share has been. What is still unknown.
- H5. Late developers. Test: how Korea, Taiwan, Malaysia and China paid for their technology push (state banks, directed credit, foreign investment, development funds, the Malaysian Industrial Development Authority, Penang's electronics zone from 1972, Khazanah), and how well it worked.

## Lanes
- R17A, history and data: H1, H2, H3, with numbers.
- R17B, the present and the late developers: H4, H5, both sides' best case, and sourced human detail.
