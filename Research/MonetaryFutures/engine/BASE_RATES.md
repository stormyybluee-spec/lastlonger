# Reference classes for Unseen mode: things once secret and later documented

Status: SEED LIST, written 2026-11-11 from the desk's memory. Nothing here has been checked against a source yet. The first Unseen mode round must verify every row (dates, numbers, how it came out) and add a URL before any row is used as a prior or quoted on the site. Used by PLAN.md section 4.7 (grade I3, Pattern, and the Precedent move).

Why this file exists: the desk reads published sources, so it leans toward the official record. The owner's point (Case 3) is that much of what matters is undisclosed. This list is the correction: a record of how often, how big and for how long real things stayed hidden, and how they came out. It cuts both ways. It also records plans that were never carried out and confident claims that turned out false.

## A. Hidden, later documented
| # | Case | What was hidden | Hidden for about | How it came out | Lesson for grading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Iran, 1953 | The US and UK role in removing Prime Minister Mossadegh (under President Eisenhower) | 60 years until a formal US acknowledgment, with press and memoir accounts much earlier | Memoirs, a leaked internal history, then declassified files | Covert regime change by intelligence services is a documented class |
| 2 | US and Saudi Treasury purchases, from 1974 | Saudi holdings of US Treasuries were bought outside normal auctions and reported inside a regional total | About 42 years, until a country figure was released in 2016 | A journalist's records request | A large financial fact can sit inside an aggregate line for decades. Look at what a category merges |
| 3 | Stay-behind networks in NATO countries | Secret armed networks prepared for a Soviet invasion | About 40 years, until an Italian prime minister confirmed them in 1990 | Official admission after a judicial inquiry | A multi-country structure can hold for decades. The documented structure and the disputed acts linked to it are graded separately |
| 4 | CIA press relationships, 1950s to 1970s | Paid and unpaid relationships with journalists | About 25 years, until a Senate committee reported in 1975 to 1976 | A legislative inquiry | The popular label is wider than the documented scope. Grade the documented part |
| 5 | Tobacco industry research | Internal findings on harm and addiction | About 40 years | Litigation discovery and a whistleblower in the 1990s | Lawsuits are a main way corporate secrets come out |
| 6 | Iran-Contra, 1985 to 1986 | Arms sales to Iran and money sent to Nicaraguan rebels | About 1 year | A foreign magazine, then a downed aircraft | Operations with many middlemen leak fast, often from the other side (the Mirror move) |
| 7 | LIBOR rate rigging | Traders at several banks nudging a benchmark rate | About 5 to 7 years, exposed in 2012 | Regulators and the banks' own chat logs | Rivals do coordinate when it pays daily and the channel is routine. It leaves written records |
| 8 | Czechoslovak gold, March 1939 | Gold held in London through the BIS was moved to the German central bank's account after the occupation | About 2 months | Press reports and questions in Parliament | Central bank plumbing can be traced quickly when someone reads the right ledger |
| 9 | The wartime atomic bomb project | A programme with over 100,000 workers | About 3 years from the public. Soviet intelligence knew during the project | Use of the weapon, then official histories | Big secrets hold for a few years under wartime law, and reach rivals before the public |
| 10 | Mass surveillance programmes revealed in 2013 | Bulk collection of communications data | Several years | One insider with documents | Capability claims that sounded extreme were partly documented. One insider is enough |
| 11 | Offshore company leaks, 2016 | Who stood behind shell companies | Years to decades | A leak of one law firm's files | The structures were in registries all along. Only the owners were hidden |

## B. Looked like a hidden act, and was not what was claimed
| # | Case | What was claimed | What the record shows | Lesson for grading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | Operation Northwoods, 1962 | That the US staged attacks on its own people | A written proposal by military chiefs for staged incidents, rejected by the civilian leadership and declassified about 35 years later | A plan on paper is not an operation carried out. Grade the plan and the act separately |
| 13 | Gulf of Tonkin, 4 August 1964 | A second attack on US ships | The second attack did not happen as reported. An official history released about 40 years later says so | An official account of a trigger event can be wrong. The official story is one reading, not the prior |
| 14 | Iraq weapons claims, 2002 to 2003 | Stockpiles of banned weapons | None found | Confidence in a statement is not evidence. Ask for the marks |
| 15 | "The petrodollar deal expired in June 2024" | A 50-year treaty ran out | No treaty text with an expiry has been produced | A real hidden arrangement (row 2) attracts a false sequel. Check that the document exists |

## C. What the seed suggests (to confirm once the rows are verified)
- Standing arrangements and structures (rows 1 to 5) stayed hidden for decades. One-off operations with many middlemen (rows 6 and 8) came out within months to a couple of years.
- The ways out: an insider with documents, a lawsuit or regulator, the other side's press, an archive law or records request, a legislative inquiry.
- A model worth checking in the first round: published estimates of how fast a secret leaks as the number of people in it grows (a 2016 paper in PLOS ONE by D. R. Grimes is one). Use it for the secrecy load in the coordination test only after reading it.

## D. How to add a row
Case, what was hidden, the date it started, the date and manner it came out, how many people or institutions had to know, the source URL, and the one-line lesson. Add false positives to section B as carefully as true ones.
