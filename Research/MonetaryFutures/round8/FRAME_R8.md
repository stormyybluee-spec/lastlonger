## 3. Hidden hypotheses (each is a claim to falsify)

From the owner's words:
- H1 "money moving between baskets": assumes money actually leaves one basket and enters another. Ledger hypothesis to test: when a buyer of STRC pays dollars, those dollars do not enter Bitcoin. They end in the bank account of whoever sold the Bitcoin. Dollars only change hands (Set 1 stays the same size), while ownership of the Set 2 asset changes. The flip: "flow into Bitcoin" is a price effect, not a transfer of money.
- H2 "future": assumes a trend. Test whether digital credit is a category (several issuers, growing in many currencies) or one issuer's funding tool with copies (Strive SATA and others). Size against the pools it draws from.
- H3 "this concept": assumes the pipe runs one way. Test the reverse valve: in a bear market the same wrapper can force Bitcoin sales into dollars (Set 2 to Set 1).
- H4 (owner's standing thesis, PLAN 3 and 4.4) "Set 1 absorbs Set 2": the instrument is a Set 1 legal claim (Delaware preferred stock, SEC registered, Nasdaq listed, held in index ETFs) wrapped around a Set 2 asset. Could be read as absorption (Set 1 law and plumbing capture Bitcoin) or as leakage (Set 1 cash leaves T-bills for Bitcoin). Both may be true at once, by regime.

From the talk (selected, full grading is Q2):
- H5 "digital credit is credit": legally it is equity (preferred stock). In bankruptcy it ranks behind all debt. Dividends are paid only when declared and legally allowed.
- H6 "strongest credit in the world" (S19): a superlative with no rating cited.
- H7 "six times over-collateralized" (S18): depends on what counts as senior claims, whether cash is netted, and the Bitcoin price used.
- H8 "48 years of dividends covered" (S16): coverage in Bitcoin means selling Bitcoin to pay. Coverage in dollars is about 3 years (anomaly check below).
- H9 "a bank account paying 24%" (S19): depends on the tax status of distributions (return of capital needs no current earnings and profits), the holder's tax rate, the basis step-down and later capital gains tax, and residency.
- H10 "credit on an S&P index paying 7% would be illegal under the 1940 Act" (S31): registered closed-end funds do issue preferred stock under asset coverage rules. The claim may confuse "would make you an investment company" with "illegal".
- H11 "conservation of volatility" (S32): consistent with the Modigliani and Miller idea that leverage moves risk between claims without destroying it, but the low measured volatility of STRC may come partly from the issuer defending par (buybacks, rate changes), which is managed, not structural. Gap risk is not captured by volatility.
- H12 "$10 billion of credit is one year of supply" (S6): arithmetic of the post-2024 issuance schedule at a given price.
- H13 the return table since August 2020 (S30): start date chosen by the speaker (the month Strategy began buying, near a local gold high). Needs a start-date sensitivity check.
- H14 the Bank of England founding story (S12): the Bank was founded in 1694 to lend to the Crown for war, consols came in 1751 to 1752 as government debt, and Britain's legal gold standard dates from 1821 and ended in 1931. To verify, but the talk's version looks compressed or wrong.
- H15 "par target" means a promise: holders have no right to redeem at $100. Par is held by the issuer's selling above and buying below, and by its rate setting.
- H16 "a stablecoin paying 8%" (S40, S41): runs into the US rule that payment stablecoin issuers may not pay interest or yield to holders (GENIUS Act, July 2025, to verify wording and any 2026 rule changes).
- H17 "Strategy alone can buy all Bitcoin mined forever" (S35): a flow claim that needs the funding rate, which needs the premium.

## 4. Answer specs (what an expert answer must contain)

### Q1 spec: the instrument
1. Contract terms from the prospectus or certificate of designations (T1): stated amount, how the monthly rate is set and the limits on cuts, cumulative or not, cash only or not, call rights, holder put rights on a fundamental change, ranking against STRF, STRK, STRD, STRE (if any) and the convertible notes, voting rights (the August filing says no general voting and no director election on nonpayment), and the new daily dividend mechanics.
2. Legal status: equity in Delaware, dividends need legal surplus, bankruptcy waterfall position. Why "credit" is a marketing word.
3. Balance sheets (Ledger lane): buyer, the buyer's money fund, the fund's T-bill or repo counterparty, the broker, Strategy, the Bitcoin seller. Two floors: the deal moves downstairs deposits only, and touches upstairs only if a money fund draws down Fed reverse repo or sells bills to a dealer.
4. GAAP versus economics: preferred classification, fair value accounting of Bitcoin, why "earnings" can swing by billions with no cash.
5. Tax: why distributions count as return of capital (earnings and profits), what that does to cost basis, what happens if earnings and profits turn positive, the US withholding treatment for a nonresident.
6. A comparison table with these columns: issuer, backing, maturity, who holds the par (contract, market maker, issuer discretion), redemption right, rank in default, regulator, typical yield and spread, liquidity, historical failure mode. Rows: British consols, gold-convertible bank notes or gold-backed bonds, an ordinary bank preferred, a closed-end fund preferred (40 Act asset coverage), a stablecoin under GENIUS, a tokenized money fund, STRC, Strive's SATA.
7. Overcorrection guard: what STRC genuinely does well (transparency of holdings, liquidity, a real collateral pool that is large today) alongside its weak points.

4. ## 5. Blind-spot list (dimensions the answer could flip on)
1. Gross versus net coverage: Bitcoin against STRC alone, against everything senior to STRC, against the whole stack, with or without netting the cash.
2. Bitcoin coverage versus dollar coverage: years of dividends in Bitcoin (about 40) against years in dollars (about 3).
3. Price date: every coverage ratio moves with Bitcoin, and the talk, the filings and today use different prices.
4. mNAV definition: enterprise value over Bitcoin (company's measure) versus market cap over Bitcoin. They give different answers to "is the premium below 1" (see anomaly).
5. Legal form versus marketing word: credit versus preferred equity.
6. Contract versus discretion: what the certificate requires against what management says it will do (rate setting, buybacks, reserve).
7. Flow versus price: dollars do not enter Bitcoin, they reach sellers. The price impact depends on who sells.
8. Stocks versus flows: $10B outstanding against pools of trillions, but marginal flows against about $13B a year of new Bitcoin.
9. Regime: bull versus bear, rate cut versus hike. The 2026 Fed hike (PLAN 2.4) is the live regime.
10. Holder type: US taxable, US tax-exempt, index fund, nonresident (Malaysia), DeFi protocol.
11. Tax law on paper versus practice: return of capital status depends on earnings and profits, which can change.
12. Upstairs versus downstairs: does any part of the pipe touch Fed reserves or reverse repo?
13. US versus elsewhere: STRC-style products in other currencies (the talk expects 400 to 500 bp over each local risk-free rate).
14. Survivorship: one issuer with a 6-year Bitcoin track record and a "sophomore year" of credit (S43). Start-date choice in return tables.
15. Concentration: one founder with large voting control (coverage put Class B voting power near a third, to verify).
16. Who sells the Bitcoin: miners, long-term holders, ETFs. Whether buying "all new supply" moves price depends on them.

### What disagrees with the story
1. The flywheel in the talk is "triple" (credit, equity, reserves). The data say the equity leg has been near or below its break-even since at least August: company mNAV 1.01x in August and about 1.08x now, market cap below the Bitcoin held (about 0.86x). A secondary recap put management's accretion line near 1.2x (T4, to verify). If true, the pipe is currently powered by preferred stock (Set 1 cash), and leverage on the stack rises as it runs. This is the round's anomaly: the speaker describes a premium machine while the premium is roughly gone, and the dividend promise to Set 1 savers is what is growing.
2. "Six times over-collateralized" is close to the company's own 5.7x, but only under its ranking and netting method. The whole stack is about 3x gross. Both are true, they answer different questions.
3. "48 years of dividends" is a Bitcoin number. In dollars the cover is about 3 years, set by policy at a 12-month minimum. Saylor's own lesson (S24: buyers value three years of dollars over thirty years of Bitcoin) points to this gap.
4. Par is managed, not promised: STRC was $96.18 in August after a summer below par and $1.5B of buybacks (S23), and $99.85 on 28 October. Daily dividends do not change the total paid.
5. Consistent with the talk: "$10B of credit is about one year of supply" holds at $61,000 to $80,000 Bitcoin (about $13B a year now). Holdings near 4% of all Bitcoin also hold.

## 9. Radiant-map idea
"The pipe": a circular map with STRC at the center.
- Left half (Set 1 colour): the sources of cash, sized by pool (money funds, T-bills, deposits, stablecoins), with a thin arrow into the center sized by actual STRC and preferred flows, so the reader sees the pipe is a straw in a lake.
- Right half (Set 2 colour): the Bitcoin side, with the final sellers (miners, long-term holders, ETF redemptions) as the real destination of the dollars.
- Top arc: the wrapper (Delaware law, SEC shelf, Nasdaq, index funds, tokenized copies, daily dividends).
- Bottom arc: the reverse valve (dollar reserve, rate cuts on STRC, Bitcoin sales, buybacks below par), with gauges for mNAV, STRC price versus $100 and months of reserve.
- Outer ring: precedents placed like a clock (1720, 1929 trusts, 2000 MicroStrategy, 2002 zeros, 2021 to 2022 GBTC, 2022 lenders and Terra), each linked to the brake that broke it.
- A regime toggle (bull, bear, cut, hike, regulatory) that flips arrow directions and widths, and a counter-pipe drawn in the opposite direction for stablecoins buying T-bills, so "Set 1 absorbs Set 2" can be seen both ways at once.
